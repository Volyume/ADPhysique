1. What Volyume is

Volyume is a strength-training app with a coach built in. The free side is a serious training logbook: pick or build a plan, log every set, and watch your lifts and volume trend over months. The paid side (Pro) is the coach: it reads your training, your weight trend, and your food, then every week it adjusts your plan and your calorie and protein targets and tells you, in plain words, exactly what it changed and why.

It's Android-first, built with React Native and Expo, backed by a UK-hosted Supabase database, and it works offline: everything is logged on the phone first and synced to the cloud when there's a connection.

Roughly 70 screens, five bottom tabs, a deterministic coaching engine, and a full offline-sync layer. It is a large, mature codebase, not a prototype.

This boundary is enforced in code and was audited end to end: no leaks were found. A free user genuinely cannot reach the Pro screens.

The trial (your locked decision: 14 + 7)

  1. 14 days free, no card. A new account gets the full Pro coach for 14 days. No payment details asked. If they do nothing, on day 14 they drop to Free.
  2. 7 more days free, via Google. When they subscribe, Google Play adds its own 7-day free trial (card on file), then bills monthly. So the card is only ever asked for at day 14, never at signup.

Prices by launch window: £0.99/month (launch), £1.99 (founders), £3.99 (standard). Whoever signs up keeps their price for as long as they stay subscribed.

When a lapsed free user later taps "Upgrade", they go straight to the paywall and Google decides whether they still get the 7-day trial (first time) or pay immediately (already used it). That matches your "no grandfather" decision.

4. How people move through it

Free signup

Welcome → choose Free → create account → agree to the health-data consent → quick setup (name, units) → into the app. Pick a plan or build one, start logging. Progress charts fill in as they train.

Pro signup

Welcome → choose Pro → create account → health-data consent (this starts the 14-day trial) → a 5-step coach setup (profile, training, recovery, plan generated, nutrition generated) → a summary of the plan and targets → into the app with the coach live.

A normal training day

Open Train → tap the next session → log each set with the rest timer → finish → fill the short feedback form (how it felt, soreness, sleep) → see tonnage, muscle volume and any PRs → optionally share the card. It syncs in the background.

The weekly coaching loop PRO

Weigh in most mornings. Once a week, do the check-in (energy, sleep, soreness, how the week went, adherence). The coach reads that plus your training and weight trend and produces "Your week": what it changed (calories up/down, volume hold/push/cut, deload if needed), what it left alone, and why. It deliberately holds big changes until it has about two weeks of weigh-ins, so the first real adjustment lands around day 14.

Upgrading, cancelling, signing out, deleting

  • Upgrade: tap a locked Pro feature → the upgrade sheet → it routes to the paywall, where Google handles payment (with the 7-day trial if eligible).
  • Cancel: You → Subscription → Cancel deep-links into Google Play (Google requires their own screen). Pro stays until the paid period ends, then drops to Free.
  • Sign out: pushes any unsynced data to the cloud first, then wipes the phone's local copy. Sign in anywhere to get it back.
  • Delete account: two confirmations, then it deletes everything from the cloud and the phone.

5. The coaching brain

The "intelligence" is a set of deterministic engines, not a chatbot. Same inputs always give the same answer, which is what you want for coaching people can trust. In plain terms:

  • Plan engine — builds your training block from your goal, experience, equipment and weak points, with sets-per-muscle inside the science-backed range, and progresses the volume week to week.
  • Weekly coach — reads recovery (soreness × energy) against performance (adherence × PRs), and decides push, hold or cut volume, or call a deload. It holds its decision when it doesn't have enough weigh-ins yet, and says so.
  • Nutrition engine — works out maintenance calories and protein from your weight (and body-fat % if given), sets a sensible deficit or surplus, adjusts calories from your actual weight trend (capped, never on rapid loss), and suggests a diet break after long cuts.
  • Safety layer — watches for too-fast weight loss and under-fuelling, and can hold or lock changes. This is the eating-disorder safeguard, and it stays on for everyone.
  • Cardio & recovery — cardio adds to a fatigue signal the coach reads; calories burned are shown as feedback only, never added to your target.

7. Data & privacy

What it holds: workouts, body weight and measurements, the food diary, weekly check-ins (energy, sleep, mood), the eating-habits screening answers, steps, cardio, and anonymous usage stats. The body, food and check-in data is health-sensitive.

Where it lives: on the phone in storage the operating system encrypts, and in the cloud (Supabase, UK). Signing out wipes the phone's copy entirely.

Who can see it: only the user. Row-level security means one account can never read another's data. Crash reports go to Sentry with every sensitive field stripped off first (this was checked, the scrubbing is thorough). Google sees billing only. Food databases see only the barcode or search term, never your data.

Consent: a health-data consent screen blocks the app until the user agrees, and every agreement is logged for the audit trail. They can withdraw it later.

Identity: everyone has a real account (no anonymous mode), each row is tied to its owner and that link never changes, and signing out leaves nothing behind on the device.

The privacy posture is genuinely strong. The gaps below are about promises in the policy that the code doesn't yet keep, not about data leaking.

Check-in reminders use UTC for the week boundary SHOULD-FIX

One reminder calculation works out "which week is it" using UTC instead of UK local time. In British Summer Time, and around Sunday/Monday midnight, that can land the reminder a day off or fail to suppress a duplicate. This is exactly the timezone class of bug you've flagged before. Confirmed in the code.

"Deleted within 30 days" but the code deletes instantly SHOULD-FIX

The privacy text promises deletion within 30 days; the code actually hard-deletes everything immediately. Deleting faster than promised isn't a legal problem, but the wording should match reality (say "immediately") or the docs should agree. Several docs disagree on this (24 hours vs 30 days).

Promised under-16 age block isn't enforced SHOULD-FIX

The policy says under-16s are blocked at signup, but there's no age check anywhere in the signup flow. Either add the check or change the wording. For a health app this is worth getting right.

src/screens/LoginScreen.js / WelcomeScreen.js (no age gate found)

Eating-disorder detection isn't named in the consent text SHOULD-FIX

The app automatically watches for disordered-eating patterns (a good safety feature), but the consent screen mentions the screening questions without disclosing the automated flagging. Under GDPR, automated decisions on health data should be disclosed. Add a line to the consent and policy.

src/screens/Article9ConsentScreen.js · src/lib/edPatternDetector.js

If Google charges but the cloud write fails, status reconciles only later SHOULD-FIX

If a purchase succeeds at Google but the app's own record fails (poor connection), the user briefly looks un-upgraded until the payment webhook or next sync catches up. There's a Close button so they're not trapped, but a "we're confirming your purchase" state would be cleaner. The webhook (once deployed) is what makes this self-heal.

src/screens/PaywallScreen.js · CascadeGateScreen.js

Sign-out wipe has a 5-second escape hatch SHOULD-FIX

Sign-out pushes data up first and waits for sync to settle before wiping the phone, which is correct. But the wait gives up after 5 seconds, and in theory a slow sync could still be writing as the wipe starts. There's a guard in place; the safe hardening is a longer or smarter wait. Real but bounded, not the cross-account leak an automated pass feared.

src/store/useAppStore.js:332-371

A few sync edge cases could drop a row silently SHOULD-FIX

In some failure patterns (one workout in a batch fails while a later one succeeds; a chunk of sets fails its upload), the failure isn't always counted, so the system can believe everything synced when one item didn't. Rare, needs a real failure to trigger, but worth tightening the error counting since it touches user data.

Data export exists in code but has no button SHOULD-FIX

The policy offers data export (a GDPR right) and the code can produce it, but there's no visible Settings option to trigger it. Add the button, or the right isn't really exercisable.

9. Words & facts

The writing is in good shape: plain, direct, British English, no chatbot tells, terse error messages, no false encouragement, scientific claims kept to where they belong. The fixes are small and listed above (em dash in the store name, the "free during beta" listing, stale RevenueCat). One judgement call for you:

The Welcome screen badge says "Free for 14 days." That's the in-app trial length and it's accurate. But because the full journey is 21 days free (14 + 7), an automated pass called it a contradiction. It isn't wrong, but if you'd rather not anchor people on a single number before they see the full terms, change it to "Start free" and let the policy screen explain. Your call, low stakes.

10. What to fix to make it elite

Must-do before charging anyone

  1. Finish the payment rail: merchant account verified, the Pro product + 7-day offer created in Play Console, the payment webhook deployed (one env var), and one real test purchase on a device.
  2. Apply the pending database migrations (a short, additive batch: 059–065).
  3. Flip the beta switch off, last, on that verified build.
  4. Fix the compliance copy: the under-16 age check (add it or reword), the 30-day deletion wording (match the code), and name the eating-disorder detection in the consent text. Remove the em dash and the "free during beta" listing.

Should-do, before or just after launch

  1. Move the check-in week boundary from UTC to UK local time.
  2. Tighten the sync failure-counting and the sign-out wait so a row can never be silently dropped.
  3. Add the "confirming your purchase" state for the rare charge-but-not-recorded case (the webhook makes it self-heal once deployed).
  4. Add the data-export button.
  5. Turn on remote push (token table + send function) so payment-failure alerts reach people.

To genuinely feel elite

  • Drive the paywall's "7 days free vs pay now" wording from Google's real eligibility, so a returning user is never shown a trial they can't have.
  • A single onboarding line that sets the expectation: "log your weight daily; your first coaching adjustment lands in about two weeks." It's the highest-leverage change for turning trials into subscribers, because it ties week one to the moment they'll pay for.
  • A "support viewed your data" audit log before you hire support.
Bottom line: this is a deep, genuinely capable product with a real coaching engine, strong privacy, and clean tier gating. Nothing here is a rebuild. It's a finishing list, and most of it is yours to action (merchant account, deploys, the switch) rather than more code. Clear the must-do list and you have something you can confidently charge for.
