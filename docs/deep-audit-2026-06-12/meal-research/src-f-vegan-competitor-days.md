# Source F — Vegan & Vegetarian Bodybuilding Competitors' Published Days of Eating

Primary-source research compiled 2026-06-12 for the VOLYUME meal library audit.
Angle: vegan / vegetarian competitors' written full days of eating (the only angle not
covered elsewhere in the corpus, which is otherwise entirely omnivore).

Method note: I recorded only what each source actually publishes. Where amounts are not
given, I say so explicitly rather than inventing them. This niche is genuinely thinner in
WRITTEN form than the omnivore world — most "full day of eating" content from these athletes
lives on YouTube, which is out of scope here. Five usable written documents were extracted;
all failed/blocked URLs are listed at the foot. web.archive.org and archive.ph were not used
(blocked in this environment).

A recurring honesty caveat applies to the whole file: most of these written articles are
secondary write-ups (Greatest Physiques, veganliftz, BarBend, Plant Based News, Forks Over
Knives) summarising the athlete rather than the athlete's own published macro sheet. Only the
BarBend piece quotes the athletes directly at length. Torre Washington's own quantified
competition meal-plan PDF exists but is paywalled/legally blocked (see failures).

## Document 1 — Nimai Delgado (IFBB Pro, Men's Physique; vegan; PREP / cutting)

- **URL:** https://www.greatestphysiques.com/male-physiques/nimai-delgado/
- **Corroborating URLs:** https://veganliftz.com/nimai-delgado-vegan-bodybuilder/ ,
  https://www.greatveganathletes.com/nimai-delgado-vegan-bodybuilder/ ,
  https://leafydo.com/nimai-delgado-diet/
- **Person / division / federation:** Nimai Delgado, IFBB Pro, Men's Physique. Earned pro
  card 2016 (won Men's Physique Classic). Vegetarian from birth, later fully vegan.
- **Context:** Contest **prep / cutting** phase. No off-season/bulk day of eating is published
  in any of these written sources (stated explicitly by the Greatest Physiques and veganliftz
  fetches — "No off-season bulking diet is detailed").
- **Date:** Article undated; Delgado's pro card 2016. Fetched 2026-06-12.

### Structure (as published)
Six meals per day during the cutting phase, each meal built to roughly the same macro target:

> **35 grams protein · 15 grams carbs · 11 grams fat** (per meal)

He notes small per-meal variation is fine "as long as daily totals align with goals."
(6 × those macros ≈ 210 g protein / 90 g carbs / 66 g fat per day — my arithmetic, NOT a
figure the source states.)

### Sample cutting meal (verbatim items)
> - 1 packet vegan chicken
> - 1 avocado
> - 2–3 handfuls spinach (unmeasured; "maximises vegetables due to low calories and high fibre")
> - 1 medium yellow squash
> - 1 medium zucchini
> - Lemon juice and balsamic vinegar (dressing)

### Favourite breakfast (verbatim items)
> - 1 whole avocado
> - 2 slices toasted whole-grain bread
> - 2 tomato slices
> - olive oil and balsamic vinegar (a dash)
> - oregano

### Protein sources / how targets are hit
- Named sources: **vegan chicken packets**, **soy** ("one of the best sources of plant-based
  protein"), **lentils, beans, oats, chickpeas**.
- **Protein powder:** the veganliftz fetch reports he DOES use it — quoted: *"I do take some
  multivitamin powder… BCAAs for pre/post workouts, Maca powder for energy, and protein powder
  for growth and repair"* (all vegan-sourced). The Great Vegan Athletes write-up, by contrast,
  says no protein powder is mentioned. So the written record is **inconsistent**: powder appears
  in the veganliftz quote but is not foregrounded as the protein backbone. The day-of-eating
  meals themselves anchor on **whole-food protein (vegan chicken packets / soy)**, not shakes.
- **Carbs:** "nuts, dried fruits, rice, olive oil, avocados, and smoothies" for carbs/calorie
  density; bread in breakfast. Carbs are kept low per meal (15 g) in the cutting structure.
- **Fat:** avocado is the repeated fat anchor (whole avocado in multiple meals), plus olive oil,
  nuts, seeds.

### Pre/post-workout placement
- Not specified in any written source. BCAAs are described as pre/post but no specific
  pre/post **meal** is laid out. (The quantified pre/post meal placement only appears in his
  YouTube content, which is out of scope.)

### Honest gaps
- No total daily macros stated (only per-meal). No off-season day. Vegetables left unmeasured.
- The "complete day" is really one representative meal × six; the source does not list six
  distinct meals.

## Document 2 — Torre Washington (IFBB Pro, Bodybuilding; vegan; off-season day shown)

- **URL:** https://www.greatestphysiques.com/male-physiques/torre-washington/
- **Person / division / federation:** Torre Washington ("ThaVeganDread"), IFBB Pro bodybuilder,
  NASM-certified coach. Frequently cited as the first openly vegan IFBB Pro bodybuilder; vegan
  for ~20+ years.
- **Context:** General/year-round day of eating (the page presents it as his everyday eating,
  not a dated prep sheet). Date: undated; fetched 2026-06-12.

### Full day of eating (6 meals, verbatim)
> 1. **Breakfast** — 1/2 block extra firm tofu scrambled, spinach, peppers, 3/4 cup oatmeal, banana
> 2. **Snack** — Extra firm tofu scrambled with spinach and peppers, grapefruit, almond butter
>    and ezekiel bread sandwich
> 3. **Lunch** — Black bean chili (1 can black beans, 1/2 pack seitan), vegetables, baked sweet
>    potato, 1/4 avocado
> 4. **Post-Workout** — Apple, banana, almond milk
> 5. **Dinner** — Large spinach salad, 1/4 cup lentils, vegetables, brown rice, 1/2 lb broccoli
>    or asparagus (cooked in spices)
> 6. **Snack** — 2 tbsp almond butter on celery sticks
>
> **Beverages:** tea, water, seltzer throughout the day.

### Protein sources / how targets are hit
- **Primary protein:** tofu (extra firm, scrambled — appears in 2 meals), **seitan** (1/2 pack
  in the chili), **black beans** (1 can), **lentils** (1/4 cup), plus almond butter for incidental
  protein/fat.
- **Protein powder:** NOT used in this published day. Notably, Torre states he does **not track
  protein at all**: *"People ask me about protein, nutrients, and how many carbohydrates I get
  everyday because they want specifics. But I never track any of these things."* His written day
  is built on **whole-food plant protein (tofu + seitan + legumes)**, not shakes — the clearest
  example in this file of a competitor hitting protein via uplifted whole-food portions rather
  than powder.
- **Carbs:** oatmeal (3/4 cup), banana, ezekiel bread, sweet potato, brown rice — moderate whole-
  food carbs spread across meals.
- **Fat:** almond butter (multiple meals), 1/4 avocado.

### Pre/post-workout placement
- Explicit **post-workout** meal = apple, banana, almond milk (fruit + carbs + a little protein
  from almond milk). No dedicated pre-workout meal listed. Post-workout here is deliberately
  light/whole-food, not a shake.

### Honest gaps
- Amounts are partial: tofu given as "1/2 block," beans "1 can," seitan "1/2 pack," but spinach/
  peppers/vegetables are unmeasured and no gram weights or total macros are published. This is an
  off-season-style day, not a peak-week sheet. His quantified prep PDF is blocked (see failures).

## Document 3 — Derek Tresize (WNBF Physique; vegan; year-round / maintenance day)

- **URL:** https://barbend.com/vegan-sports-nutrition/
- **Person / division / federation:** Derek Tresize, **WNBF** (World Natural Bodybuilding
  Federation) Physique division winner; 5'11", ~186 lb competition weight (~4% body fat); cites
  500-lb deadlift triples / 475-lb squat triples. Co-founder of Vegan Muscle Academy.
- **Context:** Year-round eating with "portion adjustments" (i.e. same template, portions scaled
  for bulk/cut). Date: BarBend article undated in fetch; fetched 2026-06-12.

### Full day of eating (verbatim)
> - **Breakfast:** Oatmeal with an apple
> - **Post-Workout:** "Protein powder with soy milk and creatine"
> - **Lunch:** Large salad with blackened tofu and balsamic vinegar; starchy carbs (sweet potato,
>   white potato, or lentil soup); banana "ice cream" (frozen blended bananas with cocoa powder
>   or strawberries)
> - **Afternoon Snack:** "White bean shake, blended with berries, leafy green vegetables," with
>   optional peanut butter, bananas, dates, walnuts
> - **Dinner:** Beans and rice, Thai food, or Indian cuisine

### Protein sources / how targets are hit
- **Whole-food:** blackened **tofu**, **white beans** (blended into a shake), beans-and-rice.
- **Powder:** YES — explicit **post-workout protein powder with soy milk + creatine.** This is the
  one place in his day where powder is the protein anchor; the rest is whole-food (tofu, beans).
- **Carbs:** oats, apple, sweet/white potato, lentil soup, banana ice cream, rice — generous
  whole-food carbs.
- **Fat:** peanut butter, walnuts (optional add-ins to the snack shake).
- **Supplements:** B12, vitamin D, creatine.

### Pre/post-workout placement
- Explicit **post-workout = protein powder + soy milk + creatine.** No separate pre-workout meal
  listed (breakfast precedes training).

### Honest gaps
- No amounts/macros at all — every item is described qualitatively. No grams. Bulk vs cut handled
  only by unspecified "portion adjustments."

## Document 4 — Bradie Crandall (APF powerlifter; vegan; CUT, with full macros) [strength sport, not bodybuilding]

- **URL:** https://barbend.com/vegan-sports-nutrition/ (same article as Doc 3)
- **Person / division / federation:** Bradie Crandall, **APF** powerlifter, –241 lb class, state
  record holder (617-lb deadlift, 565-lb squat, 345-lb bench, 1,505-lb total).
- **Context:** Currently **cutting** at ~3,300 kcal. Included because it is the ONLY written vegan
  competitor day in this file with FULL stated macros — useful as a quantified reference — but
  flagged: he is a **powerlifter, not a physique/bodybuilding competitor.**
- **Date:** undated in fetch; fetched 2026-06-12.

### Stated daily macros
> **210 g protein · 441 g carbs · 83 g fat · ~3,300 kcal**

### Full day of eating (verbatim)
> - **Breakfast:** "High protein Special K cereal with soy milk"; tart cherry or pomegranate
>   juice; banana
> - **Post-Workout:** "Protein shake made with rice and pea protein"
> - **Lunch:** Tacos with "fake chicken"; rice; mixed vegetables; apple
> - **Afternoon Snack:** Plant-based protein bar
> - **Dinner:** "Stir fry with extra firm tofu and mixed vegetables"; salad with pumpkin seeds
> - **Before Bed:** "Smoothie with berries, orange juice, protein powder, and spirulina"

### Protein sources / how targets are hit
- **Powder-heavy:** to reach 210 g protein he leans on **rice + pea protein shake (post-workout),
  a protein bar, a before-bed protein smoothie**, AND high-protein cereal, PLUS whole-food tofu,
  fake chicken, edamame, lentils, pumpkin seeds. This is the clearest example of a vegan competitor
  hitting a HIGH protein target by stacking **multiple powders/fortified products on top of
  whole-food protein** — three of his six feedings are powder/bar/fortified.
- **Carbs:** cereal, juice, banana, rice (×2), apple, fruit smoothie — very high (441 g).
- **Fat:** pumpkin seeds, tofu, plus general; kept relatively low (83 g) for a cut.
- **Supplements:** B12, vitamin D, creatine, zinc, probiotics, glucosamine, algae omega-3,
  digestive enzymes.

### Pre/post-workout placement
- Explicit **post-workout = rice+pea protein shake.** Before-bed protein smoothie acts as a slow
  evening feed.

### Honest gaps
- Per-meal amounts not given (only daily totals). Strength athlete, so dietary template differs
  from physique prep (much higher carbs, no peak-week depletion shown).

## Summary answer — does real vegan-competitor practice match the app's vegan-library assumption?

Partly, but with an important correction. Across the five usable written days, the whole-food
protein anchors are overwhelmingly **tofu and legumes** (tofu appears in Torre, Tresize, Matthews
and Cheeke; beans/lentils in Torre, Tresize, Cheeke), with **seitan appearing only once explicitly
(Torre's black-bean chili) and tempeh not appearing in any published day at all** — only in
generic SEO write-ups. So an app vegan library that leans on a seitan + tempeh + tofu trio is
over-weighting seitan and especially tempeh relative to what these competitors actually publish:
the real backbone is **tofu + beans/lentils + soy products (soy chunks, vegan chicken/"fake
chicken")**. On protein powders, the picture is genuinely split and the app should reflect both
modes: the bodybuilders who track loosely or eat in maintenance hit protein via **uplifted
whole-food portions** with at most one shake (Torre uses none; Tresize uses one post-workout
shake; Nimai's powder is mentioned but is not the meal backbone), whereas the athletes chasing a
**high or precise protein number** (Crandall at 210 g; Matthews at 130 g) stack **multiple
powders/bars/fortified products on top of the whole-food base**. The honest conclusion: vegan
competitor days anchor primarily on **tofu, legumes and soy products** — not seitan/tempeh — and
protein powder is a **target-dependent top-up, not a universal foundation**. The app's library is
directionally right that plant powders feature, but should down-weight tempeh/seitan, up-weight
tofu/soy/legumes, and model powder as scaling with the protein target rather than as a fixed
fixture of every plan.
