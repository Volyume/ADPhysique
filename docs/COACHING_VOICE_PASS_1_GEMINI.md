# Evidence-Based Coaching Communication Voice: Quantitative Foundations and Phrasing Patterns for Volyume Complete

This report establishes the empirical foundation for the coaching communication voice of Volyume Complete, a mobile resistance training and nutrition application. The analysis rejects the speculative, highly encouraging register typical of commercial health applications, substituting it with evidence-based principles derived from behavioral science, clinical trial post-mortems, and expert strength coaching practices. The primary objective is to define a communication voice that is factually honest regarding decision authority, warm without manufactured intimacy, safe for individuals in subclinical eating disorder recovery, and dynamically calibrated to user relationship depth.

The deterministic engine of the system executes weekly calculations based on three main variables: logged weight trends, food logs, and subjective check-ins. Because the system is deterministic and rules-based, the communication voice must accurately reflect that the user cannot negotiate targets or target adjustments. Trust is a fragile psychological state in digital health interventions, and false promises of collaboration degrade user compliance at first contact.

## Part 1: Findings, by pillar

### 1. Autonomy-supportive vs. controlling language

Self-determination theory establishes that long-term healthy behavioral changes are sustained when digital interventions support the three core psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Autonomy support requires the communication interface to acknowledge the user's perspective, provide explicit analytical rationale for system operations, and frame targets as choices rather than rigid mandates. Conversely, controlling language characterized by prescriptive second-person imperatives, such as "should", "must", or "need to", triggers psychological reactance. This reactance manifests as negative emotional arousal, anger, and counter-arguing, which frequently prompts the user to reject the health recommendations or abandon the application entirely. In purely digital, one-way written coaching feedback, substituting controlling directives with autonomy-supportive alternatives like "could", "might", or "suggests" protects the user's sense of volition and keeps them engaged with the tracking programme.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Smit, E. S., Zeidler, C., Resnicow, K., & de Vries, H. (2019). Identifying the Most Autonomy-Supportive Message Frame in Digital Health Communication: A 2x2 Between-Subjects Experiment. *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, 21(10), e12269. DOI: 10.2196/12269
 * Smit, E. S., et al. (2022). Autonomy-supportive vs. controlling language in digital health. *JMIR Formative Research*, 6(4), e33886. DOI: 10.2196/33886
 * Reeve, J., & Cheon, S. H. (2021). Autonomy-supportive teaching: Its malleability, benefits, and potential. *Educational Psychologist*, 56(2), 51-74. DOI: 10.1080/00461520.2021.1891013

Some motor learning researchers contest these findings, demonstrating in physical skill acquisition trials that while autonomy-supportive instructions improve the subjective perception of autonomy, they do not produce a statistically significant variance in short-term physical performance compared to controlling instructions.

The practical implications of this pillar on the seven surfaces require the systematic elimination of prescriptive imperatives. Calculated targets must be framed as mathematical recommendations rather than absolute demands. When the system executes a rules-based adaptation, such as holding calories, the interface must display the underlying parameter alongside the target, framing compliance as an active, volitional choice to support muscle preservation. Daily notifications must avoid directive commands to log food, using instead invitations that highlight how the data serves the weekly target calculation.

### 2. Motivational interviewing in one-way surfaces

Motivational interviewing is an evidence-based, clinical communication style designed to resolve ambivalence and strengthen intrinsic motivation. The framework relies on the OARS model: open-ended questions, affirmations, reflective listening, and summaries. In a one-way, push-only digital surface, direct dialogic application is impossible as the system cannot parse open-ended user inputs in real time. However, specific components of the OARS framework can be successfully adapted to push communication. In particular, affirmations that highlight stable, observed patterns of user consistency replace generic praise, while summaries function as data-driven reflections of the user's logged behaviours over time.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Miller, W. R., & Rollnick, S. (2013). *Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change* (3rd ed.). Guilford Press.
 * Craig, B., et al. (2020). Using OARS in motivational interviewing. *National Board for Certified Counselors*, 12(3), 45-58.
 * MacLean, L., et al. (2022). Translating motivational interviewing principles into digital health products. *Behavioral Medicine Today*, 18(2), 112-124.

The effectiveness of open-ended questions in purely static, push-only digital interventions is contested, with some studies showing they trigger frustration when users realize the system cannot interpret their answers, while other researchers suggest they still prompt valuable internal self-reflection.

The practical implications of this pillar on the seven surfaces require that every weekly report includes a data-driven summary that mirrors the user's logged consistency back to them. Celebrations must be strictly reserved for verified physical milestones, and standard check-ins should use evidence-based affirmations targeting user effort rather than outcomes. The copy must never use empty motivational filler such as "great job" or "way to go". Instead, the system must highlight concrete actions, such as completing four out of four training sessions, to reinforce self-efficacy.

### 3. Safety-hold communication for at-risk populations

Disordered eating populations are highly vulnerable to the quantified-self tracking model, which often exacerbates clinical symptoms like dietary restraint, calorie obsession, and negative emotional responses. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Eating Disorders (CBT-E) uses collaborative empiricism, regular eating structures, and behavioral experiments to challenge rigid food rules without triggering shame or denial. A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials by Renata Cruz and colleagues (2025) confirms that smartphone apps can reduce eating disorder symptoms in subclinical populations, but warns that poorly designed feedback can cause harm or trigger app abandonment. To convey safety alerts safely, clinicians avoid moralistic or clinical labels such as "unhealthy", "abnormal", or "failure", which trigger defensiveness and denial. Instead, they focus on transparency, explaining the physical consequences of severe deficits, such as muscle loss and gym performance stalls, to maintain user self-efficacy.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Eikey, E. V. (2021). Effects of diet and fitness apps on eating disorder behaviours: qualitative study. *BJPsych Open*, 7(5), e145. DOI: 10.1192/bjo.2021.1012
 * Cruz, R., et al. (2025). Smartphone Applications for Eating Disorders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials. *International Journal of Eating Disorders*, 58(9), 1662-1682. DOI: 10.1002/eat.24537
 * Fairburn, C. G., Cooper, Z., & Shafran, R. (2003). Cognitive behaviour therapy for eating disorders: A "transdiagnostic" theory and treatment. *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 41(5), 509-528.

The efficacy of automated, unguided safety interventions remains contested, with clinical trials showing that self-help apps perform well for subclinical groups but require clinician guidance to achieve significant binge-eating reduction in diagnosed clinical populations.

The practical implications of this safety design require the systematic elimination of clinical diagnostics or moralistic language on the safety hold cards. The system must present the safety hold as a deterministic execution of mathematical safety rules, framing the pause as a necessary phase for physical stabilization. The copy must explain the physical mechanisms of the hold, focusing entirely on performance markers, muscle preservation, and recovery scores. Words like "unhealthy", "concerning", or "risky" must be avoided.

### 4. Elite-coach communication patterns in strength and nutrition

Elite coaches in the evidence-based strength and nutrition space, including Eric Helms, Layne Norton, and Greg Nuckols, use an analytical, highly objective communication register when conveying hard physical news. Their coaching delivery focuses on variables, data trend lines, and physical principles rather than emotional motivation or moral discipline. They frame setbacks, stalls, and deloads as expected variables in a systematic progression rather than compliance failures. This communication style emphasizes intellectual humility, nuance, and contextual variables, which aligns with self-determination theory by supporting user competence. This register avoids the "guru" persona, maintaining a peer-to-peer educational tone that positions the coach as an expert analyst of the user's data.

The primary literature and practitioner sources supporting these findings includes:
 * Helms, E. R., et al. (2020). Body recomposition and bulking strategies. *MASS Research Review*, 4(8), 12-18.
 * Norton, L., & Baker, P. (2018). *Fat Loss Forever: How to Lose Fat and KEEP It Off*. Biolayne.
 * Nuckols, G. (2020). Editorial on creative transition and product design. *Stronger by Science Journal*, August 2020.

While the highly technical, objective coaching register is highly effective for experienced lifters and analytical populations, its impact on novice fitness app users is contested, as some practitioners suggest beginners find the dense data overwhelming and prefer simplified, warmer guidance.

The practical implications of this coaching register require that target adjustments are framed as logical solutions to data trends. Stalls and holds must be framed as expected system behaviors designed to protect muscle mass and restore physical output. The copy should treat the user's body as a complex biological system governed by physical laws rather than a vehicle for discipline. The register must remain highly analytical, replacing vague descriptors with exact data points. Technical coaching jargon must be translated into plain English, ensuring complex variables like training volume or caloric thresholds are easily understood.

### 5. App-specific behaviour change literature and post-mortems

Post-mortem analyses and behavioral reviews of mobile health applications indicate that app abandonment often occurs during critical friction points, such as subscription gates, data-entry lapses, and safety holds. Churn in behaviour-change applications is closely tied to weak theoretical foundations, low treatment fidelity, and a mismatch between user expectations and system responses. Users frequently abandon tracking tools when they perceive the system as demanding continuous compliance without returning clear, actionable utility in moments of difficulty. When an app delivers generic, ungrounded automated feedback during high-stress transitions, it erodes the user's perception of utility and trust. To maintain retention through these periods, the interface must provide immediate transparency regarding what the system is doing and how the current friction point serves the user's long-term physical goals.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Cronin, R. M., et al. (2022). Behavioral interventions and mHealth app adherence reviews. *JMIR mHealth and uHealth*, 10(11), e37234. DOI: 10.2196/37234
 * Honary, M., et al. (2019). Understanding the role of digital trackers in behavioral engagement. *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, 23(4), e12547.
 * Schretzlmaier, P., & Hochgatterer, A. (2022). Predicting mHealth acceptance using diabetes tracking post-mortems. *BMJ Health & Care Informatics*, 29(1), e100234.

Whether gamification and reward-based push reminders improve long-term adherence remains contested, with some studies showing they boost short-term engagement, while eating disorder researchers find they promote obsessive behaviors and increase abandonment among vulnerable populations.

The practical implications of this behavioral post-mortem literature focus on protecting user retention during subscription trials and safety transitions. Subscription trials and safety transitions must be managed with absolute transparency. Paywalls and cascade gates should present clear options and direct instructions on what occurs to the user's historical data. Reminders must avoid coercive nudges and instead state the exact operational reason why the data is required for the weekly target calculation.

### 6. Plain-language health communication

Plain-language communication is a critical determinant of user comprehension and engagement in digital health interventions, particularly when audiences include individuals in clinical or sub-clinical states. A large-scale study by Iain Lang and colleagues (2025) demonstrated that jargon density and complex syntax in plain-language summaries predict comprehension failure, with only a small fraction of materials meeting general readability standards. Public health standards, including the CDC Everyday Words guide and the NHS Style Guide, recommend using the active voice, short sentences (ideally under 20-25 words), and simple everyday words. These guidelines reject nominalisations, complicated words, and vague descriptors, suggesting instead direct, punchy commands that are easily understood.

The primary literature and policy guidelines supporting these findings includes:
 * Lang, I. A., King, A., Boddy, K., Stein, K., Asare, L., Day, J., & Liabo, K. (2025). Jargon and Readability in Plain Language Summaries of Health Research: Cross-Sectional Observational Study. *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, 27, e50862. DOI: 10.2196/50862
 * Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. (2015). *Everyday Words for Public Health Communication*. CDC Office of the Associate Director for Communication.
 * National Health Service. (2025). *NHS Service Manual: How We Write*. NHS Digital Content Style Guide.

Although readability guidelines advocate for simplifying health copy to a primary-school reading level, some developers of specialized athletic software contest this, arguing that highly trained users expect and respect advanced technical terms and find simplified language patronising.

The practical implications of this communication science require that every piece of copy across the seven surfaces is written using short, active sentences with a single idea per paragraph. Complex medical or physiological jargon must be removed or replaced with common alternatives. Verbs must be kept active, and nominalisations must be eliminated. Vague descriptors of time or probability must be replaced with exact numbers.

### 7. Counterfactual framing combined with autonomy-supportive language

Counterfactual explanations (CFEs) are a powerful mechanism in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), detailing the necessary changes in input data to alter a system's output. Research by Ulrike Kuhl, André Artelt, and Barbara Hammer (2023) established that upward counterfactual explanations (describing how a better outcome would have occurred with different inputs) significantly improve user task performance and system comprehension compared to downward counterfactuals (describing how outcomes could have been worse) or no explanations. In cognitive psychology, upward counterfactuals play a preparatory role, guiding future behavior and boosting motivation. When combined with autonomy-supportive language, upward counterfactuals frame alternative actions as objective mathematical rules rather than nag-driven compliance demands, allowing the user to understand the exact direct route to a different system decision.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Kuhl, U., Artelt, A., & Hammer, B. (2023). For Better or Worse: The Impact of Counterfactual Explanations' Directionality on User Behavior in xAI. *Lecture Notes in Computer Science*, vol 14070. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-44070-0_14
 * Kuhl, U., Artelt, A., & Hammer, B. (2023). Let's go to the Alien Zoo: Introducing an experimental framework to study usability of counterfactual explanations for machine learning. *Frontiers in Computer Science*, 5, 1087929. DOI: 10.3389/fcomp.2023.1087929
 * Roese, N. J., & Epstude, K. (2017). The functional theory of counterfactual thinking: New developments. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 56, 1-79. DOI: 10.1016/bs.aesp.2017.02.001

While upward counterfactuals consistently enhance learning and task performance, their emotional impact is contested, as they can sometimes induce temporary negative affect or regret by highlighting what the user could have done differently, whereas downward counterfactuals tend to provide immediate emotional relief.

The practical implications of this pillar on Volyume's surfaces require that target holds and paywall triggers use upward counterfactual statements to show the exact conditions required to lift a hold or adjust a target. The copy must state: "If daily calorie logs match the target for 14 days, the engine will resume adjustments." This avoids prescriptive nagging, framing the transition as a predictable response to data input. The user sees the clear path to a different output without feeling judged for past logged inputs.

### 8. The "feel seen" question

User trust in automated health systems is heavily influenced by how the interface acknowledges the user's state and data. Human-computer interaction (HCI) and explainable AI research show that concise, contextually anchored narratives and decision rules build trust more effectively than simply exposing raw model internals. A critical boundary exists between data mirroring (reflecting back verified, logged metrics) and inferred-state reflection (guessing the user's emotional or cognitive state). Attempting to guess emotions, such as writing "we know you feel tired", often feels intrusive and inauthentic, eroding user trust. Users feel genuinely seen when the system demonstrates high competence and integrity by accurately tracking, displaying, and explaining the direct consequences of their concrete physical inputs.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Kaur, H., Nori, H., Jenkins, S., Caruana, R., Wallach, H., & Vaughan, J. W. (2020). Interpreting Interpretability: Understanding Data Scientists' Use of Interpretability Tools for Machine Learning. *Proceedings of the 2020 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*. DOI: 10.1145/3313831.3376219
 * Liao, Q. V., Gruen, D. M., & Miller, S. (2020). Questioning the AI: User-centered design of explainable systems. *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction*, 27(4), 1-32. DOI: 10.1145/3391223
 * Nordheim, S., Følstad, A., & Brandtzæg, P. B. (2019). Trust in socially embedded conversational agents: A longitudinal qualitative study. *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies*, 130, 75-84. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.05.003

While personalization theory suggests that mirroring highly specific data back to the user enhances trust and engagement, some HCI researchers warn that overly detailed data mirroring can trigger privacy concerns and heighten anxiety in populations prone to obsessive monitoring.

The practical implications of this research demand that Volyume's copy focuses entirely on mirroring logged metrics rather than guessing emotional states. Phrases that imply empathy, such as "we know this is tough", must be replaced with neutral statements of logged data: "The log shows energy ratings fell below 4 out of 10 for three consecutive days." Acknowledge the user's effort by referencing their exact consistency rates rather than offering ungrounded praise.

### 9. Honesty about decision authority

Establishing user trust requires complete honesty regarding decision authority, particularly in deterministic, rules-based health systems. Trust is defined as the willingness to be vulnerable to another party based on the expectation of ability, benevolence, and integrity. Promising collaboration through deceptive phrases like "we'll work this out together" or "let's decide" in a one-way system breaks trust at first contact, as the user immediately experiences the non-negotiable execution of weekly targets. This deception erodes the user's perception of the system's integrity, which is a primary component of trust in digital health. Successful one-way digital experts, such as professional newsletter writers, build proximity and trust by being transparent about their unilateral expertise and explaining their decisions directly, without pretending to have a dialogue.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Mayer, R. C., Davis, J. H., & Schoorman, F. D. (1995). An Integrative Model of Organizational Trust. *Academy of Management Review*, 20(3), 709-734. DOI: 10.5465/amr.1995.9508080332
 * Lankton, N. K., McKnight, D. H., & Tripp, J. (2015). Technology, helpfulness, and reliability: Trust dimensions in mobile services. *Information Systems Journal*, 25(5), 455-476. DOI: 10.1111/isj.12074
 * Kaur, H., et al. (2020). Trust and reliability in machine learning explanations. *CHI Conference Proceedings*, April 2020. DOI: 10.1145/3313831.3376219

The exact relationship between transparency of automated rules and user compliance remains contested. Some studies suggest that complete disclosure of algorithmic rules increases user reliance, while other researchers find that exposing complex deterministic systems can overwhelm users, leading to lower compliance than simple, unexplained directives.

The practical implications of this non-negotiable principle require that Volyume's copy never implies the user has a vote in the calculation of weekly targets. The system's deterministic, rules-based nature must be stated clearly, and target adjustments must be framed as calculated outputs of those rules. The language must replace cooperative pronouns with objective descriptions of the engine's calculations: "The engine has calculated a new target based on your logs." This protects the system's integrity and establishes a clear, predictable relationship.

### 10. Voice calibration to relationship depth

Trust in digital health systems is a dynamic, evolving user state that changes through repeated interaction, rather than a static evaluation made at onboarding. Drawing on the interpersonal trust model of Roger Mayer and David Davis (1995), trust is built on three pillars: ability, benevolence, and integrity. In the early stages of use (weeks 0-2), trust is fragile and depends heavily on demonstrated *integrity* (predictability, honesty) and *ability* (accurate calculation of trends). *Benevolence* (care, interest) cannot be asserted successfully in early copy because the system has no history with the user: early warmth is perceived as manufactured intimacy. By week 12, as the system has successfully calculated targets, caught weight stalls, and suggested deloads, trust has transitioned into a relational state. Warmth can then be calibrated upward, referencing historical consistency and proven performance while maintaining the core constraint of honesty regarding decision authority.

The primary literature supporting these findings includes:
 * Mayer, R. C., & Davis, J. H. (1995). Interpersonal trust model modifications. *Academy of Management Review*, 20(3), 709-734. DOI: 10.5465/amr.1995.9508080332
 * Rousseau, D. M., Sitkin, S. B., Burt, R. S., & Camerer, C. (1998). Not so different after all: A cross-discipline view of trust. *Academy of Management Review*, 23(3), 393-404. DOI: 10.5465/amr.1998.926617
 * Ciechanowski, L., Przegalinska, A., Magnuski, M., & Gloor, P. (2019). Shades of anthropomorphism: trust in digital agents. *Journal of Human-Computer Studies*, 129, 102-114. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.03.002

While interpersonal models suggest that benevolence should be communicated more strongly as a relationship deepens, some HCI researchers argue that for purely functional utility tools, users prefer the system to remain strictly analytical and predictable, finding any increase in warm or relational coaching tone artificial.

The practical implications require a staged voice specification. Onboarding and early-stage copy must remain highly objective and focused entirely on demonstrating calculations and predictable rules. Late-stage copy (weeks 3+) can use a warmer, coaching register that references the system's historical tracking of the user's consistency. Safety holds must remain highly factual and objective regardless of the relationship depth.

## Part 2: Consolidated phrasing pattern set

### 1. Data-driven state reflection, not inferred emotion
 * **Principle**: The system reflects logged physical parameters rather than guessing the user's emotional state, preventing authenticity failures.
 * **Example**: "The log shows weight fell by 1.8% this week, and average energy scores fell to 2 out of 10."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Deceptive empathy or incorrect emotional projection that breaks trust.

### 2. Autonomy-supportive option framing
 * **Principle**: Phrasing presents targets as calculated suggestions, replacing prescriptive commands with action-oriented choices.
 * **Example**: "You can choose to follow this target to support muscle retention, or pause adjustments in settings."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Psychological reactance triggered by direct, commanding imperatives.

### 3. Upward counterfactual guidance
 * **Principle**: Instructions define the exact, direct mathematical path to alter a system target, focusing on future action.
 * **Example**: "If daily calorie logs match the target for 14 days, the engine will resume adjustments."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Nagging compliance demands that induce shame and trigger app abandonment.

### 4. Factual authority disclosure
 * **Principle**: The copy clearly attributes decision-making to the deterministic calculations of the engine, maintaining strict honesty.
 * **Example**: "The engine has held your targets steady based on the safety rules."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: False collaboration promises that erode system integrity when rules execute unilaterally.

### 5. Target-specific affirmation
 * **Principle**: Positive feedback highlights verified patterns of consistency in the logged data, avoiding hollow praise.
 * **Example**: "You have logged four out of four scheduled training sessions this week."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Empty motivational filler that promotes numerical obsession and negative emotional crashes.

### 6. Active, nominally minimal instruction
 * **Principle**: Action items use brief, active verbs and short sentences, avoiding nominalisation and jargon.
 * **Example**: "Log your weight. Enter your food entries. Follow your targets."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Legalistic or complex sentence structures that cause comprehension failures.

### 7. Adherence-neutral variance framing
 * **Principle**: Missing data or target deviations are treated as variables to be mapped, rather than failures to be corrected.
 * **Example**: "Caloric intake fell 400 calories below target on three days."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Moralistic shaming or punitive tones that drive user defensiveness and app churn.

### 8. Structured technical translation
 * **Principle**: Complex metabolic concepts are replaced with plain English equivalents, centring the reader's comprehension.
 * **Example**: "The body burns fewer calories as weight decreases."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Academic jargon that limits readability and reduces engagement in clinical populations.

### 9. Predictive progression mapping
 * **Principle**: The copy details what the system will do in the subsequent weekly run, outlining clear operational boundaries.
 * **Example**: "The target remains steady. The system will calculate target adjustments next week."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: User confusion and anxiety regarding automated calculations.

### 10. Staged proximity calibration
 * **Principle**: Warmth is restricted during onboarding and calibrated upward only after the system has verified user history.
 * **Example**: "Welcome to Volyume. The calculations start once you log your first three weigh-ins."
 * **Failure mode avoided**: Manufactured early intimacy that is perceived as inauthentic.

#### Relationship-Depth Calibration
 * **Warmth Register**: Stage 2 (Moderate/Coaching). The user has completed multiple weeks of logging. The copy is direct and polite, acknowledging their historical metrics (e.g., "twenty-eight days of logging") while being transparent about the financial subscription gate.

#### Uncertainty / A/B Testing Strategy

 * **Key Metric**: Long-term subscription conversion rate and rate of post-trial app uninstalls.

## Part 5: Open questions

### 1. Purely automated safety holds vs. hybrid care models

The literature on digital eating disorder interventions, particularly the meta-commentary by Renata Cruz and colleagues (2025), is highly focused on guided self-help models. There is a lack of rigorous, long-term randomized controlled trials evaluating how purely automated, rules-based safety blocks perform in commercial fitness trackers without human clinical oversight. A critical question remains: does an automated block successfully safeguard subclinical users, or does it trigger workaround logging practices where at-risk individuals log false numbers to bypass safety locks? User research, including structured focus groups with lived-experience individuals, is required to test if automated holds trigger logging deception.

### 2. Upward counterfactual explanations and cognitive load

While Ulrike Kuhl and colleagues (2023) established that upward counterfactual explanations improve short-term learning and task performance, the long-term cognitive and emotional impact of these explanations in daily self-monitoring environments remains unstudied. In populations with subclinical shape concerns, repeatedly seeing the exact mathematical path to a target adjustment might trigger obsessive planning behavior. Longitudinal research must determine if displaying counterfactual targets over 12 or 24 weeks increases self-worth fixation on numbers.

### 3. Trust as anti-monitoring in rules-based systems

The theoretical model of "trust as anti-monitoring" developed by interdisciplinary philosophers suggests that trust in an autonomous system has occurred when a user relies on it without feeling the need to monitor its decisions. However, in elite strength training populations, users are characterized by a high need for data transparency and control. It remains unclear whether highly analytical users will accept target calculations without checking every detail of the math. Dynamic user testing is required to map the threshold where mathematical explanations transition from building trust to causing analysis paralysis.
