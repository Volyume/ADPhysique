# VOLYUME — CAMPAIGN 25 MASTER PROMPT AMENDMENT
## DISABILITY-FIRST PRODUCT COMPLETENESS, FREE ROUTINE LIBRARY, BROAD COVERAGE, USER VALIDATION & MARKETING READINESS

**Status:** Binding amendment to the Campaign 25 master brief.  
**Purpose:** Raise the end-state from “capability-aware enough to technically work” to “genuinely inclusive enough to support and responsibly market Volyume directly to disabled strength trainees and disability communities.”

---

# 1. EXPANDED END GOAL

The capability-aware architecture is NOT complete merely because Volyume can filter out incompatible exercises.

The founder intends Volyume to become a product that can be **confidently and truthfully marketed directly to disabled users**, including serious strength and hypertrophy trainees.

The target is not:

> “A disabled user can probably make Volyume work with enough manual changes.”

The target is:

> “Volyume was deliberately built so people with disabilities and long-term physical restrictions can join, describe how they actually train, receive useful training from day one, use the same progression and coaching system as everyone else, and feel that the product was designed with them rather than patched around them.”

This must become a first-class completion criterion of Campaign 25+.

Do not stop at:

- injury avoidance;
- one generic capability field;
- one token “adaptive” routine;
- a few exercise exclusions;
- technically avoiding crashes.

Build the complete product foundation required to support this honestly.

---

# 2. HARD FOUNDER DECISION — CORE DISABILITY ACCOMMODATION IS NOT PRO-ONLY

The founder decision is:

**Core disability/capability accommodation must not be paywalled.**

If an unrestricted free user can use Volyume’s workout planning/logging functionality, a disabled free user must not need to subscribe merely to make that same core product usable for their body.

Unless a hard technical dependency makes this impossible and is explicitly raised, FREE users must have access to the core capability layer required to train properly, including:

- capability-aware onboarding;
- baseline physical capability profile;
- durable functional restrictions;
- capability-aware exercise filtering;
- capability-aware routine-library browsing;
- free disability/capability-aware routine installation;
- manual programme building that respects relevant capability;
- logging adapted/custom exercises;
- necessary unilateral/asymmetrical logging needed for correct workout logging;
- core accessibility behaviour required to operate the app;
- ability to inspect/update current capability later.

Advanced Pro coaching can continue to follow Volyume’s normal entitlement rules.

But the basic ability to receive and use an appropriate workout must not become an accessibility surcharge.

Audit current entitlement boundaries and mechanically protect this invariant.

---

# 3. BUILD A REAL FREE CAPABILITY-AWARE ROUTINE LIBRARY

Do not rely only on automatic plan generation.

Volyume should also contain a **high-quality free routine library deliberately designed for a broad range of functional capabilities**.

This is valuable for:

- free users;
- people who prefer a known routine over generated programming;
- users who do not want to disclose much information;
- users who want to browse possibilities first;
- people who distrust automatic generation;
- onboarding confidence;
- discoverability;
- marketing;
- demonstrating deliberate inclusion.

Audit the existing library for hidden assumptions such as:

- standing ability;
- floor transfer/access;
- two functioning arms;
- two functioning legs;
- conventional grip;
- symmetrical loading;
- unrestricted balance;
- unrestricted ROM;
- ability to move freely between benches/machines/floor;
- assumption that every unilateral exercise can be mirrored identically.

Design a routine-library architecture capable of making capability-aware routines first-class content.

---

# 4. ABSOLUTELY DO NOT BUILD ONE GENERIC “DISABLED WORKOUT”

Reject the idea of a single generic:

> Disabled Workout

or:

> Adaptive Workout

that attempts to represent an enormous population.

Disabled people are not one training profile.

Routine families should primarily be based on **functional training capability and exercise requirements**.

Candidate routine families to RESEARCH rather than blindly implement include:

- seated-only full body;
- seated upper-body strength;
- wheelchair-based gym strength;
- wheelchair-based home strength;
- no-floor-access full body;
- no-standing full body;
- externally-supported / balance-limited training;
- one-arm / unilateral upper-body capability;
- one-leg / unilateral lower-body capability;
- lower-body training with limited upper-limb loading;
- grip-limited pulling alternatives;
- machine-supported hypertrophy;
- limited-overhead-capability routines;
- limited-knee-flexion capability;
- low-equipment adapted strength;
- resistance-band adapted strength;
- accessible beginner gym programme;
- accessible intermediate hypertrophy programme;
- accessible experienced hypertrophy programme;
- accessible strength-focused programme where evidence/product scope permits;
- routines designed around independently loadable unilateral equipment;
- routines that require no conventional floor transfer;
- routines suitable for users needing substantial external support/stability.

This is an ideation seed only.

Fable must audit the live exercise library, research actual needs, and decide the final routine families.

# 5. BROAD DISABILITY SUPPORT — TWO COMPLEMENTARY LAYERS

The founder wants broad, ambitious coverage and does not want the workstream to stop after a handful of examples.

However, “complete” must NOT be interpreted as:

> “invent one routine for every diagnosis.”

Many disabilities and long-term conditions vary enormously person-to-person. Diagnosis-first templates can be misleading and medically risky.

Use two complementary layers:

## LAYER 1 — CAPABILITY-LED ROUTINES AND PROGRAMMING

This is the scalable core.

Plans/routines are generated, filtered or selected according to real functional capability and exercise demands.

This should cover a very broad range of people without requiring diagnosis-specific logic.

## LAYER 2 — EVIDENCE-INFORMED POPULATION ROUTINES / GUIDANCE

Where a disability, impairment group or long-term condition has sufficiently specific, credible resistance-training evidence and/or authoritative community guidance, Volyume may provide curated starting routines, collections or guidance designed for that population.

Populations to independently RESEARCH may include:

- spinal cord injury;
- wheelchair users;
- upper-limb loss / limb difference;
- lower-limb loss / limb difference;
- cerebral palsy;
- multiple sclerosis;
- Parkinson’s;
- stroke-related long-term asymmetry;
- dwarfism / short stature where exercise setup materially differs;
- other mobility-related disabilities;
- other neurological or musculoskeletal long-term conditions where a fitness-product routine can responsibly be defined.

Do not assume every named population belongs in the final list.

Research each independently.

Where condition-specific programming would cross into rehabilitation/medical treatment, do NOT build it as generic app logic.

# 6. IMPORTANT DISTINCTION — NOT EVERY DISABILITY NEEDS A DIFFERENT TRAINING ROUTINE

If Volyume intends to market broadly to disabled people, support must extend beyond physical exercise selection.

Different needs may require different product responses.

## PHYSICAL / MOBILITY DISABILITY

May affect:

- exercise selection;
- setup;
- body position;
- equipment;
- floor access;
- transfer;
- balance/support;
- unilateral loading;
- programme generation.

## MOTOR / DEXTERITY DISABILITY

May affect:

- touch targets;
- tiny controls;
- drag/drop;
- long-press-only actions;
- one-handed operation;
- timer interactions;
- rapid set logging;
- switch/voice access.

## VISUAL DISABILITY

May affect:

- VoiceOver/TalkBack;
- semantic labels;
- focus order;
- non-colour cues;
- charts;
- exercise instructions;
- timers.

## HEARING DISABILITY

May affect:

- timer cues;
- future verbal exercise instruction;
- video demonstrations;
- alerts;
- need for captions, visual and/or haptic equivalents.

## COGNITIVE / LEARNING DISABILITY

May affect:

- information density;
- onboarding complexity;
- plain language;
- step-by-step clarity;
- choice overload;
- exercise instruction format;
- Easy Read-style or simplified modes where justified by user research.

Do not create a “special routine” where the real requirement is accessible delivery of the same training.

# 8. RESEARCH FINDINGS ALREADY IDENTIFIED — REVERIFY, THEN EXPAND

These are research leads, not product laws. Fable should re-check current authoritative sources before relying on them.

## UK CHIEF MEDICAL OFFICERS — DISABLED ADULTS

The refreshed 2026 UK CMO guidance for disabled adults says disabled adults should do strength and balance activities on at least two days per week, and emphasises that even small amounts of activity are beneficial.

This supports the principle that strength/resistance training belongs in inclusive general fitness provision.

It does NOT support one universal “disabled strength programme”.

## ACTIVITY ALLIANCE — 2025–26 SURVEY

Current Activity Alliance findings emphasise:

- disabled people want more opportunity to be active;
- accessibility remains a barrier;
- affordability/flexibility matter;
- belonging matters;
- inclusive practice matters;
- disabled people need choice.

Treat these as product-quality criteria.

Volyume should not merely say “inclusive”; the experience must feel inclusive in practice.

## SPINAL CORD INJURY

There are published international evidence-based exercise guidelines for adults with chronic spinal cord injury, including resistance training of major functioning muscle groups.

This is an example where population-specific evidence may support a curated starting routine or routine family.

Do NOT extrapolate SCI guidance to wheelchair users generally.

## LIMB LOSS / LIMB DIFFERENCE

UK specialist organisation LimbPower publishes practical gym/activity resources for amputees and people with limb difference and stresses adapting participation around individual ability and needs.

Research upper-limb, lower-limb, unilateral, bilateral, prosthetic and non-prosthetic situations separately.

Do not produce one generic amputee routine.

## MULTIPLE SCLEROSIS

The MS Society explicitly notes there is no single “MS exercise” because MS affects people differently, while still supporting resistance/strengthening activity.

This strongly supports capability-led programming over diagnosis-first templates.

For some populations, condition-specific support may primarily affect:

- fatigue-aware UX;
- available variants;
- setup;
- education;
- capability selection;

while the actual programme remains capability-led.

Find other populations where this same pattern applies.

## ANDROID

Current Android accessibility guidance recommends:

- meaningful labels/semantics;
- not relying solely on colour;
- alternatives to gesture-only flows;
- large/simple controls;
- touch targets of at least 48dp;

## APPLE

Current Apple documentation emphasises:

- VoiceOver navigation;
- meaningful accessibility labels/hints;
- logical navigation order;
- support for Switch Control;

Use official iOS/Android guidance during the real accessibility audit.
