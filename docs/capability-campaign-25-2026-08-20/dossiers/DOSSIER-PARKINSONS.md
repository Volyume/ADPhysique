# EVIDENCE DOSSIER: Parkinson's

profileId: parkinsons

## 1. Target population or problem
Adults with Parkinson's; strong literature (18-study meta-analysis,
large strength effect; named professional recommendations; R5 section 8).

## 2. Purpose
Question selection (balance, grip, impact), family pointers, cited
education. No Parkinson's-labelled programming.

## 3. Variability
Movement, balance and steadiness vary between people and across the day;
one-sided onset is common (laterality note in the profile).

## 4. Sources (live-verified 2026-08-21)
- Gollan et al. 2022, Journal of Parkinson's Disease,
  https://doi.org/10.3233/JPD-223252 (T3; DOI recorded verbatim in R5).
- Parkinson's UK exercise resources,
  https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/information-and-support/exercise (T2).
- Parkinson's Foundation / ACSM 2021 joint recommendations (T2; R5).

## 5. Supported strength-training principles
Resistance training meaningfully improves strength; exercise is part of
recognised standard guidance for Parkinson's.

## 6. Unsupported conclusions
Resistance training is NOT superior to other active interventions (R5
trap); no Parkinson's-specific dose templates.

## 7. Movement and setup considerations
Seated/supported positions remove balance cost; grip-light equipment
absorbs tremor-related precision demands.

## 8. Exercise-library requirements
Balance-supported (264), grip-light (142), seated (241) coverage.

## 9. Movement metadata requirements
balance_demand, grip_demand, impact; all live.

## 10. Generalisable parameters
Only that strength work is supported and part of standard guidance.

## 11. Parameters that remain individual
