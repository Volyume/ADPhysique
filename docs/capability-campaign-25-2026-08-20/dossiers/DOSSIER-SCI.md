# EVIDENCE DOSSIER: chronic spinal cord injury

profileId: spinal_cord_injury

## 1. Target population or problem
Adults with chronic spinal cord injury (the guideline population: 18 to
64, at least one year post onset, neurological level C3 and below,
traumatic or non-traumatic, tetraplegia and paraplegia included).

## 2. Purpose
Question selection, setup notes and family pointers in the directory
profile; the strongest population-specific dose statement found in the
survey is carried as cited education, never as automatic programming.

## 3. Variability
Which muscle groups function is set by level and completeness of injury
and is the single largest determinant of training content. The guideline
itself encodes this by prescribing per "major functioning muscle group"
rather than naming muscles (R5 section 2).

## 4. Sources (live-verified 2026-08-21)
- Martin Ginis et al. 2018, Spinal Cord 56:308-321,
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41393-017-0017-3 (T1; GRADE
  moderate-high for chronic SCI).
- SCIRE Community exercise guidelines page (updated 2023),
  https://community.scireproject.com/topic/exercise-guidelines/ (T2).
- Hutchinson et al. 2023, Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism,
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37816259/ (T3 physiological review).

## 5. Supported strength-training principles
"3 sets of strength exercises for each major functioning muscle group,
at a moderate to vigorous intensity, 2 times per week" (2018 fitness
guideline, verbatim). Exercise improves fitness and cardiometabolic
health in chronic SCI (GRADE moderate-high, all reviewed outcomes except
bone health).

## 6. Unsupported conclusions
The 2011 "3 sets of 8-10 repetitions" range was DROPPED in the 2018
update; any rep range must be sourced to 2011 or omitted (R5 trap).
Nothing supports condition-specific exercise selection beyond function.

## 7. Movement and setup considerations
Stable transfer surfaces; adjustable-seat cable stations; position
changes during long seated sessions. All carried in the profile's
setup notes.

## 8. Exercise-library requirements
Seated-compatible coverage per muscle (registry rows: Seated profile 241
exercises); grip-light alternatives; unilateral-loadable options.

## 9. Movement metadata requirements
position, floor_access, grip_demand, bilateral_lower, balance_demand;
all live at 94 to 100 percent coverage.

## 10. Generalisable parameters
The dose SHAPE only (2x weekly, 3 sets, moderate to vigorous, per
functioning muscle group), quoted with scope.

## 11. Parameters that remain individual
Which muscle groups function; environment/temperature handling; session
tolerance; skin and pressure management.

## 12. Clinical boundary
Autonomic dysreflexia (CR-1), thermoregulatory dysfunction (CR-2), skin
breakdown (CR-3), shoulder overuse policy (CR-4): never encoded, never
taught in-app; the profile routes them to the specialist as
clinicianConfirm topics without teaching content. CLIN-5 stands.

## 13. Claim and language risks
Never "safe for SCI"; never imply assessment of level or readiness; the
guideline scope (18-64, chronic, C3 and below) must accompany any use of
the dose statement.

## 14. Accessibility considerations
One-handed, chair-based operation of logging and timers (shipped app
law); no time-pressured flows.

## 15. Routine implications
Families: Seated Full Body, Seated Upper Strength, Supported Machine
Builder, Grip-Light Machine Circuit. No SCI-labelled plan ships before
EXPERT + LEG-23 (GC-D5).

## 16. Check-in implications
The generic conditional restriction question suffices; no
condition-specific check-in content is supportable.

## 17. Reintroduction implications
Generic conservative model only (R8 reintroduction check: no
deterministic guideline-level return rules exist).

## 21. Prohibited claims
