# R6 — Competitor Matrix: Disability / Capability Support in Strength &amp; Hypertrophy Apps

**Research agent:** R6. **Date:** 2026-08-20. **Method:** public web only
(WebSearch/WebFetch) — vendor sites, help centres, App Store/Google Play
listings, official press releases, and (clearly marked) secondary sources
(review sites, forums, AppleVis community accessibility directory). No
installs, no binary inspection, no fabrication. Where a dimension could not
be determined from public sources, it is marked **UNKNOWN** — that is a
required, honest output, not a gap in the research.

**Sourcing tiers used throughout:**
- **Vendor-verbatim** = quoted directly from the vendor's own site, help
  centre, App Store/Play listing, or official press release.
- **Secondary/reviewer** = a review site, forum, or aggregator's paraphrase
  of a vendor feature. Flagged explicitly wherever used; never presented as
  if it were the vendor's own words.
- **Anecdote** = a user review/forum comment. Quoted sparingly, linked, and
  kept out of the matrix's verbatim-evidenced tier.

Some vendor pages (Hevy Help Centre, Fitbod Help Centre, AppleVis, Google
Play listing pages) returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; where that happened,
the quotes below are reconstructed from search-engine result snippets of
those same URLs and are marked accordingly.

## PART 1 — PER-PRODUCT EVIDENCE

### 1. Hevy (mainstream logger)

- Injury Management (launched as a Hevy Trainer feature): "Injury Management
  lets you flag injuries so Trainer can adapt your program around them."
  Source: search snippet of https://www.hevyapp.com/community-updates/july-26/
- How it works, via Hevy's Help Centre (snippet, page 403'd to direct
  fetch): "scroll to the 'Injuries' tab in the program settings, tap the
  'Add Injury' button, and select the ones you have — your program will
  adjust automatically by replacing high-risk exercises or adding a warning
  for you to be extra cautious during some movements." Removing a healed
  injury "will remove warning messages from certain exercises and may also
  update your program, in case there were excluded movements." Disclaimer:
  "this is not medical advice — it's always best to consult a professional
  for a proper assessment, especially in case you're dealing with a more
  severe or chronic issue." Source:
  https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/38385724273047-Hevy-Trainer-Explained-How-It-Builds-Your-Workout-Program
- Exercise substitution (general, not injury-specific): swap an exercise
  "for just one session or for all future workouts." Source:
  https://www.hevyapp.com/features/exercise-programming-options/
- **Paywall placement:** Injury Management sits inside Hevy Trainer, which
  is Pro-only: $2.99/month, $23.99/year, or $74.99 lifetime; not available
  on Hevy's free tier. Source: https://www.hevyapp.com/features/workout-plan-generator/
- **App Store accessibility declaration:** "The developer has not yet
  indicated which accessibility features this app supports." Source:
  https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350
- **Community accessibility anecdote (AppleVis, blind/low-vision user
  directory):** listed assessment is that some parts of the app are
  accessible with VoiceOver "but not enough to make it usable" (per search
  snippet of the AppleVis listing, direct fetch 403'd). Source:
  https://www.applevis.com/apps/ios/sports-activities/hevy-workout-tracker-planner

### 2. Strong (mainstream logger)

- **App Store accessibility declaration:** "The developer has not yet
  indicated which accessibility features this app supports." Source:
  https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577
- **AppleVis directory rating (community-assessed, not vendor-declared):**
  Strong is tagged in AppleVis's own usability taxonomy as "The app is fully
  accessible with VoiceOver, but the interface could be easier to navigate
  and use." One AppleVis commenter reported not understanding why they
  couldn't save a routine, though attributed this to unfamiliarity with the
  app type rather than a VoiceOver defect (per search snippets; direct
  fetch of applevis.com 403'd). Source:
  https://www.applevis.com/apps/ios/health-fitness/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log
- No injury flagging, disability onboarding, seated-programme, or
  disability-specific content of any kind found on strong.app or in App
  Store copy. Source: https://www.strong.app/

### 3. Fitbod (generated-plan)

- Injuries and Limitations feature: accessed via "My Plan → Injuries /
  Limitations", tap "Current Injuries / Limitations" to flag an area; "When
  you're ready to train that area again, you can tap Mark As Not Injured."
  (Search snippet of page; direct fetch returned HTTP 403.) Source:
  https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/37629269518103-Injuries-and-Limitations
- Mechanism: "If you're injured or avoiding certain movements, Fitbod may
  schedule that muscle group as an accessory or avoid it for the session,
  instead focusing on 'fresh' groups." Source: same page, via search snippet.
- Unilateral/per-side logging — vendor-verbatim from Fitbod's own help
  article: "Single-leg or single arm exercises are known as unilateral
  exercises that should be performed on both sides… Dumbbell Lunge will
  display # of reps PER LEG and the amount of weight PER ARM." Source:
  https://fitbod.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019866414-For-unilateral-exercises-do-I-log-reps-and-weight-for-both-sides-or-per-side-
- Pregnancy content (adjacent to, not itself, disability): "Fitbod has over
  30 pregnancy-safe exercises perfect for your changing body… Modify the
  intensity of your training to your needs. Remember to consult your doctor
  when designing prenatal and postnatal exercise routines." Source:
  https://fitbod.me/blog/pregnancy-safe-exercises/
- **App Store accessibility declaration:** "The developer has not yet
  indicated which accessibility features this app supports." Source:
  https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543
- **Paywall placement:** Fitbod has no persistent free tier — 3 free
  workouts, then subscription required ($12.99/month or $79.99/year per
  secondary pricing sources); the Injuries/Limitations feature sits inside
  the same paid product, not separately gated. Source (secondary, pricing):
  https://dr-muscle.com/fitbod-cost-free-alternative/

### 4. RP Hypertrophy (Renaissance Periodization)

- Delivery model: browser-based web app ("save the page to your home screen
  to use it like any other app"), not a native store app. Source
  (secondary/reviewer): https://dr-muscle.com/rp-hypertrophy-app-critique/
- Core autoregulation mechanic (corroborated across multiple independent
  secondary sources describing RP's public methodology consistently): users
  give session feedback on "pump, soreness, perceived effort, 'disruption'
  (general fatigue and strength loss), joint pain, and performance," and the
  app "autoregulates volume progression by increasing or decreasing weekly
  sets depending on recovery markers like soreness and joint pain." Source
  (secondary/reviewer, not vendor-verbatim):
  https://dr-muscle.com/rp-hypertrophy-app-for-strength-training-expert-review/
- **Flagged claim, single secondary source, not vendor-confirmed:** the same
  review site states the app "during setup, prompts users to indicate any
  physical constraints, adjusting exercise selection accordingly" and gives
  a shoulder-pain example. This is a reviewer's description, not a vendor
  quote — treated as PARTIAL/unconfirmed in the matrix.
- **Accessibility — explicit reviewer finding, not vendor-declared:** "the
  RP app scores highly on customization and autoregulation but lags in
  accessibility and integration, especially compared to all-in-one
  competitors." Source (secondary): https://dr-muscle.com/rp-hypertrophy-app-for-strength-training-expert-review/
- Pricing: standard $34.99/month, promotional $24.99/month, annual
  $224.99/year ($18.75/month equivalent), 30-day money-back guarantee direct
  from RP. Source: https://rpstrength.com/pages/hypertrophy-app and secondary
  pricing aggregation.
- No disability-specific content, seated-programme option, or accessibility
  statement found on rpstrength.com.

### 6. Boostcamp

- Exercise substitution exists but is partially paywalled: "Some basic
  functions like substituting an exercise are locked behind a subscription,"
  per a Google Play review the reviewer called "ridiculous"; fuller
  "exercise-swap options" sit in Boostcamp Pro (~$15/month or $60/year per
  secondary pricing sources). Source (Play Store reviews, secondary):
  https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bpmhealth.boostcamp
- **Confirmed accessibility gap, company-acknowledged:** user feedback
  reports the app "does not respect system accessibility settings" and has
  "tiny fonts" with "no customization options." BPM Health Co. (Boostcamp's
  developer) responded in-store: "We hear you on the font size and
  accessibility settings," directing further reports to
  support@boostcamp.app. This is the clearest confirmed, vendor-acknowledged
  accessibility shortfall found anywhere in this survey. Source (Play Store
  listing + developer reply, secondary/reviewer-relayed):
  https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bpmhealth.boostcamp
- No injury-flagging system, disability onboarding, or disability-specific
  content found on boostcamp.app.

### 7. Alpha Progression

- **Persistent baseline capability profile (vendor-verbatim, the strongest
  example of this dimension found among all generated-plan/logger apps):**
  "You can scroll to the bottom and select which exercises to be: Always
  enabled — for movements you can always do, no matter the circumstances,
  or Always disabled — for instance, movements you don't have equipment
  for, can't do, or simply don't like." This is explicitly framed as a
  standing list, not a healing/removable injury flag. Source (via search
  snippet of vendor help content, alphaprogression.com):
  https://alphaprogression.com/en
- Separate, injury-framed exclusion: "The ability to exclude certain
  equipment, exercises or muscle groups is great if you're recovering from
  injury and need to rest up your lower body, for example." Same source.
- **App Store accessibility declaration:** "the developer has not yet
  indicated which accessibility features this app supports." Source:
  https://apps.apple.com/us/app/alpha-progression-gym-tracker/id1462277793
- Pricing: Pro $12.99/month or $79.99/year, 14-day free trial; free tier
  covers basic logging only. Source (secondary pricing aggregation):
  https://push-pull.app/blog/push-pull-vs-alpha-progression

### 15. Dedicated wheelchair/adaptive app cluster — state of the small-app market

This cluster is documented together, as instructed, to capture the state of
the dedicated-app space rather than treat each as a full peer of the
above products.

**Wheel Fit** (wheelfit.app) — vendor-verbatim: "ultimate adaptive workout
app for wheelchair users," "Designed for Everyone ♿️," "Start a
comprehensive wheelchair exercise program at home with personalized
routines that evolve with your progress," "30+ Exercises." Source:
https://www.wheelfit.app/. Pricing: $5 to download, with most workouts
locked behind an additional monthly subscription (secondary/App Store
review synthesis). **Credibility problem, anecdote-level but specific and
repeated:** reviewers report the app uses AI-generated images of wheelchair
users rather than real people or trainers, citing concrete errors — "an
image of a woman in a transport chair that was too big for her at the gym,"
and "a man in a chair not meant for basketball on the basketball court with
the spokes all messed up (and no axle)" — and characterise the app as "a
low-effort money grab rather than a genuine resource." Source (App Store
reviews, secondary): https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wheel-fit-wheelchair-fitness/id6464079536

**Wheel With Me Adapt Fit** (wheelwithmeadaptfit.com) — built by wheelchair
users: "created by Jesi Stracham &amp; Nikki Walsh," who "were tired of
having to piece together workouts from nondisabled trainers." Content:
"Strength Programs, Functional Mobility, Bands, Floor workouts, Cardio,
Strength, Daily Inspiration, Private Facebook Group, Community." User
review (anecdote): "the trainers in the videos also use wheelchairs and
know exactly what they are doing." Modest scale: 108 reviews, 3.4 rating on
Google Play. Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wheel-with-me-adapt-fit/id1609250295
and https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=breakthroughapps.com.wheelwithme

**Push Zones** (Bred Ventures Inc) — narrow scope, metrics only, not a
programming app: "five vibrant, color-coded heart rate zones… custom-
calibrated for wheelchair-specific workouts," tracks "pushes." Actively
maintained (recent bug-fix updates found at research time). Anecdote,
positive: "the only app I can find that works well with the Wheelchair and
[is] accurate with pushing." Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/push-zones-wheelchair-fitness/id1515857732

**No Limits Mobile** — free, built by an MD/PhD student (Ben Conner) with
the U.S. Paralympic Rugby strength coach and Ability 360: "an encyclopedia
of exercises and how to properly perform them," modelled by adaptive
athletes on video, "scaffolded to further accommodate overall ability and
level of disability." No confirmed evidence of recent (2026) update
activity was found in this research pass — **UNKNOWN** whether actively
maintained. Source: https://www.azbio.org/app-created-by-md-phd-student-provides-accessible-training-resource-for-adaptive-sports

**Move United On Demand** — a disability-sport nonprofit's class library,
not a strength-programming app: "Move United On Demand, for people with
disabilities," "More than 35 classes ranging from HIIT to Yoga and
Stretching," explicitly adapts to the viewer: "meets the athletes where
they are and challenges them, whether they work out seated, need audio
cues or recurring instruction." Launch incentive: "The first 1000 people to
register for Move United On Demand will receive the program for free."
Ongoing pricing beyond the launch window — **UNKNOWN.** Source:
https://athletechnews.com/move-united-platform-for-disabled/

**Reading of this cluster as a whole:** none of the five combine (a) an
algorithmic/generated strength programme, (b) a persistent capability
profile, and (c) the polish, review volume, and maintenance cadence of the
mainstream apps in Part 1. The most credible (Wheel With Me, No Limits
Mobile) are built by/with disabled people but are small (double-digit to
low-hundreds review counts) and content-library-shaped rather than
programming engines. The least credible (Wheel Fit) actively damages trust
in the category through fabricated (AI-generated) representation of the
community it claims to serve. This is the strongest direct evidence in the
whole survey for the brief's "thin/abandoned" hypothesis.

## PART 3 — FINDINGS: WHERE THE MARKET HAS GENUINE GAPS

1. **No commercial strength-training/logging/generated-plan app has a
   disability-first persistent capability profile.** Every injury-handling
   system found in this survey (Hevy, Fitbod, JuggernautAI, Boostcamp,
   KeyLifts) is built on an injury/heal metaphor — flag it, the system warns
   or excludes, then you remove the flag once "healed" and the exclusions
   disappear. Alpha Progression's "Always enabled / Always disabled"
   exercise list is the sole exception found anywhere in the logger/
   generator category — a standing list explicitly framed for "movements
   you can always do, no matter the circumstances" — but it is generic
   (also covers "don't have equipment for… or simply don't like"), not
   disability-labelled, and sits alongside, not instead of, a separate
   injury system. A genuinely disability-first baseline profile — persistent
   by design, not framed as temporary or healing, and distinct from an
   injury mode a user can also use for actual injuries — was not found
   anywhere in the commercial generated-plan space surveyed.

2. **Seated / no-floor / no-standing programme GENERATION is completely
   absent from every plan-generating and human-coach product checked.**
   Hevy, Strong, Fitbod, RP Hypertrophy, JuggernautAI, Boostcamp, Alpha
   Progression, KeyLifts, Caliber, Future, and Ladder — eleven products —
   show zero evidence of an algorithm or coach-documented workflow that
   produces a full seated/no-floor strength programme. The only products in
   the entire 15-entry survey with seated/no-floor content are pre-recorded
   video-class libraries (Apple Fitness+'s wheelchair workouts, Peloton's
   Seated Adaptive Strength) and the small dedicated wheelchair apps — none
   of which generate a periodised strength/hypertrophy programme the way
   Volyume's category does. This is the cleanest, best-evidenced
   differentiation candidate in this research: a plan-GENERATING app that
   can produce seated/no-standing programmes appears to not exist yet.

3. **The Apple Accessibility Nutrition Label is universally blank across
   every native strength-training app checked.** Hevy, Strong, Fitbod,
   JuggernautAI, Alpha Progression, and KeyLifts all show the identical
   Apple-supplied placeholder: "The developer has not yet indicated which
   accessibility features this app supports." Community assessment
   (AppleVis) suggests Strong is workably VoiceOver-navigable in practice
   and Hevy only partially so, but none of the six has made a formal
   declaration. This stands in stark contrast to Peloton (dedicated
   accessibility page, hardware screen-reader support, published contact
   channel) and Apple's own Fitness+. Simply completing and substantiating
   the accessibility nutrition label would already put a strength app ahead
   of the category norm.

4. **Boostcamp is the only product in this survey with a
   vendor-acknowledged, un-remediated accessibility complaint** (font size
   / system accessibility settings not respected, confirmed by the
   developer's own in-store reply). This is worth naming carefully in any
   claim: it is evidence of an unresolved gap in ONE named competitor, not
   evidence about the category as a whole.

5. **The dedicated adaptive-app space is real but genuinely thin, and one
   entrant actively damages trust in it.** Accessercise is the most
   credible dedicated product (Paralympian-founded, press-covered,
   coach-education partnership) but explicitly does not generate
   programmes — by design, on the philosophy that "disabled users are
   their own experts." The small wheelchair-specific apps (Wheel Fit, Wheel
   With Me, Push Zones, No Limits Mobile) are single-purpose,
   small-audience (double-digit to low-hundreds reviews), and mostly static
   content libraries or metrics trackers rather than programming engines.
   Wheel Fit's use of AI-generated, technically-inaccurate wheelchair
   imagery, and its reviewer characterisation as "a low-effort money grab,"
   is a concrete, citable example of the harm low-effort entrants do to
   this specific market — useful context for why credibility (not just
   presence) matters in any differentiation claim.

6. **No product anywhere in the survey names a "flare mode" or
   fluctuating/chronic-condition workflow as a distinct, documented
   feature.** RP Hypertrophy's per-session pump/soreness/joint-pain feedback
   loop is the closest mechanical analogue to flare-responsive
   programming — but it is marketed as general autoregulation for
   hypertrophy training, never as chronic-condition or disability support.
   Human-coach products (Caliber, Future, Ladder) could plausibly absorb
   fluctuation through the coach relationship, but none document or market
   this as a named capability. This is a second strong, cleanly-evidenced
   differentiation candidate.

7. **Learning/personalisation provenance is UNKNOWN industry-wide, exactly
   as expected.** No vendor (Hevy Trainer, Fitbod, RP Hypertrophy,
   JuggernautAI) publishes how a temporarily-flagged injury or limitation
   interacts with the underlying recommendation/autoregulation algorithm
   once the flag is removed — whether the system cleanly resets or whether
   the reduced-capability period quietly depresses future recommendations.
   Any claim Volyume makes here should be scoped to Volyume's own known
   behaviour, not framed as a competitor failing, since it is genuinely
   undocumented on all sides.

8. **Graded, staged reintroduction after injury is thin everywhere it was
   checked.** Both Hevy (remove injury flag → exclusions lift) and Fitbod
   ("Mark As Not Injured" → area returns to normal programming) show binary
   toggles, with no vendor-documented ramped/staged volume return. If
   Volyume has an explicit graded-reintroduction protocol, this is a third
   differentiation candidate with clean evidence of absence elsewhere.

9. **Marketing-language pattern:** every legitimate disability-specific
   claim found in this survey is attached to a named disabled/adaptive
   credential — Peloton's Logan Aldridge (amputee athlete) and the Reeve
   Foundation partnership; Apple's cited adaptive athlete Amir Ekbatani;
   Accessercise's Paralympian founder Ali Jawad; No Limits Mobile's
   Paralympic-rugby-coach collaboration; Wheel With Me's wheelchair-using
   founders. No product in this survey makes a disability-specific
   marketing claim without such a named, checkable credential standing
   behind it. Any Volyume claim in this campaign should hold itself to the
   same bar — attribute claims to specific, checkable capabilities or
   collaborators, not vague "we support everyone" language, both because
   it's the honest thing to do and because it is what distinguishes the
   credible entrants in this space from the ones (Wheel Fit) that damage
   trust in it.
