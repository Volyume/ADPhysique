## 2. How far UK gym members actually travel

- **Half of UK gym survey respondents would travel less than 1 mile to the gym; 36% would travel 1–3 miles** — reported by a WebSearch AI summary of results including [Coach — "Here's how close your gym needs to be to ensure you go regularly"](https://www.coachweb.com/fitness/6456/here-s-how-close-your-gym-needs-to-be-to-ensure-you-go-regularly). On fetching that article directly, **it does not contain this UK 1-mile/3-mile figure** — the article's only confirmed content is the Dstillery US study below. **The "half within 1 mile, 36% within 1–3 miles" claim is therefore unverified — its originating source page could not be identified or fetched.**
- **US data (Dstillery, confirmed by direct fetch of the Coach article):** analysis of anonymised location data from 7.5 million people at US gyms over a four-week period found people living within **3.7 miles** of their gym attended **five or more times a month**, while those travelling **5 miles or more** attended only **once a month**. Premium/boutique chains (Equinox, SoulCycle) drew members from further away — Equinox members travelled a median of **5.7 miles**. Source: [coachweb.com](https://www.coachweb.com/fitness/6456/here-s-how-close-your-gym-needs-to-be-to-ensure-you-go-regularly), fetched 2026-09-07. This is **US data, not UK**, and is presented as such — it is directionally relevant evidence for "closer = more frequent attendance" but must not be quoted as a UK statistic.
- **PureGym's own "UK Fitness Report 2025/26"** ([puregym.com/blog/uk-fitness-report-gym-statistics](https://www.puregym.com/blog/uk-fitness-report-gym-statistics/), fetched directly) contains regional exercise-participation rates (e.g. London 60% vs East of England 40% of adults exercising) but **no travel-distance, catchment-radius, or urban/rural distance-split data**. Confirmed by direct fetch — not present in the source.
- **Sport England Active Lives Survey**: searched directly; found the survey's headline participation figures (64.6% of adults doing 150+ minutes of activity/week; 904,000 more adults doing fitness activities year-on-year) but **no travel-distance-to-gym metric appears in the indexed material found**. The ONS piece "More adults are active in areas with a higher number of sports facilities" ([ons.gov.uk, 2024-03-07](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/wellbeing/articles/moreadultsareactiveinareaswithahighernumberofsportsfacilities/2024-03-07)) links facility density to activity levels but was not fetched for a specific distance figure — **flagged for a possible follow-up fetch if this becomes decision-relevant; not used as evidence here**.
- **Operator "X% of members live within Y miles" statements** (the pattern the brief asked about): PureGym's site-selection material states the company targets "critical population mass within 15 minutes of travel time" for new sites (WebSearch summary of an ICSC article on PureGym's North American expansion) — this is a **site-selection target, not a measured existing-member statistic**, and is US-expansion context, not UK. No UK operator "80% of members live within X miles"-style statement was found for PureGym, The Gym Group, or David Lloyd in this pass.
- **ONS commuting distance as a proxy**: not retrieved in this pass — no commuting-distance dataset was fetched. **Unverified / not pursued.**
- **Academic gym-catchment studies**: none surfaced in the queries run.

**Conclusion for Q2:** The only **fetch-confirmed, citable distance-behaviour figure** is the US Dstillery study (3.7 miles / five-plus visits per month vs 5+ miles / once per month). No UK-specific, source-confirmed figure for gym travel distance was found. The commonly-repeated "half travel under 1 mile" claim could not be traced to a primary source and should be treated as **unverified**.

## 5. Location permission UX best practice

### Platform guidance (fetched/confirmed where marked)

- **Android — permission timing (confirmed via developer.android.com, fetched):**
  - Location permission requests should be deferred until "the user interacts with the feature" that needs it — e.g., don't request until the user taps "Share location". Source: [developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions), fetched 2026-09-07 (via WebSearch summary of this page's content; not independently re-fetched for this exact quote — treat the quote as high-confidence but not directly page-verified).
  - Apps should request only the permission type "critical to the user-facing feature" and disclose this to users.
  - Foreground and background location must be requested incrementally, not simultaneously; Android 11+ (API 30+) enforces this — a simultaneous request is silently ignored and neither permission is granted.
  - If a user denies, the app "must help them understand the implications" and make clear which features don't work as a result.
  - Source for all of the above: `developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions` and `developer.android.com/training/permissions/requesting`, both surfaced 2026-09-07.

- **Android — accuracy figures (confirmed by direct WebFetch of `developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions`, fetched 2026-09-07):**
  - **`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` (Approximate):** "accurate to within about 3 square kilometers (about 1.2 square miles)" when sourced from `LocationManagerService`/`FusedLocationProvider`.
  - **`ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` (Precise):** "usually within about 50 meters (160 feet) and is sometimes as accurate as within a few meters (10 feet) or better."
  - On Android 12+, if the user grants only Approximate, the app receives only approximate location regardless of which permissions it declared in the manifest — the OS enforces the ceiling.
  - **Direct answer to the brief's sub-question ("is coarse/approximate sufficient for a 5-mile gym finder?")**: at ~3 km² accuracy (roughly a 1 km-scale positional uncertainty, not the 1–3 km figure assumed in the brief — Android's own figure is closer to "accurate to within about 3 km²" which is a smaller cell than a 1–3 km radius framing implies, but still coarser than needed to distinguish two gyms a few hundred metres apart), Approximate location is precise enough to centre a 5-mile radius search (the search radius is two orders of magnitude larger than the position uncertainty) but is **not precise enough to reliably tell which of two nearby branches of the same chain the user is standing in or nearest to**. This is an inference from the confirmed accuracy figures, not a directly stated conclusion from Android's own docs — labelled as inference.

- **iOS — accuracy figures (NOT independently fetched from Apple's own developer documentation — Apple's HIG "Privacy" page returned only a title on fetch, and Apple's own precision-quantisation numbers were not located on an apple.com page in this pass):**
  - A WebSearch summary (citing third-party sources, not Apple directly) states iOS "approximate location" quantises the user's position into regions "typically about 5 km" in radius, "a few kilometres" in dense urban areas, and "10 km or more" in rural areas, with roughly four location updates per hour while only approximate access is granted.
  - AppleInsider (cited in the WebSearch summary, not independently fetched) reportedly described iOS 14 approximate location as limiting positioning to "within 10 square miles."
  - **These iOS figures are unverified against Apple's own documentation** — they come from third-party technical write-ups referenced in a WebSearch summary, not a page this research fetched directly. Treat with lower confidence than the Android figures above, which were confirmed by direct fetch of Google's own docs.
  - Apple's mechanism is reported to work by geographic-region quantisation (not adding random noise to a raw fix), preserving city-level identity and keeping the user on the correct side of a border where relevant — again, third-party-sourced, not Apple-primary-sourced in this pass.

- **Apple HIG guidance on permission UX (NOT independently fetched — the HIG Privacy page returned no body content on WebFetch):**
  - A WebSearch summary states Apple recommends "Allow While Using the App" as the permission most apps should request (not Always); recommends against requesting precise location when approximate would serve the purpose (its own example: "a social app usually does not need your exact location unless you intentionally tag a place" — directly analogous to a gym-location use case); and recommends a purpose string explaining the request.
  - Both Apple's HIG and Android's guidance are reported (by the same WebSearch summary) to recommend contextual, just-in-time permission requests rather than asking at first launch.
  - **This entire bullet is sourced from a WebSearch AI summary of secondary/aggregator pages, not from a fetched Apple HIG page — flagged as lower-confidence / partially unverified**, though it is broadly consistent with Apple's publicly known design philosophy and the confirmed Android guidance above, which independently reaches the same conclusion (ask in context, minimise precision, explain the "why").

### Denial-rate statistics

- Opt-in rates for location permission are reported (WebSearch summary of an Incognia white paper and blog, not independently fetched) to vary sharply by category and perceived value: navigation/maps-type apps ~90% opt-in; delivery ("online-to-offline") apps ~85%; fintech apps ~75%; audio-streaming apps as low as ~50%; ecommerce apps as low as ~24%.
- A separate WebSearch summary states "effective pre-permission messaging can boost opt-in rates by 20–40 percentage points."
- **These figures are from a single secondary source (Incognia, a location-verification vendor with a commercial interest in this topic) and were not independently corroborated or fetched from a primary Incognia document — treat as indicative industry figures, not verified fact.** No UK-specific or fitness-app-specific denial rate was found.
- **Conclusion for Q5's denial-rate sub-question: unverified / could not determine to a rigorous standard** — the only figures available are vendor-sourced aggregate industry ranges, not a study of gym/fitness-app location-prompt denial specifically.

## 6. Postcode-search UX

- **Partial outward code behaviour**: several commercial UK postcode/address APIs (Postcodes.io, Postcoder, ePostcode, getAddress.io, findAddress.io, Ideal Postcodes) advertise autocomplete/outcode lookup and partial-postcode matching as a category of feature — confirmed via their marketing pages (WebSearch results), e.g. Postcoder's "autocomplete/find" endpoint accepts a partial address query and returns suggestions. **However, none of these vendor pages were fetched to confirm the exact behavioural distinction the brief asks about — how "ML1" (a complete outward code for Motherwell) is handled differently from "ML11" (a complete, different outward code for Lanark), since both are actually valid, complete outcodes rather than one being a truncation of the other.** This is a factual correction to the brief's framing: **ML1 and ML11 are not "partial vs full" versions of the same code — they are two distinct, independently valid Scottish outcodes** (ML1 = Motherwell, ML11 = Lanark), so the real UX question is how a finder disambiguates similarly-prefixed-but-distinct outcodes, not truncation. This distinction is asserted from general postcode-structure knowledge, not from a fetched authoritative source in this pass, and should be verified against Royal Mail's PAF documentation before being relied on. **Flagged: needs a direct fetch of a Postcodes.io or Royal Mail outcode-format reference to confirm with certainty; not done in this pass.**
- **Town-name ambiguity (e.g. "Newport")**: confirmed there are at least three commonly-cited, materially different UK "Newport" locations — Newport (Wales, city, NP postcode area), Newport, Shropshire, and Newport, Isle of Wight — plus numerous smaller villages of the same name (a WebSearch summary citing Quora/Wikipedia-sourced lists names 14 UK places called Newport). **No fetched primary source was found describing how any specific gym finder, Google Maps, or NHS service-search actually disambiguates a bare "Newport" query** (e.g. whether it shows a picker of matching towns, defaults to the largest/most prominent, or requires the user to add a county/postcode). This is the sharpest gap in the research: **the existence of the ambiguity is well evidenced; how any product resolves it in its UI is unverified / could not determine** in this pass.
- **Autocomplete behaviour generally**: multiple UK address-API vendors (Postcoder, ePostcode, findAddress.io) market real-time, type-ahead suggestion as a standard feature of UK address/postcode search — confirmed as an industry-standard pattern via their own marketing copy — but the specific interaction details (minimum characters before suggestions appear, how many results shown, ranking logic) were not fetched/confirmed for any of them in this pass.

**Conclusion for Q6:** largely **unverified / could not determine** at the level of detail the brief requests (specific product behaviour for ML1 vs ML11, specific product behaviour for ambiguous town names, exact autocomplete trigger thresholds). What is confirmed is that (a) partial/outcode-level postcode search is a standard offered feature across the UK address-API market, and (b) UK town-name collision is a real, well-documented problem (multiple genuine "Newport"s), so any gym-finder search-by-town feature will need an explicit disambiguation UI — but no existing product's specific disambiguation mechanism was confirmed via a fetched source.

## 7. Onboarding-step placement ("Where do you train?" during setup vs later)

- **General onboarding drop-off benchmarks** (WebSearch summary citing aggregator/blog sources — not academic, not fetched directly, and of uneven rigour):
  - "Global average onboarding completion rate after 30 days sits at just 8.4%, and over 90% of installs never make it through onboarding at all" — sourced from a marketing-content aggregator, not independently corroborated. **Treat as indicative industry commentary, not a rigorous statistic.**
  - "Only 19.2% of users complete onboarding, while top-performing apps see completion rates of 40–50%" — same caveat.
  - "If 60% bail when you request location access, you asked too early" and "over 82% of users want a clear reason for each permission request before they grant it" — same caveat; no primary study named or fetched.
  - "Making onboarding available but optional, with progress indicators, improves completion by approximately 12%" — same caveat.
- **Baymard Institute** (a more credible, primary UX-research body, but **not independently fetched from baymard.com in this pass** — figures below are from a WebSearch summary of secondary pages referencing Baymard, not from Baymard's own site):
  - Reported: "every additional form field reduces conversion by 1–2%."
  - Reported: "a 2023 study by Baymard Institute showed that complex sign-up processes lead to a 25% abandonment rate."
  - Reported guidance: defer non-critical fields to a post-activation "nudge," use a visible progress indicator ("Step 2 of 4" / "60% done") to bound perceived effort.
  - **These are plausible and consistent with Baymard's known general positions on form-field friction, but were not confirmed by fetching a Baymard.com page directly in this pass — flagged as secondary-sourced, not primary-verified.**
- **Nielsen Norman Group** (WebSearch summary, not independently fetched from nngroup.com):
  - Progressive disclosure (Nielsen's own 1995 concept) is reported as recommending a maximum of two disclosure levels per interaction, and deferring advanced/rarely-used inputs to a secondary screen rather than the main flow.
  - Cited examples of conditional/progressive field disclosure in the wild: Stripe's account setup (country + business name first, KYC/banking/tax later) and Booking.com's traveller-count form (extra fields only appear if a child traveller is added) — both from secondary sources describing these products' known onboarding patterns, not fetched from Stripe/Booking.com directly.
- **Direct evidence on "ask location/venue during onboarding vs later" specifically**: no dedicated academic or first-party study of this exact question (as distinct from generic form-field-count research) was found. The closest applicable finding is the generic progressive-disclosure and "defer non-critical fields" guidance above, which supports **making an optional "where do you train" step skippable with a later nudge**, but this is an **extrapolation from general onboarding-friction research, not a study of gym/fitness-app location-step placement specifically.**

**Conclusion for Q7:** the strongest, most reusable evidence is generic UX research (progressive disclosure, form-field-count friction) rather than anything specific to gym-finding or "where do you train" as a question type. No comparative study of asking this exact question early-in-onboarding vs later, or of skip-then-nudge patterns specifically for a location/venue field, was found. This should be treated as **directionally suggestive, not conclusive** evidence for the Community onboarding design decision.
