### Q1 — What visual qualities make an app feel premium WITHOUT feeling cold?

**Finding 1.1 — Premium = intentionality you can feel.** Linear's premium
reputation comes from design that is "very thoroughly considered and carefully
designed", drawing on Apple standards so it "feels native", backed by a large set
of modular components that keep a minimalist interface visually interesting rather
than barren.
- NEWBIE: minimalism reduces overwhelm; the app feels calm and trustworthy on day one.
- ATHLETE: the modular-component depth means density can scale up without the app feeling cheap.
- Source: https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/linear-app-case-study — VERIFIED
- Source: https://medium.com/@ananyavhegde2001/i-finally-tried-linear-and-now-i-get-the-hype-c5d488840278 — VERIFIED

**Finding 1.2 — Craft proves "premium" is typography + spacing + animation, not features.** Reviewers describe Craft as "Typography is carefully considered.
Spacing feels intentional," with animation quality and Apple-convention polish that
"exceeds most competitors", giving the impression "that someone cared deeply about
how the app looks and feels rather than building functionality first."
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical — both read intentional spacing and good type as "quality" pre-consciously.
- Source: https://calmevo.com/craft-app-review/ — VERIFIED

**Finding 1.3 — Warmth is what stops minimalism reading as cold; it comes from animation and a single brand accent.** Monzo adds warmth to an otherwise clean,
clutter-free banking app through animation ("key moments gained warmth and
clarity") and intentional use of its Hot Coral accent "as a moment of delight and
brand personality." Animations "guide users, reinforce understanding, and make
moments of discovery feel rewarding" rather than being decorative.
- NEWBIE: warmth lowers the intimidation of a numbers-heavy domain (finance ≈ fitness metrics).
- ATHLETE: a single disciplined accent keeps a data-dense screen feeling crafted, not clinical.
- Source: https://www.creativebloq.com/web-design/ux-ui/monzos-brilliant-ui-design-is-a-delight-to-use — VERIFIED
- Source: https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/04/how-we-design-magic-moments-at-monzo — VERIFIED
- Source: https://www.lottielab.com/case-studies/monzo — VERIFIED

**Finding 1.4 — Haptics and microinteractions are a measurable premium signal.** "Apps with good haptics feel more polished, more expensive, and more professional";
microinteractions "make products feel more polished and trustworthy, which helps
with retention and word-of-mouth." Caveat from the same body of work: "If
microinteractions don't add clarity or delight, they probably shouldn't be there"
— restraint is part of the premium feel.
- NEWBIE: subtle haptic confirmation on logging a set/meal reassures the action registered.
- ATHLETE: rhythm and confirmation speed up high-frequency logging without visual clutter.
- Source: https://medium.com/@chandra.welim/haptic-feedback-the-secret-to-apps-that-feel-premium-7463fdc1ccca — VERIFIED
- Source: https://www.influencers-time.com/haptic-storytelling-boosts-ux-and-conversion-in-mobile-apps/ — VERIFIED

### Q2 — How do the best apps balance data density with breathing room?

**Finding 2.1 — Density should match context and user expertise, not a fixed rule.** "Density should match context — a consumer onboarding flow needs breathing room
while an enterprise analytics dashboard needs compactness," depending on "user
expertise, task complexity, and how frequently the interface is used."
- NEWBIE: lead with breathing room and a few summary numbers.
- ATHLETE: allow opt-in compact/dense views for power users who want everything at once.
- Source: https://www.ruixen.com/blog/ui-density — VERIFIED
- Source: https://envylabs.com/insights/interface-information-density-best-practices — VERIFIED

**Finding 2.2 — Whitespace is the tool that makes the important number stand out.** "Ample whitespace around important metrics helps isolate them from less critical
data, giving them visual breathing room and drawing the user's eye." "When
everything feels important, nothing stands out," which is why hierarchy is the
governing principle in dense interfaces.
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical principle; the *amount* of surrounding data differs, the hierarchy discipline does not.
- Source: https://refgrow.com/blog/dashboard-design-best-practices — VERIFIED
- Source: https://datasense.to/2025/10/05/dashboard-design-best-practices/ — VERIFIED

**Finding 2.3 — High-level-to-low-level is the proven density pattern.** Manage
data density by leading with high-level summary and letting users drill to
low-level detail, minimising "extraneous load (confusion caused by poor design)
while preserving germane load (productive understanding)."
- NEWBIE: the high-level summary may be all they ever need.
- ATHLETE: the drill-down path satisfies the deep-diver without crowding the default view.
- Source: https://www.freshconsulting.com/insights/blog/ui-ux-principle-52-manage-data-density-high-level-to-low-level/ — VERIFIED

**Finding 2.4 — Real-world contrast: Oura (summarise) vs Whoop (expose everything).** Oura is "easy to use with four scores that summarise your data" with "important
data front and centre paired with calming imagery"; Whoop "is harder to navigate at
first because it's chock-full of information" and its "home page feels cluttered" —
but that density "makes it ideal for anyone ready to deep-dive into their
biometrics."
- NEWBIE: the Oura model (few summary scores, calm imagery) wins.
- ATHLETE: the Whoop model is *acceptable to the right user* — density is a feature for deep-divers, but it must be opt-in, not the default.
- Source: https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/sleep-tech/whoop-vs-oura-i-tested-each-sleep-tracker-for-two-weeks-heres-my-winner — VERIFIED
- Source: https://cybernews.com/health-tech/whoop-vs-oura/ — VERIFIED

INTERPRETATION: the Oura/Whoop split maps directly onto Volyume's dual audience.
A summary-first default with an opt-in dense/detailed mode would let one design
serve both ends of the spectrum rather than forcing a compromise.

### Q3 — Most-praised empty-state and loading-state designs

**Finding 3.1 — Best empty states do three jobs: what's happening, why, what to do.** "A useful empty state will let the user know what's happening, why it's happening,
and what to do about it." Structure: headline first ("No results found"), then a
secondary explanation, then a CTA.
- NEWBIE: empty states double as onboarding — they teach what the screen is for.
- ATHLETE: keep them terse; a returning power user needs the CTA, not the lesson.
- Source: https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/empty-state-ux — VERIFIED
- Source: https://www.toptal.com/designers/ux/empty-state-ux-design — VERIFIED

**Finding 3.2 — Named best-in-class empty states.** Gmail's empty state "tells
users how to customise the inbox's tabs"; Airbnb's "has clear and concise copy"
plus a CTA to reset filters; Linear and Notion "use simple, monochrome
illustrations that blend into the interface while still offering warmth and
clarity."
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: the monochrome-illustration approach (Linear/Notion) keeps empty states warm without feeling childish to advanced users.
- Source: https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/empty-states-ux-examples/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://mobbin.com/glossary/empty-state — VERIFIED

**Finding 3.3 — Skeleton screens beat spinners on perceived speed and emotion.** Users perceive skeleton-screen loads as roughly "20–30% faster than identical
spinner loads" despite identical real load times; skeletons "performed best on the
emotional level," and can "reduce bounce rates by 9–20%." A spinner says "wait"; a
skeleton says "the page is already here, the content is coming." Facebook,
LinkedIn and YouTube use skeleton screens.
- NEWBIE: less anxiety on first loads when the app feels already-present.
- ATHLETE: high-frequency users feel the app is fast, reinforcing the premium impression.
- Source: https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/skeleton-loading-screen-design/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://medium.com/@elenech/the-psychology-of-waiting-skeletons-ca3b309e12a2 — VERIFIED
- Source: https://uxdesign.cc/what-you-should-know-about-skeleton-screens-a820c45a571a — VERIFIED

INTERPRETATION (offline-first relevance): Volyume reads from local storage, so
true loads are rare — but during sync or first-fetch, skeleton placeholders shaped
like the real content will read as faster and calmer than a spinner.

### Q4 — How the best dark-mode fitness apps handle colour/contrast

**Finding 4.1 — Never pure black, never pure white.** Pure black (#000000) "causes
eye strain, reduces legibility" and a "halation effect"; use dark greys like
#121212. Avoid pure white text (#FFFFFF); use off-whites such as #E0E0E0 or
#C9D1D9. Bright text on pure black "can cause visual vibration and perceived blur,
especially for users with astigmatism."
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical accessibility requirement; affects everyone, especially in low-light gym/bedroom use.
- Source: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/04/inclusive-dark-mode-designing-accessible-dark-themes/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://uxcel.com/blog/12-principles-of-dark-mode-design-627 — VERIFIED

**Finding 4.2 — Desaturate accent colours in dark mode.** "Saturated colours have
difficulty passing WCAG colour-contrast ratio tests, whereas desaturated colours
are likely to agree with the AA standard of at least 4.5:1." Tone vibrant hues down
to ~70–80% saturation. Required ratios: 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large/bold text.
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical; the brand accent must be re-tuned for dark mode, not reused at full saturation.
- Source: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/04/inclusive-dark-mode-designing-accessible-dark-themes/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://dubbot.com/dubblog/2023/dark-mode-a11y.html — VERIFIED

**Finding 4.3 — Signal depth with elevation/tonal shifts, not heavy shadows.** "Many accessible dark-mode designs rely on elevation through contrast, where
slightly lighter background layers, subtle borders, or tonal shifts signal depth
more clearly than shadows alone."
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical; layering by tone keeps a dense dark screen legible.
- Source: https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2025/04/inclusive-dark-mode-designing-accessible-dark-themes/ — VERIFIED

NOTE / GAP: I did not find a clean, named source critiquing a *specific* fitness
app's dark mode (e.g. Strong/Hevy/Whoop dark theme reviews). The colour/contrast
findings above are from authoritative dark-mode UX literature, applied to the
fitness context by inference. Marked accordingly; a follow-up pass on specific
fitness-app dark themes is recommended before the blueprint locks colours.

### Q5 — What makes a fitness app feel supportive vs clinical

**Finding 5.1 — Control-and-numbers framing backfires emotionally.** Designs
"focused on control and numbers like calories, grams of fat, carbohydrates or
protein may backfire emotionally for some users." In real use, "notifications
become nagging, streak resets erase months of effort, and small slip-ups feel like
failure, with many users experiencing shame, avoidance, and lost motivation."
- NEWBIE: highest risk group — rigid targets and nagging reminders drive shame and drop-off.
- ATHLETE: tolerates and often wants the numbers, but still resents fake-streak punishment and rigid framing.
- Source: https://studyfinds.org/fitness-app-motivation-study-myfitnesspal/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://www.newsweek.com/fitness-apps-study-says-they-can-do-more-harm-than-good-10913928 — VERIFIED

**Finding 5.2 — What reads as supportive: autonomy, flexible goals, kind rules, no judgement.** Better-performing designs "support autonomy and enjoyment", with
"flexible goals that adapt to context, clearer feedback about accuracy, kinder
streak rules, and prompts that encourage consistency without judgment."
- NEWBIE: flexible/adaptive goals are essential to avoid early failure spirals.
- ATHLETE: "clearer feedback about accuracy" matters — they want precision, delivered without moralising.
- Source: https://studyfinds.org/fitness-app-motivation-study-myfitnesspal/ — VERIFIED

**Finding 5.3 — Gentler Streak is the gold standard for supportive feel.** ADA 2024
Social Impact winner. "No streaks, no scolding, and no overwhelming dashboards";
stats are "translated into words" as a "daily fitness status" rather than raw
numbers; tone is "supportive but not cheesy, motivating but not fake-hyped";
characters/illustrations create "a soft, cozy, warm, homey atmosphere"; users can
mark themselves "sick, injured, or on a break when life happens." A user: "The
design feels calming and thoughtful and genuinely motivates you to move in a more
balanced way."
- NEWBIE: the explicit "life happens" states and word-based status remove the shame trigger.
- ATHLETE: the *Activity Path* reframes load/recovery balance — useful even to advanced trainers, and it avoids punishing layoffs.
- Source: https://www.sketch.com/blog/gentler-streak/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3m0ht22s — VERIFIED
- Source: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/apple-announces-winners-of-the-2024-apple-design-awards/ — VERIFIED

**Finding 5.4 — Language sensitivity is part of feeling supportive.** For clinical
populations, sensitivity in language matters, e.g. "avoiding terms like 'easy' for
exercises that are genuinely difficult for someone with limitations." Conversational
microcopy that avoids jargon reads as human, not clinical.
- NEWBIE: never call a hard thing "easy"; it alienates the struggling beginner.
- ATHLETE: still benefits from precise, non-patronising language.
- Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8209532/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://thisisglance.com/blog/healthcare-app-psychology-building-trust-through-design — VERIFIED

INTERPRETATION (safety-system relevance only — no engine change implied): the
"life happens" pattern and word-based status framing align with a supportive,
non-shaming tone. This is a *design/copy* observation; it is not a recommendation
to alter the ED safety system in `src/coaching/safety/`.

### Q6 — Design elements that drive trust in a health context

**Finding 6.1 — Clean layout + whitespace = competence signal.** "Clean,
professional layouts with plenty of white space immediately signal competence";
icons should be "instantly recognisable and consistent throughout."
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical; clutter reads as amateur/untrustworthy to both.
- Source: https://medium.com/@artonest.design/healthcare-app-ui-design-that-patients-trust-c4693dcccfbf — VERIFIED

**Finding 6.2 — Predictable navigation = a feeling of control = trust.** "Good UI
keeps navigation simple, intuitive, and predictable, so users always feel in
control", knowing "where they are, how to go back, and what happens next."
- NEWBIE: predictability is what stops them feeling lost and abandoning.
- ATHLETE: predictability lets them move fast on muscle memory.
- Source: https://www.insivia.com/designing-for-trust-web-design-elements-that-enhance-credibility-in-healthtech-platforms/ — VERIFIED

**Finding 6.3 — Transparency, conversational microcopy, and credibility signals.** "Transparent data-usage policies are essential"; use "clear, conversational
microcopy and avoid jargon… especially when asking for sensitive information"; bake
in credibility via real testimonials and certifications/security badges. Blue is
noted as "naturally" reading as secure/trustworthy in health contexts.
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical; both want to know where their health data goes.
- Source: https://thisisglance.com/blog/healthcare-app-psychology-building-trust-through-design — VERIFIED
- Source: https://www.solutelabs.com/blog/build-trust-healthtech-apps — VERIFIED

INTERPRETATION (architecture alignment): Volyume's EU-Dublin residency and
no-PII-to-third-parties stance are exactly the transparency signals this literature
says drive trust — *if surfaced in the UI*. The research supports making the
privacy posture visible, not just true.

### Q7 — Psychology of premium app design

**Finding 7.1 — The aesthetic-usability effect: beautiful is perceived as more usable, more trustworthy, more valuable.** Users "perceive aesthetically pleasing
interfaces as easier to use, more trustworthy, and more valuable, regardless of
actual underlying functionality." First measured by Kurosu & Kashimura (1995, 26
ATM-UI variants, 252 participants): aesthetic appeal correlated more strongly with
*perceived* ease of use than with *actual* ease of use.
- NEWBIE: a beautiful UI lowers perceived difficulty — directly improves activation/retention.
- ATHLETE: beauty raises perceived value, supporting the premium/Pro positioning.
- Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetic%E2%80%93usability_effect — VERIFIED

**Finding 7.2 — Beauty buys forgiveness for *minor* flaws only.** "People are more
tolerant of minor usability issues when the design is aesthetically pleasing" — but
"a pretty design can make users forgiving of minor usability problems, not of large
ones."
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical; polish is not a substitute for the core flow working.
- Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/ — VERIFIED
- Source: https://phenomenonstudio.com/article/the-beauty-trap-why-beautiful-design-doesnt-always-mean-a-good-product/ — VERIFIED

**Finding 7.3 — Emotion is what's remembered.** "Humans don't remember every
detail of a product, they remember how it made them feel" — the case for
emotionally resonant microinteractions and warmth over pure information delivery.
- NEWBIE/ATHLETE: identical.
- Source: https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/microinteractions-and-emotion-tiny-details-huge-impact-efec5714a6a8 — VERIFIED

## 5. PROPOSAL INPUT (sourced only — for the blueprint session)

These are inputs derived from VERIFIED findings; they are not engineering
decisions. Anything touching billing, the coaching engine, or `src/coaching/safety/`
is explicitly out of scope here.

1. **Default to summary-first, offer opt-in density.** Adopt the Oura model as the
   default (few headline numbers + breathing room) and let advanced users opt into a
   denser/detailed view — serving newbie and athlete from one design. (Findings 2.1,
   2.4)
2. **Build warmth in deliberately: one disciplined brand accent + restrained
   animation + haptics on key actions.** Monzo's Hot Coral and Headspace's warm
   neutrals are the model; haptics are a measurable premium signal. (Findings 1.3,
   1.4, 7.3)
3. **Invest in typography and spacing as the primary "premium" lever**, before any
   feature gilding — it is what users read as "someone cared." (Finding 1.2)
4. **Use skeleton placeholders shaped like real content during sync/first-fetch, not
   spinners.** ~20–30% faster perceived; calmer. Fits offline-first (loads are rare,
   so this is cheap to do well). (Finding 3.3)
5. **Empty states = what / why / what-to-do, with simple monochrome illustration.** For newbies they double as onboarding; keep them terse for returning users.
   (Findings 3.1, 3.2)
6. **Dark mode: no pure black (use ~#121212), off-white text (~#E0E0E0), accent
   desaturated to ~70–80%, depth via tonal elevation. Target WCAG AA 4.5:1 / 3:1.** (Findings 4.1–4.3)
7. **Adopt a supportive, non-judgmental tone in copy and status framing**
   (word-based status, "life happens" allowances, no shaming streak resets,
   never call hard things "easy"). Design/copy only — no safety-engine change.
   (Findings 5.1–5.4)
8. **Surface the privacy posture (EU residency, no third-party PII) in the UI** as a
   trust signal, alongside clean layout, predictable navigation, and conversational
   jargon-free microcopy. (Findings 6.1–6.3)
9. **Treat beauty as functional, not cosmetic** (aesthetic-usability effect raises
   perceived ease, trust and value) — but do not let it paper over a broken core
   flow; beauty forgives only minor flaws. (Findings 7.1, 7.2)
