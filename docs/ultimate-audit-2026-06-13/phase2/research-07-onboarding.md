## 2. FINDINGS (grouped by brief question)

### Q1 — How do apps identify user type WITHOUT condescending?

**Finding 1.1 — Ask experience level directly, then immediately ask the
forward-looking goal (Fitbod).** Fitbod's onboarding "start[s] by identifying
the user's experience level (beginner) followed by the user's weekly workout
goal," and each session is then "custom-built for your fitness level and
equipment," supporting "advanced, intermediate, and beginner levels."
The non-condescending move is pairing the level question with a goal/output the
user owns, not labelling them.
*NEWBIE:* the level question feels like care, not a test, when it leads
straight to an appropriate starting difficulty. *ATHLETE:* the same question
prevents the app from forcing them through beginner content.
Status: VERIFIED — https://fitbod.me/blog/what-fitness-app-is-best-for-you-how-fitbod-adapts-to-any-fitness-level-goal-or-gym-setup/

**Finding 1.2 — Behavioural archetype profiling via binary either/or choices
(Noom).** Noom segments with binary choices such as "I want to reach a lower
weight OR change my lifestyle," routing users into archetypes (e.g. "Peak
performer"). It avoids condescension by framing every question as *about the
user's intent*, then reflecting it back with acknowledgement copy.
*NEWBIE:* archetype framing dignifies them as a "type of person", not a novice.
*ATHLETE:* lets the app skip motivational hand-holding they would resent.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding
(NOTE the same source flags a downside: Noom asks "intensely personal
information before building any trust" — segmentation that goes too personal
too early is itself a condescension/abandonment risk.)

**Finding 1.3 — Crude 3-box segmentation reads as condescending / "one size
fits all" (Nike Training Club).** Reviewers describe NTC as: "The quiz simply
shoves you into one of three boxes: beginner, intermediate, or advanced.
There's no personal touch — this is a one-size-fits-all deal." This is the
anti-pattern: a level label with no personalisation feels reductive to both
ends of the spectrum.
*NEWBIE:* "advanced/intermediate/beginner" with no follow-up gives them no
confidence the plan fits. *ATHLETE:* "intermediate" box feels generic and
under-serving.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.fitnessnav.com/insights/madmuscles-vs-fitbod-vs-betterme-vs-freeletics/

**Finding 1.4 — Continuous calibration replaces the up-front label
(Freeletics).** Freeletics asks goals, level, equipment, days/week and time per
session, then "after every workout you rate difficulty, energy levels, and
whether you completed everything, and the AI uses this to recalibrate." The
declared level matters less because the app earns accuracy through behaviour —
so a mis-stated level self-corrects, removing the pressure to "prove" a level.
*NEWBIE:* no penalty for picking the wrong level — it adapts down. *ATHLETE:*
the app quickly ramps up to their real capacity instead of trapping them.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.fitnessnav.com/insights/madmuscles-vs-fitbod-vs-betterme-vs-freeletics/

**Finding 1.5 — Coach-matched onboarding sidesteps self-labelling (Ladder,
Caliber).** Ladder "assesses your experience level" but frames the first action
as choosing a *training style and coach*, "creating a personal training-like
environment." Caliber uses an onboarding video call where a coach explains the
process. Letting the user choose a coach/style is identity-affirming rather
than test-like.
*NEWBIE:* picking a coach feels supportive and human. *ATHLETE:* selecting
style/coach signals the app respects their existing preferences.
Status: VERIFIED — https://madmuscles-review.com/workout-apps/ladder-app-review/ ;
https://barbend.com/caliber-fitness-app-review/

### Q2 — Maximum onboarding length before drop-off (WITH evidence)

This is the most evidence-rich question and the answer is genuinely
counter-intuitive: **for fitness/subscription apps, longer onboarding can
*raise* conversion — but only when every screen returns value.** Two bodies of
evidence pull in opposite directions; both are real.

**Finding 2.1 — Generic friction data: completion collapses as steps rise.**
"Completion drops from 72% at 3 steps to 16% at 7 steps." Separately,
"72% of users abandon apps if onboarding requires too many steps," and "every
additional field increases drop-off by 12–18%."
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.saasfactor.co/blogs/why-users-drop-off-during-onboarding-and-how-to-fix-it ;
https://userguiding.com/blog/user-onboarding-statistics ;
https://uxmag.com/articles/the-psychology-of-onboarding-first-impressions-rule-the-brain

**Finding 2.2 — Industry rule-of-thumb for data-gathering: 7–8 screens.**
General best-practice guidance is "Seven to eight screens are enough to gather
background data on the user's fitness level, available equipment, core fitness
goals, and other information."
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.fitnessondemand247.com/news/fitness-app-onboarding

**Finding 2.3 — Counter-evidence: long onboarding raised trial starts (Lose
It!, RevenueCat).** RevenueCat's growth teardown reports that at Lose It!,
"trial [start] rates went up double digits as onboarding got longer," and the
team "kept making it longer and longer until we got diminishing returns." The
piece explicitly contradicts Apple's "keep it brief" guidance for the H&F
category and names Me+ (~45–50 screens, 7–10 min), Noom, Fitbod, Balance and
RISE as long-onboarding examples. RISE's co-founder: "If we can do something
that creates more value… but that requires more friction… we're going to do
it." Critically, the piece says to measure **Realised LTV per customer**, not
trial starts alone.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/why-your-onboarding-experience-might-be-too-short/

**Finding 2.4 — Noom proves screen-count is not the variable; "emptiness" is.**
Noom's onboarding runs ~77 steps (one source) to ~113 screens (another) and
takes 10–15 minutes, yet converts well because "Length isn't the enemy;
emptiness is." Engagement is held by: feedback on nearly every screen
("Thank you for sharing. That's an important (and hard) first step."), a
weight-loss projection date that updates every ~21 screens, section progress
bars, and inline education.
Status: VERIFIED — https://growthwaves.substack.com/p/the-113-screen-onboarding-that-doesnt ;
https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/

**Finding 2.5 — Progress indicators measurably lift completion of longer flows.**
"Progress bars increase completion by 22%" and "onboarding checklists improve
task completion by 67%." These are the mechanisms that make a long flow
survivable.
Status: VERIFIED — https://userguiding.com/blog/user-onboarding-statistics

**SYNTHESIS (the honest answer):** There is no single "max length". The
evidence supports: (a) if the onboarding is *empty data-collection*, keep it to
~3–8 screens because completion falls off a cliff after that
(72%→16% by step 7); (b) if every screen *returns value* — acknowledgement,
education, an updating projection, a visible progress bar — length can extend to
40+ screens / 7–15 min and still raise paid conversion (Noom, Me+, Lose It!).
The deciding variable is value-per-screen, not screen count.
*NEWBIE:* tolerate fewer empty screens; need quick wins and reassurance early.
*ATHLETE:* will tolerate a longer, denser intake *if* it visibly buys them a
sharper, more credible plan; resent it if it is generic.
INTERPRETATION (separate from sourced findings): for Volyume's dual audience,
the safest design is a *branching* onboarding — a short, value-returning core
for everyone, with an optional deeper intake that experienced users opt into
and beginners can skip. This is an inference from the combined evidence above,
not a single sourced claim.

### Q3 — Communicating intelligence/science WITHOUT overwhelming

**Finding 3.1 — Teach inside the questions (Noom green/yellow/red).** Noom
"teaches while gathering information": food questions "explain calorie density
and introduce their green/yellow/red food system, transforming data collection
into education. Users finish understanding the methodology itself." Science is
delivered as a by-product of answering, never as a lecture screen.
Status: VERIFIED — https://growthwaves.substack.com/p/the-113-screen-onboarding-that-doesnt

**Finding 3.2 — Operational transparency / labour illusion conveys "this is
sophisticated" (Noom).** Loading/analysis screens "show[ing] all the work that
they're doing for us" make the algorithm feel intelligent; the source ties this
to Harvard research that "if you show people the work you're doing for them,
they appreciate the results more." The same source warns it can be "a mirage" —
the perceived science must be real or it erodes trust.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding

**Finding 3.3 — Progressive disclosure / just-in-time education is the core
anti-overwhelm pattern.** "Progressive onboarding introduces information and
features gradually… reducing cognitive load," and "works particularly well for
… fitness apps." Just-in-time: "Instead of explaining everything up front, you
highlight features as they become relevant." Tooltips/walkthroughs deliver
depth "without overloading the UI."
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/what-is-progressive-disclosure/ ;
https://www.appcues.com/blog/essential-guide-mobile-user-onboarding-ui-ux

**Finding 3.4 — Non-shaming framing signals credibility to sophisticated users
(MacroFactor).** MacroFactor "combines innovative coaching algorithms with
proven nutrition and behavioural science," and pointedly: "you'll never see
warnings, red numbers, or shaming when you go over targets, unlike in other
macro tracker apps." Tone itself communicates seriousness to experienced users.
Status: VERIFIED — https://apps.apple.com/us/app/macrofactor-macro-tracker/id1553503471
*ATHLETE:* the absence of nagging reads as respect for their judgement.
*NEWBIE:* removes fear of "doing it wrong", protecting fragile early motivation.
(Note for Volyume: this aligns with the ED-safety stance in CLAUDE.md —
non-shaming tone is both a credibility and a safety feature.)

**Finding 3.5 — Imprecise/abstract science backfires (Noom critique).** The
behavioural-scientist critique flags Noom citing "78% of participants… lost
weight" as sounding "like BS", and reporting "5% weight loss" instead of
relatable pound figures "basically nobody thinks about." Lesson: translate
science into the user's own units and avoid suspiciously round stats.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding

### Q4 — Most common reason users abandon onboarding

**Finding 4.1 — Friction before perceived value (the umbrella cause).**
"Slow onboarding flows, forced sign-ups, feature overload, unclear value
propositions, and aggressive permission requests increase drop-offs before
users fully understand the product." And: most abandonment happens when
"friction delays them from experiencing real value."
Status: VERIFIED — https://sparklin.com/blog/why-users-abandon-apps-during-onboarding

**Finding 4.2 — Permission/data requests are a top single trigger.**
"60% of users abandon onboarding and uninstall an app when they discover how
much personal information the app is requesting"; apps requesting permissions in
the first session see "up to 60% lower opt-in rates"; "86% of users avoid apps
that ask for too many permissions."
Status: VERIFIED — https://sparklin.com/blog/why-users-abandon-apps-during-onboarding

**Finding 4.3 — Forced signup / registration walls.** "43% of users abandon
onboarding due to friction from identity verification"; "68% of consumers have
abandoned … applications mid-onboarding because the process was too long or
complex." Duolingo's "lazy registration" (defer signup until value is felt) is
the named counter-pattern.
Status: VERIFIED — https://sparklin.com/blog/why-users-abandon-apps-during-onboarding ;
https://medium.com/@kotarina832/building-effective-onboarding-experiences-lessons-from-duolingo-7aa2af536020

**Finding 4.4 — Unclear next steps / no understanding of value.** "43% of churn
stems from unclear 'next steps' during onboarding"; "90% of users churn without
understanding product value in the first week."
Status: VERIFIED — https://userguiding.com/blog/user-onboarding-statistics

**Finding 4.5 — First-session damage is durable.** Forrester (2024, via UX
Magazine): "84% of long-term dissatisfaction originates in the first session,"
and early friction decreases trust "by 47% even when later steps improve."
Status: VERIFIED — https://uxmag.com/articles/the-psychology-of-onboarding-first-impressions-rule-the-brain

**SYNTHESIS:** The single most common reason, across sources, is **friction
encountered before the user perceives value** — most acutely as (a) personal-
data/permission demands and (b) forced signup, both occurring before the app
has demonstrated worth.

### Q5 — How do apps create an aha moment during onboarding?

**Finding 5.1 — Definition + rule.** An aha moment is "the instant a user first
realises your product's core value." Best practice: "eliminate every step
between signup and the aha moment" and engineer onboarding so the user
"complete[s] one core action that delivers immediate value within the first
session" — for a running app, "completing a 5-minute guided run."
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.appcues.com/blog/aha-moment-examples ;
https://www.appcues.com/blog/aha-moment-guide

**Finding 5.2 — Personalised plan preview as the aha (Headspace, fitness
pattern).** Headspace asks experience/goals then delivers "a personalised recap
of their guided meditation plan, ready to start right away." For fitness, "the
moment of value might be a personalised 8-week plan preview"; the pattern is
"personalisation → first meaningful action → aha moment, all within a single
onboarding session," with the paywall placed "immediately after the aha moment."
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.appcues.com/blog/aha-moment-examples ;
https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/5-steps-app-onboarding-before-the-paywall

**Finding 5.3 — Value before signup / let them do the thing (Duolingo, Canva,
Sprout).** Duolingo "guides visitors through a quick translation exercise,
showing how quick and easy it is… before asking users to commit with a signup,"
reaching first action in 3–4 minutes. Canva "lets users design before signup";
Sprout Social shows "fake data set" so users play immediately. The aha is the
user *doing the core action*, not watching a tour.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.appcues.com/blog/aha-moment-examples ;
https://medium.com/@kotarina832/building-effective-onboarding-experiences-lessons-from-duolingo-7aa2af536020

**Finding 5.4 — Updating projection as a manufactured aha (Noom).** Noom shows a
goal date that "keeps moving closer… you feel momentum, like the app is working
for you" — the aha is seeing a concrete, personalised outcome ("you'll be 148
pounds by May 22"). Caveat: §3.5 warns the numbers must feel credible.
Status: VERIFIED — https://growthwaves.substack.com/p/the-113-screen-onboarding-that-doesnt ;
https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding

**Finding 5.5 — Fitbod's muscle-recovery map is its tangible aha.** Fitbod
"shows users a muscle-recovery map and builds workouts" custom to level and
equipment — a visible, intelligent artefact produced from their inputs.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/fitbod-app-review

*NEWBIE aha:* a personalised, doable plan / first small win (a 5-min run, a
first logged set) that says "you can do this." *ATHLETE aha:* a credible,
data-driven artefact (recovery map, calibrated plan, sharp projection) that says
"this is sophisticated enough for me."

### Q6 — What newbies need in the first 5 minutes vs experienced users

**Finding 6.1 — Newbies: encouragement, simple wins, low intimidation.**
"Someone who hasn't exercised in months doesn't need access to advanced workout
plans… on day one. They need encouragement and simple wins. Start with basic
goal setting… Small victories build confidence." Beginners carry "high
intimidation around gym environments and equipment, fragile motivation that
depends on early wins and positive reinforcement, and uncertainty about goals."
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.appcues.com/blog/essential-guide-mobile-user-onboarding-ui-ux ;
https://www.fitnessondemand247.com/news/fitness-app-onboarding

**Finding 6.2 — Couch to 5K is the canonical "defuse intimidation" model.**
C25K uses "run/walk intervals that slowly build your endurance without asking
you to run nonstop on day one," explicitly "perfect for beginners who feel
intimidated by running." Verbatim beginner relief in §3.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.nonetorun.com/blog/couch-to-5k-running-plan-alternative ;
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couch_to_5K

**Finding 6.3 — Experienced users: speed-to-tool and depth, minimal
hand-holding.** Hevy lets serious users "create an account and log your first
set in under 90 seconds"; it suits "experienced lifters who know what they're
doing and just need a reliable tracker." MyFitnessPal "still has a real
audience: experienced trackers who need granular data, a large database… willing
to pay the premium" — its *minimal* onboarding suits them but leaves beginners
"dropped into the app with little guidance, which can be confusing."
Status: VERIFIED — https://repreturn.com/hevy-app-review/ ;
https://www.nutrola.app/en/blog/myfitnesspal-vs-lose-it-vs-fatsecret-free-tier-2026

**Finding 6.4 — The same app rarely serves both with one flow (Lose It! vs
MFP).** "If you are brand new to macro tracking, Lose It's onboarding is
friendlier and less overwhelming than MyFitnessPal," whereas MFP's depth-first,
guidance-light approach favours experienced users. This is direct evidence that
beginner-friendly and expert-friendly onboarding pull in different directions —
the core dual-audience tension.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.nutrola.app/en/blog/myfitnesspal-vs-lose-it-vs-fatsecret-free-tier-2026 ;
https://calorietrackerbuddy.com/blog/lose-it-vs-myfitnesspal-complete-2026-comparison/

**SYNTHESIS — first 5 minutes:**
- NEWBIE needs: reassurance, an obvious small win, no jargon, no intimidation,
  a clear "you can do this" plan, ability to skip/defer heavy data entry.
- ATHLETE needs: get to the tool fast (<90s to first log), depth and granular
  control available immediately, credible data artefacts, no motivational
  hand-holding, no being forced through beginner content.

### Q7 — Psychology of first impressions in fitness apps

**Finding 7.1 — First impressions form in milliseconds, decision in seconds.**
Emotional impression at "0.2 seconds", cognitive at "3 seconds", stay-or-leave
decision at "10–30 seconds"; "emotions process information 5× faster than
conscious reasoning." Implication: the *feel* (tone, visual calm, perceived
effort) lands before any feature is understood.
Status: VERIFIED — https://uxmag.com/articles/the-psychology-of-onboarding-first-impressions-rule-the-brain

**Finding 7.2 — Cognitive load and predictability drive abandonment.** "Mental
fatigue begins after 2 consecutive decisions"; users "abandon flows 27% faster
when they cannot predict effort." Progress bars (predictability) lift completion
22% (§2.5). So showing remaining effort is itself a first-impression lever.
Status: VERIFIED — https://uxmag.com/articles/the-psychology-of-onboarding-first-impressions-rule-the-brain ;
https://userguiding.com/blog/user-onboarding-statistics

**Finding 7.3 — Commitment / IKEA / sunk-cost effects via personal input.** Apps
"ask users to input personal information such as their fitness goals, current
fitness level, and preferences, leveraging the commitment bias where individuals
are more likely to stick to a course of action once they've made a personal
commitment." Noom's 10–30 min investment is explicitly framed as sunk-cost
priming for conversion: "By the time Noom shows pricing, users have already
invested time, effort, and emotional energy."
Status: VERIFIED — https://amalgama.co/the-psychology-behind-fitness-apps-onboarding/ ;
https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/

**Finding 7.4 — Acknowledgement / empathy at vulnerable moments.** Noom
responds to a weight entry with "Thank you for sharing. That's an important
(and hard) first step." Emotional acknowledgement at sensitive inputs protects
the first impression for vulnerable users.
Status: VERIFIED — https://growthwaves.substack.com/p/the-113-screen-onboarding-that-doesnt
(Volyume note: this maps directly onto the ED-safety / non-shaming requirement.)

**Finding 7.5 — Social proof and the "knows me" feeling.** Noom cites "we've
helped 3,627,436 people lose weight" and demographically-similar testimonials;
light personalisation makes the product "feel like it knows me" via "saved
preferences, pre-filled fields, and goal-based templates." Good onboarding
raises 30-day retention 3.5× (Forrester) and personalised onboarding lifts
retention 40% vs generic.
Status: VERIFIED — https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding ;
https://uxmag.com/articles/the-psychology-of-onboarding-first-impressions-rule-the-brain ;
https://userguiding.com/blog/user-onboarding-statistics

## 5. PROPOSAL INPUT (sourced only)

1. **Branch the onboarding by self-declared experience, then prove it adapts.**
   Ask level + goal together (Fitbod, F.1.1), avoid bare 3-box labels (NTC anti-
   pattern, F.1.3), and recalibrate from behaviour so a wrong choice self-
   corrects (Freeletics, F.1.4). Lets one app serve newbie→elite without
   condescension (the Lose It!/MFP tension, F.6.4).
2. **Make every screen return value; only then is length safe.** Empty data-
   collection must stay ~3–8 screens (72%→16% by step 7, F.2.1–2.2). A deeper
   intake is acceptable *only* with acknowledgement copy, inline education, an
   updating projection, and a progress bar (Noom, F.2.4–2.5). Track Realised LTV,
   not trial starts (RevenueCat, F.2.3).
3. **Teach the science inside the questions, not on lecture screens** (Noom
   green/yellow/red, F.3.1); use progressive disclosure / just-in-time tooltips
   (F.3.3); translate science into the user's own units and avoid suspiciously
   round stats (F.3.5).
4. **Defer signup and heavy permissions until after the aha.** Permission/data
   demands and forced signup are the top abandonment triggers (F.4.2–4.3);
   Duolingo-style lazy registration is the counter-pattern (F.5.3).
5. **Engineer one concrete aha in the first session:** a personalised plan
   preview / first doable action (F.5.1–5.2). Newbie aha = a doable plan + first
   small win; athlete aha = a credible data artefact (recovery map / calibrated
   plan). Place any paywall immediately after it (F.5.2).
6. **First 5 minutes, split by audience:** newbies get reassurance, a small win,
   no jargon, skippable data entry (F.6.1–6.2); experienced users get <90s to
   first log, immediate depth, no hand-holding (F.6.3).
7. **Respect the first-impression physics:** the *feel* lands in 0.2–3s, decision
   in 10–30s (F.7.1); show remaining effort via progress bars (predictability,
   F.7.2); acknowledge vulnerable inputs with empathy copy (F.7.4) — which also
   serves Volyume's non-shaming ED-safety stance.

INTERPRETATION (kept separate): the single highest-leverage decision for
Volyume's dual audience is a **branching onboarding** — a short value-returning
core everyone completes, plus an optional deep intake experts opt into and
beginners skip. This is inferred from the combined evidence (F.2 synthesis +
F.6.4), not a single sourced claim.
