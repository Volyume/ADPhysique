## 2. FINDINGS (grouped by dispatch questions)

### Q1 — How do the best apps explain calorie targets to a newbie?

**Finding 1.1 — The strongest newbie pattern is "set it for them, explain the why
separately."** Welling sets "personalised calorie and macro targets based on your body
stats and goal without requiring you to understand what those targets mean in detail to
get started." Most apps derive TDEE from age/sex/height/weight/activity (Mifflin-St Jeor
is the common formula) and subtract ~500 kcal for ~1 lb/week loss.
- NEWBIE: a number with zero explanation is overwhelming; the best onboarding gives a
  usable target immediately, then teaches.
- ATHLETE: wants the formula and the levers exposed, not hidden.
- Source: https://www.welling.ai/articles/best-calorie-tracking-apps-beginners-2026 ;
  https://www.amyfoodjournal.com/blog/calorie-counting-for-beginners — VERIFIED.

**Finding 1.2 — Noom replaces the raw number with a behavioural frame.** Noom assigns a
daily calorie budget but "the emphasis remains on understanding the psychology behind
eating patterns rather than simply adhering to numerical limits," delivered as daily
2–5 min bite-sized lessons + quiz. It centres on "replacing less nutrient-dense calories
with other more filling foods" (Fortune tester).
- NEWBIE: education-first lowers the intimidation of the number. STRONG model for Volyume's
  newbie tier.
- ATHLETE: too slow / hand-holding; an athlete wants the dashboard.
- Source: https://thisisamandaliu.medium.com/noom-case-study-4c404a3e2dde ;
  https://fortune.com/article/best-calorie-counter-apps/ — VERIFIED.

**Finding 1.3 — WW abstracts calories into a "points budget"** to make targets feel less
clinical, but a medical reviewer warned the points system "contributes to obsessive
tracking or disordered eating," and the "ZeroPoints" label confuses beginners.
- NEWBIE: abstraction reduces fear of numbers but can obscure what's actually happening
  (relevant to Volyume's ED-safety stance).
- Source: https://fortune.com/article/best-calorie-counter-apps/ — VERIFIED.

**Finding 1.4 — Cronometer deliberately does NOT explain or coach.** "Users set their own
precise calorie and macronutrient targets... This isn't an accountability app." Good for
the knowledgeable; poor for a newbie who needs guidance.
- NEWBIE implication: a blank-target app is a non-starter for beginners.
- ATHLETE implication: full manual control is a feature, not a bug.
- Source: https://fortune.com/article/best-calorie-counter-apps/ — VERIFIED.

### Q2 — How do they make macros approachable for someone new?

**Finding 2.1 — Approachable macros = fewer numbers, plain language, and visual cues.**
Beginner-positioned apps (Macro Champ, Macro Simple) explicitly market "no overwhelming
dashboards or hidden paywalls" and a "clean macro counter," tracking only protein/carbs/
calories at first.
- NEWBIE: start with protein + calories; introduce fat/carb split later.
- ATHLETE: wants gram-precise P/C/F simultaneously from day one (Yazio tracks macros
  "simultaneously").
- Source: https://apps.apple.com/cd/app/macro-champ-macro-tracker/id6502575124 ;
  https://blog.eatthismuch.com/best-macro-tracking-apps/ — PARTIAL (vendor/store framing).

**Finding 2.2 — Hand portions are the most approachable macro on-ramp.** Precision
Nutrition: palm = protein, cupped hand = carbs, fist = veg, thumb = fat; "the size of your
adult hand remains constant" and scales to body size. Reported "95 percent as accurate as
other tracking methods — but without the effort of meticulous measurement."
- NEWBIE: removes the scale and the database entirely; huge friction reduction.
- ATHLETE: a fallback for travel/eating-out, not a primary contest-prep method.
- Source: https://www.precisionnutrition.com/hand-portion-math-to-track-macros — VERIFIED.

**Finding 2.3 — Noom's green/yellow/orange and WW's points** both convert macro density
into a colour/number heuristic so newbies don't reason about grams.
- Source: https://fortune.com/article/best-calorie-counter-apps/ — VERIFIED.

### Q3 — How do they stay precise enough for a competitor?

**Finding 3.1 — Adaptive-TDEE recalibration is the competitor-grade feature.** MacroFactor's
adaptive algorithm "recalibrates targets weekly, catching metabolic slowdown that a static
calculator would miss during prolonged calorie restriction." Caveat: it "does not have
dedicated contest-prep phases or reverse-dieting protocols."
- ATHLETE: catches metabolic adaptation across a 16–20 week prep — essential.
- NEWBIE: same engine works invisibly; they just see updated targets.
- Source: https://nutrola.app/en/blog/cronometer-vs-macrofactor-vs-carbon-2026 — PARTIAL
  (third-party comparison) ; corroborated by MacroFactor help docs (below) — VERIFIED.

**Finding 3.2 — Carbon and RP add explicit diet periodisation / nutrient timing.** Carbon's
"phased coaching algorithm maps directly to diet periodisation that physique athletes
follow, with metabolic-adaptation tracking particularly valuable during 16–20 week contest
preps." RP Diet "adjusts your carb and fat intake based on when you work out." Carbon built
by Layne Norton (PhD) + Keith Kraker (RD); RP by PhDs/IFBB pros/RDs.
- ATHLETE: phase-aware coaching + nutrient timing is what separates competitor tools from
  generic trackers.
- Source: https://nutrola.app/en/blog/cronometer-vs-macrofactor-vs-carbon-2026 ;
  https://www.joincarbon.com/ ; https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1330041267 — PARTIAL/VERIFIED.

**Finding 3.3 — Micronutrient sufficiency during restriction = Cronometer.** "Excellent for
bodybuilders who want to ensure micronutrient sufficiency during restrictive diet phases,"
~3.5% data variance vs MFP's ~6.8%.
- ATHLETE: during deep cuts, micronutrient floor monitoring matters.
- Source: https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/macrofactor-vs-cronometer-2026-62a278ee64 — PARTIAL.

**Finding 3.4 — Database accuracy is the precision ceiling.** Academic work cited that MFP
underestimated protein ~7.8% and carbs ~6.4% vs reference databases, with weak sodium/
cholesterol correlations — because most entries are unverified member submissions.
- ATHLETE: crowdsourced DB error is unacceptable at competition precision; verified DBs win.
- Source: https://www.mynetdiary.com/best-calorie-tracker-database-accuracy.html — PARTIAL
  (vendor citing academic work).

### Q4 — Most common nutrition-app frustrations across all users?

**Finding 4.1 — Inaccurate / crowdsourced database entries.** MFP "member-submitted entries
go live without staff verification"; a wrong entry "becomes instantly searchable by millions."
Fortune: MFP "is known to underestimate calorie needs or have inaccurate nutrition data."
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MyFitnessPal ;
  https://www.mynetdiary.com/myfitnesspal-alternatives.html ;
  https://fortune.com/article/best-calorie-counter-apps/ — VERIFIED.

**Finding 4.2 — Core features moved behind paywalls.** MFP removed the barcode scanner from
the free tier — a frequently cited grievance.
- Source: https://fortune.com/article/best-calorie-counter-apps/ — VERIFIED.

**Finding 4.3 — Hidden / opaque pricing.** Cal AI "does not display its pricing on its
website, App Store listing, or anywhere public — you only see the price after completing a
detailed onboarding process."
- Source: https://www.eesel.ai/blog/cal-ai — PARTIAL (summarises Reddit/store sentiment).

**Finding 4.4 — AI photo tools mis-estimate portions and miss hidden ingredients.** Cal AI
"has trouble spotting hidden ingredients like cooking oil or sugar... often gets portion
sizes wrong."
- Source: https://www.eesel.ai/blog/cal-ai — PARTIAL.

**Finding 4.5 — Number-focus fuels obsessive/disordered eating.** "Heavy focus on numbers,
green positive signals and red warning signs... can create a harmful mindset"; users
tracking for "weight-control/shape reasons were more likely to report the app contributed
to ED symptoms (food preoccupation, all-or-none thinking, food anxiety, purging)." BBC: a
calorie-counter app "allowed eating disorder to spiral."
- NEWBIE: highest-risk group; design must avoid red/shame signals.
- ATHLETE: also at risk during prep; signposting matters.
- Source: https://therapist.com/disorders/eating-disorders/calorie-counting-apps/ ;
  https://feeds.bbci.co.uk/news/uk-england-shropshire-48875271 — VERIFIED.
- DIRECTLY RELEVANT to Volyume's ED safety system (`src/coaching/safety/`) — do not touch
  that system; this finding only validates its existence.

**Finding 4.6 — Decline in adherence over time is universal.** "All calorie-counting apps
saw a decline in adherence over time." Maintenance-phase self-monitoring adherence is
especially low.
- Source: https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e64139 ;
  https://mhealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e45057 — VERIFIED (peer-reviewed).

### Q5 — How are coaching adjustments to nutrition communicated?

**Finding 5.1 — MacroFactor: transparent, weekly, conservative, no shame.** During a weekly
check-in it "looks for changes in expenditure, weight, and goal to provide a new set of macro
targets." It "won't immediately overreact and drastically slash your energy intake... it will
gradually reduce your energy intake recommendations over a period of weeks."
Verbatim reassurance language (help docs):
  - "It should take MacroFactor about 2–3 weeks to dial in a good energy expenditure estimate."
  - "We don't want to over-react to short-term weight fluctuations that may not be reflective
    of actual rates of muscle and fat gain/loss."
  - "Tracking isn't something that should stress you out. In fact, quite the opposite — food
    logging is your opportunity to feed the app fantastic data."
- NEWBIE: framing changes as "the app learning you" removes blame for a stall.
- ATHLETE: weekly cadence + expenditure trend chart is the expected coaching loop.
- Source: https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/26-how-should-i-interpret-changes-to-my-energy-expenditure ;
  https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/222-how-does-macrofactor-make-adjustments-for-a-weight-gain-or-weight-loss-goal — VERIFIED.

**Finding 5.2 — "Adherence-neutral" is the standout communication philosophy.** MacroFactor
is "adherence neutral... it will meet you where you are and work to get you to your goal
without shaming, judgment, or the requirement that you adhere to your targets perfectly for
the app to make sensible program adjustments."
- NEWBIE: a missed/over day doesn't break the plan or trigger guilt — critical for retention
  and ED safety.
- ATHLETE: lets the algorithm work off real logged data even when adherence wobbles.
- Source: https://nutrola.app/en/blog/most-recommended-calorie-trackers-on-reddit-2026 (theme)
  + MacroFactor help docs above — VERIFIED.

**Finding 5.3 — Carbon/Avatar/RP communicate via scheduled weekly check-ins.** Avatar "requires
weekly check-ins... automatically adjusts your macros based on progress." Carbon "will adjust
the plan as you progress... if you hit a plateau or stall, Carbon will make adjustments."
- Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/avatar-nutrition-macro-coach/id1507989271 ;
  https://www.joincarbon.com/ — PARTIAL/VERIFIED.

### Q6 — What app features drive nutrition adherence (research)?

**Finding 6.1 — Self-monitoring is the single strongest predictor, but only with low friction
and accuracy.** "Self-monitoring [is] one of the most reliable predictors of progress... but
holds only when the logging is accurate and the tool sits in front of the user at the moment
of the behaviour with no tax to start."
- Source: https://www.healthcarebusinesstoday.com/nutrition-app-retention-was-never-about-features-it-was-always-about-adherence/ — PARTIAL (industry article; fetch was 403, used as search-surfaced summary).

**Finding 6.2 — Peer-reviewed engagement numbers.** A 2021 systematic review: 58% (11/19) of
dietary self-monitoring interventions achieved ≥50%-of-days engagement; engagement declines in
maintenance. A meta-analysis of 34 articles studied app adherence across diet/weight/activity
self-monitoring.
- Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8928602/ ;
  https://mhealth.jmir.org/2022/4/e35479 — VERIFIED (peer-reviewed).

**Finding 6.3 — Low-friction beats feature-breadth.** Industry data: "low-friction tools that
prioritise ease of logging appear more effective than feature-rich apps"; cohort showed users
logging ~2.7 meals/day at day 60 (vs 1.9 for competitors) sustained the habit.
- INTERPRETATION (labelled): supports a fast-logging default for Volyume over a feature-dense
  food diary. (The 2.7-vs-1.9 figure is industry, not peer-reviewed — treat as PARTIAL.)
- Source: https://www.healthcarebusinesstoday.com/nutrition-app-retention-was-never-about-features-it-was-always-about-adherence/ — PARTIAL.

### Q7 — How do apps handle users who don't want to count every gram?

**Finding 7.1 — STRONGEST evidence in this whole report: simplified tracking ≈ same weight
loss, far higher adherence.** JMIR randomised pilot: a simplified "red-zone foods checklist"
was self-monitored on **97% of days** vs **49%** for full Fitbit food logging, yet weight loss
was statistically similar (−3.29 kg vs −3.41 kg at 3 months). 95% of simplified-arm completers
rated it "very/extremely helpful" vs 56% detailed-arm. Authors: simplified tracking "resulted
in high engagement, high ratings of acceptability, and clinically meaningful weight loss."
- NEWBIE: a simplified mode is not a compromise — it is *better adhered to* with equal results.
- ATHLETE: detailed mode remains for those who want precision; the two should coexist.
- Source: https://formative.jmir.org/2022/12/e42191 — VERIFIED (peer-reviewed).

**Finding 7.2 — Hand portions / visual cues / AI photo are the practical no-gram methods.**
Hand-portion ~95% as accurate as weighing; AI photo within ~10–15% of manual for most meals;
SnapCalorie ±80 kcal (Pro iPhone) on a 500-kcal dish vs ±265 eyeballing. "Consistency beats
precision... a method that is 90% accurate and you use daily beats one 99% accurate that you
quit after three days."
- Source: https://www.precisionnutrition.com/hand-portion-math-to-track-macros ;
  https://www.getkalohealth.com/blog/track-calories-without-weighing-food ;
  https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1574239307 (SnapCalorie) — VERIFIED/PARTIAL.

**Finding 7.3 — Noom/WW abstract away grams** via colour zones and points so users never
weigh. (See 1.2, 1.3, 2.3.) — VERIFIED.

## 5. PROPOSAL INPUT FOR VOLYUME (sourced only)

1. **Offer two nutrition modes, not one.** A simplified mode (hand portions / red-zone style /
   protein+calories first) AND a precise gram-level mode. Evidence: simplified tracking gave 97%
   vs 49% adherence at equal weight loss (JMIR e42191) and hand portions are ~95% as accurate
   (Precision Nutrition). This directly answers "users who don't want to count every gram."
2. **Set the newbie target for them, teach the why separately** (Welling/Noom pattern). Don't
   present a bare number with no context — but don't gate getting started on understanding it.
3. **Adopt MacroFactor's adjustment-communication pattern** for Precision Coaching nutrition
   changes: scheduled cadence, show the trend, change gradually, never shame, never require
   perfect adherence for the engine to adjust. (Note: Volyume's engine is deterministic/no-LLM
   per CLAUDE.md — borrow the *communication tone and cadence*, not any algorithm description.)
4. **Use a verified food database, not crowdsourced.** Crowdsourced DBs are the #1 cited
   accuracy frustration and break competitor-grade precision (MFP ~7.8% protein under-estimate).
5. **Avoid red/shame signalling and "countdown" pressure UI.** These are repeatedly linked to
   disordered eating (therapist.com, BBC, Fortune/WW). Reinforces — and never replaces — the
   existing ED safety system in `src/coaching/safety/`.
6. **Prioritise low-friction fast logging over feature breadth** for adherence (JMIR reviews +
   industry data). Feature-dense diaries adhere worse.
7. **For the athlete tier, expose periodisation/phase awareness** (Carbon/RP pattern) and a
   micronutrient floor view (Cronometer pattern) — the precision competitors expect.
