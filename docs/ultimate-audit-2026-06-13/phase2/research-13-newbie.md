## 2. FINDINGS (grouped by brief question)

### Q1. What complete beginners say they need but never get

**F1.1 — A clear, realistic plan instead of endless choice.** A 2-month hands-on test of 7
beginner apps concluded the core split is "a clear, realistic plan or just endless choices" and
"form and progression or just count reps and celebrate streaks." Top tester quote: *"This is the
first time I feel like someone has an actual plan for me without having me sort out what to watch."*
**Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://www.indiehackers.com/post/best-workout-apps-for-beginners-2026-i-tested-7-fitness-apps-for-2-months-442af07d69
- NEWBIE: the single biggest unmet need is *being told what to do next*, not a menu.
- ATHLETE: athletes want the opposite — control, overrides, and the ability to deviate from the plan.

**F1.2 — Teaching, not cheerleading.** Same test: "Teaching beats cheerleading; apps must explain
*why* exercises appear repeatedly." Beginners want the reasoning, not just streak confetti.
**Status: VERIFIED.** Source: as F1.1.
- NEWBIE: a one-line "why this exercise / why this weight" is high value.
- ATHLETE: athletes already know the why; for them the "why" should be *programming rationale*
  (block periodisation, RIR targets), not basics.

**F1.3 — Instructiveness ranks #1 in qualitative study.** Academic qualitative study of female
fitness-app users coded five valued attributes; **Instructiveness** (professional guidance for
safety/effectiveness) was mentioned most (24×), ahead of Personalization (21×), Interaction (20×),
Ease of Use, and Convenience. **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11537609/
- NEWBIE: safety-credible instruction is the top driver, not aesthetics.
- ATHLETE: instructiveness matters less; personalisation/precision dominates.

### Q2. What scared beginners off an app

**F2.1 — Overwhelm and lack of structure; "noisy casino".** The 7-app test named the failure modes:
"overwhelming options and lack of structure" and a "noisy casino of streaks, notifications and
random workouts." **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: as F1.1.

**F2.2 — High-intensity defaults unsuitable for true beginners.** Same source flags
"high-intensity defaults" as a thing that scared beginners off. Freeletics-style intensity cited.
**Status: VERIFIED (claim) / PARTIAL (Freeletics attribution).** Source: as F1.1.
- SAFETY NOTE for Volyume: default intensity for an unknown new user should err gentle.

**F2.3 — Being treated as "a data point in a dashboard".** Apps that "treat users as a data
point[s] in a dashboard rather than individuals" lost beginners. **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: as F1.1.

**F2.4 — Fitbod specifically overwhelms total beginners.** Multiple independent reviews: "Beginners
will likely be overwhelmed and may not get the best workouts for them"; "some newbies may feel
intimidated by the variety of features and customization options." (Mitigated by its Experience
setting.) **Status: VERIFIED.** Sources: https://fitnessdrum.com/fitbod-review/ ,
https://flexfitnessapp.com/blog/fitbod-review/
- ATHLETE: the very options that scare newbies are what athletes praise in Fitbod.

**F2.5 — Paywall before value / meaningless onboarding questions.** "The most common mistake is
moving to the paywall before the user has stated a goal or experienced any value." And: asking
personalisation questions "that don't change anything … are decorative and annoying." **Status:
VERIFIED.** Sources: https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/5-steps-app-onboarding-before-the-paywall ,
https://dev.to/paywallpro/fitness-app-onboarding-guide-data-motivation-completion-an0
- NEWBIE: every onboarding question must visibly change the plan, or it reads as a bait-and-switch.

**F2.6 — Hard-to-cancel / billing friction.** Category-level Trustpilot/review complaints of
fitness apps being difficult to cancel and confusing once set up (e.g. Lose It! "not easy to use
once set up"). **Status: PARTIAL** (aggregator paraphrase). Source: https://ch.trustpilot.com/review/loseit.com

### Q3. What made a beginner feel an app was built for someone else

**F3.1 — App assumes a higher fitness level.** UX/research guide: "beginners who feel the app
assumes a higher fitness level will feel discouraged," and "users who perceive judgmental messaging
disengage." **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://cleverx.com/blog/fitness-app-user-research-a-complete-guide-for-health-and-fitness-product-teams/

**F3.2 — Narrow beauty/body ideals.** Qualitative study: traditional standards ("paleness,
thinness, youthfulness") make apps feel exclusionary; apps "must counter narrow beauty ideals to
feel inclusive." **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11537609/
- ATHLETE: physique competitors *want* aesthetic/lean-focused framing — so this content must be
  contextual, never the default first impression.

**F3.3 — Class library aimed at the already-fit.** Apple Fitness+ offers only
beginner/intermediate/advanced with "limited individualization"; NTC praised precisely *because*
"it's immediately obvious what level each class is designed for." Lack of clear level-signalling is
what makes content feel "for someone else." **Status: VERIFIED.** Sources:
as F1.1 (Fitness+) , https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/nike-training-club-app

### Q4. How apps educate beginners without condescending

**F4.1 — Plain-English fundamentals.** A reviewed strength app "focuses on fitness fundamentals
like energy balance, progressive overload, and nutrition, and breaks it down into plain English so
beginners can understand the importance of each step." **Status: PARTIAL** (review paraphrase,
unnamed app). Source: https://mevolife.com/blog/fitness/coaching/fitness-coach-app-review

**F4.2 — Coach tips delivered in-context, in the coach's voice.** Future embeds per-exercise form
videos with audio + visual cues and "motivating audio messages from your coach … tips and tricks
throughout." Education arrives at the moment of the exercise, framed as a coach helping, not a
textbook lecturing. **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://onbetterliving.com/future-app/

**F4.3 — Demos + prep tips attached to every movement.** "Fitness Coach: Weight Loss": "Every
workout plan includes detailed instruction and preparation tips to ensure correct form, prevent
injuries"; reviewers note "demos on how to do each exercise." **Status: VERIFIED.** Source:
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=fitnesscoach.workoutplanner.weightloss

**F4.4 — Story/gamified teaching removes the lecture tone.** Zombies, Run! teaches running form,
warm-ups and pacing inside a story; reviewers call it "a hell of a lot more fun than normal couch
to 5k apps." Education hidden inside narrative is non-condescending by construction. **Status:
VERIFIED.** Sources: https://breakingmuscle.com/tech-review-zombies-run-5k-training-fitness-game/ ,
https://runningoncoffeeblog.wordpress.com/2013/09/12/review-zombies-run-5k-training/

> INTERPRETATION (kept separate): across F4.1–F4.4 the non-condescending pattern is consistent —
> (a) plain English, (b) "why" attached to the action, (c) delivered in a human/coach voice at the
> point of need, not as a wall of theory up front. This is an inference from the four sourced cases.

### Q5. What casual gym-goers (2–3×/week, no performance goals) want

**F5.1 — Fast, frictionless logging that "doesn't get in the way of lifting."** Tracker comparisons
converge: "fast to use mid-workout, help you see progress clearly, and don't get in the way."
**Status: VERIFIED.** Sources: https://www.hevyapp.com/best-workout-tracker-app/ ,
https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/best-app-for-tracking-workouts

**F5.2 — No forced planning; log when you show up.** Strong's model praised: "create exercises
once, then access them whenever you need to log sets — you don't need to pre-plan workouts or follow
a rigid structure." **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/id464254577

**F5.3 — Simple progress visualisation + the free tier is enough.** Casual users want history,
strength graphs, total volume, frequency — and "most gym-goers can stick with the free version and
have everything they need." **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/best-free-apps-for-tracking-workouts
- NEWBIE/CASUAL: maps directly to Volyume's FREE tier (logging, PBs, progress stats). A casual user
  must get a complete, satisfying loop without ever hitting a Pro wall — consistent with CLAUDE.md gating.
- ATHLETE: athletes additionally want planned sessions, RIR/RPE, periodisation — the Pro surface.

### Q6. Terminology that confuses non-athletes (used unexplained by fitness apps)

**F6.1 — Confirmed confusing terms + plain definitions** (compiled from beginner glossaries):
- **RPE** — Rate of Perceived Exertion, 1–10 scale of how close to failure you are (10 = no more reps).
- **RIR** — Reps In Reserve, how many more you could have done.
- **AMRAP** — As Many Reps/Rounds As Possible.
- **EMOM** — Every Minute On the Minute.
- **1RM / ORM** — One-Rep Max.
- **PB** — Personal Best.
- **DOMS** — Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness.
- **HIIT** — High-Intensity Interval Training.
- **WOD** — Workout Of the Day.
- **Progressive overload** — gradually increasing weight/reps/frequency over time.
- **Macros / BMR / BMI** — also flagged as beginner-confusing (nutrition side).
**Status: VERIFIED.** Sources: https://www.fitshop.co.uk/blog/fitness-jargon-decoded-your-a-z-guide-to-popular-fitness-acronyms/ ,
https://www.kairos.online/blog/workout-terms-gym-slang , https://www.moveyourbonespt.com/blog/strength-and-conditioning-terms

**F6.2 — Terminology is a top-3 starting barrier.** A search snippet claims "terminology confusion
ranks among the top three barriers" and "68% of new gym-goers reported they felt intimidated by
unfamiliar fitness vocabulary." **Status: PARTIAL** — the 68% figure appeared only in a search
snippet I could not trace to a primary study; **do NOT cite the 68% as fact** without sourcing.
- NEWBIE: RPE/RIR/AMRAP/macros must be glossed inline (tap-to-define) the first time shown.
- ATHLETE: these terms are the *expected* working vocabulary; glossing must be dismissible/optional,
  never forced on advanced users.

### Q7. How apps make beginners feel they belong

**F7.1 — Peer "sister" support inside the app.** Qualitative study verbatim: *"When I first began,
I often struggled to find my rhythm, but whenever I sought help through pop-ups, a fellow 'sister'
promptly responded."* **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11537609/

**F7.2 — Private, judgment-free space lowers the entry barrier.** Same study: *"I usually work out
in my living room using the fitness app. I don't have to worry about being approached, and I can
focus entirely on my workout"*; "shyness and fear of ridicule have been significant factors limiting
women's participation." **Status: VERIFIED.** Source: as F7.1.

**F7.3 — Social connectedness has the strongest link to well-being / retention.** Well-being study:
"social connectedness has the strongest positive link with overall well-being," and community
challenges "increase users' sense of belonging and motivation." Apps with social features see
"20–35% lower monthly churn." **Status: VERIFIED.** Sources:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11907615/ , https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/fitness-apps
- NEWBIE: belonging is built by (a) judgment-free privacy by default, (b) low-stakes peer help.
- ATHLETE: belonging comes more from leaderboards/competition framing — *opposite* mechanism.
- NOTE for Volyume: any social/community feature must respect the offline-first + no-PII-to-external
  architecture in CLAUDE.md. Flagging this as a constraint, not building it.

### Q8. Simplest version of a coaching app that still delivers real results

**F8.1 — StrongLifts 5x5 is the canonical answer.** Two alternating workouts, five compound lifts,
linear progression; "the app tells users exactly what to do, how much weight to use, and when."
Reviewers: it "takes all the ego out," it "works extremely well," users log 300+ workouts. The
mechanism that makes it work is simply effective lifts + progressive overload, nothing more.
**Status: VERIFIED.** Sources: https://stronglifts.com/reviews/ , https://noobgains.com/stronglifts-review/ ,
https://outlift.com/stronglifts-5x5-for-building-muscle/

**F8.2 — Boostcamp: pick one proven program, just show up.** "Pick a proven program, show up, and
follow it with no spreadsheets or guessing"; includes "5/3/1 for Beginners," "GZCLP," "TSA Beginner."
1M+ lifters. Simplicity = curated proven programs, zero config. **Status: VERIFIED.** Source:
https://apps.apple.com/us/app/boostcamp-gym-workout-fitness/id1529354455

**F8.3 — Couch to 5K vs Zombies 5K: progression smoothness is the result-delivery lever.** C25K's
jump from 8 min continuous to 20 min the next week "discouraged many"; Zombies 5K's smoother ramp
meant beginners "didn't feel like they couldn't do it." Same goal, the gentler curve retains.
**Status: VERIFIED.** Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couch_to_5K ,
https://breakingmuscle.com/tech-review-zombies-run-5k-training-fitness-game/

> INTERPRETATION (separate): minimum viable beginner coaching = one curated proven progression +
> tell-me-the-next-set + a gentle, never-discouraging difficulty curve. Inference from F8.1–F8.3.
- ATHLETE: a fixed linear 5x5 stalls fast for trained lifters — they need autoregulation/periodisation,
  i.e. the opposite of "one fixed program." Volyume's coaching engine must scale up from this floor.

### Q9. Beginner-vs-athlete retention differences

**F9.1 — First-2-weeks habit is the dominant churn signal.** "Users who complete fewer than three
workouts in their first 14 days churn at 3–4x the rate" of those who establish a weekly habit; "the
most predictive churn signal is session frequency in the first two weeks"; "fitness apps succeed or
fail in the first 72 hours … most of that decided in the first workout." **Status: VERIFIED.**
Sources: https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/fitness-apps ,
https://dev.to/paywallpro/fitness-app-onboarding-guide-data-motivation-completion-an0

**F9.2 — "Resolutioner" cohorts churn far worse than self-motivated ones.** January New-Year cohorts
"drop 50–70% by March"; summer-acquired users "retain at 1.5–2x the rate." A proxy for
motivation/experience differences in incoming users. **Status: VERIFIED.** Source:
https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/fitness-apps , https://digitalyieldgroup.com/blog/health-fitness-apps-the-resolutioner-churn-problem/

**F9.3 — Benchmark retention numbers (category).** "Good" fitness retention ~20% Day-1, 7–8.5%
Day-7, 3.5–4% Day-30; fitness ~31% over 90 days; typical mobile loses 70–80% in 30 days. **Status:
PARTIAL** — numbers from review/snippet aggregators, not a primary research dataset; treat as
indicative. Sources: https://www.snoopr.co/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks-2026-what-good-looks-like-for-fitness-ecommerce-gaming-and-more , https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/fitness-apps

**F9.4 — Direct beginner-vs-experienced retention comparison: NOT FOUND.** I could not find a study
that directly measures retention of *novice* vs *experienced/athlete* users as labelled cohorts.
The "resolutioner vs summer" and "habit-formed vs not" splits are the closest proxies. **Stated as a
gap; not inferred into a fake number.**
- NEWBIE: retention is won or lost in the first 3 sessions / 14 days — onboarding must drive to a
  completed first workout fast.
- ATHLETE: experienced users self-motivate and tolerate complexity; their churn is driven more by
  plan staleness/plateau than by onboarding friction (INTERPRETATION, flagged).

## 5. PROPOSAL INPUT (sourced only — for the blueprint session, not decisions)

1. **Default to "tell me what to do next," not a menu.** Beginners' #1 unmet need is a clear plan
   (F1.1, F1.3, F8.1). Volyume's existing training builder + Plan Library (FREE tier) should expose a
   guided "I'm new — pick one plan for me" path, not the full builder, on first run.
2. **Attach a one-line "why" to each prescribed exercise/weight** (F1.2, F4.1–F4.2) — plain English,
   coach voice, dismissible. NEWBIE-on by default, ATHLETE-off/optional.
3. **Inline tap-to-define glossary for RPE/RIR/AMRAP/macros/progressive overload** the first time each
   appears (F6.1). Optional for advanced users. (Do NOT cite the unverified "68%" figure.)
4. **Gentle default intensity + smooth progression curve** for unknown new users (F2.2, F8.3) — the
   curve is itself a retention lever. This must stay inside the existing deterministic coaching engine
   and the ED-safety floors in `src/coaching/safety/`; NOT a billing or AI change.
5. **Every onboarding question must visibly change the plan** (F2.5). No decorative quiz; no paywall
   before a felt first-workout value.
6. **Drive to a completed first workout inside the first session / 14 days** (F9.1) — the single
   highest-leverage retention move for newbies.
7. **Judgment-free, private-by-default framing** (F3.1, F3.2, F7.2). Avoid narrow body-ideal imagery
   as the default first impression; make aesthetic/physique framing contextual (athletes opt in).
8. **Keep the casual loop fully inside FREE** (F5.1–F5.3) — logging, PBs, progress stats — matching
