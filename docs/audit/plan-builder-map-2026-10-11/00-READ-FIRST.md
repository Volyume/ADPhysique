## How a plan is made today, in brief (details and line references in 01)

1. **Which code runs.** With the switch on and no style pool, the new planner builds the plan. With a style pool, the older generator still runs (01 section 1, 02 section 6).
2. **Inputs.** Days a week (2 to 6, a beginner at most 4), session length, equipment, goal or category, experience, phase, recovery rating and up to three focus muscles, plus the person's logged history and learned recovery when present (01 section 2).
3. **Exercises.** For each muscle, an ordered list of exercises the person's equipment allows, preferring exercises they have logged. Each compound credits half a set to its helper muscles. Front delts have exactly one exercise in every equipment setting (01 section 3).
4. **Roles and floors.** Each muscle is a focus muscle, a standard muscle or a maintenance muscle, with a weekly peak, a growth floor and, since 10 Oct, a per-category direct floor (01 section 4).
5. **Week structure.** Physique categories have exactly one structure per day count: a hand-written session list. General and bodybuilding choose among several general structures (01 section 5).
6. **Placing the sets.** Sixteen priorities apply in a fixed order (01 section 9). In short: the day count and the category's list come first, then caps per exercise and per session, then every trained muscle's first exercise, then the floors. The floors are placed past the session length and the 8-exercise and 25-set ceilings. After that come the structure search, session length and ceilings, growth above the floors, session order, the light and heavy split, and the recovery promise. The recovery promise is fixed by structure, then by volume, but only if no floor is lost; otherwise it is reported and the plan is saved anyway.
7. **The block.** Six weeks: week 1 starts well below the peak, the climb reaches the peak in week 5, and week 6 is the recovery week. A week server splits each week's sets across sessions and exercises (01 section 7).
8. **After the build.** Seventeen paths can change a plan. None of them checks which sessions sit next to each other, and few check recovery (02 section 7).

## Every observed problem, grouped (sources in brackets)

**Structure and recovery**
- Physique categories have one hand-written session list per day count, so the planner cannot choose a different structure when recovery fails (01 section 9 item 2).
- Men's physique 4 days now has three upper sessions that each list the same six upper muscles. Shoulders + Arms nearly copies Upper A (03 sample A; 05).
- On 9 Oct, men's physique 4 days had two identical back-and-delts sessions (05, founder's plan by version).
- The recovery check is not a gate: a failing plan is saved (01 section 9 item 11; `planner.js` chooseFamily).
- The light and heavy split is not recomputed when the order changes (01 P3).
- The rotation's clash term outweighs recovery about a thousand to one (01 P6).
- In the worked trace the recovery promise fails for four muscles and no fix is possible (01 P5).

**Floors outrank limits (since 10 Oct)**
- Session length does not limit any set placed by a floor (01 P1).
- The 8-exercise and 25-set ceilings are exceeded (01 P2; 02 P5; 04).
- Credits push muscles above their peak, so the "lower the peak" fix does nothing (01 P4).

**Exercise choice**
- Front delts have one exercise in every equipment setting, so the "thin equipment" bonus raises its cap to 6 sets (01 P7; lead check). A 6-set overhead press appears in samples (05).
- The same exercise appears in back-to-back sessions in 74% of plans (04).

**After the build**
- A planner plan loses its planner targets at the next block and whenever it is set active again (02 P1).
- Template rows for muscles the planner does not target stay in the block and leak into screens and swaps (01 P11; 02 P2).
- A strong check-in adds far less than its card says, and the card counts the plan's own climb (02 P3).
- The check-in opens exercises on manual plans but never on planner plans (02 P4).
- Different screens serve different weeks when the programme position lags the calendar (02 P10, traced from code, not run).
- The rebuild at the next session discards applied check-ins (02 P11).
- "Update your plan" with the block kept overwrites protected rows (02 P12).
- A permanent swap into another muscle is not held to the destination's limits (02 P13).
- A permanent swap keeps shortened rest as if the person set it (02 P14).
- The reintroduction ramp treats week 1 as the current week (02 P15).
- Plans without v2 facts are served by an uncapped multiplier (02 P17).
- Check-in later-week writes are outside the transaction (02 P18).

**Older generator**
- It writes exercises above the per-exercise cap, which the multiplier then raises further (02 P6).
- Its men's physique 4-day plan delivers no abs and drops listed muscles (02 P7).
- Its exercise ranking adds the raw pool index, contrary to its comment (02 P8).
- Screens describe the older generator's plan while the saved plan is the new planner's (02 P9).

**Inputs and data**
- The aggressive-cut peak reduction cannot be reached from a profile (01 P8).
- The ramp is skipped for a person with no recent training (01 P9).
- The strength-phase session-length estimate uses the wrong rest (01 P10).
- Focus picks are cut to three before duplicates are merged (01 P13).
- The plan's day count can differ from the profile's (01 P14).
- The style-pool test differs between save and generation (01 P15).
- The demonstrated-structure read is computed but not used (01 P16).
- Comments disagree with the code (01 P12; 02 P16).

**Process**
- The planner's tests use a fixture exercise list with no front-delt exercises, so they never exercised an overhead press. The lead's verification on 10 Oct used the same fixture.
- No test checks structure: back-to-back muscles, repeated exercises or near-copy sessions. The tests check sets, caps and floors.
- On 10 Oct the lead saw the recovery check fail for men's physique at 4 days and shipped the plan anyway.
