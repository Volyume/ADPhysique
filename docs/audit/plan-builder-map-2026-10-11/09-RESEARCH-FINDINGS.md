# Volyume Structure Engine: Research Findings and Rulebook Specification

The builder's faults are structural, not a matter of tuning. The single highest-value fix is one hard rule: **no muscle may be trained directly (or heavily indirectly) in two sessions that sit next to each other in the rotation, including last-to-first.** Five exceptions are allowed, and the rule is applied through one fixed template per category and day count, not through a permutation search. The best meta-regression to date finds that training frequency has a negligible effect on hypertrophy once weekly volume is equated. That means this strict rule costs almost no growth while it removes the back-to-back overload, front-delt pressing clusters and 120-plus-minute sessions seen in the map.

## TL;DR
- **Replace the allocation and structure core; do not repair the 10 Oct planner or revert to the older generator.** Use one written template per category × day count, built so that no muscle repeats in adjacent rotation slots. Volume is counted as "fractional" sets (indirect = 0.5), capped at about 11 fractional sets per muscle per session, with session size derived from the chosen length. A build that fails any hard check is never saved.
- **The evidence supports the founder's constraints.** In Pelland et al. (Sports Medicine 2026; 67 studies, 2,058 participants), the posterior probability for frequency's effect on hypertrophy "was less than 100%, indicating compatibility with negligible effects" at equal volume. Men's physique legs once a week therefore stands, backed by the IFBB/NPC criteria and Ali Bilal's own published split. Overhead pressing is unnecessary for front-delt growth: in one MRI-measured randomised controlled trial (RCT), bench press "promoted a greater hypertrophic response of the pectoralis major compared to the triceps brachii and pectoralis minor, but not different from the anterior deltoid" (Lanza et al., J Bodyw Mov Ther 2024). Shoulder impingement signs track lateral raises and upright rows above 90°, not bench or behind-the-neck pressing (Kolber et al. 2014).
- **Much of the numeric detail is coach-consensus grade (D).** Per-muscle recovery hours, joint limits, deload design and category tiers are under-researched. The rulebook below marks every value's grade so it can be tuned later without changing the engine.

# PART A — THE REPORT

## Evidence base used (and what was not found)

**Verified sources:**
- **Pelland et al., Sports Medicine 2026;56:481–505 (DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w).** 67 studies, 2,058 participants. "Fractional" counting (indirect = 0.5) fitted best. Volume shows diminishing returns. Frequency is "compatible with negligible effects" on hypertrophy. Efficiency tiers: 5–10 fractional sets/week higher; 11–18 intermediate; 19–29 lower; 30–42 lowest.
- **Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos, "Is There Too Much of a Good Thing?" (SportRxiv 2025 preprint, doi 10.51224/SRXIV.537, not peer reviewed).** The point of undetectable outcome superiority occurs "at ~2 'direct' sets for strength and ~11 'fractional' sets for hypertrophy" per muscle per session.
- **Schoenfeld 2016, 2017 and 2019.** Graded volume dose–response. Frequency "does not significantly or meaningfully impact muscle hypertrophy when volume is equated."
- **Robinson et al. 2024 (Sports Med 54:2209–31); Refalo et al. 2024.** Closer to failure helps hypertrophy, but RIR training matched failure training for quadriceps growth.
- **Morán-Navarro et al. 2017 (Eur J Appl Physiol 117:2387–99).** Training to failure "slows down recovery up to 24–48 h."
- **Singer et al. 2024 (Front Sports Act Living).** "A small hypertrophic benefit to employing inter-set rest interval durations >60 s"; effects beyond 90 s unclear.
- **Miller et al. 2005 (J Physiol 567:1021–33).** Tendon collagen synthesis peaked at 24 h and stayed elevated at 72 h.
- **Kassiano et al. 2022 (JSCR 36:1753–62).** "Excessive rotation… may actually hinder muscular adaptations."
- **Deloading.** Coleman et al. 2024 (PeerJ): a one-week stop at mid-block did not affect hypertrophy but reduced lower-body strength gains. Bell et al. 2023 (Delphi consensus). Rogerson et al. 2024 (n = 246): deloads lasted 6.4 ± 1.7 days, every 5.6 ± 2.3 weeks; volume was cut and frequency kept.
- **Siewe et al. 2014 (Int J Sports Med 35:943–8, PMID 24886919).** 71 competitive bodybuilders from German clubs.
  - Injury rate "0.12 injuries per bodybuilder per year (0.24 injuries per 1 000 h of bodybuilding)"; 45.1% had pain while training.
  - Career disorders: lumbar 39.4%, shoulder 36.6%, elbow 33.8%, knee 31%. "Athletes over 40 exhibited higher rates of injury (p=0.029)."
  - Off-season training 3.5 days/week at 87.5 min.
- **Kolber et al. 2014 (JSCR 28:1081–9).** 46 weight trainers vs 31 controls.
  - Shoulder pain: 74% in the past year. Impingement cluster positive: 20% vs 4.8% of shoulders.
  - Associated with impingement signs: lateral raises above 90° (Φ = 0.32) and upright rows above 90° (Φ = 0.30). External-rotator work was protective (Φ = −0.38).
  - Not associated: flat bench, incline bench, dips and behind-the-neck press.
  - Kolber 2010: "up to 36%" of weight-training injuries occur at the shoulder.
- **Lanza et al. 2024 (J Bodyw Mov Ther 40:1417–22).** 10-week bench-only RCT with MRI. Anterior deltoid growth was "not different from" the pectoralis major; the medial deltoid grew least.
- **Campos et al. 2020 (J Hum Kinet 75:5–14).** EMG. Medial deltoid: lateral raise 30.3% of maximum voluntary contraction (MVIC), bench 5%. Posterior deltoid: lateral raise 24%, bench 3.5%. Anterior deltoid: shoulder press 33.3%, the highest.
- **da Silveira et al. 2025 (Front Sports Act Living).** Survey, n = 154: "split routines (85.5%), train 4–7 times per week, target major muscle groups twice weekly, and sessions last 60–90 min."
- **Spanish natural elite bodybuilders, pre-contest (Sports 2026;14(1):20).** "Weekly direct sets per muscle group averaged ~8–17." Back was highest (amateur 15.3, professional 17.0) and hamstrings lowest (8.56, 8.10).

**Federation rules read:** NPC Men's Physique page; IFBB Men's Physique 2017 PDF; NPC Worldwide rules (ifbbpro.com, updated 15 Sep 2026); NPC bikini page; 2Bros Events (UK); Wellness Q&A via Muscle Insider.

**NOT FOUND:**
- UKBFF, PCA, BNBF, NABBA UK and WNBF UK rulebooks.
- da Silveira per-category medians: the tables were not retrieved, so they are not used.
- Primary programmes from coaches other than Ali Bilal.
- Iversen (supersets), Nunes (exercise order), McGill (spinal loading), Pareja-Blanco 2020.
- Bench-only vs bench-plus-OHP comparisons for front-delt growth.

### 1.2 Off-season programming
- **Survey evidence (C).** Split routines, 4–7 sessions a week, major muscles twice a week, 60–90 min sessions (da Silveira 2025). This is self-report, and the female categories have very small samples.
- **Men's physique (D).** Ali Bilal's own site gives: Shoulders / Back / Chest + Arms / Shoulders / Back / Legs ("maintenance volume"), and says "Shoulders twice per week is not optional," with legs "Low" priority because "excessive leg mass can hurt the V-taper." Other coaches' splits are reported second-hand as legs once (Hendrickson/Buendia) or legs twice (Grant, Terry); neither could be verified. The founder's legs-once rule is **not contradicted**: frequency has a negligible effect at equal volume (A).
- **Other categories (D).** No primary coach programmes were verified. The structures follow the federation text and the recovery rule.

### 1.3 Rotation consequence
A rotation is a cycle, so the last session is adjacent to the first. Under the strict rule:
- **2–3 days:** every session is adjacent to every other, so each muscle trains once (2d = Upper/Lower; 3d = split routines).
- **4–5 days:** at most two non-adjacent slots per muscle.
- **6 days:** two slots per muscle in the templates.

Sessions may be cyclically rotated (start anywhere) but never permuted, which removes the permutation search.

## 2. Muscle model
Track 18 keys plus 2 opt-in:
- **Upper body:** chest, lats, upper_back, front_delts, side_delts, rear_delts, biceps, triceps, forearms, traps.
- **Lower body and trunk:** quads, hamstrings, glute_max, glute_med, adductors, calves, abs.
- **Opt-in:** neck, tibialis.

Reasons for the splits:
- **Delts (B/C).** Front, side and rear are separate because the bench barely activates side and rear (Campos 2020), and bench training grows front but not side (Lanza 2024).
- **Back (D).** lats (vertical pull) vs upper_back (rows), so width and thickness can be emphasised separately.
- **Glutes (D).** glute_max (extension) vs glute_med (abduction), so glute categories can train both on adjacent days without loading the same function.
- **Indirect-only by default (D):** front_delts, traps, forearms, and adductors except in wellness.

## 3. Volume
- **Counting rule (A).** Count weekly fractional sets: direct + 0.5 × indirect (Pelland 2026). Credit assignments are judgement (D; table B2).
- **Tiers** for an intermediate are below. Each category's tier per muscle is in table B3.

| Tier | Min, 4–6 days | Min, 3 days | Min, 2 days | Typical | Max | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P (priority) | 10 | 8 | 6 | 14 | 20 | B (Pelland tiers) / C (surveys) |
| S (standard) | 6 | 5 | 4 | 10 | 14 | B/C |
| M (maintenance) | 4 | 3 | 2 | 6 | 8 | C/D |
| I (indirect only) | 0 | 0 | 0 | — | 4 direct | D |

- **Per-session ceiling (C).** 11 fractional / 8 direct sets per muscle, from the Remmert preprint. This is why the minimums are lower at 2–3 days, where every muscle trains once.
- **Focus (D):** +4 fractional sets/week, capped at 20. Placed only in sessions that already list the muscle, or in a non-adjacent session within the caps; never taken from another muscle.
- **Experience (D):** beginner × 0.7 of typical; advanced × 1.15, capped at max.

## 4. Recovery
**Evidence.**
- Training to failure delays recovery by 24–48 h (Morán-Navarro 2017, B).
- Tendon collagen synthesis stays elevated to 72 h (Miller 2005, C).
- No validated per-muscle recovery hours exist: NOT FOUND. The map's base hours are unsourced and dropped.

**Dose classes.**
- *Light:* ≤4 direct sets, isolation only, every set RIR ≥2. Needs 24 h.
- *Moderate:* ≤8 direct and ≤11 fractional, compounds RIR ≥1. Needs 48 h.
- *Heavy:* anything above moderate, or any compound set at RIR 0. Needs 72 h for the large muscles, 48 h for the others.

**Rules.**
- **The engine never prescribes heavy.** Non-adjacency then guarantees at least 48 h, if at most one session is trained per calendar day. The app warns if a session starts under 20 h after the previous one (D).
- **Indirect load counts (D).** A muscle counts as "trained" in a session at ≥2 direct or ≥4 fractional indirect sets.
- **Next-day exceptions (D).** side_delts, rear_delts, calves, abs, forearms, neck and tibialis may appear in adjacent sessions only if the earlier dose is light. This is coach consensus with no direct evidence.

**When the standard does not fit** (nothing is removed silently):
1. Build only the minimums first; typical, focus and growth sets are added only while capacity remains.
2. Trim rest to the floor and show the real length.
3. Consolidate into fewer exercises with more sets each, within the caps.
4. Optional antagonist pairing, off by default (D).
5. If the minimums still exceed length + 5 min at floor rest, do not save. Show the minimum minutes needed at this day count and the minimum days needed at this length.

## 6. Joint protection
- **Shoulder (36.6% of bodybuilders report disorders, Siewe).**
  - No mandatory direct front-delt work (Lanza 2024, B).
  - At most one OHP session per week; none by default in men's physique (D).
  - No adjacent sessions that both carry ≥4 fractional front-delt sets (D).
  - Lateral raises to ≤90°, no high-elbow upright rows (C, Kolber 2014).
  - Weekly ≥3 sets of face pull or external-rotation work (C).
- **Lumbar (39.4%).** Spinal-axial heavy exercises never in adjacent sessions; ≤2 per session; ≤2 heavy-hinge sessions a week (D).
- **Elbow (33.8%).** ≤10 elbow-isolation sets per session; ≤4 skull-crusher or overhead-extension sets (D).
- **Knee (31%).** Knee-dominant compounds never in adjacent sessions (C/D).

## 7. Exercise selection
- **Taxonomy:** table B2. "Same exercise" means the same catalogue ID; "same pattern" means the same pattern key.
- **Adjacent sessions:** the same exercise is never allowed in adjacent sessions.
- **Variety within a block.** A muscle trained twice a week uses a different role on each visit (keep the older generator's rotation and fix its ranking bug). Exercises are held fixed for the block (Kassiano 2022, C).
- **Order (D):** heavy compound, then moderate compound, then machine, then isolation. Within a tier, P before S before M; focus first only within its tier.
- **Equipment.** The +5-set allowance applies only when a muscle has exactly one pattern in that kit, never in a full gym. If a P or S muscle has zero patterns in the kit, report it as untrainable. The catalogue needs a plate lateral raise for barbell-and-plates kits.

## 8. Progression
- **Block:** 6 weeks, five loading weeks plus a deload (C, Rogerson 2024).
- **Week 1:** max(2, round(0.7 × peak)) sets per exercise.
- **Climb:** ≤+1 set per exercise per week; peak in week 5.
- **RIR ladder:** 3, 2, 2, 1, 1, then 4 in the deload. Compounds never reach RIR 0 (B).
- **Deload:** 50% of peak sets, same exercises and frequency (C).
- **Check-ins:**
  - *Raise:* +1 set per exercise on ≤2 muscles, only if all hard checks still pass.
  - *Hold:* no change.
  - *Reduce:* −20% on the named muscles, not below minimum unless pain is flagged.

## 9. Conflicts
Order and type are in B8. The hard items (recovery, joints, hard limits, category minimum, honest length display) are never traded. If they cannot all be met, the build fails with the computed minimum days or minutes, and the user chooses.
