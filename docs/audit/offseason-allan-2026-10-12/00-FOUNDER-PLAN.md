# Al: 4-Day Men's Physique Plan (Health → Growth), Machines and Cables Only

**The plan:** four upper-biased sessions a week. Lats are trained in three sessions, side delts in all four, and legs get one complete session. The exercises stay the same throughout; only sets and RIR change between the Health and Growth phases. Before the Finals on 18 October, nothing changes. After the show, run Week 0 (transition), then Health, then a three-week ramp, then Growth.

## TL;DR
- **Split:** Mon Back-width A | Tue Upper chest/arms | Thu Legs + delts + lats | Sat Back-width B/arms. Rest Wed, Fri and Sun. Side delts are trained in all four sessions (12 direct sets a week in Health, 20 in Growth). Lats are trained in three (12 in Health, 17 in Growth). Legs get one session of 10 sets in Health and 14 in Growth.
- **Evidence:** more weekly sets give more growth, with diminishing returns. Frequency adds little once volume is matched. Training closer to failure gives modestly more growth, but failure isn't required. Machines build muscle as well as free weights. The real reasons to spread lat and delt volume across sessions are to keep each session's dose out of the diminishing-returns zone and to keep set quality high, not that frequency itself grows muscle.
- **Shoulders:** no flat pressing, dips, upright rows, behind-the-neck work, deep fly stretches or heavy overhead pressing. Laterals are done in the scapular plane, up to roughly shoulder height. Pain stays at or below 3/10 and must settle within 24 h. Persistent symptoms mean a physio assessment.

| Day | Session | Purpose |

| Tue | **Day 2 – Upper Chest + Side Delts + Triceps + Abs** | Only chest day; no lat or back overlap with Monday |
| Wed | Rest | |
| Thu | **Day 3 – Legs (quads, hams, calves) + Side Delts + Lat isolation** | The only leg day; low-fatigue upper work bolted on |
| Fri | Rest | |
| Sat | **Day 4 – Back Width B + Upper Back + Rear Delts + Side Delts + Arms** | Second heavy lat day, 5 days after Day 1 |
| Sun | Rest | |

**Spacing logic:**
- Lats are hit Mon, Thu (low-fatigue isolation only) and Sat, so there are at least 48 h between lat exposures.
- Side delts are hit Mon, Tue, Thu and Sat. Lateral raises are low-fatigue, which is why back-to-back Mon/Tue is acceptable.
- No muscle is hit hard on consecutive days except the side delts.
- If Saturday doesn't work, Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri is acceptable. Expect Friday's lat performance to be slightly worse after Thursday's straight-arm work. If that happens, move the straight-arm pulldown to Day 2.

**Phase key used in all tables:**
- **H** = Health phase, **G** = Growth phase.
- "Sets H / G" means working sets, excluding warm-ups.
- RIR = reps in reserve.
- Warm-ups: 1–3 ramp sets on the first exercise for each muscle, then 1 feeder set on subsequent exercises if needed.

**Global intensity rules:**
- Compounds (pulldowns, rows, presses, leg press or hack squat): RIR 2–3 in H, 1–2 in G.
- Isolation (laterals, flies, curls, extensions, leg curls, calves): RIR 1–2 in H, 0–1 in G. Only the final set of a machine or cable isolation exercise may go to true failure, and only in G.
- No forced reps, negatives or past-failure techniques in H. In G, lengthened partials are optional on the final set of calves, laterals and leg curls only (see §6).

## 2. Sessions in full

### Day 1 (Mon) – Back Width A + Side Delts + Rear Delts + Biceps + Abs
H: 16 sets, ~55 min | G: 24 sets, ~75 min

| # | Exercise & setup | Sets H / G | Reps | RIR H / G | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Machine lat pulldown** (plate-loaded or selectorised; pronated grip ~1.5× shoulder width or wide handles). Thigh pad snug, chest tall, slight lean back. | 3 / 4 | 6–10 | 2–3 / 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 2 | **Single-arm cable pulldown** (high pulley, D-handle, kneeling or seated side-on) | 2 / 3 per arm | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 90 s |
| 3 | **Chest-supported machine row**, neutral grip, elbows tucked ~30° from torso (lat-biased) | 2 / 3 | 8–12 | 2 / 1 | 2 min |
| 4 | **Single-arm cable lateral raise** (cable at hand height, handle crossing in front of or behind the body, whichever is pain-free) | 3 / 5 | 12–20 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 5 | **Reverse pec deck** (rear delts), neutral or pronated handles | 2 / 3 | 12–20 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 6 | **Machine preacher curl** | 2 / 3 | 8–12 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 90 s |
| 7 | **Kneeling cable crunch** (rope) | 2 / 3 | 10–15 | 2 / 1 | 60 s |

**Cues:**
1. Pull the handles to the upper chest, never behind the head. Think "elbows to back pockets". Let the arms rise fully at the top only as far as the shoulder feels comfortable, then start each rep by depressing the shoulder blade.
2. Let the working arm reach up and slightly across the body for a stretch, within a pain-free range. Drive the elbow down to the hip. Keep the torso still.
3. Keep the chest on the pad. Pull the elbows towards the hips rather than straight back, and pause for 1 s at the end.
4. Raise in the scapular plane (see §5) to about shoulder height. Lead with the elbow and keep the thumb neutral or slightly up, not "pouring the jug". Take 2–3 s on the lowering phase.
5. Set the handles so the start position is in front of the chest. Sweep wide and stop when the arms are in line with the torso. Don't squeeze the shoulder blades hard together; this is rear delts, not traps.
6. Use the full stretch at the bottom, but stop short of the elbow fully locking under load if the elbow complains.
7. Flex through the spine (ribs to pelvis). Hips stay still.

**Substitutions:**
- (1) Cable lat pulldown with a wide bar or a lat-pulldown attachment with neutral wide handles.
- (2) Single-arm iso-lateral pulldown machine (e.g. Hammer Strength style).
- (3) Seated cable row with a close neutral handle, or a T-bar machine row with a chest pad.
- (4) Machine lateral raise.
- (5) Cable reverse fly from pulleys set at shoulder height.
- (6) Cable curl facing the stack on a preacher bench, or a biceps curl machine.
- (7) Ab crunch machine.

### Day 2 (Tue) – Upper Chest + Side Delts + Triceps + Abs
H: 16 sets, ~55 min | G: 23 sets, ~75 min

| # | Exercise & setup | Sets H / G | Reps | RIR H / G | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Incline machine press** (plate-loaded or selectorised; back pad ~30° if adjustable; neutral or semi-neutral handles if available) | 3 / 4 | 6–10 | 2–3 / 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 2 | **Converging chest press machine**, seat set low so the handles line up with the lower edge of the upper chest | 2 / 3 | 8–12 | 2 / 1 | 2 min |
| 3 | **Low-to-high cable fly** (pulleys at the lowest setting; standing staggered stance) | 2 / 3 | 12–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 90 s |
| 4 | **Machine lateral raise** (pad on the outer forearm or upper arm) | 3 / 5 | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 5 | **Cable overhead triceps extension** (rope, facing away, pulley at chest height) | 2 / 3 | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 90 s |
| 6 | **Cable triceps pushdown** (rope or straight bar) | 2 / 2 | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 7 | **Ab crunch machine** | 2 / 3 | 10–15 | 2 / 1 | 60 s |

**Cues:**
1. Shoulder blades set down and back before unracking. Elbows sit ~45–60° from the torso, not flared to 90°. Lower until the handles are about level with the chest or the elbows are just behind the torso line, whichever is pain-free. **Never chase a deep stretch.** Press up and slightly in.
2. Let the machine's converging arc bring the hands together. Same depth rule as exercise 1. Squeeze for 1 s with the hands together.
3. Start with the hands roughly in line with the hips and slightly behind the torso, never behind it with the shoulder hyperextended. Sweep up and in to chin height. Keep a slight bend at the elbow.
4. Align the shoulder joint with the machine's pivot. If the machine forces the arm into a pure side plane and that hurts, take the cable option instead. Stop at about shoulder height.
5. Shoulder-sensitive modification: lean the torso forward so the upper arms sit at roughly 90–110° of shoulder flexion rather than fully overhead. Stretch the triceps, not the shoulder capsule.
6. Pin the elbows to the sides and lock out fully.
7. Exhale and curl the ribs down.

**Substitutions:**
- (1) Incline Smith machine press at ~30° with a slightly narrower-than-bench grip. This is a fixed bar path but not a free weight; use it only if pain-free.
- (2) A second incline machine (e.g. iso-lateral incline) or a seated cable press.
- (3) Incline-bench cable fly with pulleys low.
- (4) Single-arm cable lateral raise.
- (5) Single-arm cable overhead extension, or cross-body cable extension.
- (6) Dip machine set to stop at a shallow range (no deep shoulder extension), or a triceps extension machine.
- (7) Kneeling cable crunch.

### Day 3 (Thu) – Legs + Side Delts + Lat Isolation
H: 15 sets, ~55 min | G: 22 sets, ~75 min

| # | Exercise & setup | Sets H / G | Reps | RIR H / G | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seated leg curl** (lean the torso forward slightly; thigh pad tight) | 3 / 4 | 8–12 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 90 s–2 min |
| 2 | **Hack squat or 45° leg press** (feet mid-platform, shoulder width) | 2 / 3 | 8–12 | 2–3 / 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 3 | **Leg extension** (seat set so the knee lines up with the pivot; slight backward lean or reclined back pad if adjustable) | 2 / 3 | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 90 s |
| 4 | **Standing calf raise machine** (or leg press calf raise) | 3 / 4 | 8–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 5 | **Single-arm cable lateral raise** | 3 / 5 | 12–20 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 6 | **Straight-arm cable pulldown** (rope or straight bar, pulley high, slight hip hinge) | 2 / 3 | 12–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |

**Cues:**
1. Hips back and torso leaning forward to put more stretch on the hamstrings. Control the return to near-full knee extension. Pause briefly at full flexion.
2. Go as deep as the hips and knees allow without the pelvis tucking under. Push through the whole foot and stop just short of locking out.
3. Lift and hold at the top for 1 s, and control the lowering. A reclined back position puts the rectus femoris under more stretch (biomechanical reasoning, not tested).
4. Pause for 1–2 s in the deepest stretch on every rep. Go all the way up to the toes.
5. Same rules as Day 1.
6. Start with the arms only as high as the shoulder tolerates; skip the extreme overhead stretch. Sweep the hands to the thighs with the elbows nearly straight. Feel it in the lats, not the triceps.

**Substitutions:**
- (1) Lying (prone) leg curl. This is a weaker choice for growth (see §6), so use it only if no seated curl is available.
- (2) Pendulum squat or belt squat machine.
- (3) Single-leg leg extension.
- (4) Seated calf raise, though this places less load on the gastrocnemius; standing or leg-press versions are preferred.
- (5) Machine lateral raise.
- (6) Cable pullover from a bench, or a machine pullover if pain-free (see §5).

**Why this is enough leg work for MP:** see §3 and §6.

### Day 4 (Sat) – Back Width B + Upper Back + Rear Delts + Side Delts + Arms
H: 16 sets, ~55 min | G: 24 sets, ~75 min

| # | Exercise & setup | Sets H / G | Reps | RIR H / G | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Close neutral-grip pulldown** (parallel handles or V-bar, or a neutral-grip pulldown machine) | 3 / 4 | 8–12 | 2–3 / 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 2 | **Iso-lateral high row / unilateral machine pulldown** (one arm at a time) | 2 / 3 per arm | 10–12 | 2 / 1 | 90 s–2 min |
| 3 | **Seated cable row, wide neutral or pronated handle**, elbows ~45–60° (upper back emphasis) | 2 / 3 | 10–12 | 2 / 1 | 2 min |
| 4 | **Machine lateral raise** (or cable, whichever was *not* used on Thursday) | 3 / 5 | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 5 | **Reverse cable fly**, pulleys at shoulder height, cross-over or single-arm, chest supported on an incline bench if possible | 2 / 3 | 12–20 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 6 | **Cable curl** (straight bar or EZ bar, facing the stack) | 2 / 3 | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |
| 7 | **Cable overhead or cross-body triceps extension** (pick whichever is pain-free) | 2 / 3 | 10–15 | 1–2 / 0–1 | 60–90 s |

**Cues:**
1. Lean back 10–20°. Drive the elbows down and back to the ribs. Pause at the bottom, then control the return to a full but pain-free stretch at the top.
2. Brace the free hand on the machine. Pull the elbow down in an arc towards the hip, and let the shoulder blade rise and rotate at the top for the stretch.
3. Pull to the lower chest or upper abdomen with the elbows wider than on Day 1. Squeeze the shoulder blades together, and don't let the torso rock more than ~10°.
4. Scapular plane, about shoulder height, 2–3 s lowering.
5. Sweep with straight-ish arms in the plane of the shoulder blades. Stop when the arms are in line with the torso.
6. Elbows slightly in front of the torso; no shoulder hyperextension.
7. Same shoulder modification as Day 2.

**Substitutions:**
- (1) Neutral-grip machine pulldown or an underhand shoulder-width pulldown.
- (2) Single-arm cable pulldown (as on Day 1) or a single-arm seated cable row with a high pulley.
- (3) Wide-grip chest-supported machine row.
- (4) Single-arm cable lateral raise.
- (5) Rear delt machine (reverse pec deck).
- (6) Biceps curl machine.
- (7) Triceps extension machine.

## 3. Weekly direct-set table (Health vs Growth)

"Direct" = sets where the muscle is the main target. Indirect work (e.g. rows for lats and biceps, presses for triceps and front delts) is noted, and under the "fractional" approach used by Pelland et al. it counts as roughly half a set.

| Muscle | Health direct sets | Growth direct sets | Sessions/week | Max per session (G) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lats** | **12** | **17** | 3 (Mon, Thu, Sat) | 7 | Plus ~2–3 fractional sets from rows; ≈ 14 H / 20 G fractional |
| Upper back / mid-traps | 4 | 6 | 2 | 3 | Plus indirect from pulldowns |
| **Side delts** | **12** | **20** | 4 (every session) | 5 | Highest-priority isolation volume in the plan |
| Rear delts | 4 | 6 | 2 | 3 | Plus indirect from rows |
| Chest (upper-biased) | 7 | 10 | 1 | 10 | Kept under ~11 sets per session |
| Front delts | 0 | 0 | — | — | Heavy indirect load from incline pressing; no direct work needed |
| Biceps | 4 | 6 | 2 | 3 | Plus indirect from all pulls |
| Triceps | 6 | 8 | 2 | 5 | Plus indirect from pressing |
| Abs | 4 | 6 | 2 | 3 | Flexion only; no weighted oblique work (waist) |
| Quads | 4 | 6 | 1 | 6 | |
| Hamstrings | 3 | 4 | 1 | 4 | Seated curl as primary |
| Calves | 3 | 4 | 1 | 4 | Visible on stage; trained with deep stretch |
| **Total working sets** | **63** | **93** | | | |

**Why lats and side delts get extra:**
- The weekly volume dose-response is positive with diminishing returns (Schoenfeld et al. 2017; Pelland et al. 2026). That means the marginal set is worth most where volume is currently lowest relative to need, and for this athlete the judged weak points are back width and the delts that form the V-taper.
- The plan therefore gives lats and side delts the largest weekly allocations (20 and 17 sets in G). Chest gets 10 in a single session, and legs get a minimum-effective single session.
- Splitting lats over 3 sessions and side delts over 4 keeps every session at ≤7 direct sets for those muscles. That is well under the ~11 fractional sets per session that Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos's 2025 SportRxiv preprint identified as the point of undetectable outcome superiority for hypertrophy.
- **Judgement:** the specific numbers (17 and 20) are coaching judgement, not values derived from any study.

**Why one leg day is sufficient for MP:**
- Board shorts cover the quads and most of the hamstrings. The legs need to be conditioned, proportionate and present, with calves visible, not built for size.
- 4–6 quad sets, 3–4 hamstring sets and 3–4 calf sets a week sits inside the 5–9 weekly set band that still produced measurable growth in Schoenfeld et al.'s 2017 meta-analysis. Reaching that band in one session is consistent with Pelland et al.'s finding that frequency has negligible independent effect on hypertrophy once volume is matched.
- Prep Coach UK reports an IFBB/NPC announcement that "judges would now be looking at how much the legs filled out the shorts and overall calf development." Treat that as a reason not to neglect legs, not as a reason to add a second day.
- **Judgement:** whether this dose fully maintains Al's leg size during an enhanced growth phase is coaching judgement. If his legs look flat in the shorts at check-ins, add one set each to quads and hamstrings within the same session.

## 4. Progression, ramp and deload rules

### Progression: double progression with RIR guardrails
1. Every exercise has a rep range (e.g. 8–12). Keep the load fixed until **every working set** reaches the top of the range at or above the target RIR, with clean form and a pain-free range.
2. Then add the smallest available increment (one stack plate, ~2.5–5 kg; or 1.25–2.5 kg via add-on weights or a magnetic plate on isolation work). Restart at the bottom of the range.
3. **Isolation work with big stack jumps** (laterals, reverse flies): if the jump drops reps by more than 4, progress via reps up to 20, then add the load.
4. **Log the machine, seat and pad settings and the cable pulley height** for every exercise. Progress only counts on the same machine and setup. Changing machines resets the log.
5. In the Health phase, don't push loads; hold or match previous bests at RIR 2–3. Load progression is mainly a Growth-phase tool.
6. **Stall rule:** if an exercise fails to improve in load or reps across 3 consecutive sessions while fatigue markers are fine, first check sleep and food. Then swap to the listed substitution for 4–6 weeks. Don't add sets to a stalled exercise.

**Ramp method:** add sets to the existing exercises in the order lats and side delts → rear delts and upper back → chest and arms → legs. Never add a new exercise.

### Deload / volume-reduction triggers
Take a reduction week (half the sets, RIR 3+, same exercises) when **two or more** of these occur:
- Load or reps drop on the same exercise in two consecutive sessions, at the same RIR, with sleep and nutrition unchanged.
- Joint pain during or after a session is ≥ 4/10, or doesn't settle within 24 h, on two occasions in a week.
- Persistent soreness that stops you hitting the next session's rep minimums.
- A sustained rise in resting heart rate or fall in sleep quality, poorer appetite, or no motivation to train, lasting over 5 days.
- Twelve or more weeks since the last reduction week.

**Evidence on deloads:** Coleman et al. 2024 (PeerJ 12:e16777) is the only data I have. In 39 resistance-trained adults (per a DT Fitness London summary), stopping all training in week 5 of a 9-week block "appears to negatively influence measures of lower body muscle strength but has no effect on lower body hypertrophy, power or local muscular endurance." Taking a reduction week therefore seems unlikely to cost growth, but there's no evidence that it boosts growth either. Use deloads to manage fatigue, not by ritual.

## 5. Shoulder rules and substitutions

**Universal rules (clinical and coaching judgement; the specific diagnosis is unknown):**
1. **Pain-monitoring rule:**
   - Pain up to 3/10 during a set is acceptable if it doesn't build across sets and is back to baseline by the next morning.
   - Pain above 3/10, sharp or catching pain, pain that builds through the session, or night pain the following night means modifying the exercise. Shorten the range, change the grip or plane, or use the listed substitution.
2. **Range-of-motion rule:** every exercise's range is "full **pain-free** range". Stretch under load is useful, but never take the shoulder to its painful end range to get it.
3. **Autoregulation rule:** on a bad shoulder day, keep the session but move all pressing and overhead work to RIR 3–4 and cut those sets by a third. Lat, leg and lateral-raise work usually continues as normal if it's pain-free.
4. **Warm-up:** before Days 1, 2 and 4, do 2 light sets of cable external rotation and 1–2 very light sets of the first exercise.
5. **Escalation:** symptoms that persist beyond 2–3 weeks despite these modifications, night pain, weakness, clicking with pain, or numbness or tingling need a **physio or sports-medicine assessment**. Don't train around them indefinitely.

**Movements removed or modified, and why:**

| Movement | Status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Flat pressing (any) | Removed | User's requirement; upper chest is the MP priority anyway |
| Upright rows | Removed | Combines abduction with internal rotation, which reviews (Schoenfeld et al., *Strength Cond J*) describe as risky for subacromial impingement. Lateral raises cover the side delts without it. |
| Behind-the-neck pulldown or press | Removed | Extreme abduction plus external rotation. One EMG study (Padovan et al. 2024) found higher lat excitation for behind-the-neck in the lifting phase but higher for front pulldowns in the lowering phase. That is not enough to justify the shoulder risk. |
| Deep pec deck / fly stretch | Modified | Hands stop roughly in line with the torso. No hanging into the stretch. |
| Dips | Removed | Deep loaded shoulder extension. Triceps are covered with cables. |
| Heavy overhead pressing | Removed by default | Front delts already get a large indirect load from incline pressing. In Growth, a machine shoulder press at RIR 2+ can be trialled for 2 sets *only* if completely pain-free (coaching judgement). |
| Pullovers / straight-arm work | Modified | Start below the painful overhead range |
| Overhead triceps extension | Modified | Lean forward to reduce shoulder flexion, or switch to cross-body |

**Chest exercise choices, justified:**
- **Incline machine press (~30°):**
  - An EMG study (Rodríguez-Ridao et al. 2020) found clavicular pec activity peaked at 30°, with the front delt taking over at 45–60°. That is acute EMG, not hypertrophy data.
  - The fixed path, neutral handles and controlled depth reduce the need for stabilisation and let you cap the range exactly. This is biomechanical reasoning.
- **Converging machine:** the arc brings the hands together, adding horizontal adduction at the end of the press, without a free-weight bottom position. Coaching judgement.
- **Low-to-high cable fly:** constant tension and an adjustable start point, so the stretch can be capped at the torso line. Coaching judgement.

**Lateral raise setup:**
- **Scapular plane:** raise the arms ~30° forward of straight out to the side. Expert and clinical sources (Tino & Hillis 2010; Escamilla et al. 2014) recommend scapular-plane "full-can" elevation to preserve subacromial space. Thigpen et al. 2006 found empty-can (thumb-down) elevation reduced subacromial outlet space more than full-can. **There is no intervention trial showing scapular-plane laterals reduce shoulder pain**, so this rests on mechanism and expert opinion.
- **Height:** stop at about shoulder height (~80–90° abduction).
- **Rotation:** neutral or slight external rotation. Coratella et al. 2020 found internally rotated raises shift activity towards the rear delts, triceps and upper traps.
- **Implement:** pick by comfort. Larsen et al. 2025 found cable and dumbbell laterals produced similar side-delt growth in trained lifters. No peer-reviewed machine-lateral data were found.

## 6. Rationale for key decisions

| Decision | What the evidence says | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly volume as the main driver | Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (*J Sports Sci*; 15 studies): graded dose-response, with 10+ weekly sets the most effective category. Pelland et al. 2026 (*Sports Med* 56:481–505; 67 studies, 2,058 participants): hypertrophy rises with weekly volume with diminishing returns, best modelled by counting indirect sets as half sets. | Meta-regression (mostly young, untrained to trained, natural lifters) |
| Frequency used as a tool, not a goal | Pelland et al. 2026: frequency's effect on hypertrophy is "compatible with negligible effects" once volume is accounted for. A 2026 SportRxiv re-analysis preprint of the Pelland dataset (220 effects, 35 RCTs, 963 participants) found that "in mostly male samples training 1 to 4 sessions per muscle per week, weekly volume was associated with hypertrophy and its division into sessions made no detectable difference." | Meta-regression / preprint |
| Capping per-session dose | Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos 2025 (SportRxiv preprint, not peer-reviewed): the point of undetectable outcome superiority falls "at ~2 'direct' sets for strength and ~11 'fractional' sets for hypertrophy" per muscle per session. The authors note that "there is insufficient data with very high per-session set volumes." | Preprint meta-regression |
| RIR 0–3 | Robinson et al. 2024 (*Sports Med* 54:2209–2231): hypertrophy increases as sets end closer to failure; strength barely changes. Refalo et al. 2023 (*Sports Med* 53:649–665): no evidence that failure beats non-failure, and the relationship may be non-linear. Hence close to failure, with true failure reserved for the last isolation set in Growth. | Meta-analysis / meta-regression |
| Machines and cables only | Haugen et al. 2023 (*BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil*; 13 studies, 1,016 participants): no hypertrophy difference between free-weight and machine training (SMD −0.055). | Meta-analysis |
| Seated over prone leg curl | Maeo et al. 2021 (*Med Sci Sports Exerc*): seated curls grew the whole hamstrings more than prone (+14% vs +9%), driven by the biarticular heads | RCT (untrained, within-subject) |
| Overhead-style triceps work | Maeo et al. 2023 (*Eur J Sport Sci*): overhead cable extensions grew the long head more than neutral-arm pushdowns (+28.5% vs +19.6%) | RCT (untrained, within-subject) |
| Long-length bias / lengthened partials | Wolf et al. 2023 (meta-analysis, *IJSC*): full ROM generally ≥ partials, with a possible small benefit for partials at long muscle lengths. Pedrosa 2022/2023 and Kassiano 2023 found long-length partials beat short or full ROM for quads, distal biceps and calves, **mostly in untrained people**. Wolf et al. 2025 (*PeerJ*, trained lifters): lengthened partials ≈ full ROM. Larsen 2025: cable laterals (more load at long length) ≈ dumbbells. **Mixed:** use full pain-free ROM by default and treat lengthened partials as an optional Growth-phase finisher, not a driver. | Mixed RCTs / meta-analysis |
| Lat pulldown grip choice | Andersen et al. 2014 (*JSCR*): similar lat EMG across grip widths of 1–2× shoulder width. A 2026 narrative review (Di Fonza et al., *J Funct Morphol Kinesiol*): pulling the bar to the front was most consistently linked to higher lat EMG, while grip-width effects were inconsistent. **Grip width is not a back-width lever**; two grips are used for variety and joint comfort. | Acute EMG |
| Straight-arm pulldown over pullover | Muyor et al. 2022 (*Applied Sciences*): straight-arm pulldowns produced higher lat EMG than barbell pullovers, which shift work to the pecs | Acute EMG |
| Unilateral pulldowns and rows | **No peer-reviewed EMG or hypertrophy data found.** Included for range of motion, stretch and left–right balance. | Coaching judgement |
| Lats trained at long lengths | No completed lat-specific hypertrophy trial found. A within-subject MRI trial (NCT07360236) is registered. The long-length rationale is extrapolated from other muscles. | Biomechanical hypothesis |
| Two-to-three back sessions in MP practice | Ryan Terry, listed by Fitness Volt as a four-time Men's Physique Olympia winner (2023–2026), "consistently trained his back twice weekly — Mondays and Fridays" for his 2023 win, using front lat pulldowns, chest-supported rows, cable seated rows and machine pullovers (BarBend). He says he needs a plate-loaded pulldown early in the session to "get a good connection". His list includes rope pullovers and supported reverse cable flies. **He uses free weights and isn't a masters athlete**, so take the structure from him, not the template. | Coaching practice (secondary media reports) |

**Population caveat:** almost all of this research is on young, mostly natural lifters, and Pelland's sample averaged ~25 years. There are no controlled data on enhanced masters MP athletes. Recovery capacity and volume tolerance for Al are matters of individual judgement, which is why the plan ramps up rather than starting at full Growth volume.

## 7. Where Gemini and ChatGPT went wrong (brief)

- **Gemini:** I couldn't access the linked document (the share link wasn't fetchable and no Drive access was available here), so I haven't assessed its specifics.
- **ChatGPT's Upper/Lower with two leg days:** this is the wrong tool for MP.
  - The only advantage of a second leg day is frequency, and the best meta-regression evidence (Pelland et al. 2026) finds frequency has a negligible independent effect on hypertrophy once volume is matched.
  - The plan therefore spent a quarter to half of the training week on muscles hidden by board shorts. It bought nothing that a single well-dosed leg session can't, while capping the upper-body sessions where Al's judged weak points (lats and side delts) need the extra sets and exposures.
  - A "fact-check" that keeps that structure didn't check the one assumption that mattered.

## Caveats
- This plan doesn't promise any specific amount of growth. Individual response varies widely and no study predicts outcomes for an enhanced masters athlete.
- Volume numbers above Schoenfeld's "10+" category are coaching judgement informed by dose-response trends, not prescriptions validated in trials.
- Health-phase decisions about drugs or medical markers are outside the scope of this plan and should be made with a doctor.
