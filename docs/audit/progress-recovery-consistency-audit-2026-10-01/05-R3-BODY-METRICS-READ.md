## 5. What the reference products do that this screen does not (candidates, not rulings)

From my own knowledge of weight-trend apps (MacroFactor, Happy Scale, Libra, Apple Health, RP Diet): (a) a smoothed trend line over the raw weigh-ins drawn as faint dots, so the person sees noise and signal at once and the headline number is the trend, named "trend weight", not a mix of raw and smoothed (the dead COMP-004 card was built this way; its goal band was deferred); (b) a target-rate corridor or goal line, so "is it doing what the plan wants" is a glance, not a sentence; (c) a weekly-change headline ("This week's average 82.3 kg, 0.2 kg below last week") ahead of any chart, which is what a daily weigher opens the app to learn; (d) a "weighed N of 7 mornings" denominator under the headline, which also tells the reader how much to trust the week (it must stay a denominator, never a streak or a nudge, per the programme's own law); (e) a typical-swing band ("your day-to-day swing is usually within 0.4 kg") so a 0.3 kg overnight rise is read as ordinary; (f) a "since start" or "since plan start" total, and a stated rate per week with "estimated" or "so far" in the sentence; (g) a typed-weight plausibility prompt before saving; (h) weekly buckets in the history list (average per week) rather than 12 undated-weekday rows. Each is a candidate for the design lane; none is ruled. (a), (c), (d) and (e) fit the "describe, never instruct" register; (b) needs the plan's rate on the screen, which Q6 declined for the Progress root only.
