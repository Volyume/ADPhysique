## B. Patterns recurring in 3+ products

1. Number plus a short plain-word band, 3 to 5 bands, with colour as a third channel: Whoop 3 colours [WH1]; Oura 4 bands [OU1]; Garmin 5 colours with words and a "short message" [GA1]; Apple Readiness 4 words [AP3]; Apple Training Load 5 words, number hidden [AP2]; Strava 3 range words [ST3]; GitHub 5 named swatches [GH1]; Strava muscle map 4 named levels [ST4]. The words are advisory, not graded ("Pace Yourself", "Time to slow down", "Pay attention").
2. Compared with your own baseline, and both directions flagged: Oura balance contributors, 14-day vs two months, "Higher Activity Balance may indicate that you are overexerting ... Lower ... not challenging your body enough" [OU1, OU2]; Garmin HRV "Unbalanced" = "above or below your baseline range" [GA5, GA6]; Apple 7 vs 28 days [AP5], 90 vs 365 days [AP1], "typical range" [AP6]; Strava range from three-week average [ST3]; Gentler 60-day Normal Range [GS3].
3. Explicit learning state with a stated duration, no verdict before enough data: Whoop [WH2, WH3]; Oura [OU2, OU4]; Garmin 1 to 2 weeks, 7 days, 3 weeks [GA2-GA5]; Apple 180 days / 7 of 49 nights [AP1, AP4]; Strava [ST2]; Gentler [GS3].
4. Score decomposed into named contributors: Oura nine, as plain questions [OU2, OU3]; Garmin six factors [GA1]; Apple Activity / Vitals / Sleep with Typical / Favorable / Slightly off / Notably off [AP3]; Whoop inputs, tap in for HRV/RHR [WH1, WH5].
5. One advice sentence attached to every state, non-judgemental: Garmin [GA2, GA4]; Strava [ST3]; Oura [OU1]; Apple Trends coaching [AP1]; Gentler daily message [GS2].
6. A range band rather than a single target line: Strava white band [ST3]; Garmin optimal range [GA3]; Gentler green path [GS2]; Apple "steady" and typical range [AP2, AP6]; Oura "balance" contributors [OU2].
7. Explicit rest/sick/break override that suspends judgement: Gentler statuses [GS1, GS3]; Apple pause rings up to 90 days without breaking the streak [AP8, AP2]; Oura Rest Mode [OU3].
8. Short window paired with long window: Apple 7/28 [AP5]; Garmin 7-day load and 4-week focus [GA3, GA4]; Oura 14-day/two-month [OU1]; Strava week-so-far/three-week [ST3]; Hevy last 7 days vs calendar week [HV1]; Gentler Today/10/30 days [GS3].
9. Tap or click the visual to reach the cause: Fitbod [FB2]; JeFit [JF1]; Hevy [HV1]; GitHub [GH3]; Strava [ST1, ST2]; Apple Training Load to workouts [AP5]; Oura cards [OU4].
10. Colour never carries meaning alone: Garmin, Whoop, Oura (colour plus "pay attention" message) [GA1, WH1, OU3]; GitHub named swatches, tooltips, and square size under forced-colors [GH1, GH5]; Strava named shading levels [ST4].
Observation: week anchor varies. Apple's weekly review lands "Every Monday" [AP8]; Hevy lets users set the first day of the week (image alt text on [HV4]); GitHub's fetched markup runs Sunday to Saturday (WF) [GH1].

## C. Anti-patterns and documented complaints

- Score contradicts how the person feels: DC Rainmaker on Whoop, "it has the least correlation between my actual feelings and what the score says"; "I still somehow got a 97% recovery score despite feeling like crap" [WH5]. Garmin forum: "I might feel top notch and well rested, like I could run an ultra marathon that day - but the watch tells me I am not ready ... it has actually prevented me a few times to go out for a run" [GA7b].
- Opaque weighting: "the singular difference between a green and yellow score is simply keeping my HRV value above 54, everything else be damned" [WH5]. Strava itself concedes F&F "can be difficult to understand at first" [ST1].
- Hidden number: Apple shows a label only. "without actually knowing if 65% above is good or bad"; Apple seems "concerned that showing an actual value ... might be intimidating or confusing" [AP7].
- Red-state spiral: "my Recovery score dropped to 33, putting me firmly in the red, and naturally I spent the rest of the day spiralling. So, a warning to my healthy-anxiety girls: set some boundaries with your data" [WH7]. "Just like the numbers on a scale, people can get obsessed with numerical sleep metrics" [OU5]. Peer-reviewed (sleep trackers only, not exercise): "a perfectionistic quest for the ideal sleep" [OR]. Counterweight: "There's no clear evidence that large numbers of wearable users are developing serious problems" [TR1].
- Streak guilt: "A long-running criticism about the Fitness app is there's no way to take a day off without ruining a streak" [GS4] (Dec 2022, before Apple's 2024 pause feature). Psychotherapist: "Closing exercise rings ... can turn into a compulsion ... Anxiety peaks at the mere thought of not hitting numbers" [TR1]. Prof. Yang Wei: shift "from daily streaks to weekly consistency", "often more realistic and less guilt-inducing" [AP10]. Hevy's weekly streak: "The streak starts from week one once you log a session" [HV5], though Hevy also says it is "highly motivating to continue training even when you don't feel like it" [HV5].
- Planned rest shown as loss: user thread, "my fitness is decreasing every day according to Strava, and my freshness is increasing. I do feel good"; reply: "Strava f&f shows you losing fitness because your plan told you to take a break" [ST5]. Garmin "Unproductive" for a user whose "HRV is balanced, my training readiness is super high ... load optimal" [GA7a].
- Heatmap colour failures: GitHub dark mode, "The mid-level shades of green blend in with the lightest shades"; GitHub staff: "we haven't quite got it right yet!" (WF) [GH4]. JeFit's dark-blue/light-blue selection state [JF4].
- Over-prompting: Oura "will happily tell me it's time to get up, wind down or go to bed ... I don't necessarily need another piece of technology gently parenting me" [WH7]; "a complete data paralysis" [WH7].
- Documentation drift: Whoop 0-100% [WH1] vs "1 to 99%" [WH4]; Oura seven contributors [OU3] vs nine [OU2]; Oura Readiness "Pay Attention" vs Health Areas "Needs Care ... extra love" [OU4].

## D. Body map specifics

- Regions drawn: no vendor page states a count for Fitbod, JeFit or Hevy. Evidence nearby: Strava's map has 15 groups (Biceps, Triceps, Forearms, Shoulders, Chest, Upper Back, Lats, Lower Back, Abs, Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves, Abductors, Adductors) [ST4]. Hevy's data model (third-party client mirroring its API, not a statement of what the heatmap draws) has 17 anatomical values plus cardio and full_body [HV7]. Arvo: "11+ muscles" [AR]. JeFit's BodyMap has muscles and "sub-muscles — like chest, traps, lats, quads, and hamstrings" [JF2].
- Front and back: Strava shows both "side by side" in display and flips in the editor [ST4]. Fitbod [S]: "swipe left or right to rotate the avatar and view the front or back of the body" (UNVERIFIED, help article unreadable). JeFit and Hevy: not stated.
- "No data" vs "fully recovered": Fitbod defines 100% as fully recovered, no-data state not stated [FB3]. Strava separates "Not worked (gray)" inside a populated map from a hidden map with no data ("You won't see an empty or gray figure") [ST4]. Arvo has a separate "untrained" state from "fresh" [AR]. GitHub's first swatch is its own "No contributions." [GH1].
- Figure vs list: figure first, list second. Hevy puts the diagram on top and "Below, you can see the training volume per muscle group" [HV1]. Fitbod's headline is the heat map; the list control is "Fresh Muscle Groups" [FB3]. JeFit: tap image or percentage [JF1]. Strava: figure only, list in editor [ST4].
- Weighting: Strava gives secondary muscles half credit [ST4]; Hevy's article advises the same by hand: "count the primary muscle as one set and the secondary muscles as half a set" [HV6].
- Recovery windows stated by vendors: Fitbod "up to 7 days"; "In general, 48–72 hours is enough" [FB1, FB3].

## E. Colour guidance

1. Sequential single hue (light to dark) for ordered magnitude: "Sequential schemes are suited to ordered data that progress from low to high. Lightness steps dominate the look of these schemes, with light colors for low data values to dark colors for high data values" [CB]. "On bright backgrounds, we associate dark colors with a high number" [DW2]. A dark-means-more gradient "works without a color key" [DW1].
2. Diverging only with a meaningful midpoint, and always with a key: ColorBrewer, "a critical value in the data such as the mean, median, or zero" [CB]; Datawrapper lists "a target, e.g., revenue below and above the quarterly target" but warns "your readers need a color key, otherwise they're lost. Which color is 'low' ... Which one is 'better'?" [DW1]. Crameri: "If the data is divergent about a central value ... a divergent or a multi-sequential colour map should be chosen" [CR].
3. Categorical hues for unordered states: "Qualitative schemes do not imply magnitude differences between legend classes ... best suited to representing nominal or categorical data" [CB]. "Use hues when you can't order your color-encoded values" [DW2]; shades of one hue for categories "will imply a ranking" [DW3].
4. Colour-vision safety: avoid red-green at similar lightness. "Colour maps that include both red and green colours with similar lightness cannot be read by a large fraction of the readership ... 0.5% of women and 8% of men" [CR]. Vary lightness: "readers with a color vision deficiency will still be able to distinguish your colors" [DW3]; cividis is "perceptually uniform in hue and brightness, and increases in brightness linearly" [NU]. Okabe-Ito: "For red, vermilion is used since it is recognizable also to protanopes"; "bluish green is chosen so that it won't be confused with red or brown" [OI]. Practical: keep red/green meaning "by varying the perceived brightness of the hues" (WF) [WI]. Wong 2011 gives 8% / 0.5% (preview only) [WO].
5. Never colour alone, label it, keep contrast: WCAG 1.4.1 "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information ... or distinguishing a visual element" [WG1]. Okabe-Ito: "Do not convey information in color only. Show difference BOTH in color and shape"; "label elements ... on the graph itself rather than making a separate color-coded key" [OI]. WCAG 1.4.11: graphical objects and selected states need "a contrast ratio of at least 3:1"; heat-map-like gradients are a listed exemption but "this exemption can be problematic when these graphical objects also act as user interface components" [WG2]. Crameri: "It is imperative to ensure the colour bar is included" [CR].
