# Progress tab user sentiment

What lifters say they want from progress tracking, and what they complain about,
from review aggregations and community discussion (June 2026). Sources at the
end. This is lighter than the competitor research by nature: it is sentiment,
not specification, and it points the same way.

## What lifters consistently say they want

- **Previous performance, instantly.** The most-repeated want is to see your
  last sets for this exercise the moment you walk up to the rack: weight, reps,
  and whether you beat it. Quick reference to past performance is valued above
  graphs and social features.
- **A small set of reads that mean something.** Volume, frequency and per-muscle
  progress, shown clearly. "Concrete data that turns effort into tangible
  progress" over flashy numbers.
- **Trust in the data.** Reliability, no lost logs, respect for their data. A
  progress surface is only believed if the underlying log is believed.
- **Choice over clutter.** Different lifters care about different numbers, so
  the surfaces that win let the important read sit on top and keep the rest out
  of the way.

## What they complain about

- **Breadth without use.** The single loudest theme: "more features do not equal
  a better app", "50 features you'll never use is worse than 10 that work".
  Bloated dashboards are a common one-star reason.
- **Clutter that buries the read.** Charts that look good in a screenshot but
  are noise in practice. MyFitnessPal's 2026 redesign drew the category's
  loudest backlash for adding taps and burying a previously visible read; the
  lesson is do not bury the thing people came for.
- **Vanity metrics.** Numbers that move but do not mean anything. Lifters
  discount them quickly.

Read against Volyume: the founder's own description ("mismatched, messy, patched
together") is the breadth-without-hierarchy complaint, from the inside. The
fourteen-card landing is exactly the pattern reviewers punish. The good news is
the underlying log and engine are strong, so the trust precondition is met; the
work is presentation.

## The tension for Volyume specifically

Volyume sits alongside coaches and positions as a precision instrument, not a
casual logger. So it cannot go fully minimal the way Strong does: a coached,
serious user genuinely wants the volume-vs-landmark depth and the readiness
signals. The craft is to keep the depth but rank it: one glanceable read on top,
the depth one tap down. That matches both the sentiment (lead with the read) and
the positioning (keep the instrument).

## Implications for the proposal (doc 05)

1. Put "what did I do last, and did I beat it" within easy reach (the Lifts
   surface, and the existing per-lift record).
2. Lead every surface with one meaningful read; push depth down a level, do not
   delete it.
3. Cut vanity and cut duplicates; both register as clutter.
4. Hold the no-gamification line; the sentiment does not ask for streaks, and
   the house rules forbid them.
