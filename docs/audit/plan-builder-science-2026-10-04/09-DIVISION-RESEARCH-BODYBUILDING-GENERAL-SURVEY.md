### A1. Design, sample, terminology

- Online cross-sectional survey (Google Forms, English and Portuguese), April to December 2020. Inclusion: affiliated to a federation, competed at least once in the last two years, training written down or on a spreadsheet.
- n = 154. Federations: NPC Pro League 85, IFBB Elite Pro 39, SPFF 19, WBFF 2, IFBB SP 1, NABBA 1, WBFF Pro 1, WFF 1, other 5.
- The paper says "pré-contest", not "in-season". I map "in-season" to pré-contest below. No other period (peak week, post-show) is reported.
- All periods are self-reported from written programmes; values are weekly sets (median, with min to max in brackets). Table 1 also gives body mass and height by category.

### A2. What counts as a set (definition and counting rules)

The paper contains no sentence that defines "a set" and no direct versus fractional rule. The methods text, verbatim from the PDF:

> "The weekly training volume for each muscle group was calculated based on equations used in prior studies (17, 18)."

followed by three listed inputs:

> "Number of exercises per muscle group per training session / Number of sets per exercise in each training session / Weekly training frequency per muscle group"

The muscle-group sentence, verbatim:

> "The study focused on analyzing the most commonly trained muscle groups in hypertrophy-oriented programs: pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi, deltoids, biceps brachii, triceps brachii, gluteal muscles, quadriceps, hamstrings, gastrocnemius (triceps surae), and abdominal muscles. For each muscle group, commonly performed exercises (e.g., French press for the triceps brachii) were included in the analysis. Table 2 provides a comprehensive list of exercises considered for each muscle group. All variations of these exercises were included, regardless of the training modality utilized, such as free weights, machines, pulley systems, or bodyweight exercises."

Observed consequence: weekly sets per muscle = exercises per session x sets per exercise x weekly frequency, summed over the exercises in the paper's Table 2 list. Fractional or indirect credit is not mentioned anywhere I read (NOT FOUND). Lower back appears as an eleventh row in the result tables although it is not in the muscle-group sentence.

Table 2 (exercises counted, as extracted; the PDF layout is garbled, so row-to-muscle alignment of a few entries is my reading):

Deltoids: one pooled "Deltoid" row (front, side, upright row); rear-delt work is not itemised. Back/lats: one "Latissimus dorsi" row that includes closed-grip rowing, pulldowns, pull-ups and deadlift, so it is a whole-back figure, not lats alone. Abs: one "Abdominals" row, trunk flexion only. Lower back is a separate row. Because the deadlift appears under lats and stiff deadlift under hamstrings, one movement can be counted toward more than one group; the paper does not say (NOT FOUND).

### A5. Paper-level statements worth carrying

- Men higher upper-body volume, women higher lower-body volume (Discussion; abstract snippet in search result: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2025.1536360/pdf ). In the PDF text: men lower-body 23.8 +/- 11.2 vs women 11.5 +/- 7.0 sets (the line I read in the discussion was cut by column layout; treat the exact pairing as unverified).
- Most categories reduced volume pré-contest for most muscles, with abdominals and cardio the exception (cardio rose; abdominals up for Bodybuilding Classic and Master).
- Self-reported, 2020, mostly Brazilian-affiliated sample (SPFF, São Paulo ethics approval), n small in four divisions. It is not a prescription.

### B7. Gaps and risks for use

- NOT FOUND: any 3-day open-bodybuilding split from a named pro or coach; Aceto, Rambod (own five-day), Henselmans (page-level), Nuckols (programme-level) and RP "off-season" template with muscles per day.

- Three of the seven pro splits are media or fan write-ups, not the athlete's publication. Treat them as descriptions of a style, not an athlete's verified programme.
- Part A is a 2020 self-report with n = 3 in two divisions and no fractional-set rule; its figures are descriptive of practice, not a dose recommendation.
