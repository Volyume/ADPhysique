Volyume · workout logger · research record · 6 October 2026

The logger, derived from the field

Twelve real active-workout screens, measured. The platform type scales and the reading-distance evidence, cited. Our logger measured the same way. Then the redesign, weighed against our users, and, in section 11, Boostcamp studied and Volyume drawn in its grammar.

Nothing on this page comes from an earlier brief, blueprint or options page. Where a pinned repository law constrains the design (the guard tests that protect the logger's structure), it is named as a law, not as a design source.

1. Method and limits

What was looked at. Current App Store listings (6 October 2026) for thirty apps, fetched through Apple's public lookup and search endpoints, 176 screenshots in all. From those, the twelve that show an active logging screen (a set being entered or just entered) were cropped and enlarged. Apps whose listings show only summaries, charts or marketing frames were not measured. Things 3 and WHOOP were kept as references for calm density outside the category and are not in the table.

How the sizes were measured. Each crop was scaled so the device mock inside the marketing frame is 390 points wide, the width the app renders at on a standard phone. Cap heights were then read against that scale and converted to a font size. The figures are rounded to the nearest point and should be read as ±2 pt: marketing screenshots are sometimes enlarged for effect (Caliber's second set, Liftin's rest pill), and where that is visible the un-enlarged element was measured instead. The conclusions below rest on medians and clusters, not on any single figure.

Limits. These are App Store (iOS) screenshots; our primary platform is Android, where the same apps ship the same screens and the point and dp units coincide. A store screenshot shows a chosen moment, not every state. Row heights were read from visible row rhythm. No app was installed; nothing here is a claim about behaviour that is not visible in the screenshot.

4. What the field agrees on, and where it splits

Layout model
Split 6 / 6. Session sheet (every exercise on one scroll): Hevy, Strong, Boostcamp, Musklr, RepCount, Gymaholic. One exercise in focus: Fitbod, Setgraph, Gravl, Alpha Progression, Caliber, Liftin'. The split is not random: the six focus apps are the ones that prescribe the sets (Fitbod, Caliber, Gravl, Alpha, Setgraph's targets, Liftin's programmes); the sheets are free loggers. Volyume prescribes, and its one-exercise workspace is also pinned law.
Row grammar
A column table in 7 of 12 (Hevy, Strong, Boostcamp, Musklr, Gymaholic, Alpha, Gravl), 6 of them with the set number first and a previous or last column beside the values. Label-over-value cells in RepCount and Fitbod's first row; a sentence row ("5 × 300 lbs") only in Liftin'.
Previous in the row
7 of 12 put last time's figures beside this time's (Hevy, Strong, Boostcamp, Musklr, Gymaholic, Caliber under each box, Alpha as a card). Our own user-voice evidence (lane A5, 380 dated quotes) has "shows what I did last time" as the second most-praised trait of any logger, at 21.9% of delights.
Input shape
Boxed cells in 7 of 12 (Strong's pending row, Boostcamp, Fitbod, Gravl, Caliber, Musklr, Setgraph's value fields); plain text cells in Hevy, Alpha, RepCount, Liftin', Gymaholic. Steppers (− +) only in Setgraph; increment pads in Setgraph and Liftin's plate graphic.
Current-set cue
A fill or outline on the row itself: Alpha (blue pill fill), Gravl (accent outline), Liftin' (filled circle, accent value), Strong (boxed cells appear on the pending row). The sheets otherwise rely on "the first row that is not green".
Done cue
A green check in 5 of 12 (Hevy, Strong, Musklr, Alpha, Setgraph's button), three of them also tinting the whole row green. The focus apps mostly show no done mark in these frames.
Confirm
Per-row check in the sheets (5); boxed cells that count as logged when filled (3); one big check button (Setgraph). No app in the twelve uses a full-width bottom "log" button; Volyume's is pinned by the single-CTA contract and by the design system ("Complete set is always the largest button on the active-workout screen").
Rest timer
In the logging frame it is small: a 13 text line (Hevy), chips at 11 to 15 (Musklr, Gravl, Fitbod), a 20 pill (Boostcamp), a round button (Alpha). It is large (34 to 44) only on a dedicated rest surface (Liftin'). Volyume's strip is pinned at 44 dp, docked outside the scroll.
Finish
Filled pill in the sheets (Strong green, RepCount teal, Musklr blue), text in Hevy; icon-only in Caliber and Gymaholic's crops. Volyume's icon-only Finish is a founder law.
Imagery
Exercise photos or thumbnails in 8 of 12. Volyume's exercise-media programme is held by founder ruling and is not proposed here.
Accent colour
One accent each, used on the exercise name or the current row, never on every element. No app fills its logging button with the brand colour except Setgraph (green check) and the Finish pills.

5c. Reading distance

digitalsignage.com, typography and viewing distance (fetched): "about 1 inch (25 mm) of letter height for every 10 feet (3 m) of viewing distance reads comfortably"; "1 inch for every 20 feet is the minimum". As formulas: "Comfortable letter height (in) = viewing distance (ft) × 0.1" and "Minimum letter height (in) = viewing distance (ft) × 0.05".

Applied to a phone, where one dp is 1/160 inch and a capital letter in Inter is 0.73 of the font size, with the two distances that matter in a gym: the phone in the hand while the number is adjusted, and the phone on the bench or floor during the rest, glanced at.

What this does and does not justify. A 24 dp value is comfortable in the hand (25) and clears the minimum for a glance from the bench (22). It also sits exactly where the field's largest live values sit (Fitbod 24, Setgraph 26) and on the house h2 role and Material's headlineSmall. The signage rule would make 43 dp "comfortable" from the bench and 72 dp from the floor. Nothing in the field does that on the logging screen, the task at that distance is recognising a number the lifter typed seconds earlier rather than reading a sign, and a value that large has been judged on device as oversized. So the evidence supports 24 as the live value with 32 (the h1 role, Material headlineLarge) as the one defensible step up for the glance case, and supports nothing above it.

The same table says why 13 to 16 dp is the wrong size for a live value: 16 is "the lower edge of comfortable" in the hand and below the 22 minimum from the bench. It is the size our logger uses today.

6. Our logger today, measured the same way

Read from the source (SetEntry.js, NowCard.js, LoggedSetRow.js, RestTimer.js, WorkoutHeader.js, layout.js), not estimated:

  • Live values "70" and "9" are type.num('bodyStrong'): 16, inside 36 dp stepper buttons. The smallest live value of the thirteen screens on this page.
  • Logged rows ("1 · 70kg × 8") are type.num('bodySm'): 13, with an 11 number badge, on a 36 dp minimum row. The field's row values are 17 on 44 to 48 rows; 13 is the size the field uses for the previous column.
  • Last session ("Last session: 72.5kg × 8 · Use") is caption: 11. The field shows previous at 13 to 15.
  • Exercise name 17 (title); position line 16; field labels 13; record line 13 over 11; rest digits 16 in the 44 strip; elapsed 17.
  • Nesting. The active set is a bordered card holding a tinted last-session strip, two bordered stepper fields and a tinted record strip: four surfaces deep. The field runs one or two.
  • Set states. Warm-up is an amber-tinted pill, logged sets are grey lines with a number badge, the upcoming set is a bare line: three different grammars for one sequence, and no done mark.

The design system's own rule (docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md, "Numbers are the hero — sized large, tabular, legible at a glance") is not met by the logger: no number on the screen is above 17.

7. The redesign

The first design drawn from this research (6 October, version 4 of this page) kept the current screen's skeleton and re-used a set table with boxed inputs, the grammar of the sheets above and of a rejected concept. It is withdrawn. What follows starts from the lifter's job and the evidence, not from the existing screen or from any earlier document.

The job, and what the evidence says about it

A set has two moments with the phone. In the hand (about 35 cm) for a few seconds: read what to do, then record what happened. On the bench (60 cm or more) for one to three minutes: see how long is left and what to load next. The distance table in §5c says these need different type: 13 to 25 dp in the hand, 22 to 43 dp from the bench. No logger in §2 changes with the moment; each shows one static layout and leaves the far moment to a separate rest sheet or a small chip.

Volyume prescribes every set. In a prescriber, most sets are done as planned, so recording should be confirmation, and changing a number the exception. The user voice behind this audit (lane A5, 380 dated statements) ranks "simple, fast, no fluff" first among delights at 23.5% and "shows what I did last time" second at 21.9%; redesigns that bury controls or add taps are 14.8% of complaints.

Six principles, each tied to a row of evidence

  1. Two states on one screen. Lift and Rest alternate automatically when a set is logged and when the rest ends. The screen is built for the distance it is read at (§5c). Nothing in the field does this (§4, timer row).
  2. The plan is the default. Weight and reps arrive filled from the coach; one tap logs them. The button says what it will record (prescriber model, §4; A5 delights and complaints).
  3. Last time lives on the control. An amber tick on the weight ruler at 72.5 and an amber ring on rep 8 show last session where the hand is, plus one line in words (A5, 21.9%; 7 of 12 show previous, all of them in a column).
  4. No table, no boxes, no cards. One object on the background; time and progress are thin amber lines (the design system's "hierarchy through contrast, not complexity"; the field's tables are what the user voice calls clutter).
  5. Numbers at platform sizes, no further. 32 for the pair in the hand (Material headlineLarge, the house h1, above the field's 26), 40 for the one countdown read from the bench (the house display, "the one hero number on a screen"; §5c comfortable at 60 cm is 43), 24 for the next set during rest (clears the bench minimum of 22), 13 and 11 for everything secondary (iOS 13 and 11, Material 12 and 11).
  6. One accent, on the live thing. Amber only for the session line, the rest line, the last-time marks, the selected rep and Finish. Green only for a done mark.

Lift (left)

  1. Header. Close, the session clock small in the centre, the icon-only Finish in amber. Under it a full-width hairline that fills amber as the session's sets are logged: this is the only progress display.
  2. Exercise. Eyebrow "Upper A · Exercise 2 of 6" at 11, which opens the session sheet (every exercise and set, reorder, swap, add); the name at 24 with the chevron that opens exercise details; the set's prescription at 13.
  3. Weight. The value at 32 over a ruler whose step is the exercise's load increment from the engine (2.5 kg for a barbell as drawn, 2 kg for a dumbbell rack, the stack's step for a machine), labels every 10. Drag the ruler with a detent per step; the white marker is the value, the amber tick is last session's 72.5. Any weight off the grid is typed on the pad behind a tap on the number. It is a weight scale, never a plate readout.
  4. Reps. The value at 32 over a rep picker: a row of counts centred on the target (here 3 to 15 for a 6 to 10 target), the target range filled, last time's 8 ringed in amber, the chosen 9 amber. Tap a count or drag along the row with a detent per rep; the row scrolls for counts beyond the window. Each count is a 48 dp tall target. The big number and the button label repeat the choice.
  5. Record line. One line at 13 with the one trophy glyph, in plain words. The PR itself is still the calm toast after Log.
  6. This exercise. Warm-up, set 1 with its green check, set 3 as the plan, each a sentence line at 15, anchored above the button where the thumb is; tap a logged line to edit it. Three or more logged sets fold behind one line, as today.
  7. Log. The house primary button, charcoal with the 1 dp border, and it names what it will record: "Log 70 kg × 9".

Rest (middle)

  1. The moment Log is tapped the entry object becomes the rest object: "Rest · 2:00" as the eyebrow (the exercise's rest; the default is 90 s), the countdown at 40, a hairline that drains, −15 / +15 / Skip as 48 dp text targets. The session hairline at the top advances.
  2. Next at 24: set 3 of 3, 70 kg × 6 to 10, with last time at 13. Readable from the bench.
  3. The logged set appears in the list with Edit. The button becomes "Start set 3"; at zero the screen returns to Lift by itself, with the haptic and notification as today. Moving to the next exercise is always the user's tap, never automatic.

Lift with fields (right)

The same screen with two quiet fields instead of the ruler and dots. Tapping a field opens the number pad sheet (with 2.5 and 1.25 kg steps on it). This is the conventional path and the accessibility path; it can also sit behind the ruler for people who prefer typing.

Not on any of the three: a table, an input box per set, a card inside a card, an exercise photo, a plate calculator, an RPE field, a filled amber button, a number above 40, a second accent.

10. Weighed against our users and their psychology: the final two

Read on 7 October from the production database as aggregates only (no row or person was read), and from the behavioural literature listed in the sources. The cloud mirrors of the logging tables are partial, so the logging figures are directional; the profile figures cover the whole base.

Who they are

Base
25 profiles, 20 past first run, sign-ups July to October; 21 deletions, all user-requested (Android 15, iOS 6).
Who
Sex, which onboarding makes people choose: male 15, female 2 of 17 body profiles. Primary goal "general" 14 of 17. The kg, bodybuilding and "3 years training" columns are database defaults, not choices (a first version of this section read them as choices; corrected). Experience, age, equipment and platform split are not known from data. The repo's intended user is an intermediate UK lifter training 3 to 6 times a week; the repo records no user research of its own.
Sessions
27 cloud sessions from 5 users, 18 of them from one account: median 53 minutes, 23 sets, 6 exercises, 3 sets per exercise; no session notes; pre-workout intent set on 22 of 27.
Sets
610, three quarters of them from one account, so these describe at most two people's habits: straight 609, warm-up 1; RIR and RPE never written; reps within the prescribed range 87%, above it 10%, below 3%; reps median 15 (a quarter are 20 or more); weight median 40 kg; on a 2.5 kg grid 75%, on 1.25 only 13%, off both 12% (dumbbell racks, machine stacks); edited later 8.5%.
Between logs
Median gap between consecutive logs of one exercise 107 s (that holds the rest and the next set, not pure rest). At 23 sets that is about 41 of 53 minutes: the screen would sit in the state the redesign calls Rest for about three quarters of a session, which today is a 44 dp strip with 16 pt digits.
Signals
Sessions skipped 9, ended early 3; in-session swaps 73; the three in-app feedback entries all sit on the workout summary (two "helpful", one "love").

The psychology that applies

  • Attention under effort. Reduced prefrontal resources during and just after hard exertion (Dietrich's transient hypofrontality; the resistance-training evidence is mixed, so the claim is kept modest). One visible action; the button names what it will do.
  • Defaults. Meta-analysis of 58 studies: defaults move choices strongly (d = 0.68), most when they read as an endorsement by a trusted source (Jachimowicz et al. 2019). The coach's prescription pre-filled and logged by one tap is that default; 87% of sets land inside the prescribed range (the cloud cannot tell an accepted prefill from a typed value). A wrong default is sticky too, so deviation must be one gesture away and last time visible.
  • Progress. Effort rises as the goal nears (goal-gradient, Kivetz et al. 2006) and visible progress raises completion (endowed progress, Nunes and Drèze 2006). The session hairline and the position eyebrow, no numeric nagging.
  • Competence and autonomy. Self-determination theory reviews tie adherence to competence and self-endorsed choice; mastery experiences are the strongest source of self-efficacy (Bandura). Last time and the record belong at the point of action as information; the rep range is an invitation, not a demand.
  • Grip and precision. Grips change constantly and people touch the centre most accurately (Hoober 2013 and his 2014 follow-up, which the repo's own research holds against the thumb-zone chart); one-handed thumb targets degrade below 9.2 mm, about 58 dp (Parhi, Karlson and Bederson 2006); finger occlusion and tremor punish small targets (Fitts, FFitts). So: the entry object in the middle two-thirds, the once-per-set Log button large and low because it is the most frequent action, 48 dp as the floor for every other target, gestures that snap to a detent.
  • Habit. Automaticity grows with repetition in a stable context (Lally et al. 2010). One identical loop every set.
  • Memory of the session. The end of a bout shapes how it is remembered (Zenko, Ekkekakis and Ariely 2016). The finish and summary are part of the logger's job, and the summary already draws positive feedback.
  • What to avoid. Badges and streaks give short-term gains and broken streaks demotivate; fitness tracking has been linked to eating-disorder symptoms (Simpson and Mazzeo 2017). The logger informs and never pushes; the record line states the best, the rest state shows the plan.

What the evidence settles

Two states: yes. The screen sits between logs for three quarters of a session, the far-distance physics stands, one stable loop is what habit needs, and the field's own users ask the rest screen to show the next set's load. Sizes 32 / 40 / 24: yes, the countdown is the most-read number of the session. The button names the set: yes, it removes a re-read under fatigue and catches a slip. A ruler without a pad: eliminated twice over: 12% of weights are off the grid, and the field's users say the entry control "is a taste, not a fact", wheels annoy people who must scroll and keyboards annoy people who must type every time, and three products that changed input mode in 2026 drew complaints. Typing stays first-class in either choice. Scope: the user-optimal answer is the whole logger in one campaign with a device walk between stages, because the finish and summary carry the memory of the session; the cost is the founder's call.

Two things the data changed in the drawings: the rep control is a windowed picker centred on the target (the median set is 15 reps and a quarter are 20 or more, so fifteen fixed dots could not carry it), and the ruler's step follows the exercise's equipment with the pad behind the number for anything off the grid. A warm-up line appears only when one is logged.

The final two

Shared by both: the two-state screen, the plan filled in and logged by one tap, the button naming the set, 32 / 40 / 24, the session hairline, last time in words on every set, the calm record line, Edit from Rest, the sets as sentence lines above the house charcoal button, amber only on the live thing, every safety rule and the locked voice.

Choice A
Ruler and picker
The instrument. Weight on a ruler stepped to the exercise's equipment, dragged with a detent per step, last session as an amber tick. Reps on a picker centred on the target, the range filled, last time ringed; tap or drag, 48 dp tall hit areas. The pad behind a tap on either number is first-class: any value, people who type, screen readers.
For: the above-range case is one tap, a 2.5 kg step is one flick, last time is where the hand is, drag-and-snap forgives a sweaty thumb, and the detent is a small pleasure every set. Against: new on first use (one quiet hint), slip-prone without the tall hit area and the live readout, the heavier build. Suits: this base, experienced lifters making small, frequent, mostly upward adjustments.
Choice B
Fields and pad
The conventional. Two quiet fields with last time under each value; a tap opens a number pad with 2.5 and 1.25 kg steps. Nothing else changes.
For: zero learning, exact entry of anything, the fewest visible controls, the simplest accessibility, the lowest build risk. Against: every change costs three or four taps, paid on the 13% of sets that deviate and on every edit; last time is text only; nothing about it is tactile or distinctive. Suits: people who type their numbers, and a faster first device walk.

The lead's recommendation: Choice A, on the product-best criterion: the base is experienced, adjusts by small steps and mostly upward, and spends most of the session in the state this design builds for. Choice B is the safer build, not the better product. The go-ahead is the founder's: A or B, and the scope.

What its users say about the look

Praise for design is rare across the field (1.7% of 1,675 high-star reviews) and Boostcamp takes 3 of those 29 mentions: "This app has a very well designed UI" (March 2026); "Love the new UI. Best feature is having the previous weight and reps on current workout" (March 2026). The same March 2026 redesign drew the other side: "Strongly dislike the new one" and "custom workouts hard to find" (March 2026), "increasingly cluttered" (June 2026); and later releases brought "the rest timer UX is significantly worse" (September 2026), "the timer is all but useless as the app resets when you change apps" (August 2026), lost data (September 2026) and accidental swipe-deletes (July and September 2026). The look is admired; the regressions are what people leave over.

Why it looks good (the lessons)

  1. Depth from tone, not lines. Three greys a few points apart (page, section, well) make layers read without outlined boxes. Our ladder has the same three steps: background, surface, surface elevated.
  2. Full-bleed sections. The table uses the whole width; density without clutter. No rounded card per exercise.
  3. One accent with three jobs. The exercise name (where you are), the done check (what is finished), the finish. Our amber already does the first two on other screens.
  4. Two-line cells. The number on top, its context under it in grey 13. Dense and scannable.
  5. One object per row for input. Joined wells, one check. The row's state is visible at a glance.
  6. Moderate type, weight contrast. 15 to 18 for content, bold where it matters, nothing shouting.
  7. Named tools. Glyph over label in the toolbar; no mystery icons.
  8. A steady rhythm. 62 pt rows, hairlines, 56 pt headers, 12 pt bands.

What we take and what we leave

Taken, in our tokens: the full-bleed sections and bands; the three-step tonal depth; amber for the exercise name, the done check and the W badge; sentence-case 13 labels; two-line cells with Last and Target (our target's second line is the coach's rule for the set, "+2.5 at 10", "warm-up", "last set"); joined wells with white values on logged rows and grey placeholders on pending ones; the 32 pt check per row, amber when done, an amber ring on the next set; the W and F markers as tinted badges; the footer of Add set, Swap and the overflow; the toolbar with Rest and Notes as named tools and the session clock in a well; the docked custom keypad with 2.5 kg step keys and Next. Left: the plate calculator (D15, D57), RPE and RIR fields (settled), the yellow-filled Finish (the house has no filled amber button; Finish stays the amber glyph), swipe-to-delete (its users lose sets to it), the programme-note block (our coach's prescription is the Target column), the pause on the session clock.
