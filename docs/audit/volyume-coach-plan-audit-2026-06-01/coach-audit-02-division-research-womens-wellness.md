# Women's Wellness Division Research

Status: COMPLETE | Timestamp: 2026-06-01 | Division: Women's Wellness

Research date: June 2026. All coaching claims are attributed to a named coach or
published methodology. Science claims cite the study or researcher. Documented
numbers are sourced inline; numbers we inferred are tagged [synthesis].

## 1. Definitive aesthetic standard and current judging

Wellness is the most lower-body-dominant women's division in the IFBB Pro League
and NPC. The official standard rewards pronounced glute and leg mass set against
an upper body that is developed but deliberately held back, so the lower half
visibly outweighs the upper half.

What the rules and judging notes reward:

- Full, round glutes with slight separation between the glute and hamstring tie-in,
  and glutes larger than current Bikini competitors carry. (NPC/IFBB Wellness rules,
  reported via NPC News Online and Center Podium division guides.)
- Pronounced hamstrings and dense overall lower-body development. Quads with an
  athletic sweep showing slight separation. (BarBend, Wellness vs Bikini leg
  training.)
- Conditioning judged close to Bikini level but with a little more muscle: slight
  separation, no striations, a soft and smooth finish rather than the hard,
  dry look of Figure or Women's Physique. (NPC/IFBB Wellness rules via Center
  Podium and Roxstar Fitness.)
- An upper body that is toned and proportionate, shoulders and arms slightly more
  developed than Bikini and slightly less than Figure, but never wide, blocky, or
  capping the lower body. (Sheru Classic, Built By Sam Lee, division comparison
  pages.)

The specific lower-to-upper balance judges want: the silhouette should read
bottom-heavy. The hips, glutes, and thighs are the headline; the upper body is
support, not story. Posing reinforces this. Wellness competitors are coached to
pose the upper body smaller than the lower body, slightly retracting the scapula,
keeping lats quiet, and not flaring the shoulders wide, so the eye lands on the
legs and glutes. Toes are turned out slightly in quarter turns to accentuate the
quad sweep. (Posing Coach Australia, IFBB/NPC and ICN Wellness posing guides.)

What is marked down: too much upper-body mass, especially wide delts and a thick
back that closes the X and makes the frame read like Figure; flat or under-built
glutes; smooth, undefined legs with no hamstring or quad separation; and at the
other extreme, conditioning that crosses into striated, stringy, Figure-hard
territory. The division penalises both ends. Too little lower body loses to the
field; too much upper body or too much dryness moves the athlete out of the
division's look. (Synthesis of NPC/IFBB Wellness rules and the division
comparison sources above. [synthesis] on the relative weighting.)

## 2. Top 5 coaches in the world for Wellness

The division is Brazil-led, and the coaching that produces Olympia-level Wellness
reflects that.

1. **Ricardo Pannain (Team Pannain, Brazil).** Widely described as the leading
   Wellness coach in the world, with more than 20 years working with high-performance
   athletes. He coaches Francielle Mattos, the most decorated Wellness Olympia
   competitor, and Anne Luise Freitas, a five-time Pro champion and multiple-year
   Olympian (2012/13/14/16/18) who now also coaches under the Team Pannain banner.
   Pannain attributes Brazil's Wellness dominance to how readily Brazilian athletes
   add glute and leg mass and builds his programming around that. (Team Pannain,
   Wings of Strength, urskalecinski.com profile of Francielle Mattos.)

2. **Anne Luise Freitas (Team Pannain).** The first Brazilian to win the absolute
   world title in women's bodybuilding and a five-time Pro champion. She came up
   under Pannain and now coaches Wellness athletes within his team, carrying the
   same lower-body-first methodology. (Team Pannain, Wings of Strength.)

   The remaining three seats reflect the wider coaching field around the division.
   Naming is harder here because the highest-profile athletes keep their day-to-day
   programming private, so these are credited to documented training relationships
   and division reputation rather than to a published ranking.

3. **Charles Glass (Gold's Gym, Venice, USA).** The "Godfather of Bodybuilding"
   and a former NPC Nationals and IFBB World middleweight champion. He has coached
   Wellness and women's-division athletes including Angelica Teixeira, and his
   machine-led, angle-obsessed approach to glute and leg shaping is widely used by
   Wellness competitors training in the US. (Fitness Volt, Charles Glass workout
   coverage.)

4. **Eduarda Bezerra's preparation team (Brazil).** Bezerra won the 2025 Wellness
   Olympia, the third woman to take the title, with what the prejudging coverage
   called an all-time-best look. Her specific coach is not consistently named in
   public coverage, so this seat credits the Brazilian Wellness coaching pipeline
   that produced her rather than a confirmed individual. (Fitness Volt and Box Life
   2025 Wellness Olympia coverage; coach name not documented. [synthesis] on
   attribution.)

5. **Renaissance Periodization (Dr Mike Israetel and team, methodology).** Not a
   single Wellness coach, but the volume-landmark framework (MV/MEV/MAV/MRV) that
   a large share of evidence-based Wellness and physique coaches now program
   against for glutes, hamstrings and quads. Included as a published methodology,

   quad training guides.)

## 3. Programming philosophy

The dominant documented philosophy is high-frequency, high-volume lower-body work
with the upper body trained for maintenance, not growth.

- **Frequency.** Francielle Mattos, under Ricardo Pannain, trains legs and glutes
  five times per week. This is the clearest documented data point for elite Wellness
  frequency. (urskalecinski.com profile citing Pannain.) The published glute
  literature supports frequencies in this range: RP Strength states most people
  recover from glute work on a timecourse allowing 2 to 5 glute sessions per week
  at MEV-to-MRV volumes. (RP Strength glute guide.)
- **Volume.** High for the lower body, sub-maximal for the upper body. The 2025
  Frontiers in Physiology meta-analysis on gluteus maximus hypertrophy found a
  moderate overall training effect (11 studies, SMD 0.71, 95% CI 0.50 to 0.91) and
  concluded that a varied programme combining a glute-specific single-joint exercise
  with compound work produces the best growth. (Maul et al., Frontiers in Physiology,
  April 2025.)
- **Exercise selection.** Glute-biased hip extension as the spine of the programme:
  hip thrusts, deep and full-range squats, leg press, lunges, Romanian deadlifts,
  plus machine isolation for glutes, hamstrings, quads and adductors. (BarBend
  Wellness vs Bikini leg training; Mattos training coverage showing hip thrusts,
  leg extensions and hamstring curls.)
- **Load and range of motion.** Heavy compound work paired with deliberate
  long-muscle-length training. The 2025 meta-analysis found full or parallel-depth
  back squats taken through the maximum attainable range significantly increased
  gluteus maximus cross-sectional area. (Maul et al., 2025.) For hamstrings, Maeo
  et al. (2021) found the seated leg curl, which loads the hamstring in a lengthened
  position, produced 14% volume growth versus 9% for the prone curl over 12 weeks.
  (Maeo et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc, 2021, PMID 33009197.)
- **Progression.** Standard progressive overload within volume landmarks: start a
  block near MEV, add sets or load week to week toward MAV/MRV, then deload. (RP
  Strength volume-landmarks framework.)

## 4. Muscle-group priority ranking and why

Primary, in order of judging payoff:

1. **Glutes.** The single most rewarded structure. Judges want full, round glutes
   with a clean glute-hamstring tie-in. Trained for both mass and shape across the
   upper and lower regions (see Section 6). (NPC/IFBB rules; Maul et al., 2025.)
2. **Hamstrings.** Pronounced hamstrings are explicitly part of the standard, and
   the glute-ham tie-in depends on them. (BarBend Wellness vs Bikini.)
3. **Quads.** An athletic sweep with slight separation, turned out in posing to
   widen the lower frame. Built but not so striated it crosses into Figure. (Posing
   Coach Australia; division rules.)
4. **Adductors.** The inner-thigh sweep that fills the lower body and supports the
   bottom-heavy X. Trained directly because squats and hip thrusts do not load the
   adductors through their full range. (Chris Beardsley / NSCA on adductor training.)

Maintained, not grown:

- **Upper body (delts, back, arms, chest).** Kept toned and proportionate.
  Upper-body volume is deliberately limited because added delt and back width
  closes the X-frame and pushes the athlete toward the Figure look the division
  marks down. Wellness shoulders sit just above Bikini and below Figure. (Sheru
  Classic; Built By Sam Lee; division comparison pages.) The practical mechanism:
  Bikini athletes train the upper body more and the lower body less, Wellness
  inverts that. (BarBend Wellness vs Bikini leg training.)

How upper-body volume is limited in practice: maintenance-level set counts (low
frequency, low volume, often a single short session covering back, delts and arms),
no dedicated width work for the delts and lats, and posing that hides rather than
showcases the upper body. ([synthesis] on the exact upper-body set prescription;
the principle of upper-body maintenance is documented, the specific set counts are
not consistently published per athlete.)

## 5. Weekly volume per muscle group

Documented anchor: Francielle Mattos trains legs and glutes five times per week
under Pannain. (urskalecinski.com.) Published glute frequency guidance: 2 to 5
sessions per week at MEV-to-MRV. (RP Strength.) The RP framework defines the
landmarks (MV maintenance, MEV minimum effective, MAV maximum adaptive, MRV maximum
recoverable) but the search did not surface RP's exact per-muscle numbers, so the
set ranges below are tagged as synthesis built on that framework and on the
documented five-day lower-body frequency.

Off-season (building), weekly direct sets [synthesis except where noted]:

- Glutes: 16 to 22 sets, spread across 3 to 5 sessions. (Frequency band 2 to 5 is
  documented via RP; five-day lower-body frequency is documented via Mattos.)
- Hamstrings: 12 to 18 sets, 2 to 3 sessions, with at least one lengthened-position
  movement (seated curl or RDL) every session. (Lengthened-position rationale:
  Maeo et al., 2021.)
- Quads: 10 to 16 sets, 2 to 3 sessions, full-depth squat or press included.
  (Full-ROM rationale: Maul et al., 2025.)
- Adductors: 6 to 10 sets, 2 to 3 sessions of direct machine or cable work.
- Upper body total (back, delts, chest, arms combined): roughly 6 to 12 sets across
  the week at maintenance, deliberately low to avoid upper-body growth. (Principle
  documented; numbers [synthesis].)

Prep (contest), weekly direct sets [synthesis]:

- Lower body: hold volume as high as recovery allows for as long as possible, then
  trim only as fatigue and food drop force it. Prep is about keeping the lower-body
  mass already built, not adding to it, while conditioning comes in.
- Upper body: hold at maintenance, never increase. Adding upper-body work in prep
  risks unbalancing the look judges reward.
- Deload every 5 to 6 weeks, dropping volume 40 to 60% for around 6 days. (Deload
  cadence: Coleman et al. deloading survey, PMC10948666; BarBend deload coverage.)

The headline for this division: lower-body frequency and volume run at the top of
the recoverable range, which is higher than general hypertrophy templates prescribe,
and the documented five-day leg-and-glute week is the clearest signal of that.

## 6. Exercise selection philosophy

Glutes:

- **Barbell hip thrust** as the glute-specific anchor. Contreras et al. found the
  hip thrust produces higher gluteus maximus and biceps femoris EMG than the back
  squat at matched 10RM loads (J Strength Cond Res, 2015, PMID 26695353), and a
  2023 Frontiers training study found it is more glute-specific, building the glutes
  while sparing non-glute thigh mass. (Plotkin et al., Frontiers in Physiology,
  2023, PMC10349977.) RP recommends prioritising a single glute-specific movement
  like the hip thrust when the goal is glutes over other muscles. (RP Strength.)
- **Deep and full-range squats** for overall glute and quad mass. Full ROM
  significantly raises gluteus maximus cross-sectional area. (Maul et al., 2025.)
- **Hip abduction work** to bias the upper gluteus maximus. The upper region is a
  much stronger hip abductor than the lower region and its activation rises with
  hip flexion angle, so seated abduction and cable abduction develop the upper-outer
  glute that hip extension alone under-trains. (Chris Beardsley, "How should we
  train the gluteus maximus".)
- Rationale for using both: hip thrusts and squats both bias the lower glute
  through hip extension, so abduction is added to round out the upper region.
  (Beardsley.)

Hamstrings:

- **Seated leg curl** for lengthened-position growth (Maeo et al., 2021, the +14%
  vs +9% finding), paired with **Romanian deadlift** for heavy hip-hinge loading.
  These are complementary: RDL loads the hamstring long under hip flexion, the
  seated curl loads it long under knee extension. (Verro Training; N1 Training
  seated curl vs RDL.)

Quads:

- **Leg press and full-depth squat** for the sweep, plus **leg extension** for the
  rectus femoris and teardrop. Built for shape and slight separation, not for
  Figure-level dryness. (BarBend Wellness vs Bikini; general hypertrophy practice.)

Adductors:

- **Seated adductor machine** and **cable or band hip adduction**, chosen because
  they train the adductors through a larger range than squats reach. (NSCA on the
  adduction machine; Beardsley on adductor ROM.)

De-prioritised up top, and why:

- Lateral and rear delt width work, heavy back-width rows and pulldowns, and direct
  arm volume are pulled back to maintenance. The reason is structural, not lazy:
  delt and lat width close the X-frame and shift the silhouette toward Figure, which
  the division marks down. Wellness wants the upper body present but quiet. (Sheru
  Classic; Built By Sam Lee; Posing Coach Australia on keeping the upper body posed
  smaller.)

## 7. Practices specific to Wellness

Versus general hypertrophy:

- Lower-body frequency and volume sit at the top of the recoverable range while the
  upper body is held at maintenance on purpose. General hypertrophy programmes chase
  balanced whole-body growth; Wellness intentionally skews the balance and accepts a
  deliberately under-built upper body. (Documented Mattos five-day leg frequency;
  upper-body maintenance principle from division sources.)
- Exercise choice is driven by what the judges score, not by general performance.
  Upper-glute abduction work and adductor isolation earn their place because they
  fill specific parts of the judged silhouette. (Beardsley; NSCA.)

Versus Bikini:

- Bikini trains the upper body more and keeps glutes and thighs tight and lean but
  not large; Wellness inverts this and chases glute and leg size. Bikini athletes
  often avoid direct quad work to keep the legs from blowing up; Wellness trains
  quads directly for the sweep. (BarBend Wellness vs Bikini leg training; Top Knot
  Strong NPC Bikini vs Wellness.)
- Conditioning targets differ only slightly: both finish soft with no striations,
  but Wellness carries more muscle underneath. (NPC/IFBB rules.)
- Posing differs: Wellness deliberately poses the upper body smaller, retracting the
  scapula and keeping lats quiet, and turns the toes out to widen the quad sweep.
  Bikini posing does not suppress the upper body to the same degree. (Posing Coach
  Australia.)

## 8. Common weak points and how the best coaches address each

- **Flat or under-developed upper glutes (the upper-outer "shelf").** Hip extension
  alone biases the lower glute. Fix: add hip abduction with the hip flexed (seated
  abduction, cable abduction), which targets the upper region the abductor research
  identifies. (Beardsley.)
- **Weak glute-hamstring tie-in.** Fix: lengthened-position hamstring work (seated
  leg curl and RDL) to thicken the hamstring into the glute. (Maeo et al., 2021.)
- **Quad sweep that lacks width.** Fix: full-depth squats and leg press through full
  ROM, plus turning the toes out in posing to present the sweep. (Maul et al., 2025;
  Posing Coach Australia.)
- **Thin inner thigh / gap that breaks the lower-body fullness.** Fix: direct
  adductor machine and cable adduction through full range. (NSCA; Beardsley.)
- **Upper body too big, closing the X.** This is the division-specific weak point.
  Fix: pull upper-body volume back to maintenance, stop direct width work, and pose
  the upper body smaller. Charles Glass's angle-led shaping is used to refine rather
  than enlarge the upper body. (Division comparison sources; Posing Coach Australia;
  Fitness Volt on Glass.)
- **Conditioning crossing into Figure-hard.** Fix: bring conditioning in only to
  slight separation with no striations, keeping the soft Wellness finish. (NPC/IFBB
  rules.)

## 9. Recovery management given the heavy lower-body load

A five-day leg-and-glute week is a large recovery demand. The documented and
research-supported practices:

- **Sex-based recovery advantage.** Women tend to recover faster from resistance
  training and show lower post-exercise creatine kinase and less muscle damage than
  men, with oestrogen implicated in attenuating damage and accelerating repair
  (though the human evidence is mixed). This partly explains why elite Wellness
  women tolerate higher lower-body frequency than typical male hypertrophy templates.
  (PMC12790778 on sex differences in fatigue and recovery; PubMed 11706288 on
  reduced muscle damage in women; oova.life on oestrogen and recovery, flagged as
  not unanimous in the literature.)
- **Stimulus-Recovery-Adaptation management.** RP's SRA model is used to space glute
  sessions so most fatigue clears before the next overload, which is what makes 2 to
  5 glute sessions per week recoverable. (RP Strength glute guide.)
- **Exercise rotation across sessions.** To run high glute frequency without
  overloading the same tissue, coaches alternate movement patterns: barbell hip
  thrust one day, single-leg hip thrust or machine kickback the next. (RP Strength.)
- **Planned deloads.** Volume cut 40 to 60% for around 6 days roughly every 5 to 6
  weeks, which the deloading survey reports as typical practice in physique sport
  and which limits plateaus and supports long-term progress. (Coleman et al.,
  PMC10948666; BarBend deload coverage.)
- **Loading the lengthened position deliberately rather than chasing soreness.**
  Long-muscle-length work drives growth, but coaches manage its higher damage cost
  by programming it with adequate spacing rather than every session to failure.
  (Maeo et al., 2021 on hypertrophy; general muscle-damage management. [synthesis]
  on the spacing application.)
