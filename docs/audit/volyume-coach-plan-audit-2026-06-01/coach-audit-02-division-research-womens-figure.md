# Coach Plan Audit 02: Division Research, Women's Figure

Status: COMPLETE | Timestamp: 2026-06-01 | Division: Women's Figure

This document is sourced from live web research conducted 2026-06-01.
Documented figures are cited inline. Numbers marked [synthesis] are
the author's training-design inference, not a sourced fact, and should
be treated as a starting recommendation only.

---

## 1. Definitive aesthetic standard and current judging

Figure is judged on small-class lineups using quarter turns, not the
pose-down of bodybuilding or Women's Physique. Athletes hold a front
relaxed position, then the head judge calls "quarter turn to the right"
through four positions: front, right side, rear, left side. There is no
front double biceps, no rear lat spread, no individual mandatory posing
routine in the scored round. The presentation is closer to Men's
Physique than to bodybuilding (NPC News Online; Pro Prep Coaching).

The IFBB criteria assess symmetry, muscularity, definition
(conditioning) and stage presence, with the competitor expected to show
a V-taper running from the shoulders down to a small waist, then out to
shapely lower body (IFBB Pro rules summary via search; Center Podium).
The prized lines: wide capped deltoids, a wide flaring back (lats and
teres), a tight waist, then balanced quads and round glutes. The
shoulder-to-waist ratio is the single most valued line in the division.

What wins:
- A genuine V-taper read from the front and especially the rear. The
  rear quarter turn, showing back width plus the glute-ham tie-in, is
  where Figure placings are most often decided (NPC News Online).
- Conditioning that is clearly muscular and separated but stops short
  of striation and dryness. Figure shows "a moderate amount of muscle
  with a small amount of striations", and judges explicitly "don't want
  to see the deep musculature and striations often found in Women's
  Physique competitors" (NPC News Online; Bodybuilding Wizard).
  Reported stage body fat for the division sits roughly 8 to 12 percent
  (3DMuscleJourney; treat the exact band as approximate, prep is judged
  by look not by a number).
- Balance and shape, specifically across hamstrings, glutes, shoulders
  and back (NPC News Online).
- Stage presence: poise and confidence, holding each quarter turn so
  the strengths face the panel.

What is marked down:
- Over-conditioning into a hard, stringy, striated look that reads as
  Women's Physique. This is a division error, not just a weakness.
- A blocky or thick waist that kills the taper.
- Lower body out of proportion with the upper body, either too small to
  fill the legs or too large so the X-frame collapses into a rectangle.
- Failing to hit the quarter turns correctly. Sloppy turns score lower
  because the judge cannot read the lines (NPC News Online).

The 2025 Figure Olympia, the first after Cydney Gillon's eight-title run
ended with her October 2024 retirement, was won by Britain's Rhea Gayle,
ahead of Lola Montez with Jessica Reyes Padilla third. Gayle rose from
seventh in 2023 and fifth in 2024 to take the vacant title (Fitness
Volt; Generation Iron; Depor). That progression is itself a data point
on current standards: the winning package was the most complete V-taper
with the cleanest, not the hardest, conditioning.

## 2. Top coaches in the world for Figure

Figure has a smaller dedicated coaching pool than Bikini. The names
below are sourced to specific athletes and credentials.

### Hany Rambod (FST-7, "The Pro Creator")
Creator of the FST-7 system (Fascia Stretch Training, seven sets).
Credited across his roster with 25-plus Olympia titles (Evogen
Nutrition; hanyrambod.com). In Figure he coached Cydney Gillon to
multiple Figure Olympia titles, including the 2022 win, and they
publicly trained FST-7 back work together (Evogen Nutrition). He is the
most decorated coach currently attached to a modern Figure champion.

### Kim Oddo (Body By O)
Over 25 years in the industry. Body By O claims more NPC and IFBB Pro
League wins than any other coach, 5 Olympia titles and 7 Arnold wins,
350-plus athletes turned pro and 100-plus Olympia qualifiers
(bodybyo.com). In Figure his most notable athlete is Nicole Wilkins,
the four-time Figure Olympia champion, who worked with him for over ten
years (Muscle Insider). Oddo is the reference point for the classic
Figure build of the 2010s.

### Nicole Wilkins
Four-time Figure Olympia champion, now coaching women through
nicolewilkins.com. A working coach who codifies what she did as an
athlete: dumbbell and barbell priority for shoulders, back and legs, a
push/pull/legs structure, and a heavy emphasis on form and
mind-muscle connection drawn from her gymnastics background (Muscle
Insider; nicolewilkins.com). Included because a recent multi-title
champion now coaching the division is rare and high-signal.

### Erin Stern
Two-time Figure Olympia champion (2010, 2012), now an online coach and
one of the most prolific public educators on V-taper construction
(erinstern.com; BarBend; Fitness Volt). Largely self-taught as an
athlete. Her published method is specific and repeatable: high-incline
shoulder press at 75 to 80 degrees, incline lateral raises to kill
momentum, paused-rep overhand lat pulldowns for lat engagement, and
deliberate mind-muscle work on non-mirror back muscles (BarBend;
Fitness Volt).

### Adam Bonilla (Team Elite Physique)
Founded Team Elite Physique in 2000 around evidence-based exercise and
nutrition science. The team holds multiple world-level titles and is
described as the most IFBB Pro wins of any team over the last five
years, primarily in Bikini but coaching across Bikini, Figure, Wellness
and Men's Physique (teamelitephysique.com). Included as the current
high-volume evidence-based team operating in the women's divisions.

Honourable mention for Rhea Gayle's 2025 title team: posing via The
Posing Portal, personal training via AFS Premier, prep coaching via Fit
Body Fusion (The Posing Portal; reported via search). These are less
internationally established names than the five above but they produced
the reigning Figure Olympia champion, so they belong on a current radar.

## 3. Programming philosophy

The published philosophies converge on a few points.

Volume and frequency. The best general-population and physique evidence
puts effective weekly volume around 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle group,
delivered across two sessions per week rather than one, which fits an
upper/lower or push/pull/legs structure (Hevy App summary; EliteFTS
off-season back). Priority muscles for Figure (shoulders, back) are
trained 2 to 3 times per week (search synthesis of the volume
literature). Wilkins runs an explicit push/pull/legs split, which lands
each major group roughly twice per week (Muscle Insider).

Exercise selection. Free weights anchor the priority work. Wilkins:
"dumbbells and barbells over everything for major muscle groups,
especially shoulders, back and legs" (Muscle Insider). Stern builds the
taper from incline pressing and incline lateral raises plus paused
pulldowns, choosing variations that remove momentum so the target
muscle does the work (BarBend; Fitness Volt). Rambod's FST-7 adds a
high-rep finishing block (seven sets, short rest) on a single
isolation movement to pump and stretch the fascia after the heavy work
(Evogen Nutrition).

Load. Heavy, controlled compound work for structural size, then lighter
controlled isolation and finishers where form and mind-muscle
connection matter more than load. Stern explicitly recommends lighter
weight on laterals to control the eccentric, and pause reps to feel the
lats (Fitness Volt; BarBend).

Progression. The published material is principle-led rather than
percentage-led: add load or reps on the compounds while holding form,
use pauses and partials to keep tension when load stalls, and keep the
mind-muscle connection as the quality gate (Stern, via Fitness Volt;
BarBend). [synthesis] In practice that reads as double progression on
priority compounds (add reps to the top of a range, then add load and
drop back to the bottom) with isolation and FST-7 finishers progressed
mainly by reps and density rather than load.

## 4. Muscle-group priority ranking and why

Figure is an upper-body symmetry sport built on the X-frame. Priority,
highest first:

1. Side and rear deltoids. The width at the top of the V. Capped,
   round delts set the shoulder-to-waist ratio that the division is
   built on (IFBB criteria via search; Stern's V-taper work, Fitness
   Volt). Side delts widen the silhouette, rear delts give the back
   thickness and detail seen on the rear quarter turn.
2. Lats and teres (back width). The other half of the V, and the rear
   quarter turn is where the division is decided (NPC News Online;
   Stern's back work, BarBend).
3. Mid and upper back detail (rhomboids, traps lower fibres, erectors
   for the Christmas-tree). Adds the dense, separated look of the rear
   pose without over-thickening the waist.
4. Glutes and glute-ham tie-in. Judges scrutinise the tie-in on the
   rear pose (Team USA Physique; Bret Contreras). Round, full glutes
   that connect cleanly into the hamstring complete the rear line.
5. Hamstrings. Built proximally so the tie-in curves rather than
   blends flatly into the glute (Team USA Physique).
6. Quads. Developed for balance and shape, not for mass that overpowers
   the upper body.
7. Waist and abs. Trained for tightness, never for thickness, because
   a blocky midsection erases the taper.

How this differs from Bikini's lower-body lean. Bikini is glute-and-
hamstring-led with a soft, much less muscular upper body and the
shoulders kept rounded rather than capped and separated. Figure flips
the centre of gravity to the upper body: the shoulders and back are the
scoring muscles, and they are carried with real, visible muscle and
separation that would be over-built for Bikini. Bikini wants a soft
taper, Figure wants a structural one (Julie Lohre division comparison,
referenced via search; Bodybuilding Wizard; Clean Health).

## 5. Weekly volume per muscle group

The strongest current data source is the 2025 Frontiers in Sports and
Active Living survey by Beraldo da Silveira, de Almeida, Carvas Junior
and colleagues, which quantified weekly training volume per muscle
group across 154 competitive physique athletes from multiple
federations and categories, off-season versus pre-contest (Frontiers,
DOI 10.3389/fspor.2025.1536360, 2025). The headline finding relevant
here: most categories reduced volume for most muscle groups going into
pre-contest, while frequency for some groups held and cardio rose. The
full per-group female-category breakdown sits behind the article body,
which would not load for direct quotation here, so the category-specific
set counts below are flagged as synthesis built on that study's
direction plus the 10 to 20 set guideline.

General target band (sourced): roughly 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle
group per week is effective when split across two sessions (Hevy App
summary of the volume literature; EliteFTS).

Figure-specific weekly set targets [synthesis], priority-weighted:

| Muscle group        | Off-season sets/wk | Prep sets/wk |
|---------------------|--------------------|--------------|
| Side/rear delts     | 16 to 22           | 12 to 16     |
| Lats / back width   | 14 to 20           | 10 to 16     |
| Mid/upper back detail | 8 to 12          | 6 to 10      |
| Glutes              | 12 to 18           | 9 to 14      |
| Hamstrings          | 9 to 14            | 6 to 10      |
| Quads               | 8 to 12            | 6 to 9       |
| Chest (postural/cap)| 6 to 9             | 4 to 8       |
| Arms (biceps/triceps)| 6 to 10           | 5 to 8       |
| Calves              | 8 to 12            | 6 to 10      |
| Abs/waist (tight only)| 4 to 8           | 4 to 8       |

The pattern matches the survey: hold or only slightly cut the priority
upper-body groups in prep, trim the lower-priority groups harder, and
let rising cardio plus the calorie deficit account for the rest of the
fatigue management (Frontiers 2025).

## 6. Exercise selection philosophy

Prioritised, and why:
- Side delts: incline and standing dumbbell lateral raises, cable
  laterals. Stern uses incline laterals specifically to strip out
  momentum and load the eccentric (Fitness Volt). This is the single
  most important isolation in the division.
- Overhead pressing biased to shoulder safety and delt cap: Stern's
  high-incline press at 75 to 80 degrees (Fitness Volt).
- Lat width: wide and overhand pulldowns with paused reps, pull-ups,
  single-arm rows. Stern's overhand paused pulldown is her lat-width
  staple (BarBend). Wilkins anchors back on barbell and dumbbell rows
  (Muscle Insider).
- Rear delts and upper-back detail: face pulls, reverse pec-deck,
  rear-delt rows, to fill the rear quarter turn.
- Glutes and tie-in: hip thrusts as the primary, plus glute-biased back
  extensions, RDLs with a controlled stretch on the proximal hamstring,
  Bulgarian split squats, abductions and kickbacks (Bret Contreras;
  Team USA Physique).
- FST-7 finisher on a priority isolation (e.g. lateral raise or
  pulldown): seven sets, short rest, to pump and stretch the fascia
  after the heavy work (Evogen Nutrition).

De-prioritised or used carefully, and why:
- High-volume direct quad work (heavy leg press, deep front-loaded
  squatting, endless leg extensions). The glute-ham article warns that
  too much leg extension, leg press and leg curl can make the legs grow
  out of proportion and make the glutes look relatively smaller (Team
  USA Physique). For Figure that also threatens the upper-body-led
  balance.
- Heavy direct oblique and weighted side-bend work, which thickens the
  waist and kills the taper.
- Anything where momentum lets a bigger muscle steal the work from the
  target. Stern's whole selection logic is built on removing momentum
  (Fitness Volt).

## 7. How Figure programming differs from Bikini and from Women's Physique

Versus Bikini:
- Far more upper-body volume and intent. Figure trains shoulders and
  back as scoring muscles with real load and direct frequency 2 to 3
  times per week. Bikini keeps the upper body soft and rounded and
  spends most of its programming budget on glutes and hamstrings.
- Conditioning ceiling is higher. Figure shows visible separation;
  Bikini stays softer with no separation expected.
- Figure carries more total muscle. A competitive Figure physique would
  be over-muscled and marked down in Bikini (Bodybuilding Wizard; Clean
  Health; Julie Lohre comparison via search).

Versus Women's Physique:
- Conditioning ceiling is lower, and this is the key line not to cross.
  Women's Physique shows muscle density, clear separation and acceptable
  striations; Figure stops at moderate muscle with only a small amount
  of striation, and judges actively penalise WP-style deep musculature
  on a Figure stage (NPC News Online; Bodybuilding Wizard).
- Presentation differs structurally. Women's Physique performs an
  individual posing routine with bodybuilding-style mandatories. Figure
  does quarter turns only, no posing routine in the scored round (NPC
  News Online).
- So a Figure athlete who keeps building muscle eventually has to either
  hold back conditioning or cross up to Women's Physique. The training
  is similar in shape; the divergence is how lean and how dense you take
  the final package, and how you present it.

## 8. Common Figure weak points and how the best coaches address each

- Back width / weak V-taper. The most common scoring gap. Stern's fix
  is paused overhand pulldowns plus deliberate mind-muscle work on the
  lats, on the logic that the back is a non-mirror muscle most people
  cannot feel (BarBend; Fitness Volt). Rambod adds an FST-7 back
  finisher to drive blood and stretch into the lats after heavy rows
  (Evogen Nutrition).
- Flat or capless side delts. Stern's incline laterals and 75 to 80
  degree incline press remove momentum so the medial and rear delt do
  the work (Fitness Volt). Treat side delts as a priority isolated
  group, not an afterthought of pressing.
- Weak glute-ham tie-in. Build the glutes with hip thrusts and
  glute-biased back extensions, and build the proximal hamstring with
  controlled-stretch RDLs so the tie-in curves rather than blends (Bret
  Contreras; Team USA Physique). Warning from the same source: do not
  over-build the legs with leg press, extensions and curls or the
  glutes shrink by comparison (Team USA Physique).
- Thick or blocky waist. Stop weighted oblique and heavy side work,
  train abs for tightness only, and keep the upper-body width growing so
  the waist reads smaller by proportion [synthesis from the division
  criteria above].
- Over-conditioning into a Women's Physique look. A coaching judgement
  call in the last weeks: pull conditioning back to moderate separation
  rather than chasing striation, because in Figure the harder look loses
  (NPC News Online). The 2025 Olympia outcome supports erring on the
  side of full and balanced over stripped and hard (Fitness Volt).

## 9. Recovery management specific to this division

The general physique-sport recovery evidence applies, with Figure-
specific notes.

Deloading. The 2024 cross-sectional survey by Bell, Helms and
colleagues found physique and strength athletes deload for energy and
fatigue management, typically 6.4 plus or minus 1.7 days, integrated
roughly every 5.6 plus or minus 2.3 weeks, using a pre-planned or
autoregulated trigger when performance stalls or soreness and joint
aches climb (Deloading Practices in Strength and Physique Sports,
Sports Medicine Open, 2024). For Figure, plan the deload around the
priority upper-body groups, since they carry the most weekly volume and
fatigue.

Hormonal and mood cost of prep. The 2024 study on hormonal profiles
during competition preparation (Helms et al., European Journal of
Applied Physiology, 2024) documented cortisol rising and
testosterone falling through prep in the studied athletes, with mood
disturbance and fatigue increasing, all restored after competition once
energy availability returned. The practical reading for a Figure prep:
the back-half of prep is where recoverable volume drops, so the survey
pattern of cutting non-priority volume and adding cardio (Frontiers
2025) is recovery management, not just conditioning work.

Post-competition. Helms et al. argue for a planned recovery phase:
gradual nutritional transition (reverse rather than a binge),
psychoeducation, and mental-health-informed coaching, because the
hormonal and mood changes only resolve when energy availability is
restored (PMC, "The harder the prep, the harder the recovery", 2025;
Helms et al. 2024). For a division where athletes often hold a long
off-season to build the upper body, a clean reverse straight into a
growth phase is the logical bridge.

Practical Figure-specific recovery levers [synthesis]:
- Protect sleep first, because the priority muscles are trained at high
  frequency and pressing volume taxes the shoulders and elbows.
- Manage shoulder and elbow joint load: the heavy pressing and lateral
  volume that builds the cap is also the most common overuse site, so
  rotate pressing angles and cap finisher volume during deloads.
- Use cardio as the prep fatigue valve rather than slashing the priority
  resistance volume, matching the survey pattern (Frontiers 2025).
