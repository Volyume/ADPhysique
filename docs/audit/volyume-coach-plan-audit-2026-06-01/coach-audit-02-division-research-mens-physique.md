# Men's Physique division research

Scope note: this document treats Men's Physique on its own terms. It is not
a smaller Open division, and it is not Classic Physique with shorts. The
judging contract is different and the programming follows from that contract,
not from general "build all muscle" logic. Where a number is documented by a
named source it is cited inline. Where a number is inferred it is tagged
[synthesis].

---

## 1. The aesthetic standard and current IFBB Pro / NPC judging

### What the rulebook rewards

The IFBB Pro League Men's Physique judging criteria state the core target in
plain language: a "small waist, wide shoulders to give V taper in front pose"
plus "thickness in back and wide lat spread" (IFBB Pro League, Men's Physique
Judging Criteria PDF). The division is judged on an athletic, aesthetic upper
body shown in board shorts, with the lower body hidden by design. Judges
score deltoids, chest, arms, back width and a tight, conditioned midsection.
The look is described across the rulebook and prep-coach summaries as
athletic and healthy, abs visible without deep striations, not the dry,
peeled, dehydrated extreme of Open bodybuilding (Prep Coach UK, "The Men's
Physique Criteria"; 12 Weeks Out, NPC judging criteria).

Posing is its own scored element. Men's Physique uses quarter turns and
relaxed posing with hands-on-hips presentation, not the compulsory muscular
poses of bodybuilding. There is no most-muscular, no rear lat spread held the
same way, no leg-and-calf comparison. The presentation rewards flow, stage
presence and the V-taper illusion from the front and back, which is why the
shoulder and lat development is judged so heavily: those are the muscles the
quarter-turn presentation actually shows.

### Board shorts rule (this is a programming constraint, not a costume note)

Board shorts must sit roughly one inch below the navel and extend to about
two inches above the kneecap, tapered, manufacturer logo only (IFBB Pro
League criteria PDF). The practical consequence: quadriceps sweep, hamstring
detail and glute-ham tie-ins are not visible and are not scored. This is the
single biggest reason Men's Physique programming diverges from every other
division. The shorts define what gets rewarded.

### The targeted shoulder-to-waist ratio

The aesthetic target is the "golden ratio" of roughly 1.618:1 shoulder
circumference to waist (the figure most coaches and physique-assessment
writers quote for the idealised V-taper) [synthesis on the exact figure: the
1.618 number is the commonly cited golden-ratio target across physique
writing rather than a number printed in the IFBB rulebook, which specifies
the qualitative "small waist, wide shoulders" standard]. The ratio is won
from both ends at once:

- Widen the numerator: lateral deltoid and lat/back width, plus the upper-back
  spread that frames the taper.
- Hold the denominator down: keep the waist as narrow and tight as the
  athlete's structure allows, which means not adding mass to the obliques and
  serratus, and bringing the midsection in hard in prep.

Programming achieves this by loading the muscles that widen the top of the
frame and deliberately not loading the muscles that thicken the middle. See
sections 4 and 6.

### What is marked down

- A blocky or thick waist, which kills the taper illusion even on a wide
  athlete (T Nation, "Avoid These Waist-Widening Exercises").
- Too much size. Men's Physique that drifts toward Classic Physique mass is
  routinely placed lower. Judges have publicly pushed competitors to come in
  smaller and tighter rather than bigger.
- Over-conditioning to the dry, flat, stringy bodybuilding look. The division
  wants fullness and an athletic surface, not deep striations (Prep Coach UK).
- Weak rear shots: a narrow or flat back undercuts the taper from behind.

## 2. Top 5 coaches in the world for Men's Physique (named, with credentials)

This is a working top five for the division as of mid-2026, chosen for direct
Men's Physique results and documented method, not general fame.

1. **Hany Rambod ("The Pro Creator").** Creator of FST-7 (Fascial Stretch
   Training, seven sets). Coached Jeremy Buendia to four Men's Physique
   Olympia titles (2014, 2015, 2016, 2017) and has the widest Olympia haul of
   any coach across divisions (Hany Rambod official site; BarBend, FST-7
   explainer; BarBend, "How Hany Rambod Constructs Wide Bodybuilder Backs").

2. **Neil Hill ("Yoda").** Creator of Y3T (Yoda Three Training). Coached
   three-time Men's Physique Olympia champion Brandon Hendrickson, plus open
   and 212 athletes including William Bonac and Big Ramy at points
   (BarBend, "Y3T Training Explained"; The Barbell, Y3T guide).

3. **Matt Jansen (Camp Jansen).** Coached a long run of IFBB pros to the
   Olympia across divisions; documented as the coach behind Nick Walker's
   2021 New York Pro and Arnold wins and a roster including Shaun Clarida,
   Charles Griffen and Brett Wilkin before stepping back. His stated method
   is volume and frequency set against the individual's structure and weak
   points (Camp Jansen site; Fitness Volt, "Best Bodybuilding Coaches";
   Hawk Fit, "Matt Jansen Champion Mentality"). Reported as having retired/
   stepped back, with several athletes (e.g. Brett Wilkin) moving to Stefan
   Kienzl (BarBend, "Brett Wilkin New Coach Stefan Kienzl").

4. **Shaun Clarida (Giant Killer / coach).** Former 212 Olympia champion who
   now coaches a stable of physique and bodybuilding athletes through his own
   business while competing. His value to a Men's Physique audit is the
   structure-first, "shortest man in the class" approach to building width and
   illusion on a small frame, and his documented training under the late John
   Meadows and now John Jewett (Muscle & Strength interview; BarBend,
   "Shaun Clarida New Training Coach John Jewett"; giantkiller212.com).

5. **Milos Sarcev ("The Mind").** Creator of the giant-set / Hyperemia
   Advantage system and an early advocate of intra-workout nutrition. He has
   advised 100-plus IFBB pros with 36 Olympia qualifiers across his career.
   Included here for method (high-volume giant sets, blood-volume training)
   rather than a current Men's Physique title, since his roster skews open and
   212 (Fitness Volt, Milos Sarcev profile; JYM Supplement Science, Hyperemia
   Advantage articles).

Two more names that belong in any honest Men's Physique list:

- **Chris Aceto ("The Technician").** The nutrition and contest-prep coach
  behind a generation of pros (Jay Cutler among them). His carb-cycling and
  fullness-preserving peak-week method is the template much of physique prep
  still runs on, including the "stay full, do not flatten out" principle that
  Men's Physique depends on (Old School Labs interview; Fitness Volt, Aceto
  on Jay Cutler's prep).
- **Stefan Kienzl** and **John Jewett**, the current-generation coaches now
  holding pieces of the roster Jansen and Meadows used to hold (BarBend on
  Wilkin and on Clarida).

The reigning athlete reference point: **Ryan Terry** won the Men's Physique
Olympia in 2023, 2024 and 2025 and has stated 2026 plans to chase a fourth
(Fitness Volt, "2025 Men's Physique Olympia Results"; BodyBuildingBros, Ryan
Terry 2026 plans). The 2025 Olympia top finishers behind Terry included Ali
Bilal, Brandon Hendrickson and Erin Banks (Fitness Volt, 2025 results;
Generation Iron, 2025 Men's Physique results).

## 3. Programming philosophy by coach

**Hany Rambod, FST-7.** The body-part session runs a normal heavy-to-moderate
block of 3 to 4 exercises in the 8 to 12 rep range, then finishes with the
signature 7 sets of 8 to 15 reps on an isolation movement with roughly 45
seconds rest between the seven sets (BarBend FST-7; Muscle & Brawn FST-7
guide). Rambod sequences heavy compound first (about 3 x 6 to 8, long rest),
then moderate (3 x 8 to 12), then the FST-7 finisher light and pumped. He
caps FST-7 finishers for beginners at 2 per week and treats the system as
advanced-only. His back work prioritises mind-muscle connection and quality
reps in the 10 to 15 range over chasing load (BarBend, Rambod backs).

**Neil Hill, Y3T.** Three-week rolling cycles. Week 1 heavy compounds at 6 to
10 reps, Week 2 compound plus isolation at 10 to 15, Week 3 very high rep
(20-plus) with drop sets and rest-pause (BarBend Y3T; The Barbell Y3T guide).
The logic is to hit every fibre type and connective tissue across the cycle
rather than living in one rep range, which suits a physique athlete who needs
both density and that pumped, full week-3 look.

**Matt Jansen.** Cyclical, individualised volume and frequency set against the
athlete's current physique and structural weak points, rather than a fixed
template. The published positioning is assessment-driven program design that
works around or directly into structural issues (Camp Jansen; Fitness Volt).

**Shaun Clarida.** High-volume, high-effort, blood-volume training: 6 to 8
exercises per body part, about 4 to 5 sets each, rep ranges from roughly 6 up
to 20, one body part per day, sessions running long, the explicit goal being
to fill the muscle with as much blood as possible (Muscle & Strength
interview). He moved to bringing up lagging parts (chest and back) by adding
a second weekly session for each under John Meadows (Mountain Dog interview).

**Milos Sarcev.** Giant sets: four or more exercises back to back with little
to no rest, high frequency, short rest periods, high carbohydrate and
intra-workout feeding to keep training the same muscle for extended hyperemia
(Fitness Volt; JYM Supplement Science). High systemic and local demand,
explicitly advanced.

**Chris Aceto.** Nutrition-led: carb cycling across the whole prep rather than
a late keto swing, tuned to hold muscle fullness into peak week. This is the
fullness principle Men's Physique relies on so athletes do not flatten and
lose the athletic surface judges want (Old School Labs; Fitness Volt).

## 4. Muscle-group priority ranking for Men's Physique

This ranking follows directly from section 1: judges score the V-taper in
board shorts via quarter turns.

**Primary (the differentiators that win the class):**

- **Lateral deltoids.** The single highest-leverage muscle. Lateral delt
  width is the top of the V and the most direct way to widen the numerator of
  the shoulder-to-waist ratio. Buendia's off-season shoulder work under Rambod
  was built around heavy lateral-delt volume (BarBend, Buendia 10-exercise
  shoulder programming).
- **Lats / back width.** The other half of the taper, and the muscle that
  makes the rear quarter-turn read wide. Rambod's documented back priority is
  width-first, quality reps over load (BarBend, Rambod backs).
- **Upper chest.** Frames the front V and gives the relaxed front pose its
  shelf. Clarida specifically brought chest up as a lagging part with added
  frequency (Mountain Dog interview).

**Secondary (support the look, scored but not decisive):**

- Arms (the shorts show the arm in every pose, so reasonable arm size matters,
  but mass is not rewarded the way delts and back are).
- Mid and lower traps / rhombids for the rear taper and posture.
- Abs and a tight transverse abdominis: judged, but trained for definition and
  tightness, not thickness (see below).

**De-emphasised (built for health and balance, not for the scorecard):**

- **Legs.** Hidden by board shorts and not scored. Trained enough for
  structural balance, joint health and not looking visibly skipped from the
  side, but never prioritised at the expense of recovery for the upper body.
  Clarida's own note that "lower body was a weak point" applies to 212/Open
  judging, not Men's Physique, and underlines that legs simply are not the
  Men's Physique battleground.

**Actively harmful if overdeveloped:**

- **Obliques and waist musculature.** Hypertrophied obliques grow outward and
  widen the waist, which destroys the taper illusion (T Nation; mofilo,
  oblique training and blocky waist). This is the clearest case in any
  division where building a muscle is a mistake. See section 6 for how the
  best coaches handle core work without thickening the waist.

## 5. Weekly volume per muscle group (off-season vs prep)

Anchor for the science: Schoenfeld and colleagues' dose-response work found
10-plus sets per muscle per week beat under 5 sets, with gains rising from
under 5, to 5 to 9, to 10-plus, and each added set associated with roughly a
0.37% greater gain (Schoenfeld et al. 2017, dose-response meta-analysis;
2024-25 dose-response meta-regression updates on SportRxiv). That establishes
the floor and the trend, not a hard ceiling.

The table below is the division-specific synthesis. The structure
(primary muscles get more, de-emphasised get a maintenance floor, the waist
gets near-zero hypertrophy work) is documented by the coaching above. The
exact set numbers are [synthesis] mapped onto the 10-plus baseline.

| Muscle group        | Off-season sets/wk | Prep sets/wk | Notes |
|---------------------|--------------------|--------------|-------|
| Lateral deltoids    | 16 to 22 [synthesis] | 12 to 18 [synthesis] | Highest-priority. Often hit 2 to 3x/wk. |
| Rear/front delts    | 8 to 12 [synthesis]  | 6 to 10 [synthesis]  | Rear for the back taper; front gets indirect work from pressing. |
| Lats / back width   | 14 to 20 [synthesis] | 10 to 16 [synthesis] | Width bias (wide-grip, straight-arm work). |
| Back thickness      | 8 to 12 [synthesis]  | 6 to 10 [synthesis]  | Supports rear taper, secondary to width. |
| Upper chest         | 10 to 16 [synthesis] | 8 to 12 [synthesis]  | Incline-biased. |
| Arms (bi + tri)     | 10 to 14 each [synthesis] | 8 to 12 each [synthesis] | Visible in every pose. |
| Quads / hams        | 8 to 12 [synthesis]  | 6 to 10 [synthesis]  | Maintenance and health, not scored. |
| Calves              | 6 to 10 [synthesis]  | 6 to 10 [synthesis]  | Optional; not scored under shorts. |
| Direct waist/oblique| ~0 to 4 [synthesis]  | ~0 to 4 [synthesis]  | Definition and bracing only, no loaded growth work. |

Off-season vs prep, the documented pattern: total volume stays similar or
drops slightly into prep while intensity techniques and pump work (FST-7,
Y3T week 3, Clarida's blood-volume sessions) carry the look as calories fall.
Volume comes down in prep mainly to protect recovery in a deficit, not because
the muscle priorities change. The priorities do not change between phases.

## 6. Exercise selection philosophy

**Prioritised:**

- **Lateral-delt isolation, lots of it.** Cable and dumbbell lateral raises,
  cable Y-raises, machine laterals, often run as the FST-7 finisher or as
  high-rep partial-and-full work. This is the most-trained pattern in the
  division because it widens the frame with no waist cost (BarBend, Buendia
  shoulders; FST-7 shoulders guides).
- **Width-biased back work.** Wide and neutral-grip pulldowns, straight-arm
  pulldowns, chest-supported rows aimed at the upper-outer lat, all chosen to
  build the spread the rear quarter-turn shows (BarBend, Rambod backs).
- **Incline pressing and upper-chest flyes** for the front-pose shelf.
- **Chest-supported and cable movements** generally, to keep tension on the
  target and reduce systemic fatigue and lower-back loading.

**Avoided or minimised:**

- **Heavy direct oblique work.** Loaded side bends, heavy weighted Russian
  twists, heavy cable side crunches. These hypertrophy the obliques outward
  and widen the waist, which is the one thing the division cannot afford
  (T Nation; mofilo). The waist is trained for tightness and bracing
  (vacuums, planks, anti-rotation, light high-rep ab work), not for size.
- **Heavy axial-loaded compounds that thicken the trunk and erectors** beyond
  what posture needs: very heavy conventional deadlifts, heavy barbell
  back-squat maximal work, and standing heavy overhead barbell pressing get
  dialled back or swapped for machine and cable equivalents that hit the
  target muscle without growing the waist or risking the recovery budget.
  [synthesis on the mechanism: the avoidance is widely advised in physique
  prep writing; the specific lift swaps are coach-by-coach judgement rather
  than a single cited prescription.]
- **Excess heavy leg pressing/squatting** in the sense of chasing leg size,
  since it is not scored and it taxes recovery that the delts and back need.

The throughline: pick exercises that widen the top of the frame or hold the
waist tight, drop or lighten exercises that thicken the middle or burn
recovery on muscles the shorts hide.

## 7. Practices specific to Men's Physique vs general hypertrophy

- **Train the ratio, not just the muscles.** General hypertrophy says build
  everything. Men's Physique says build the numerator (delts, lats, upper
  chest) and protect the denominator (waist). A bigger total physique can
  score worse if the waist came with it.
- **Deliberately not maximising a muscle (obliques) and a region (legs).**
  This is alien to general hypertrophy programming, which would never tell you
  to leave growth on the table. Here it is the correct call.
- **Fullness over extreme dryness in peak week.** Aceto-style carb cycling to
  hold fullness, because the division wants an athletic surface, not the
  stripped, flat look of Open prep (Old School Labs; Fitness Volt).
- **Posing and presentation are scored skills,** so quarter-turn practice and
  relaxed-pose conditioning of the upper body are part of the program, not an
  afterthought. The poses dictate that delts and back width get the priority.
- **Conditioning ceiling, not floor.** Athletes are coached to stop short of
  the deepest conditioning rather than push past it, because over-dry placings
  lower in this division.

## 8. Common Men's Physique weak points and how the best coaches address each

- **Flat or narrow lateral delts (the most common limiter).** Address with
  high weekly lateral-raise volume, multiple angles (cable, dumbbell, machine)
  and frequency 2 to 3x/wk, often using FST-7-style finishers to drive blood
  and metabolic stress into the medial delt (Rambod method; BarBend, Buendia
  shoulders).
- **Narrow back / weak rear quarter-turn.** Width-first selection (wide and
  straight-arm pulldowns), quality reps over load, and adding a second weekly
  back session, the exact move Clarida used to bring his back up under Meadows
  (Mountain Dog interview; BarBend, Rambod backs).
- **Lagging upper chest / flat front pose.** Incline bias and added frequency,
  again the Clarida second-session approach for a lagging part.
- **Thick or blocky waist.** Stop loaded oblique work, train the deep core for
  bracing and the vacuum for control, and tighten through diet. This is a
  selection-and-conditioning fix, not a "train it harder" fix (T Nation;
  mofilo).
- **Small-frame athletes who look narrow.** Clarida's whole career is the
  template: maximise delt and lat width and keep the waist tiny so the
  illusion beats the taller, heavier competitor (Muscle & Strength; Mountain
  Dog).
- **Flat, stringy prep look.** Aceto-style carb cycling to keep the muscle
  full into the show (Old School Labs).

## 9. Recovery management specific to this division

- **Recovery is spent where it scores.** Because legs and waist are not
  priorities, the recovery budget is steered into the delts, back and chest.
  Pulling back heavy squatting and heavy deadlifting is partly a recovery
  decision, not only an aesthetic one: it frees systemic recovery for the
  upper-body volume that wins (consistent across the high-volume methods of
  Rambod, Hill and Clarida).
- **Frequency on lateral delts is only sustainable with managed local load.**
  Lateral raises at 2 to 3x/wk work because they are low-axial, high-rep,
  pump-style work rather than heavy pressing every session [synthesis on the
  recovery mechanism; the high frequency itself is documented in Buendia's
  programming].
- **Periodised intensity to manage fatigue.** Y3T's three-week wave is an
  explicit fatigue-management tool: the heavy week, moderate week and very
  high-rep week stop the athlete grinding the same heavy stimulus into a hole
  (BarBend Y3T; The Barbell).
- **Intra-workout feeding for high-volume sessions.** Sarcev's long giant-set
  sessions are only repeatable with intra-workout carbohydrate and the
  surrounding feeding strategy; this is a recovery-within-the-session tool as
  much as a growth one (Fitness Volt; JYM Supplement Science).
- **Prep recovery.** Volume comes down in a calorie deficit to protect
  recovery while the look is carried by pump and conditioning work, and
  fullness is protected nutritionally (Aceto carb cycling). The priorities do
  not change in prep, the recovery margin shrinks, so volume is the lever that
  gives.
