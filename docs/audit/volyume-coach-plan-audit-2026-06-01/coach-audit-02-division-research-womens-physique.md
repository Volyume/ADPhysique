# Coach Plan Audit 02: Division Research, Women's Physique

Status: COMPLETE | Timestamp: 2026-06-01 | Division: Women's Physique

This document is built from live web research conducted in June 2026. Every
coaching claim is attributed to a named coach, athlete, or federation
document. Science claims point to a named study or researcher. Numbers that
are documented are cited to source. Numbers that are reasoned from documented
inputs are tagged [synthesis] and are not presented as fact.

A note on scope. Women's Physique (WPD) sits between Figure and Women's
Bodybuilding. It is its own division with its own line. The whole document
treats it that way. Where it borrows from the science of female hypertrophy or
from coaches who also work Figure and Bodybuilding, that crossover is flagged,
not hidden.

---

## 1. The aesthetic standard and current judging

### What the federations actually ask for

The NPC and IFBB created Women's Physique as a division with less emphasis on
excessive muscle size, density and striated muscularity than Women's
Bodybuilding, and more attention to femininity in symmetry, shape, proportion,
muscle tone, poise and posing (IFBB, NPC division description). Judges first
assess the overall athletic appearance of the physique, taking in hair and
make-up, the overall athletic development of the musculature, a balanced and
symmetrically developed physique, skin condition and tone, and the athlete's
ability to present herself with confidence, poise and grace (IFBB Women's
Physique rules; IFBB WA rules).

The muscularity instruction is specific. Muscle groups should have a round and
firm appearance with a small amount of body fat. The physique should be
neither excessively muscular nor excessively lean, and should be free from
deep muscle separation. A physique judged either too muscular or too lean is
marked down (IFBB Women's Physique rules). Competitors are explicitly advised
against being excessively "ripped" or "shredded" (federation guidance summarised
across NPC and IFBB rule documents).

### Round structure

Athletes are brought out by height class and taken through quarter turns so
the physique is seen from front, both sides and back. There is a round of
mandatory poses, and an individual posing routine to music of up to one minute
with a ten-second warning (USBF Women's Physique; NPC rules; IFBB WA). All
poses are done with hands open, not in fists as in Bodybuilding. That open-hand
rule is the visible signature of the division (USBF; NPC).

### What wins, what is marked down

What wins, drawn from the 2025 Olympia prejudging analysis (Generation Iron)
and the rule documents:

- Full-body muscular development that reads as complete from every angle, with
  no lagging area that breaks the line.
- Conditioning that shows shape and some separation but stops short of the
  dry, striated, flat look of Women's Bodybuilding. Round and firm, not paper
  thin.
- A waist that stays small relative to shoulders and back, so the V-taper and
  the feminine line both hold.
- Posing that is feminine in flow, open hands, controlled.

What is marked down:

- Too much size or too much grain. Crossing into a Bodybuilding look loses in
  this division.
- Flat or stringy conditioning. Over-dieting that strips roundness is penalised
  the same as being soft.
- An imbalance, most often a back or shoulder that does not match the legs, or
  legs that do not match the upper body.
- A 2025 Olympia talking point: Sarah Villegas publicly accused the judging of
  rewarding excessive oil and a harder, drier look on champion Natalia Abraham
  Coelho, calling her the "OIL-ympia champion" (Fitness Volt). That dispute is
  a live signal that the muscularity-versus-femininity line is contested at the
  very top and that judges in 2025 leaned toward the fuller, harder package.

## 2. Top coaches in the world for Women's Physique

Women's Physique is a small division. Few coaches specialise in it alone, so
the leading names are either the athletes themselves coaching others, or
crossover physique coaches whose roster reaches into WPD. Listed by current
relevance.

### 1. Sarah Villegas (self-coached athlete and coach, works with @thefitnesspope)

Four-time Women's Physique Olympia champion (2019, 2021, 2023, 2024) and the
all-time winningest Ms. Physique Olympia, runner-up in 2025 (BarBend; Fitness
Volt). Personal Training Director at AFS Premier Fitness in Dallas. She trains
under a coach she credits on Instagram as @thefitnesspope and also coaches
clients in fat loss, muscle building and prep (AFS Premier). Her value as a
reference is her own physique: she set the standard the division was judged
against for half a decade.

### 2. Natalia Abraham Coelho (athlete and prep coach)

Reigning two-time Women's Physique Olympia champion (2021, regained 2025) and
two-time Arnold Classic Physique International winner (Fitness Volt; BarBend).
Doctor of Natural Medicine, personal trainer, fitness nutritionist and online
prep coach. She trains around bringing up lagging areas, currently running
upper back and shoulders twice a week, legs twice a week, and everything else
once a week, and changes the split as a body part needs attention (MPA Supps
team profile; STSFit profile). She is the most directly relevant coaching mind
for the division because she holds the current title and coaches in it.

### 3. Dana Linn Bailey (athlete, content and programming influence)

Won the inaugural Women's Physique Olympia in 2013 (Fitness Volt; BarBend). Her
back-training method is widely copied in the division: mostly compound rows,
a different grip on every exercise, an "high and wide, low and close" approach
that moves from wide grip with flared elbows to narrow grip with tight elbows
to bias upper versus mid and lower back, an 8 to 10 rep working range, heavy
use of drop sets and supersets, and straps so grip is not the limiter
(Total Shape; BarBend). She functions as a programming reference for back and
upper-body detail more than as a one-to-one prep coach.

### 4. Hany Rambod (FST-7, crossover physique coach)

The "Pro Creator". His FST-7 system has produced 25-plus Olympia titles across
divisions including Ms. Figure Olympia (Nicole Wilkins, 4x) (Evogen; Hidden
Gym; alphabodybuilders profile). He has coached female competitors on posing
and on shoulder and back detail (his own published coaching footage). He
announced his retirement from full-time coaching in November 2024, so his
method, FST-7, is now more relevant than his personal roster. His relevance to
WPD is the fascia-stretch, high-pump finishing work that suits the round,
full look the division rewards.

### 5. Pro Physiques (team, multi-division prep)

One of the most decorated US prep teams, with 9 Ms. Olympia and 5 Ms.
International titles, and hundreds of NPC athletes brought to pro cards
(Pro Physiques). The team carries certified IFBB Pro prep coaches across
Figure, Physique and Bodybuilding and runs structured off-season and prep
programming. Relevant as the institutional, deepest-bench option in the women's
muscular divisions rather than a single named coach.

Honourable mentions surfaced in research, all crossover rather than WPD-only:
George Farah (master prep coach, conditioning), Charles Glass ("Godfather of
Bodybuilding", exercise mechanics), and Jason Kozma (Los Angeles, lists Women's
Physique among his coached divisions). [synthesis] These are noted for
completeness; their WPD-specific rosters were not documented in the research.

## 3. Programming philosophy

There is no single published "Women's Physique system". The working philosophy
below is assembled from named athlete-coaches in the division plus the female
hypertrophy literature, with each strand attributed.

### Volume

The strongest peer-reviewed anchor for trained lifters is Schoenfeld, Ogborn
and Krieger (2017, Journal of Sports Sciences), the meta-analysis of 15
controlled trials. It found a dose response: under 5 weekly sets per muscle
averaged 5.4% muscle thickness gain, 5 to 9 sets averaged 6.6%, and 10-plus
sets averaged 9.8%. The practical landing point cited from that work is 10 to
20 hard sets per muscle per week for trained individuals. A 2025 Frontiers in
Sports and Active Living paper quantifying weekly set volume per muscle group
in competitive physique athletes is the most division-relevant source, though
its full text was not retrievable during this research and its numbers are not
quoted here.

### Frequency

Coelho's documented split is the cleanest division example: lagging or
priority areas (upper back, shoulders, legs) trained twice a week, maintenance
areas once a week (MPA Supps; STSFit). That maps to the female hypertrophy
literature, where 2x per week per muscle is the common recommendation to spread
volume and protect quality of sets.

### Exercise selection

Compound-led for the priority areas, isolation to finish and to detail.
Bailey's back work is the model case: compound rows first with grip and elbow
variation to hit every region, isolation and drop sets to finish (Total Shape;
BarBend). Rambod's FST-7 adds high-rep pump sets at the end of a body part to
chase the round, full look.

### Load and rep range

Bailey trains back in the 8 to 10 rep range, fatiguing each exercise before
moving on (BarBend). [synthesis] Across the division a working band of roughly
8 to 15 reps for upper body detail and 8 to 20 for legs and glutes is
consistent with both her practice and the female hypertrophy literature, where
relative gains across rep ranges are similar to men provided sets are taken
close to failure (Schoenfeld et al.). Loads are chosen so the target rep range
ends within 1 to 3 reps in reserve on working sets.

### Progression

Standard progressive overload: add reps within the range, then load, then a
set, with periodic deloads (see Section 9). [synthesis] No WPD-specific
progression model was documented; this is the mainstream physique approach.

## 4. Muscle-group priority ranking and why

Women's Physique is judged as a complete, balanced package from all four
quarter-turn angles, and the back and shoulder mandatories are where the
division is won or lost. Priority order, with reasoning tied to the judging:

1. **Back (width and detail).** The back double biceps and the quarter-turn
   rear view are decisive. Width sells the V-taper that makes the waist read
   small, and detail (mid and lower back) is what separates a placing physique
   from a champion one. Bailey's entire method is built around comprehensive
   back development for exactly this reason (BarBend; Total Shape).
2. **Shoulders (especially rear and side delts).** Capped delts widen the
   frame and feed the V-taper. Rear delts counterbalance the front, prevent a
   rounded look on stage and reinforce the broad-shoulder, small-waist line
   that judges reward (rear-delt competition guidance, The Fitness Maverick;
   Strong Curves). Coelho trains shoulders twice a week (MPA Supps).
3. **Legs (full development, fully judged).** Unlike Bikini and some Figure
   presentations, WPD legs are judged hard, front and back. Quads, hamstrings,
   glutes and a tied-in sweep all count, and a lagging lower body relative to a
   strong upper body is a common reason to lose. Coelho trains legs twice a
   week (MPA Supps).
4. **Glutes and hamstrings as a tie-in.** Required for the feminine line and
   the rear view; female training data shows women already carry higher glute
   volume than general hypertrophy recommendations (female training-pattern
   research summarised alongside Schoenfeld), so the work tends to be present,
   the issue is balancing it against quad sweep.
5. **Arms, chest, abs as supporting detail.** Biceps fill the front and back
   double-biceps mandatories, triceps fill the side triceps pose, abs and the
   serratus tie the front package together. Important, but they support the
   line rather than set the placing.

The governing principle, from the rule books, is balanced complete
development. A single lagging area breaks the symmetry the judges score first,
so the priority list exists to bring up what is behind, not to chase one
showpiece.

## 5. Weekly volume per muscle group

Documented division anchor: Coelho runs upper back, shoulders and legs at 2x
per week and other groups at 1x per week (MPA Supps; STSFit). She does not
publish set counts.

Science anchor: 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle per week for trained lifters,
from Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger (2017).

The table below combines those two. Off-season figures sit in the responsive
10 to 20 band, weighted to the division's priority areas. Prep figures hold or
slightly reduce volume so muscle is retained in a calorie deficit. All set
numbers are tagged [synthesis] because no WPD source publishes per-muscle
weekly set counts; the frequency and the 10 to 20 band are the only documented
inputs.

| Muscle group | Off-season sets/week | Prep sets/week | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back | 16 to 22 [synthesis] | 14 to 18 [synthesis] | 2x (documented for upper back) |
| Shoulders (all heads) | 14 to 20 [synthesis] | 12 to 16 [synthesis] | 2x (documented) |
| Rear delts (subset of above) | 6 to 9 [synthesis] | 5 to 8 [synthesis] | 2x [synthesis] |
| Quads | 12 to 18 [synthesis] | 10 to 14 [synthesis] | 2x (legs documented) |
| Hamstrings | 10 to 14 [synthesis] | 8 to 12 [synthesis] | 2x [synthesis] |
| Glutes | 10 to 16 [synthesis] | 8 to 12 [synthesis] | 2x [synthesis] |
| Chest | 8 to 12 [synthesis] | 6 to 10 [synthesis] | 1x to 2x [synthesis] |
| Biceps | 8 to 12 [synthesis] | 6 to 10 [synthesis] | 1x to 2x [synthesis] |
| Triceps | 8 to 12 [synthesis] | 6 to 10 [synthesis] | 1x to 2x [synthesis] |
| Calves | 8 to 14 [synthesis] | 8 to 12 [synthesis] | 2x to 3x [synthesis] |
| Abs / serratus | 6 to 12 [synthesis] | 6 to 12 [synthesis] | 2x to 3x [synthesis] |

Reasoning, not documented numbers: priority areas (back, shoulders, legs) sit
at the top of the responsive band because they are trained twice a week and
because they set the placing. Maintenance areas sit lower. In prep the goal is
muscle retention in a deficit, so volume is held or trimmed rather than pushed,
and the deficit plus cardio supplies the overload. This matches the deloading
survey finding that prep volume is managed down through fewer reps and sets
while frequency holds (Deloading Practices survey, Sports Medicine Open 2024).

## 6. Exercise selection philosophy

### Prioritised

- **Rows in many grips and angles.** Bailey's core principle: change grip on
  every back exercise, "high and wide, low and close" to bias upper versus mid
  and lower back, straps so grip is not the limiter (Total Shape; BarBend).
- **Vertical pulls (pull-ups, pulldowns).** Width for the V-taper.
- **Rear-delt work (face pulls, reverse pec deck, rear flyes, wide-elbow cable
  rows).** A combination of compound and isolation is the recommended way to
  bring up a lagging rear delt (The Fitness Maverick; Muscle & Strength).
- **Side-delt isolation (lateral raises, cable and machine).** Caps the
  shoulder and widens the frame.
- **Compound leg work (squats, presses, hack squats, RDLs, hip thrusts).** Full
  lower-body development is judged, so the foundation is heavy compound work
  with isolation for sweep and tie-in.
- **FST-7 style high-rep finishers.** Rambod's pump work for the round, full
  look the division rewards.

### Used with care or de-emphasised

- **Anything that over-builds the waist.** Heavy loaded oblique work and heavy
  weighted twisting can thicken the midsection and break the small-waist line.
  [synthesis] This follows directly from the judging emphasis on a small waist
  rather than from a single named source.
- **Movements an athlete cannot feel in the target muscle.** Bailey fatigues
  each exercise with intent on the target before moving on (BarBend); a lift
  that does not produce that connection is dropped.

### What is genuinely avoided

Nothing in the rule set bans an exercise. The selection logic is shaped by the
line: build the back, shoulders and legs, keep the waist tight, and finish for
fullness without crossing into the dry, blocky look that belongs to Women's
Bodybuilding.

## 7. How WPD programming differs from Figure and from Women's Bodybuilding

### Versus Figure

- **More size and more separation.** Figure athletes carry less muscle, want
  separation with no visible striations and a softer, more "toned" look.
  Physique wants more muscle size, visible separation and some striations,
  while still stopping short of shredded (ASFA; 3D Muscle Journey; Clean
  Health; Sheru Classic). [synthesis] In programming terms that means WPD runs
  higher volume on the priority areas and pushes the size of the back and
  shoulders harder than a Figure plan would.
- **Legs judged harder.** Figure presentations often soften the leg emphasis;
  WPD legs are fully judged front and back, so leg volume and intensity are
  higher.
- **Posing.** Figure is quarter turns plus set poses with a focus on
  conditioning and discipline. WPD adds a creative individual routine and the
  open-hand mandatory poses (search summary across federation rule docs).
- **Conditioning band.** Figure is often cited around 8 to 12% body fat,
  Physique around 8 to 10% (3D Muscle Journey; ASFA). Those numbers are
  estimates reported by those sources, not federation rules. Treat them as
  ballpark.

### Versus Women's Bodybuilding

- **Femininity is scored, and excess is penalised.** WPD keeps a feminine line.
  Too much size or too much grain is marked down. Women's Bodybuilding rewards
  maximal mass, density, deep separation and striations with far less emphasis
  on femininity (NPC and IFBB descriptions; division comparison sources).
- **Open hands, not fists.** WPD mandatories are posed with open hands. The
  fisted Bodybuilding poses are a different aesthetic, and this is the clearest
  visible separator (USBF; NPC).
- **Conditioning ceiling.** Bodybuilding goes to the driest, most striated
  finish the athlete can hold. WPD stops at round, firm and some detail. Going
  past that ceiling loses in WPD even though it would win in Bodybuilding. The
  2025 Olympia dispute over Coelho's harder, oilier look (Fitness Volt) is the
  live edge of exactly where that ceiling sits.

In one line: WPD is more than Figure in size and separation, less than
Bodybuilding in mass and dryness, and unlike either in its open-hand posing and
its scored femininity.

## 8. Common weak points and how the best coaches address each

- **Lagging back detail (mid and lower back, not just width).** Addressed by
  Bailey's grip- and angle-variation rowing, "high and wide, low and close",
  with drop sets and supersets to finish the region after the heavy compound
  work (Total Shape; BarBend).
- **Weak rear delts and a rounded-shoulder look.** The most common female
  imbalance, rear delts much weaker than front. Fixed with dedicated rear-delt
  volume combining compound rows with flared elbows and isolation (face pulls,
  rear flyes), 2x a week, with visible change reported in 8 to 12 weeks (The
  Fitness Maverick; Muscle & Strength; Strong Curves).
- **Legs lagging the upper body.** Bring legs to a 2x-a-week priority slot, as
  Coelho does, with heavy compound work plus isolation for sweep and hamstring
  detail (MPA Supps; STSFit).
- **Waist thickening that breaks the line.** Limit heavy loaded oblique and
  twisting work, keep the waist tight so the V-taper reads. [synthesis] Derived
  from the small-waist judging emphasis.
- **Flat or stringy conditioning in prep.** Managed by not over-dieting and by
  protecting training volume so muscle stays round; the deloading literature
  shows prep volume should be trimmed thoughtfully, not crashed (Deloading
  Practices survey, 2024). The division marks down too lean as hard as too soft
  (IFBB rules), so the fix is conditioning discipline, not maximal stripping.

## 9. Recovery management specific to this division

### Deloading

The clearest evidence base is the deloading research in strength and physique
sports. A cross-sectional survey (Deloading Practices in Strength and Physique
Sports, Sports Medicine Open, 2024) found athletes deload on a proactive,
pre-planned basis with a typical deload of 6.4 plus or minus 1.7 days,
integrated roughly every 5.6 plus or minus 2.3 weeks. The method is to cut
both reps per set and sets per week while holding frequency, drop the load and
raise reps in reserve so effort eases (same survey; and the Frontiers 2022
coaches' perceptions paper, "You can't shoot another bullet until you've
reloaded the gun"). The framing from that work: a deload reloads the gun,
enabling adaptation, cutting injury and illness risk, and heading off burnout.

### Sleep

The sleep-and-performance literature recommends 8 to 9 hours during prep and
deload weeks, with extending sleep from 6 to 8 hours linked to faster sprint
times, better accuracy, lower perceived exertion and improved mood (sleep and
athletic performance review, Athletic Edge PT summary). For a WPD athlete in a
deficit, sleep is the lever that protects both recovery and training quality.

### Female-specific recovery risks

This is where WPD recovery is not the same as a male physique athlete's.
Female competitors routinely experience menstrual cycle irregularity or
amenorrhoea during contest prep, which can persist for up to a year
post-competition even after body fat is regained and pre-diet energy
availability is restored (post-competition physiological case series and
related female-prep research, PMC). Low energy availability is the driver, so
recovery management for this division has to watch energy availability and
menstrual function as a primary health marker, not an afterthought.

### Practical recovery model for WPD

[synthesis] Combining the above: a planned deload every 5 to 6 weeks of about
a week, volume cut by trimming sets and reps while frequency holds, 8 to 9
hours sleep defended through prep, and active monitoring of menstrual function
and energy availability as the early-warning system that the deficit or the
volume has gone too far. None of these numbers are WPD-specific in the
literature; they are the strength-and-physique and female-athlete findings
applied to this division.
