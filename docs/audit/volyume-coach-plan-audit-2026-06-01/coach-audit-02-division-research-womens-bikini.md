# Women's Bikini: Division Research and Coaching Methodology

Status: COMPLETE | Timestamp: 2026-06-01 | Division: Women's Bikini

Scope note. This document covers the NPC/IFBB Pro League Bikini division,
the largest women's division by entrant count. Every coaching claim is
attributed to a named coach or published method. Science claims cite the
study or author. Numbers taken from a source are cited inline. Numbers I
have inferred to fill a gap are tagged [synthesis] and should be treated
as a working starting point, not documented fact.

The defining tension of this division: Bikini is the women's division
where "more muscle" stops helping and starts hurting earlier than
anywhere else. The whole craft is building enough shape in two places
(glutes, delts) to score, while deliberately NOT building several other
places past the point where the look turns hard or blocky. The 2025
arrival of the Fit Model division, pitched explicitly as "softer than
Bikini", is the federation's own admission that Bikini itself drifted
more muscular over the 2010s. That context runs through everything below.

---

## 1. Definitive aesthetic standard and current judging

### What the rulebook says

The official NPC Bikini criteria state that judging is on "balanced
physiques displaying proper shape and symmetry combined with overall
condition" and explicitly: "This is not a bodybuilding contest so extreme
muscularity will be marked down." Judges assess shape, symmetry,
balance, skin tone, poise, and stage presence. Bikini is scored on the
whole package, not on muscle size (NPC Bikini Rules, npcnewsonline.com;
NPC Worldwide Rules, ifbbpro.com).

### The look that wins

- The Bikini X-frame. Width and roundness at the shoulders (capped,
  round delts) over a tight, narrow waist, flowing into full, round
  glutes. The shoulder-to-waist taper above and the glute shelf below
  are the two anchor points. Coaches describe the target as a
  "shoulder-to-waist taper with moderate glute development and minimal
  quad mass" (BiaBody summary of physique-athlete training; Five Starr
  Physique, "What the Judges Want: Bikini Division").
- Conditioning is soft, not striated. 2026 judging guidance is that
  judges "want softer lines and natural flow, no hard conditioning",
  and that "too much lean muscle mass can work against you" in Bikini
  (Saleyla division guide 2026; Five Starr Physique). Visible
  striations, deep separation, a flat or stringy glute, and vascularity
  are over-conditioned for this division and lose points.
- Femininity and flow. Lines should be smooth and curved, not angular.
  A glute that is round and full beats a glute that is merely lean.

### What gets marked down

- Extreme muscularity (rulebook wording, marked down by definition).
- Hard, dry, striated conditioning. This is the single most common
  over-shoot. A Bikini athlete who brings a Figure-level peeled glute
  reads as the wrong division.
- A blocky or thick waist. Anything that widens the midsection (heavy
  oblique work, a thick trunk) breaks the taper.
- Quad sweep and quad mass that pulls the eye to the legs and away from
  the glute line. Bikini "emphasises a shoulder-to-waist taper with
  moderate glute development and minimal quad mass" (BiaBody).
- Trap and upper-back thickness heavy enough to square off the neckline
  and shoulders.

### The 2025/2026 Fit Model context

The IFBB Pro League and NPC launched the Fit Model division, positioned
publicly as "going to be different from Bikini" with conditioning a step
SOFTER than Bikini, judges not wanting to see "even a slight separation
anywhere". The reference look cited for Fit Model is three-time Bikini
Olympia Ashley Kaltwasser's softer-era presentation (Fitness Volt, "IFBB
Pro League and NPC Announce New Fit Model Division"; TeamUSA Physique).
The practical read for coaches: Bikini's own line has held or even firmed
slightly, and the softer end of the old Bikini spectrum now has its own
home. Coaching a Bikini athlete in 2026 means hitting Bikini conditioning
precisely, not defaulting softer (that is now Fit Model) and not
defaulting harder (that is Figure).

## 3. Programming philosophy

The consensus across the evidence-based coaches (Conlin, RP, 3DMJ,
Contreras) is consistent on principle even where set numbers differ.

- Volume. Moderate to high on the priority muscles (glutes, delts,
  hamstrings), deliberately moderate-and-capped on the rest. Bikini
  athletes report the lowest overall training volume of the women's
  divisions in survey data (BiaBody summary of the Frontiers 2025
  study, section 5).
- Frequency. Priority muscles trained 2 to 3 times a week. Laurin
  Conlin trains delts roughly 3x/week and legs 3x/week (blending
  hypertrophy and power), with abs and calves about 2x and back and
  arms about 1x (Dynamic Duo Training interview with Conlin). Bret
  Contreras runs glutes 3 to 6x/week as effectively a permanent
  specialisation (bretcontreras.com).
- Exercise selection. Compound lifts programmed for progressive
  overload form the base; accessory work is then chosen specifically to
  fix the athlete's weak areas, with total accessory volume waved by
  season (Conlin, Biolayne Bikini Blueprint). Glute-biased hip-hinge
  and thrust patterns and lateral-delt isolation dominate; quad-dominant
  and waist-thickening work is limited.
- Load and rep ranges. Mixed. Contreras's glute model spans the
  spectrum in one week: an endurance day at 12 to 20 reps (bodyweight
  and bands), a hypertrophy day at roughly 8 to 12 reps for 4 sets, and
  a strength day at 1 to 8 reps for 5 sets (Bret Contreras programming
  articles; The Fitness Maverick glute and hamstring guide). The logic
  is that glutes respond across rep ranges and tolerate high frequency
  when exercise selection is rotated to manage joint and CNS load.
- Progression. Progressive overload on the compounds first
  (Conlin/Biolayne), then RP-style volume progression within a block,
  trained 1 to 2 reps short of failure to protect recovery in a deficit
  (RP Strength). 3DMJ layer autoregulation (RPE/RIR) so prep fatigue
  does not force grinding sets.
- Cardio philosophy varies by coach. Conlin keeps cardio low because
  lifting volume is already high (Dynamic Duo interview). Bonilla caps
  cardio near 45 minutes a day and favours HIIT (Team Elite Physique).

## 4. Muscle-group priority ranking and why

Ranked from most to least emphasised for a typical Bikini athlete. The
"why" is always about the X-frame and the soft-but-shaped standard.

1. Glutes (highest priority). The glute shelf is the single biggest
   scoring asset and the most common weak point. "It's all about glutes"
   is Bret Contreras's framing of the division; glute work is hammered
   while other areas are scaled back to let glutes recover and grow
   (bretcontreras.com, "How to Train for a Bikini Competition"). Round
   and full beats lean and flat.
2. Side/rear deltoids. Capped, round delts build the top of the X and
   make the waist look smaller by contrast. Conlin lists "capped delts"
   as a named Nationals-prep target (Dynamic Duo interview). Lateral
   raises and rear-delt work are emphasised; the aim is roundness and
   width, not mass.
3. Hamstrings, especially the glute-ham tie-in. Conlin names
   "hamstrings and tie-in" as a prep priority. A full upper hamstring
   continues the glute line and gives the rear shot its curve. Coaches
   split hamstrings onto separate days from quads so quads do not take
   over the movement (Iron Man, Breena Martinez; The Fitness Maverick).
4. Upper back, trained for taper not thickness. Some lat width helps
   create the illusion of a smaller waist (Menno Henselmans,
   mennohenselmans.com). But this is deliberately measured, not maxed,
   to avoid a blocky look.
5. Quads (deliberately de-emphasised). Bikini wants "minimal quad mass"
   (BiaBody). Coaches limit quad-dominant work and prefer glute-biased
   leg patterns so the leg reads as a glute-and-hamstring line, not a
   quad sweep.
6. Traps and direct heavy upper-trap loading (de-emphasised). Heavy
   shrugs/upright-row volume can square off the neckline and reduce the
   delt-to-trap contrast that makes shoulders look capped. [synthesis,
   reasoning from the "no blocky upper body" standard; coaches limit
   rather than ban.]
7. Direct oblique/ab loading (deliberately de-emphasised). Henselmans
   is explicit: direct oblique work thickens the waist gradually and can
   blunt the taper, so heavy weighted ab work is limited; bodyweight ab
   work is preferred to avoid a "square" midsection (mennohenselmans.com).
8. Calves, chest, arms (maintenance only). Trained enough for balance,
   not specialised. Conlin runs arms and back around 1x/week (Dynamic
   Duo interview).

## 5. Weekly volume per muscle group

### Documented survey data (primary science source)

de Silveira et al. (2025), "Quantification of weekly strength-training
volume per muscle group in competitive physique athletes", Frontiers in
Sports and Active Living, 154 athletes across federations and categories
(Frontiers / PMC12345604; PubMed 40809103). Key findings relevant to
Bikini:

- Bikini and Wellness divisions place more emphasis on glutes and
  shoulders than other categories (direct study finding).
- In women, glutes and triceps surae (calves) were trained more
  frequently than lower back and abdominals.
- Most categories, Bikini included, REDUCED training volume for most
  muscle groups during pre-contest, while cardio duration increased
  significantly in pre-contest for Classic Physique, Bodybuilding,
  Bikini and Wellness.

Reported set figures circulating from this dataset and its summaries
(cite the study/BiaBody summary; ranges reflect that these are survey
means, not prescriptions):

- Larger lower-body muscles (glutes, quads, hamstrings): broadly
  15 to 30 weekly sets in the off-season for physique athletes (BiaBody
  summary of the data).
- Glutes for Bikini: reported dropping from roughly 31 sets/week
  off-season to about 15 sets/week in contest prep (BiaBody summary).
- Shoulders for Bikini: reported roughly DOUBLING from about 7.5
  sets/week off-season to about 15 sets/week in prep (BiaBody summary).
  This is the one notable place Bikini athletes push volume UP into prep,
  consistent with shoulders being a hard-to-overshoot, always-wanted
  asset.

Read carefully: glute set count falls in prep not because glutes matter
less but because total recoverable volume falls in a deficit, so coaches
protect the most fatiguing work and let absolute set numbers drop while
keeping glute relative priority high.

### Practical coach-prescribed ranges (working targets)

These synthesise the named coaches' published guidance into per-muscle
weekly set ranges. Documented anchors are cited; gaps are tagged.

- Glutes: off-season high, often 16 to 30+ sets/week given 3 to 6
  sessions (Contreras frequency model; BiaBody off-season figure ~31).
  Prep around 12 to 18 sets/week as recovery tightens (BiaBody ~15).
- Side/rear delts: off-season ~10 to 16 sets/week; prep pushed toward
  ~15 (BiaBody ~7.5 off-season to ~15 prep). Conlin trains delts ~3x/wk.
- Hamstrings: ~12 to 20 sets/week off-season, trained on a separate day
  from quads (Iron Man/Martinez; The Fitness Maverick). Prep ~10 to 14
  [synthesis].
- Quads: capped low, roughly 6 to 10 sets/week, glute-biased patterns
  preferred over heavy quad sweep work ("minimal quad mass", BiaBody;
  cap is the point). [synthesis on the exact number.]
- Back: moderate, ~8 to 12 sets/week for taper, not maxed for thickness
  (Henselmans rationale). [synthesis on number.]
- Direct abs/obliques: deliberately low, bodyweight-biased, weighted
  oblique work limited or avoided (Henselmans). Often <4 weighted
  sets/week [synthesis].
- Chest, biceps, triceps, calves: maintenance, ~4 to 9 sets/week each,
  ~1x/week for arms (Conlin). [synthesis on numbers.]

### Where coaches deliberately CAP volume

The caps are the division. Quads, traps, direct weighted obliques, and
overall back thickness are held below the level that would build size,
because past a certain point each one either widens the waist (obliques,
traps squaring the trunk), pulls focus to the legs (quads), or blocks the
shoulder cap (traps). The skill is keeping these muscles trained for
balance and proportion while refusing to chase growth on them. This is
the opposite of every other women's division except, now, Fit Model.

## 6. Exercise selection philosophy

### Prioritised

- Glute-biased hip extension. Barbell and machine hip thrusts, B-stance
  and single-leg thrusts, band hip thrusts, 45-degree hyperextensions
  loaded into the glutes, cable kickbacks, lateral band walks. Contreras
  notes band hip thrusts and lateral band work can be run almost daily
  because they load glutes hard without taxing CNS or joints
  (bretcontreras.com). The Biolayne/Conlin material features the machine
  hip thrust and 45-degree hyper as staples.
- Hip hinge for the tie-in. Romanian deadlifts, glute-ham raises, and
  hinge variations on dedicated hamstring days (separated from quads so
  quads do not dominate, per Iron Man/Martinez).
- Lateral and rear-delt isolation. Dumbbell and cable lateral raises,
  rear-delt flyes, machine lateral raises, for capped round delts
  (Conlin's "capped delts" target).
- Measured lat width work. Pulldowns/rows used to create taper, not
  trunk thickness (Henselmans).

### Avoided or limited, and why

- Heavy quad-dominant work (front squats, heavy hack squats, deep
  narrow leg press) is limited so the leg does not develop a quad sweep
  that competes with the glute line ("minimal quad mass", BiaBody).
- Heavy direct weighted oblique work (weighted side bends, heavy loaded
  twists, heavy weighted crunches) is limited because it thickens the
  waist gradually and breaks the taper (Henselmans, mennohenselmans.com).
- Heavy trap loading (heavy shrugs, heavy upright rows) is limited so
  the shoulders read as capped against a clean neckline rather than
  blending into a thick upper trap. [synthesis from the standard;
  coaches limit rather than ban.]
- Overall back over-development is avoided once enough width exists;
  some coaches drop shoulder frequency from 2x to 1x once the delts are
  competitive (coachmarkcarroll.com / Next Level Bikini Prep guidance on
  pulling frequency back once a muscle catches up).

The throughline: Bikini exercise selection is chosen as much for what it
does NOT grow as for what it grows.

## 7. Practices specific to Bikini vs general hypertrophy

- Capping muscles on purpose. General hypertrophy says grow everything
  trainable. Bikini says grow two regions, hold several others, and
  reduce a couple. There is no other common context where a coach
  deliberately keeps quads, traps and obliques small.
- Glute frequency far above the general norm. General hypertrophy
  guidance lands around 2x/week per muscle. Bikini glute specialists
  run 3 to 6x/week as a near-permanent specialisation (Contreras),
  because the glute is both the scoring asset and the slowest-developing
  weak point.
- Conditioning aimed at a soft target, not maximum leanness. Most
  physique prep chases the driest look the athlete can hold. Bikini
  prep is calibrated to stop BEFORE striations and deep separation
  appear, because over-conditioning loses points here (Five Starr
  Physique; Saleyla 2026). Peak-week and final-fortnight conditioning is
  pulled to a softer endpoint than Figure or Wellness.
- Waist management as an active training decision. Deliberately
  limiting oblique loading to protect the taper (Henselmans) has no real
  equivalent in size-maximising training.
- Pre-contest volume comes DOWN, cardio goes UP. The survey data shows
  Bikini athletes cut training volume and add cardio in prep (de
  Silveira et al. 2025), the reverse of a pure muscle-gain phase.

## 8. Common weak points and how the best coaches address them

### Glutes (the most common weak point)

- Contreras: treat the athlete as if on a permanent glute
  specialisation. Run 3 to 6 glute sessions a week, rotate exercise type
  by day to manage fatigue (heavy bilateral, single-leg, band work),
  and pyramid rep ranges across the week (endurance 12 to 20, hypertrophy
  8 to 12 x4, strength 1 to 8 x5). Scale back other body parts so glutes
  recover and grow (bretcontreras.com).
- Conlin: name "round, full glutes" as an explicit prep target and bias
  accessory selection toward them (Dynamic Duo interview).
- General coaching rule (Mark Carroll / Next Level): when a part lags,
  raise its frequency; when it catches up, drop frequency back so it
  does not overshoot.

### Delts/shoulders (second most common)

- Conlin: delts ~3x/week, lateral and rear-delt isolation for "capped"
  roundness (Dynamic Duo interview).
- Survey-backed approach: push shoulder volume UP into prep, the one
  area Bikini athletes commonly increase (BiaBody, shoulders ~7.5 to ~15
  sets). Then, once competitive, drop shoulder frequency from 2x to 1x
  to hold rather than keep growing (Next Level / Carroll guidance).

### Hamstrings and the glute-ham tie-in

- Separate hamstrings from quads on different days so quads do not take
  over and the tie-in actually develops (Iron Man, Breena Martinez; The
  Fitness Maverick). Hinge work (RDLs, glute-ham raises) prioritised.

### Waist that looks thick

- Henselmans: stop chasing weighted oblique work, shift to bodyweight ab
  work, and use measured lat width to make the waist look smaller by
  contrast rather than trying to "carve" the midsection
  (mennohenselmans.com).

### Conditioning overshoot (a weak point of execution, not anatomy)

- Coaches peak Bikini athletes to a softer endpoint and stop the diet
  before striations appear; the modern reference is the Kaltwasser-era
  softer-glute look that the federation has now formalised at the Fit
  Model end (Fitness Volt; Five Starr Physique).

## 9. Recovery management specific to Bikini

- Volume drops and cardio rises in prep, by design, so recovery debt is
  managed by reducing lifting load rather than grinding through it (de
  Silveira et al. 2025). RP's "1 to 2 reps from failure" rule exists
  specifically because a calorie deficit already impairs recovery, so
  training to failure costs more than it returns in prep (RP Strength).
- 3DMJ autoregulation (RPE/RIR) lets prep fatigue dictate daily load so
  the athlete is not locked into numbers her recovery can no longer
  support (Helms/3DMJ).
- Reverse dieting post-show. The consensus recovery practice is a
  gradual calorie increase, commonly cited as roughly +50 kcal/week from
  carbohydrate until an off-season intake is reached, to restore
  metabolic rate and hormones and limit rebound (Twin Cities Metabolism,
  Competition Recovery guide; Industry Minds; TeamUSA Physique). Next
  Level Bikini Prep argue rebound can be avoided with structured
  post-show eating whether or not it is labelled a reverse diet
  (nextlevelbikiniprep.com).
- Off-season is the build window. RP/3DMJ framing: a tracked surplus to
  add the glute and delt size that prep cannot build, then a fresh prep.
  The off-season is where Bikini muscle is actually made; prep only
  reveals it.
- Mental and behavioural recovery. Celeste Rains-Turk's work targets the
  body-image, food-relationship and over-training-compulsion problems
  common in this division (celestial.fit). Contreras has noted directly
  that many Bikini competitors he worked with had unhealthy exercise
  relationships and could not take rest days (bretcontreras.com). For a
  division defined by appearance, psychological recovery between preps is
  a genuine performance and longevity factor, not a soft add-on.
- Female-specific recovery (menstrual-cycle and hormonal disruption from
  low body fat) is acknowledged across recovery sources as a reason to
  reverse out of prep promptly, though the public coaching material is
  thinner on specific cycle-based programming than on calorie recovery
  [synthesis on the programming gap].
