# Off-season (bulk) energy target: evidence review for Volyume

Verification key: [V-full] figure read from full text this session; [V-abs] from abstract or secondary summary only (re-check before quoting in-app); [NV] could not verify. No dosing information is included.

## 1. Natural athletes: off-season guidance

1.1 Iraki, Fitschen, Espinar, Helms (2019). Nutrition Recommendations for Bodybuilders in the Off-Season: A Narrative Review. Sports 7(7):154. PMC6680710. [V-full]
- Abstract: "A hyper-energetic diet (~10-20%) should be consumed" with ~0.25-0.5% bodyweight/week for novice/intermediate bodybuilders; advanced should be "more conservative".
- Table 1: novice/intermediate +10-20% above maintenance, ~0.25-0.5% BW/week; advanced +5-10% above maintenance, ~0.25% BW/week.
- Text also gives ~0.25-0.5 kg/week "when trying to increase LBM and minimize gains in fat mass"; for advanced, "a potential 2 kg increase in body weight on a monthly basis might be too excessive".
- Cites Garthe 2013: "a 200-300 kcal per day surplus in highly trained athletes might be more appropriate than 500 kcal".
- No age-based or steroid-specific recommendation (checked first ~100k of 169k characters; the rest unread).
- Grade: expert practice built on a narrative review (not RCT evidence). Authors are practitioners, so this is "expert practice".

1.2 Garthe, Raastad, Refsnes, Sundgot-Borgen (2013). Effect of two different weight-gain interventions on body composition and performance in elite athletes. Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab? (journal not confirmed; one source lists Eur J Sport Sci for the sibling 2011/2013 papers; check before citing). [V-abs]
- 39 elite athletes randomised: counselled group ~3585 kcal vs ad libitum ~2964 kcal (about 544-600 kcal/day more). Body mass +3.9% vs +1.5%; fat mass gain larger in counselled group (abstract reports about 15% vs 3%); lean mass gain not significantly different (1.7 kg vs 1.2 kg per Iraki's summary). Authors: use surplus cautiously.
- Grade: early findings (one RCT, n=39, elite athletes of mixed sports, not physique athletes).

1.3 Helms et al. (2023). Sports Medicine - Open (PMID 37914977, PMC10620361). 21 trained lifters randomised (17 completers) to maintenance, 5% or 15% surplus for 8 weeks. Conclusion: faster body-mass gain "primarily" increases fat gain rather than hypertrophy or strength; authors caution small sample, individual optimum, and note the high-surplus group improved bench 1RM more than the others. [V-abs]
- Grade: early findings.

1.4 Ribeiro et al. (2019), J Hum Kinet, pilot, 11 male bodybuilders, 4 weeks. Higher intake (~67.5 kcal/kg) vs moderate (~50.1 kcal/kg): body fat +7.4% vs +0.8%; muscle +2.7% vs +1.1%. [V-abs, secondary summary only; exact title and volume not verified]
- Grade: early findings (tiny, 4 weeks).

1.5 Slater, Dieter, Marsh, Helms, Shaw, Iraki (2019). Is an Energy Surplus Required to Maximize Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy Associated With Resistance Training. Front Nutr 6:131. doi 10.3389/fnut.2019.00131. [V-full, partial]
- States the exact surplus needed for hypertrophy "is unknown" and was never validated in resistance-training populations. Suggests ~1,500-2,000 kJ/day (about 360-480 kcal) for weight-stable athletes and up to an additional ~4,000 kJ/day (about 960 kcal) for those who struggle to gain or have heavy loads. Cites overfeeding work in which roughly 2 kg fat was accrued per 1 kg lean mass (100-day young men surplus study), and a twin study (~353 MJ over 100 days) with 5.4 kg FM and 2.7 kg FFM gain. Notes younger, less experienced athletes have greater hypertrophy potential.
- Grade: expert practice / narrative review. Note Slater's higher figures sit above Iraki's advanced figures; the field disagrees.

1.6 Helms, Aragon, Fitschen (2014). J Int Soc Sports Nutr 11:20. Contest-prep (cutting) paper; not a source for gaining. Useful only for: ED and body image risk flag in aesthetic sport. [V-abs]

1.7 Roberts, Helms, Trexler, Fitschen (2020). J Hum Kinet 71:79-108. Physique athletes; prep and recovery; I could not verify an off-season surplus figure in it. [NV for surplus]

Overall grade for "10-20% novice/intermediate, 5-10% advanced, 0.25-0.5% BW/week": EXPERT PRACTICE (consistent across practitioner reviews; the controlled data are few and short and lean towards "bigger surplus = mostly more fat").
Grade for "larger surplus gives disproportionately more fat, not more muscle, in trained people": EARLY FINDINGS (consistent direction across Garthe, Ribeiro, Helms 2023; small samples).
Grade for "novices can gain faster": FAIRLY WELL ESTABLISHED in direction (hypertrophy potential declines with training age); exact numbers are expert practice.

## 2. Enhanced (anabolic-androgenic steroid) athletes

2.1 Bhasin et al. (1996) NEJM 335:1-7, PMID 8637535. 43 men, 4 groups, 10 weeks, supraphysiologic testosterone vs placebo, with or without lifting. Fat-free mass gain with drug plus exercise reported as 6.1 +/- 0.6 kg (abstract); other-arm figures (about 0.8, 3.2, 1.9 kg) are from a secondary slide reproduction [V-abs, others NV]. Diet was controlled/standardised; this is not a nutrition study.
2.2 Bhasin et al. (2001) Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab 281(6):E1172-81, PMID 11701431. 61 men aged 18-35, endogenous production suppressed; 20 weeks. Fat-free mass change rose dose-dependently (+3.4, +5.2, +7.9 kg across the three higher dose groups); fat mass and HDL cholesterol fell with higher dose. [V-abs]
- Both studies: healthy young men, short, no steroid-stack polypharmacy, no energy-surplus manipulation. They show lean-mass gain is real and dose-related, NOT how much energy intake an enhanced athlete needs.
- Grade for "androgens raise lean-mass gain in trained and untrained men over weeks": WELL ESTABLISHED (RCTs). Grade for "this changes optimal energy surplus": NO EVIDENCE found.

2.3 Nutrition guidance specifically for enhanced athletes: I found none from a professional body, consensus statement or peer-reviewed guideline. Dietary-intake literature on competitive bodybuilders (Sports Med 2015 systematic review, 18 studies, 385 participants; mostly 1980s-90s, poor quality; protein 1.9-4.3 g/kg in men) does not separate users. Prevalence surveys show high use in competitive bodybuilders (e.g. 72.9% in a Spanish sample [V-abs via doping.nl summary]) so the audience is real. Any enhanced-specific surplus rule would be EXPERT PRACTICE at most (coach folklore), and I cannot cite it.
- Published mechanism-level hint (not nutrition guidance): androgens shift partitioning towards lean mass and away from fat mass (Bhasin 2001 fat mass fell with dose). That supports "enhanced users may carry a surplus with less fat gain" as a plausible inference, labelled as inference, not as established.

## 3. Age 40+
- No published surplus recommendation for 40+ physique athletes found. Iraki 2019 gives none. Slater 2019 notes younger, less experienced athletes have greater hypertrophy potential. Overfeeding literature: older men retained more of the gained weight (one secondary report: older men lost only 29% of overfeeding weight gain versus young men losing all; [V-abs, original not identified]).
- Grade: no evidence for a specific age adjustment; direction (slower potential gain, easier fat retention) is early findings / expert inference. Recommend age gives no uplift to the surplus, and, if anything, no change from the training-age rule.

## 4. Health risks and upper bounds
- Overfeeding in general: 2020 systematic review of human overfeeding (PMID 32515127) reports weight gain across groups, response modified by baseline insulin sensitivity and adiposity. 8-week overfeeding study (PMC3711092 or PMC4170127; which is which not verified) found fat mass, FFM, visceral and deep subcutaneous fat all rose, fasting insulin and glucose rose, clamp glucose disposal unchanged. [V-abs] Grade: fairly well established that surplus raises fat mass including visceral fat in short term; reversibility in older people is poorer (see 3).
- Published upper bound for surplus: none as a safety limit. The only numbers are the practice ceilings above (Iraki 20%; Slater up to about 960 kcal extra).
- AAS cardiovascular: Baggish et al. (2017) Circulation, doi 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.116.026945. 140 male weightlifters aged 34-54 (86 long-term users, 54 non-users): lower LV systolic and diastolic function in users, more in those on-drug at the time; higher coronary plaque volume, related to cumulative duration. Cross-sectional, self-reported use. [V-abs] Grade: early findings to fairly well established (consistent direction in registry data).
- Windfeld-Mathiasen et al. (2025) Circulation, doi 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.124.071117, PMID 39945117. 1,189 men sanctioned for AAS use vs 59,450 matched controls; substantially increased CVD risk; hazard ratios not retrieved [NV]. A related report from the same cohort gave mortality HR 2.8 [V-abs, secondary]. No dose or duration data, so not causal. Grade: early findings (large registry, observational).
- Bhasin 2001: HDL fell dose-dependently [V-abs]. Grade well established for the direction (RCT).
- Aesthetic-sport ED and body-image risk flagged by Helms 2014 [V-abs]. Relevant to the app's ED-safety lane: a "more aggressive gain" control must stay out of reach under an open ED flag or calm mode (product rule, not research).

## 5. Proposed conservative rule set (for founder review; nothing here is a medical claim)

(a) Natural gain-rate band by training age (default; evidence grade EXPERT PRACTICE)
- Beginner (under ~1 year consistent training): +10-20% of maintenance, up to about 0.5% BW/week. Direction well established, numbers expert practice.
- Intermediate (1-3 years): +10-20%, 0.25-0.5% BW/week (Iraki 2019). Default towards the lower half.
- Advanced (3+ years or Volyume-derived classification): +5-10%, about 0.25% BW/week or less (Iraki 2019). Monthly gain cap about 1% BW.
- Example check: 204 lb (92.5 kg) at 0.25-0.5% = about 0.23-0.46 kg/week. Existing 4,077 kcal figure with range 3,669 to 4,485 is a plus/minus 10% band, which is consistent with the 10-20% practice only if maintenance is about 3,700 or so; check how the engine defines the midpoint.

(b) Enhanced status (optional, discreet; evidence grade NO EVIDENCE for surplus, early findings for the partitioning inference)
- There is no published guidance to justify a different surplus. The honest options:
  1. Do not alter the engine; use the status only to widen the slider's allowed top end to the upper range edge (still capped by (c)). Rationale to show user: "People with higher anabolic drive can sometimes gain faster with less fat. This is not studied for nutrition, so keep the range and check progress." Label: Early findings.
  2. Use the status only to move the training-age default one band up (e.g. an advanced enhanced user starts on the intermediate band), on the inference that lean-mass potential is higher. Label: expert inference, no direct evidence.
  Recommend option 1 only, plus a calm note: "This is not medical advice and Volyume gives no guidance on drug use." Never collect or display drug names, doses or cycles; store a single boolean/enum.
- Add a calm cardiovascular note on enhanced status: bigger surpluses plus androgens may stack metabolic and cardiovascular load; encourage routine GP health checks (blood pressure, lipids). Use NHS-style language; frame as general health, not a warning of drug use consequences. Grade for the underlying risk: early findings.
- Do not allow the enhanced flag to widen upper bounds if age 40+? Evidence does not support differential treatment; keep same cap (age gives no uplift).

(c) Slider maximum
- Default slider position: midpoint of current range (training-age band lower half for advanced).
- Natural maximum: top of the existing range (about +10% above the estimate) or +20% of maintenance, whichever is lower. Evidence: expert practice (Iraki upper bound 20%).
- Enhanced maximum: same hard ceiling (+20% of maintenance, never above top of range). Reasoning: Slater's higher practice figures (~960 kcal) support that bigger isn't forbidden, but fat-gain evidence is against it and there is no enhanced evidence.
- Add soft warning as the slider passes 15% above maintenance: "Gaining faster mostly adds body fat in trained people (early findings). Check your weekly average gain; if it exceeds your band, the engine will trim the target." Engine safety: re-evaluate weekly using actual weight trend; auto-reduce if gain exceeds 0.5% BW/week (natural) or the status-adjusted cap.
- All of this is a gaining-phase control. ED-safety is untouched (floors only apply to deficits), but hide the slider under calm mode/open ED flag.
