# Onboarding Audit 05 — Research

Status: COMPLETE (Phase 5 of 7). Fresh web research, June 2026. Date: 2026-06-01.
Sources listed at the end.

---

## 1. Best onboarding flows, what works and what loses people
- Personalisation that visibly changes the product lifts first-month retention.
  Ask only what changes the experience, and show that it did. (UXCam, VWO.)
- Speed: users abandon past 3 to 5 steps and decide within ~20 seconds; the first
  session should end at a product, not an empty screen. (Adapty, Glance.)
- Explain why each question is asked; "this takes 3 minutes, you can come back"
  cuts abandonment in regulated/health contexts. (VWO, WorkBright.)
- Frame fitness questions without judgement. (Glance.)
Read against Volyume: the wizard already explains "why" well and ends at a
product with an auto-generated plan. It is longer than the 3 to 5 guideline once
the duplicate account step and the consent interruption are counted.

## 2. Plan creation and re-enrolment for existing users
- Smart defaults / pre-population, always overridable; "defeat the blank page"
  with prior selections. (NN/g, Appcues.)
- Onboarding is lifecycle, not first-run; re-enrolment should be lighter and
  familiar. (NN/g.)
Read against Volyume: `ProGoalSetup` already pre-populates and is lighter. The
gaps are reachability (hidden unless Pro+active plan) and visual consistency with
the wizard.

## 3. Communicating expertise without overload
- Credibility is clarity, not jargon; show the mechanism once, plainly. Users
  distrust apps that "sell with science" they cannot back. (AAAS, MIT QSS.)
Read against Volyume: the "Why this plan, for you" rationale and the per-question
hints are this pattern done well. Risks: jargon leaks ("MAV-level volume") and
undefined product terms ("Precision Coaching").

## 4. Progressive disclosure and flow length
- Aim for a short core (3 to 5 screens, 1 to 2 personalising questions, a
  meaningful action inside 60 seconds); reveal advanced controls in context.
  Fitness is an exception that legitimately needs more inputs, so the craft is
  sequencing and justifying them. (Glance, Pendo, weareaffective.)

## 5. Look, feel and brand voice of the field (for doc 03)
- MacroFactor: clean, non-gamified, compliance-neutral data ethos, "observe and
  adjust, do not moralise".
- Whoop: dark, metric-led, authoritative, written/AI rationale.
- Oura: minimalist, calm, one readiness score.
- Future: warm, human, the named coach writes to you.
- Noom / Duolingo: bright, gamified, behavioural (Noom's chattiness can read
  cheesy).
Read against Volyume: its intended position (precision instrument, written
rationale, no gamification) blends MacroFactor's data ethos with Whoop's
authority, with a cheap human signal via the founder note. That is ownable and
credible. The flow undersells it where copy and chrome slip (doc 03, doc 04).

## Implications for the proposal (doc 06)
1. Keep and extend the "explain why" pattern.
2. Shorten the core: remove the duplicate account step, smooth the consent step.
3. Describe the in-app food process correctly in context; never point elsewhere.
4. State the model plainly once: weigh in, check in, food logging is optional.
5. Make the coached builder reachable and visually consistent with onboarding.
