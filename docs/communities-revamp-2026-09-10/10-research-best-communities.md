# Research: mechanisms from the best community products (fitness and beyond)

Founder brief 2026-09-10 (`README.md`): Community is for CONNECTING people (same gym, age band, discipline, other affinities), seeing how often cohort members trained, whether they had a PR, whether they are progressing; friend groups for encouragement and sharing a great workout, as an ELITE differentiator. This document covers MECHANISMS (identity, cohorts, signals, encouragement, groups, sharing, cold start), not visual arrangement (separate lane, `12-research-look-and-feel.md`).

Method: WebSearch/WebFetch, all sources accessed 2026-09-10 (not repeated per citation). Every line is **observed** (sourced, URL given) or **recalled, unverified** (no primary source found; background knowledge only, never asserted as fact). A brief-named mechanism I could not confirm is flagged, not invented. Already-covered ground (the six source docs named in the agent brief) is skimmed, not repeated; the Strava/Garmin blocks add ONLY new mechanism-level detail (kudos etiquette, clubs/challenges nuance).

## LANE A: fitness communities beyond the gym-app set

### Strava (new detail only; see `docs/social-discovery-2026-09-06/11-research-strava-garmin.md` for clubs/Local Legend/Flyby/challenges already on file)
- Kudos mechanic: a single free tap on a thumbs-up icon on any activity; on a group ride, a "kudos bomb" gives kudos to the whole group at once by opening Manage Group and lightly shaking the phone. Observed: ["10 Ways to Get More Kudos", Strava Community](https://communityhub.strava.com/insider-journal-9/10-ways-to-get-more-kudos-1573).
- Kudos etiquette: reciprocity is a real norm ("you get more kudos when you give more"), but giving kudos indiscriminately for every ride, however short, is called out by users as annoying and devalues the signal. The mechanic is free; the social cost is entirely in how it is used. Observed: same source above; [Roadman Cycling, "The Etiquette of Strava"](https://roadmancycling.com/podcast/ep-2174-the-etiquette-of-strava).
- "Friends trained today": no distinct, separately named feature was found under that phrasing. **Recalled, unverified**: likely the brief author's shorthand for Strava's main Feed (already documented) rather than a separate widget; do not assert a dedicated feature exists.
- Borrow: make the encouragement tap free and instant, but treat indiscriminate blanket-encouragement as a design smell (see refuse list); offer a one-tap "encourage everyone in my group today" action for group moments, mirroring the kudos-bomb shake.

### Garmin Connect (new detail only; see doc 11 for Groups/Followers/LiveTrack already on file)
- No distinct kudos-style tap-encouragement mechanic was found documented for Garmin Connect beyond the Groups leaderboard itself; unverified whether one exists under a different name.
- Group Challenges require prior group membership, and a stale (>24h no-sync) member is dropped from the ranked view entirely rather than shown greyed out, confirming the pattern already logged in `docs/community-product-audit-2026-09-07/52-research-community-presentation.md` A6.
- Borrow: nothing materially new beyond doc 11's "bundle feed+leaderboard+events+routes into one Group page" and "cooperative/collective-total view is a stated user want, not yet shipped" findings, which stand.

### Peloton
Model: opt-in Tags on a member's profile (up to 10) express identity and discipline (instructor loyalty, modality, location, alma mater, causes); members browse a Tag, see who else carries it, and filter the Leaderboard by Tag. Self-declared cohort, not algorithmic. Signal: live in-class Leaderboard by output; the real-names-default change and "Here Now" board are already covered in doc 50 and 52. Encouragement: High Five, a tap on an avatar next to a Leaderboard name, works live, on-demand, and now "out of class" too (support a friend without joining their class); rate-limited to one given or received per minute; no block-user control exists specifically for it, and a press piece documents harassment via repeated High Fives from strangers. Groups: Tags are the nearest thing to a group (unbounded, self-join, no admin). Shared object: the class attended and live output, not a curated log entry. Observed: [Peloton support](https://support.onepeloton.com/s/article/Peloton-Software-Understanding-Peloton-Tags); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/krisholt/2020/04/28/peloton-social-community-profile-tagging/); [Peloton Buddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/out-of-class-high-fives/); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/high-five-creeps-peloton-stopped-102700958.html). Borrow: self-chosen identity tags as the cohort primitive (discipline, not just gym) map directly onto the founder's "men's physique athletes" ask; the 1/minute rate limit is the cleanest documented anti-spam floor.

### Nike Run Club
Model: mutual follow via Nike/Facebook connections, no discipline tags found. Signal: distance-based friends leaderboard plus weekly/monthly Challenges a user creates and invites friends to (already noted in `docs/community-product-audit-2026-09-07/07-competitors.md` A12). Encouragement, two distinct mechanics: (1) async "Cheers", a friend is notified when you start a run and can Cheer the post (requires public/social visibility); (2) live and synchronous, a friend mid-run can receive a short audio-cheer recording played into their headphones during the run itself. Groups: Challenges are the group unit; size/privacy not returned in this pass. Shared object: the run (distance, route, pace); audio cheers attach to the live session, not a static post. Observed: [Nike Run Club](https://www.facebook.com/nikerunning/posts/now-your-facebook-friends-can-cheer-you-on-in-real-time-during-your-run-check-ou/114734175254423/); [Nike support](http://support-en-us.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/29203). Borrow: the live, in-session audio cheer is a materially different primitive from every tap-based kudos/high-five above (it interrupts the session, not a feed) despite no current Volyume live-session analogue.

### Zwift
Model: Clubs, any Zwifter can create one; a member can join up to 50 Clubs, a single Club can hold up to 10,000 members. Signal: no personal social ranking found; "Personal Leaderboards" are a private PR-over-time timeline, already logged in doc 52 A11. Encouragement: Ride On, a single free tap given to one rider, to up to 5 nearby riders at once via Companion's location arrow (a short cooldown before the next batch), or to every followed Zwifter currently riding via one button on the Companion home page; a Ride On measurably doubles that rider's sweat-drop rate for 15 seconds, a small in-world reward for the GIVER's attention, not just a notification to the receiver. Groups: Club admins/moderators create group rides and workouts and manage the Club's Companion homepage content. Shared object: the live ride/workout in progress, and Club-organised events. Observed: [Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/create-club/); [Zwift Support](https://support.zwift.com/joining-a-club-ByqXjWKID); [Zwift Insider, Ride Ons](https://zwiftinsider.com/giving-multiple-ride-ons/). Borrow: Ride On's "bulk-encourage everyone currently active" one-tap action (not person-by-person hunting) is the strongest single mechanic in this lane for a cohort training at overlapping times; the in-world reward for the giver (not just the receiver) is a distinctive incentive shape worth naming even without an equivalent reward to attach it to.

### WHOOP
Model: Teams, created by a member who invites others; visible metrics are chosen by the team's CREATOR at setup ("it will ask you what metrics you want to make visible for the team"), the closest documented answer to "what WHOOP refuses to rank" (nothing refused by policy, but nothing shown by default; community-observed behaviour, not an official policy statement). Signal: Team Averages compare strain, recovery and sleep across daily/weekly/monthly windows, three independent pillar/period selectors above one table; an unaffiliated fan leaderboard (Whoopal) separately ranks HRV, which WHOOP itself does not officially surface as a Team metric (flagged, not WHOOP policy). Encouragement: no tap-to-cheer primitive found for Teams. Groups: Teams; size/privacy unverified. Shared object: aggregate scores over a chosen window, opted in per metric by the creator, not raw daily logs. Observed: [WHOOP community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/why-cant-i-see-my-teammates-strain-and-recovery-scores/301); [WHOOP support](https://support.whoop.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044455174-WHOOP-Team-Averages). Borrow: metric-level opt-in chosen once by the group's creator, not a global default, differs from every Strava/Garmin tiered-visibility model above, and suits any cohort view that could expose bodyweight-adjacent data.

### Oura Circles
Model, signal, groups and shared object are already fully logged in `docs/community-product-audit-2026-09-07/52-research-community-presentation.md` A8 (cap 20 members, up to 10 "joined" at once, opt-in sharing of sleep/activity/readiness over a rolling 2-week window, five emoji reactions, explicitly no ranking). No materially new fact surfaced in this pass; re-verified stable. Observed: [Introducing Oura Circles](https://ouraring.com/blog/introducing-oura-circles/). Borrow: the "cap at 20, most people join far fewer" ceiling is the clearest fitness precedent for what a small, non-competitive Volyume friend group should feel like at full size.

### Apple Fitness
Model: mutual, explicit sharing invite per friend; a "hide activity" toggle is bilateral (hides yours from them and stops their notifications about you). Encouragement: a push notification fires when a shared friend closes their rings, doubles their move goal, finishes a workout, or earns an achievement, with a "Compete" action offered directly from that notification; per-friend mute exists separately from the bilateral hide. Groups: none beyond 1:1; competitions are capped at exactly two people for 7 days. Shared object: the three Activity rings and workout completions, not workout content. Observed: [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/share-your-activity-apd68a69f5c7/watchos); [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255046883). Borrow: firing the encouragement notification on a completed goal, with a direct one-tap reply action attached to that same notification, keeps the whole loop (see friend's win, respond) inside one interruption rather than two.

### Fitbit community/groups
Model: closed groups only, up to 2,000 members; Google discontinued open/public groups, Challenges and Adventures on 27 March 2023. Signal: none beyond a closed group's discussion board. Encouragement: "cheer badges" and quick reactions as low-effort engagement, alongside threaded discussion boards for tips/recipes. Groups: closed only, admin model not detailed in sources returned. Shared object: posts within the group, plus an optional weight-data-hidden-from-followers control. Observed: [Google Fitbit Help](https://support.google.com/fitbit/answer/14236717); [XDA-Developers](https://www.xda-developers.com/fitbit-challenges-discontinued/). Borrow (as a warning, not a model): a platform owner cut its OPEN/public community layer and kept only closed, invited groups; read as evidence that a small, trusted-circle group model outlasts an open public one when a product has to choose where to invest, not as a mechanism to copy outright.

### Ladder
Model: a member joins one coach's Team. Signal: none beyond the team feed. Encouragement: a team group chat where members post session results and celebrate each other's PRs on the SAME programmed day. Groups: one team per coach, size/privacy unverified. Shared object: the day's completed workout and any PR within it. Observed: [The Everygirl](https://theeverygirl.com/ladder-app-review/); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/new-ladder-teams-fitness-app-takes-on-demand-fitness-to-new-heights/). Borrow: the chat works because everyone did the SAME workout the SAME day, so a PR post is instantly legible; a Volyume group on different individualised plans loses this shortcut and needs another route to a legible shared PR.

### Runna
Model: "Runna Club", a community layer with event-specific feeds and sub-groups; Runna's own positioning treats social as secondary to the coached plan. Encouragement and groups not documented beyond general posting/replies and sub-groups; size/privacy unverified. Shared object: race/training photos, shoe and route recommendations. Observed: [Runna Support](https://support.runna.com/en/collections/16740555-app-features); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/runna-app). Borrow: a coaching-first product that deliberately keeps social secondary is the closest precedent to Volyume's own "coaching engine first, community second" shape, useful if the founder needs a reason NOT to let Community visually dominate the coached-plan surfaces.

### Centr / Fiit / Sweat (community tabs, combined; each thinner on documented mechanism than the above)
All three combine live/on-demand classes with a challenge layer rather than a persistent activity feed. Centr: community forums plus periodic named Challenges ("Limitless", "Ignite") alongside live Q&A sessions. Fiit: users can invite friends, family or colleagues (no account required for the invitee) into a live class, plus a "Teams" object aimed more at brand/workplace cohorts than friend groups, and optional on-screen leaderboards when a heart-rate monitor is paired. Sweat: members-only forums are explicitly LOCATION-based so nearby members can arrange to train together in person, alongside 5-week themed Challenges. Observed: [Fitt Insider](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/centr-challenges-global-community-to-live-your-best-life-with-all-new-program-designed-for-total-body-optimization/); [Fiit.tv](https://fiit.tv/); [Sweat](https://sweat.com/blogs/fitness/goal-based-challenges). Borrow: Fiit's no-account-needed invite to a shared session, and Sweat's location-scoped forum for arranging real in-person training, are both directly relevant to a "same gym" cohort that wants to coordinate offline, not just watch each other's numbers.

## LANE B: elite community products outside fitness

### Duolingo (three distinct mechanisms, not one)
Friend Streak: mutual, binary, both people must practise THAT day or the shared flame dies, shown as a fire icon plus day-count next to the friend's name. Friends Quest: cooperative, weekly (Tuesday to Sunday), a shared target either friend's activity contributes to, paying out a shared reward (100 gems, a 30-minute XP boost) on success, no penalty on failure beyond no reward. Leagues: individual, competitive, weekly, 30 people per league, promotion/demotion zones. Observed: [Duolingo](https://lingoly.io/duolingo-friend-streak/); [Medium](https://medium.com/duofluency/a-complete-guide-to-duolingo-friends-quests-how-to-collaborate-and-earn-rewards-81f2aadac7bc); [duoplanet](https://duoplanet.com/duolingo-leagues-the-essential-guide-everything-you-need-to-know/). Evidence of harm from the competitive one: users report Leagues generate anxiety from "always being in competition" and notification when overtaken; some say they play "to maintain position, not to learn"; Streak Freeze cut churn 21% by relieving streak anxiety, and synthesis names user demand for choosable competition intensity. Observed: [gamification synthesis](https://medium.com/@flordaniele/duolingo-case-study-research-on-gamification-90b5bac3ada0) (secondary source aggregating Duolingo's own reporting; directionally reliable, not primary). Borrow: the two-person, both-benefit Friends Quest and the binary, both-or-nothing Friend Streak are structurally cooperative and carry no documented anxiety evidence, unlike Leagues; the shape (shared target, shared reward, no ranking) is the one to copy, not the league ladder.

### Discord
Model: a server containing role-gated channels; presence (online/idle/offline) is native and visible per member. Signal: none activity-specific, presence only. Groups/sizing: guidance for healthy channel-to-member ratios scales WITH size (5 to 10 channels across 2 to 3 categories under 100 members, more structure added as it grows); once a server passes 100 members, Discord auto-hides offline ("sleeping") members from the sidebar to stop clutter. Shared object: none, it is a chat/presence substrate, not a logging app. Observed: [BuildMyDiscord](https://buildmydiscord.com/en/blog/discord-channel-organization-guide-best-channel-structure-for-active-communities); [CommunityONE](https://blog.communityone.io/discord-sidebar-member-count-explained/). Borrow: "structure scales with size, and very small groups need FEWER categories, not a scaled-down version of a big layout" applies directly to Volyume gym cohorts that may have single-digit members.

### Letterboxd and Goodreads (activity feeds around a personal log)
Model: mutual or one-way follow around a personal diary/shelf. Signal: almost every personal action (a rating, a diary entry, a watchlist add, a liked review) posts to the Activity Feed automatically, no separate "share" step. Encouragement: likes and comments on diary entries/reviews, no tap-to-cheer primitive beyond that. Groups: none found in this pass. Shared object: the log entry itself (what was watched/read and the user's own rating), not raw data. Observed: [Letterboxd FAQ](https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/). Borrow: ambient, automatic activity-from-logging (no separate "post" action) is the lowest-friction sharing model in this document, and maps well onto Volyume's existing session log as the natural shareable unit.

### Chess.com (clubs and friends)
Model: Clubs, joined by invite (up to 30 invites/24h per member) or a shareable join link. Signal: individual rating only, not community-facing here. Encouragement: none distinct from normal play. Groups: Club Matches pit one club's members against another's; each pair of matched players (by rank) plays two games, and the CLUB's win/loss is the sum of every pair's result, not any one person's. Shared object: aggregated match points; individual games stay individual. Observed: [Chess.com support](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8649115-what-are-club-matches). Borrow: representing a whole cohort's result as the SUM of many individual outcomes (not a single leaderboard number) gives a small gym cohort a shared, legible "how did we do" figure without ranking any one person against another.

### Untappd and Vivino (reactions on a personal log; the two differ)
Untappd: a dedicated one-tap "toast" (like-equivalent) plus comments on each beer check-in, and breweries themselves can toast/comment too. Observed: [Untappd Blog](https://untappd.com/blog/post/16059746119/untappd-connects-breweries-and-consumers). Vivino: no dedicated reaction verb was found; it uses standard follow/like/comment/@-tag, closer to a general social feed than a distinct toast mechanic. Observed: [Vivino](https://www.vivino.com/en/wine-news/vivino-benefits-our-new-social-features-explained). Borrow: a single, domain-named reaction verb (a "toast" for a drink log) is a small but real identity move; a generic "like" reads as borrowed from elsewhere, a bespoke word reads as belonging to the product.

### Habitica parties (cooperative accountability, borrow with caution)
Model: a Party of players (four or more recommended) on a shared quest. Signal: the boss's shared health bar. Mechanism: every party member's completed Dailies/Habits/To-Dos damages the boss; every member's MISSED Daily also damages the boss, so one person's lapse costs the whole party, not just them. Groups: party-only, no larger public structure found. Shared object: the quest's collective progress bar, not individual logs. Observed: [Habitica Wiki](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Party); [Habitica Wiki](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Boss). Borrow with caution: shared stakes are a powerful accountability primitive, but "your lapse harms your friends" is in direct tension with Volyume's no-guilt, no-shame coaching voice (`docs/COACHING_VOICE_SYNTHESIS_LOCKED.md`) and the ED-safety posture; flagged for the lead's judgement, not proposed as-is.

### Focusmate (paired, scheduled accountability)
Model: one-to-one, booked in advance, matched with a stranger or a chosen partner for a live video session (25/50/75 minutes) with a goals check-in at both ends. Encouragement: the mechanism IS the scheduled presence itself, "when someone else is expecting you, skipping feels wrong." Enforcement: a late/no-show is detected and lowers a visible timeliness score; repeated no-shows can freeze the account; free for up to 3 sessions/week. Observed: [Focusmate](https://www.focusmate.com/how-it-works/); [Focusmate FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq/). Borrow: a scored consequence for a no-show is a harder edge than anything else in this document and is named here as a mechanism to study, not copy; Volyume's ED-safety and no-shame rules would need this softened considerably if a "training partner, booked slot" idea is ever pursued.

### BeReal (time-bounded authentic sharing)
Model: mutual friends only. Mechanism: one daily notification at a random time (same moment for everyone in a time zone), a 2-minute window to post a simultaneous front/back camera photo; posting late is visible to friends; you cannot see friends' posts until you post your own. Groups/shared object: the friend list only; one photo moment, not a log. Observed: [Time](https://time.com/6167952/how-be-real-app-works/); [BeReal Help](https://help.bereal.com/hc/en-us/articles/7350386715165--Time-to-BeReal). Borrow: "you must give before you can see" (post-gated viewing) is a reciprocity-forcing mechanic worth naming, though a daily random-time prompt has no obvious analogue in a training log app.

### Steam and Xbox (friend activity, "playing now")
Model: mutual friends list with native presence. Signal: online status plus, on Steam, a feed noting e.g. "played X for the first time"; a user can set status to Invisible or hide the specific game shown while staying visibly online. Encouragement/groups: none beyond presence itself in the sources returned. Observed: [Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=835308111); [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-to-hide-steam-activity/). Borrow: granular presence controls (visible-but-activity-hidden, distinct from fully invisible) is a finer privacy gradient than a single on/off toggle, relevant to any "training now" indicator Volyume might show.

### Locket (small close-friends circle)
Model: a hard cap of 20 friends, explicitly framed against follower-count culture. Encouragement: a photo sent by one friend appears directly on the other's phone home-screen widget (no app-open required to see it); the receiver can send back a one-tap emoji reaction, which "rains down" on the sender's photo. Groups/shared object: no groups, 1:1 links only; the shared object is a photo, not a metric. Observed: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/locket-widget/id1600525061); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/01/13/locket-app-iphone-widgets/). Borrow: surfacing a friend's moment on the home screen itself, outside the app entirely, is the lowest-friction "I saw you" signal in this document; a widget showing "a friend just trained" is the fitness analogue.

## Mechanisms worth borrowing for Volyume

1. Self-chosen identity tags (not algorithmic categories) as the cohort primitive, covering discipline/gym/location/age-band. Source: Peloton Tags. Evidence: [support.onepeloton.com](https://support.onepeloton.com/s/article/Peloton-Software-Understanding-Peloton-Tags), observed.
2. Group-creator opts visible metrics IN per field at setup, no global default exposure. Source: WHOOP Teams. Evidence: [WHOOP community thread](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/why-cant-i-see-my-teammates-strain-and-recovery-scores/301), observed (community-sourced, not an official policy doc).
3. Free, one-tap encouragement with a BULK "encourage everyone active now" action, not person-by-person hunting. Source: Zwift Ride On. Evidence: [Zwift Insider](https://zwiftinsider.com/giving-multiple-ride-ons/), observed.
4. One gesture encourages an entire group at once (a "kudos bomb"). Source: Strava. Evidence: [Strava Community](https://communityhub.strava.com/insider-journal-9/10-ways-to-get-more-kudos-1573), observed.
5. Cooperative, two-person, both-benefit streak and quest mechanics with NO ranking and no documented anxiety evidence. Source: Duolingo Friend Streak and Friends Quest. Evidence: [duoplanet](https://duoplanet.com/duolingo-friend-streak/), observed.
6. Represent a cohort's result as the SUM of individual outcomes, never a single ranked table. Source: Chess.com Club Matches. Evidence: [Chess.com support](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8649115-what-are-club-matches), observed.
7. Ambient, automatic activity from the log itself, no separate "post" step. Source: Letterboxd. Evidence: [Letterboxd FAQ](https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/), observed.
8. A friend's moment surfaced on the home screen itself, outside the app. Source: Locket. Evidence: [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/01/13/locket-app-iphone-widgets/), observed.
9. Structure (channels/sections) should shrink for a small cohort, not just grow for a large one. Source: Discord. Evidence: [BuildMyDiscord](https://buildmydiscord.com/en/blog/discord-channel-organization-guide-best-channel-structure-for-active-communities), observed.
10. Invite a friend into one shared session with no account required of them. Source: Fiit. Evidence: [fiit.tv](https://fiit.tv/), observed.
11. Location-scoped forum for arranging real in-person training together. Source: Sweat. Evidence: [Sweat](https://sweat.com/blogs/fitness/goal-based-challenges), observed.
12. Fire the encouragement notification on a completed goal WITH a direct one-tap reply action attached to it. Source: Apple Fitness. Evidence: [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/share-your-activity-apd68a69f5c7/watchos), observed.
13. A hard cap of roughly 20 members is the proven size for a small, non-competitive close group to feel alive without feeling empty. Source: Oura Circles. Evidence: [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/introducing-oura-circles/), observed.
14. An earned, activity-gated (not purchasable) badge to surface genuinely active groups above dead ones. Source: Strava Verified Club. Evidence: [Strava support](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401828-verified-badge-for-clubs-on-strava), observed (already logged in doc 11, re-cited as a borrow item).
15. Granular presence (visible-but-activity-hidden vs fully invisible), not one on/off toggle. Source: Steam. Evidence: [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-to-hide-steam-activity/), observed.
16. Cold start: seed inside ONE already-dense real-world group (a single gym), the "smallest network that is stable and can grow on its own," rather than spreading thin across many. Source: network-effects literature (Andrew Chen's "Cold Start Problem"; NFX "atomic network"). Evidence: [andrewchen.com](https://andrewchen.com/how-to-solve-the-cold-start-problem-for-social-products/); [Medium/NFX](https://medium.com/immutable/critical-mass-937355e1f179), observed.
17. Design encouragement so CLOSE friends never need reciprocity to keep it flowing, while a lightweight reciprocity nudge sustains exchange among wider-cohort acquaintances. Source: study on liking and reciprocity. Evidence: [JCMC](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/28/2/zmac036/6987873), observed, peer-reviewed.
18. Peer encouragement measurably spreads exercise behaviour: less-active people are influenced by more-active friends (not the reverse), mutual-friend density strengthens the effect; direct support for the friend-group loop the founder asked for. Source: Aral & Nicolaides, Nature Communications 2017 (1.1M runners, 5 years). Evidence: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms14753); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5399289/), observed, peer-reviewed.
19. Co-participation and emotional support raise activity adherence; social connectedness was the leading predictor of 12-month adherence in a peer-walking programme. Source: systematic review, social support and physical activity. Evidence: [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/307624941_Social_support_and_physical_activity_participation_among_healthy_adults_A_systematic_review_of_prospective_studies), observed, peer-reviewed.
20. Batch encouragement/activity notifications to a daily digest, cap volume, hold overnight; fewer, higher-quality notifications lower engagement briefly then raise it over time (Meta's own 2022 finding, second-hand via this industry source). Source/evidence: [Prosocial Design Network](https://www.prosocialdesign.org/library/batching-notifications); [Courier](https://www.courier.com/blog/how-to-reduce-notification-fatigue-7-proven-product-strategies-for-saas), observed.

## Mechanisms to refuse

1. High-five/cheer taps with NO block-user control: Peloton members documented harassment via repeated High Fives despite a 1/minute rate limit. Evidence of harm: [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/high-five-creeps-peloton-stopped-102700958.html), observed. Any encourage-tap needs a block, not just a rate limit.
2. Individual competitive ranking of a personal metric: Duolingo Leagues are reported to cause anxiety and shift behaviour toward defending rank rather than the underlying goal. Evidence of harm/misfit: [gamification synthesis](https://medium.com/@flordaniele/duolingo-case-study-research-on-gamification-90b5bac3ada0), observed; consistent with Volyume's existing refusal to rank weight/bodyweight.
