# S1 banked summary — external evidence base (Opus research, 2026-08-28)

Full report delivered in session. This bank holds the load-bearing evidence
the lead design ruling will cite. Every item carries its URL in the full
report; the decisive ones are repeated here.

## The naming/entry question — decisive published evidence

1. DfE "Apply for teacher training" design history: replacing the gatekeeping
   question "Are you disabled?" with ONE ungated combined list ("Do you have
   any of these disabilities or health conditions?") took disclosure from 4%
   to 15% in matched 28-day windows (~8,000 applications). Their content
   rule: "front-load the most recognisable words and phrases".
   becoming-a-teacher.design-history.education.gov.uk
2. ONS: Equality Act definition vs Washington Group instrument — overlap
   UNDER HALF; commonly missed limitations are pain, fatigue, breathing.
   Any entry point requiring "am I the sort of person this is for?"
   misroutes a large fraction of the intended audience.
3. Activity Alliance's own definition of disability already contains
   "long-term health condition, impairment or illness" — the sector does not
   draw the injury/disability line where a two-door design would.
4. We Are Undefeatable (NHS-backed, physio-designed, co-produced): consumer
   framing NEVER uses "disability"; its app configures by TRAINING POSITION
   (sitting / supported standing / standing / floor), amount, duration —
   capability and intent, not diagnosis or identity.
5. Freeletics is the counter-case: split "limitations" (soreness-only) from
   injury, and the injury half does not exist — injured users are told to
   stop training and leave, in copy that uses GOV.UK-proscribed "suffering
   from".
=> One surface, one ungated door, duration/permanence captured INSIDE as an
   attribute; label names the effect on training; subtitle front-loads the
   recognisable words (injury, pain, long-term condition, disability...).

## Language law (GOV.UK + Scope + Activity Alliance)

- Identity-first for the category ("disabled people"), person-first for named
  conditions ("someone with diabetes"). Never: suffers from, wheelchair-bound,
  confined to, special needs, differently abled, able-bodied (use
  non-disabled), the disabled, vulnerable, victim of, afflicted by.
- Scope: "We respect other people's language choices... people's right to
  describe themselves."
- Normal tone of voice; no patronising; avoid medical labels.

## Safety/clinical wording

- NHS three-tier red-flag pattern (symptom-listed, action-tiered, never
  diagnostic): "Non-urgent advice: See a GP if:" / "Urgent advice: ...111
  if:" / "Immediate action required: Call 999 or go to A&E if:".
- Tone model (We Are Undefeatable, Dr Natasha Jones, verbatim): "Most people
  can get active without medical advice. However, if you have difficulty
  managing the symptoms of your condition, seeking medical advice before
  increasing your activity levels may be helpful." Permission first, trigger,
  softened recommendation. WHO uses "may wish to consult".
- NHS shoulder-pain Don'ts include "do not make up your own strenuous
  exercises or use heavy gym equipment" — and Do's include "do not completely
  stop using your shoulder"; keep-moving is the published norm
  (Versus Arthritis: "focus on slow and gentle movement during a flare up,
  rather than stopping altogether").
- Versus Arthritis flare deload ORDER for resistance training, verbatim:
  "reduce the load or weight for each movement, and then the number of sets."
- UK CMO disabled-adults guideline: SAME strength dose as general adults
  (2x/week) — adaptation is in HOW, not HOW MUCH. SCI guideline scopes to
  "each major functioning muscle group" — adapts the target, not the dose.
- MHRA line: signposting ("see your GP") is safe; naming/inferring diagnosis
  or treatment is device territory; disclaimers do not rescue crossed lines.
- ICO: explicit consent must specify the nature of the special-category data
  and be separate from other consents (the shipped separate capability
  consent is the right shape).

## Competitive field (full teardown in session report)

- NOBODY with a progression engine markets this. Fitbod: two disconnected
  systems, and its own docs admit Focus Exercises OVERRIDE limitations (the
  trust-killer). Hevy: "Injuries" label (invisible to disabled users), no
  duration concept, no review prompt, admits incomplete. Apple/Peloton/NTC:
  content-side only, zero configuration; Peloton's stated design insight:
  never make the user translate modifications themselves. GOWOD's App Store
  reviews carry the sharpest complaint in the lane: "the mobility assessment
  doesn't allow for the option of 'can't get into the pose'". Zing: 3 coarse
  categories, fails on specifics. Exakt: diagnosis-keyed (MHRA-exposed).
  Accessercise (peer-reviewed usability study, 12 disabled participants):
  impairment-first config validated, but NO progression engine — participants
  asked for exactly what Volyume already has.
- Common structural weakness: every product treats restriction as a
  SUBTRACTION (remove/replace/warn). None treats it as a SPECIFICATION that
  shapes what the user DOES get, with explanation, review cadence and a
  route back. That is the 10/10 opening.

## The 20-pattern digest (headlines; full text in session report)

1 ungated entry · 2 name-by-effect + recognisable-words subtitle · 3 one
surface, permanence inside · 4 plain-language statements not diagnosis/body
map · 5 "I can't do this" always a first-class answer · 6 in-the-moment
capture feeding the durable profile · 7 fixed-option WHY at capture (reason
selects the adaptation) · 8 effect preview at point of choice · 9 per-
exercise why-here/why-not-here (inverse explanation) · 10 never subtract
silently — replace toward the same training purpose · 11 graded response
(exclude / caution / reduced load; deload order load-then-sets) · 12 built
for fluctuation (day-level state never overwrites the durable profile) ·
13 configure by capability and position, not only body part · 14 never make
the user translate modifications · 15 app-prompted review + gradual
refusable route back · 16 NHS-structure signposting, never diagnosis ·
17 permission-first tone · 18 named, separate, withdrawable consent at the
surface · 19 low cognitive load (short lists, no walls of text) · 20 banned-
constructions copy audit.
