## 2. Failure-mode catalogue (with quoted cases)

### FM-1: Mid-session loss — the cardinal sin
- Strava Android: "Strava has recovered from a problem" → "the recording is lost"; long-ride crashes at hour 4–6 ([source](https://communityhub.strava.com/devices-and-connections-6/android-app-recording-crashes-during-long-bike-rides-9383)).
- Strong Apple Watch: "crashes multiple times every workout, almost every set"; "workouts will not transfer to the phone" ([source](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/464254577/strong-workout-tracker-gym-log/problems)).
- Jefit: set-tracking silently failed after a phone migration — "it didn't track the sets during the exercise and did not put a check mark" ([source](https://www.jefit.com/q&a/123475030/jayfroman/)).
- **Lesson:** mid-set persistence + crash recovery is the single highest-stakes reliability feature. Volyume already has crash/stale-workout recovery; Strava's "save what we have, even split in two" is the floor users accept.

### FM-2: Sync wipes and account-identity confusion
- Strong's own help centre: the most likely cause of "where did my workouts go?" is "you have accidentally created a new account instead of logging into your existing Strong Account" via a different SSO method — and "please do not delete the app or you risk data loss" ([source](https://help.strongapp.io/article/217-lost-data)).
- MyFitnessPal: "after being asked to log in again, all their history including recipes and diary logs disappear" ([community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10881399/lost-all-food-history-data)); a confirmed bug in 26.18.2 where Android data "was not syncing… causing the Diary on Web to appear blank" ([known issues](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032274332-Known-Issues-Android-App)).
- Patreon post title that circulates in keto/thyroid communities: "MYFITNESSPAL lost 6 years of my data." ([source](https://www.patreon.com/posts/myfitnesspal-6-48816889)).
- **Lesson:** "data loss" is usually *perceived* loss caused by identity/sync design, not storage failure. Guard the login path (detect empty-account-after-SSO, warn before fresh-start), never let uninstall be destructive without warning.

### FM-3: Update-induced wipes and migration regressions
- MyFitnessPal: "Why do the updates remove my history and I have to look everything up again" — users lost "almost all saved foods… very frustrating and time-consuming to rebuild" and asked to *uninstall updates* to get hundreds of saved items back ([community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10945657/why-do-the-updates-remove-my-history-and-i-have-to-look-everything-up-again)); "previous food diaries, weight logs and progress photos gone" ([community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10882968/previous-food-diaries-weight-logs-and-progress-photos-gone)).
- Jefit v11.35.3 rollout: "a disaster with little or no UAT" ([source](https://www.jefit.com/q&a/97270376/AJCrowley/)).
- **Lesson:** Volyume's 71 migrations + schema-drift tests are exactly the right defence; pre-migration on-device backup snapshots would close the loop.

### FM-4: Cloud-outage paralysis vs local-first grace
- Garmin 2020: cloud down for days, watches fine — "gigabytes worth of storage for past activities… would all likely sync without issue once Garmin Connect was restored" ([source](https://www.androidcentral.com/garmin-connect-suffers-multi-hour-outage)). Counterpoint: same-day steps were lost and on-device retention windows (7–14 days) created edge losses ([forums](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-andriod/234214/steps-from-ransomware-period/1113540)).
- Garmin start of 2025: another worldwide outage, "users unable to log their session stats" ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/garmin-smartwatch-update-issues-connect-app/)).
- MFP Jan 2018: food search outage surfaced as a confusing "offline mode" banner while users were online ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/my-fitness-pal-app-says-offline-mode-app-down-2636021)).
- **Lesson:** local-first wins outages, but only with adequate local retention and honest UI states ("server down" ≠ "you're offline").

### FM-5: History retention policy experienced as data loss
- MFP free users: "after almost 8 years of data collection, MyFitnessPal restricted access to only the last 2 years… for premium subscribers" ([community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10955067/lost-previous-data)).
- **Lesson:** paywalling *past* data is the fastest way to convert a loyal user into a public detractor. Volyume's tier line ("personal bests, progress stats" free) should explicitly never time-limit history.

### FM-6: Streak/derived-data fragility
- MFP: one user lost a 340-day streak approaching the one-year milestone; another lost an 85-day streak to a timezone reset and was "very angry"; MFP had to build a dedicated streak-restore tool ([community 1](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10297030/lost-my-streak), [2](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/1128959/lost-another-streak-incredibly-frustrated), [help](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032624931)).
- **Lesson:** any future streak-like mechanic in Volyume needs timezone-safe definitions and a self-service repair path.

### FM-7: Offline gaps in nutrition apps (structural, licence-driven)
- MFP: "When your device does not have internet access, you will not be able to access the food database" ([help](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032622851)).
- Cronometer: "no full offline version because the databases… are very large, and license agreements won't allow them to store these on your device"; team states **no plans** to add it; user demand threads span 2017–2022 ([forums](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/1094/offline-use), [2022](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/18680)).
- MacroFactor: by its own admission "not an offline-first application"; offline logging works, sync on reconnect ([help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/28-does-macrofactor-have-an-offline-mode)).
- **Lesson:** a bundled, fully-offline food database (Volyume has one) is structurally rare in this category — the leaders either can't or won't ship it.

### FM-8: Wearable/integration sync flakiness
- Cronometer: "Withings disconnects daily and Fitbit records exercise twice"; Apple Health entries timestamped midnight ([pet-peeves thread](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/4127/), [Nutrola analysis](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/we-analyzed-50000-calorie-tracker-reviews-what-users-actually-complain-about-2026)).
- Whoop: sync delays, Android crashes; Strong vs Hevy watch-sync gap ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/health-tech/whoop-review/), [PonteFuerte](https://www.pontefuerteai.com/blog/hevy-vs-strong-vs-pontefuerteai-workout-app-comparison)).
- **Lesson:** integrations are the largest *ongoing* complaint surface for otherwise-reliable apps. As Volyume builds wearable integration (Pro), expect this to dominate support volume; design for idempotent imports (no double-records) and visible last-sync state.

### FM-9: Performance decay through bloat, and battery
- Jefit: updates make the app "heavier… slowing down phones"; ads "interrupt your flow" ([eTechShout](https://etechshout.com/jefit-app-review/)).
- Cronometer: "slow startup time of the Android app" is a standing complaint ([forums](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/4127/)).
- Battery: health apps with background refresh "can wake every 30–90 seconds to sync data… even while a workout is active"; disabling background refresh for MFP+Strava cut an identical HIIT session's battery use to 39% ([Alibaba product insights](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/why-does-my-apple-watch-battery-drain-faster-during-hiit-sensor-overload-vs-background-app-interference.html)); runners report "phones die before workouts could upload to Strava" ([StudyFinds](https://studyfinds.org/fitness-app-motivation-study-myfitnesspal/), [Strava support](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/216918987-Extending-Battery-Life-Android)).
- **Lesson:** for non-GPS loggers, battery complaints come from background sync aggressiveness, not sensors. Volyume's fire-and-forget sync with retry queue should remain event-driven, never polling.

## 3. Benchmark numbers

### Stability (Google Play vitals — hard thresholds)
- **User-perceived crash rate: 1.09%** of DAU = "bad behaviour" threshold; **8%** on any single device model ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844486), [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2022/10/raising-bar-on-technical-quality-on-google-play.html)).
- **User-perceived ANR rate: 0.47%** of DAU overall; 8% per device model.
- Consequence of breach: reduced discoverability, per-device steering away, possible store-listing warning.

### Crash-free sessions (industry, 2025–2026 reports)
- Median across mobile: **99.95%** crash-free sessions; 75th percentile **99.99%** ([Luciq benchmark](https://www.luciq.ai/blog/benchmarking-crash-free-sessions-for-mobile-apps-whats-a-good-crash-free-rate), [developer-tech](https://www.developer-tech.com/news/general-app-stability-improves-crash-free-sessions-near-100/)).
- **Health & Fitness is the best category: median 99.98%** crash-free sessions — i.e. the bar Volyume competes against is the highest of any vertical ([Okoone/industry data](https://www.okoone.com/spark/product-design-research/mobile-app-performance-is-hitting-peak-stability/)).
- Apps below 99.9% crash-free sessions are markedly more likely to sit below 3★; the median for >3★ apps is ~99.95% ([Luciq](https://www.luciq.ai/blog/benchmarking-crash-free-sessions-for-mobile-apps-whats-a-good-crash-free-rate)).

### Speed expectations
- **49% of users expect launch in ≤2 s**; ~1 in 5 abandon at >3 s to usable; 53% abandon loads >3 s; each extra second ≈ −16% satisfaction; 80% give a poorly performing app ≤3 chances before uninstalling ([Digia startup guide](https://www.digia.tech/post/app-startup-time-performance-guide/), [Embrace](https://embrace.io/blog/why-should-you-care-about-your-mobile-apps-startup-time/), [Luciq launch-time guide](https://www.luciq.ai/blog/understanding-cold-hot-and-warm-app-launch-time)). Practical target: **cold start <2 s on mid-range Android**.
- Logging latency: no published numbers, but reviewer language is consistent — Strong praised because "the app loads quickly, which matters more than people realize when you're mid-set and trying to log before you forget your numbers" ([Setgraph](https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/best-workout-apps-free-reddit)). The implicit budget for "open app → log a set" is a single rest period fragment: a few seconds, one-handed.

### Commercial context (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, 115k+ apps)
- Health & Fitness leads revenue-per-install: **$0.48 D14 / $0.66 D60**; median annual LTV **$35.64**; renewal rates 54% weekly / 57% monthly / 25% annual ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/), [renewal benchmarks](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category/)). Reliability is a retention input: in the only category where users pay for *daily habit* tools, a single data-loss event ends the subscription.

## 4. Offline-first as a marketed feature: who claims it, who delivers

- **Strength loggers: table stakes.** Strong, Hevy, JEFIT, FitNotes, Setgraph, Gymaholic all support full offline logging, and Hevy markets it explicitly: "works fully offline and syncs your data when you reconnect… essential for gym use since many gyms have poor WiFi" ([Hevy features](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/), [Setgraph](https://setgraph.app/ai-blog/best-app-to-log-workouts)). Niche apps lead store listings with it ("fully functional workout tracker which works completely offline") ([App Store example](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gym-tracker-simple-workout-log/id1506898151)). Claiming offline here buys parity, not differentiation.
- **Nutrition trackers: structurally weak offline.** MFP can't search foods offline; Cronometer refuses full offline on licensing grounds despite five years of user requests; MacroFactor offers "limited offline mode" by its own description. **No major nutrition app ships a fully offline food database.** Volyume's bundled food DB for offline logging is a genuine category outlier.
- **Do users notice/pay?** They notice *absence* loudly (Cronometer's multi-year forum threads; MFP "offline mode" complaint threads) and reward *presence* quietly through ratings and "never lost a workout"-type word of mouth (Strong's "rock solid" reputation, FitNotes' decade-long Reddit standing). Offline-first sells as a *trust* attribute, not a feature bullet: the apps that win on it (FitNotes, Strong, Hevy) pair it with **visible export/backup**, which converts invisible architecture into a credible promise. FitNotes' privacy page — data stays on device unless you opt in — doubles as a reliability claim ([FitNotes privacy](http://www.fitnotesapp.com/privacy/)).

## 5. Implications for Volyume

**Where Volyume already leads (and should say so):**
1. **Offline nutrition logging with a bundled food DB** is rarer than offline workout logging — none of MFP/Cronometer/MacroFactor fully deliver it. This is Volyume's strongest under-marketed reliability claim: "log food in a basement gym, on a flight, anywhere — search included." Strength-side offline is parity; nutrition-side offline is differentiation.
2. **Export + full backup/restore already shipped** (`SettingsDataScreen`: CSV export, whole-database backup, "Your data is always yours. Export or back up any time, no account required."). This matches the FitNotes trust formula and exceeds Hevy (whose support cannot recover deleted workouts). Surface this in store listing and onboarding, not just Settings.
3. **Crash/stale-workout recovery + wall-clock timers** address FM-1 directly — the failure mode with the angriest quotes in the category.

**Where the category's scar tissue predicts Volyume's future risks:**
4. **Account-identity confusion (FM-2)** is the #1 *perceived* data-loss cause for the most reliable competitor (Strong). Volyume's SSO/login flow should detect "existing local data + fresh empty cloud account" and interpose a merge/confirm step, and warn on sign-out/uninstall paths.
5. **Update-migration wipes (FM-3):** with 71 migrations and counting, an automatic pre-migration local snapshot (auto-restore on failed migration) would make MFP-style "the update ate my saved foods" structurally impossible.
6. **Wearable integration (FM-8)** will be the next complaint magnet as the Pro wearable feature lands: idempotent imports, duplicate suppression, and a visible "last synced" state are the lessons from Cronometer/Whoop.
7. **Never paywall history (FM-5)** and, if streaks are ever added, make them timezone-safe and user-repairable (FM-6).

**Reliability targets (category-calibrated):**
- Crash-free sessions ≥ **99.98%** (Health & Fitness median — parity is the entry fee), stretch 99.99%.
- User-perceived crash < **1.09%** DAU and ANR < **0.47%** DAU as hard ceilings (Play vitals), with per-device monitoring (8% rule).
- Cold start < **2 s** on mid-range Android; "open → set logged" within one rest-period glance.
- Sync: event-driven only (no polling wake-ups — the documented 30–90 s background-refresh battery pattern is the category's silent battery killer).

**What leaders advertise (and don't):** Nobody advertises crash rates. The reliability leaders advertise *proxies users can verify*: offline operation (Hevy), export/no-account data ownership (FitNotes), verified data and no ads/trackers (MacroFactor, Cronometer). Volyume already possesses all three proxies; the gap is messaging, not engineering.
