## Carousel 1 — "When should you actually change your calories?"

**Working title:** The hold-or-change decision.
**Format:** decision-explainer. 6 slides.

**Slide 1 (cover).**
Heading: When should you actually change your calories?
Supporting line: Most people move too soon.
Visual: large amber question-set heading on near-black. No imagery. Type only.

**Slide 2.**
Heading: One morning is not a signal.
Supporting line: Bodyweight swings with water, food timing, salt and sleep.
Visual: a single flat dot on a baseline, small and lonely, amber on black.

**Slide 3.**
Heading: Read the weekly trend, not the daily number.
Supporting line: The line across the week tells you more than any one reading.
Number: 7 mornings, one trend.
Visual: seven small dots with a smooth amber trend line drawn through them.

**Slide 4.**
Heading: Wait for enough mornings before you judge it.
Supporting line: Three or more weigh-ins in a week give the trend something to stand on.
Number: 3+ mornings.
Visual: three dots filling in on the same baseline, the trend line firming up.

**Slide 5.**
Heading: Then decide. Change, or hold.
Supporting line: Sometimes the honest call is to leave it exactly where it is.
Visual: a simple two-way fork diagram, one path amber (change), one path amber-outline (hold).

**Slide 6 (close).**
Heading: This is the decision Volyume makes each week.
Supporting line: It reads your logged data, calls change or hold, and writes the reason down.
Visual: a compact "weekly decision" card mock in amber on black, with a short reason line beneath a change/hold label. Small wordmark.

**Caption:**
Your weight moves for a dozen reasons that have nothing to do with fat. That is why one morning on the scale should not change your plan. Volyume looks at the weekly trend across a few mornings, decides whether to change your calories or hold them, and writes down why it made that call. On Google Play if you want to see how the weekly decision works. 🟡

## Carousel 2 — "Sets per week is the number that matters"

**Working title:** Weekly sets as the unit of volume.
**Format:** decision-explainer / concept. 6 slides.

**Slide 1 (cover).**
Heading: Sets per week is the number that matters.
Supporting line: Not the number on the bar.
Visual: bold amber numeral-led heading, near-black ground. Type only.

**Slide 2.**
Heading: Count working sets, per muscle, per week.
Supporting line: That is the unit that tracks how much real work a muscle got.
Visual: a small tally diagram grouped under a muscle label, amber ticks on black.

**Slide 3.**
Heading: There is a floor and there is a ceiling.
Supporting line: Below the floor you undertrain. Past the ceiling you recover slowly and progress stalls.
Visual: a horizontal band between two amber lines marked "too little" and "too much", a comfortable zone lit in the middle.

**Slide 4.**
Heading: More sets is not automatically more progress.
Supporting line: Adding volume you cannot recover from just costs you recovery.
Visual: a curve that rises then flattens, amber on black, the flat part clearly shown.

**Slide 5.**
Heading: Add work only when the signals say so.
Supporting line: Steady logs, sets completed as planned, recovery holding up.
Visual: three small state indicators feeding into one "add a set" gate, amber.

**Slide 6 (close).**
Heading: Volyume tracks your weekly sets and decides when to add.
Supporting line: Advanced progression watches the volume and moves it only when the data supports it.
Visual: a weekly-sets counter ticking from one value to the next, with a short reason line. Small wordmark.

**Caption:**
The weight on the bar gets all the attention, but weekly working sets per muscle is what actually maps to how hard a muscle is being trained. There is a sensible range for it, and pushing past the top of that range tends to cost recovery rather than buy growth. Volyume counts your weekly sets and adds work only when your logs support it. It is on Google Play.

**First comment:**
Volyume on Google Play: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.volyume

**Format:** positioning explainer. 7 slides.

**Slide 1 (cover).**
Heading: Your logger knows what you did.
Supporting line: It cannot tell you what to do next.
Visual: two stacked lines of amber type, the second slightly dimmed, near-black ground.

**Slide 2.**

**Slide 3.**
Heading: A food app records what you ate.
Supporting line: Calories and macros, totalled for the day.
Visual: a simple daily total ring or bar, amber on black.

**Slide 4.**

Supporting line: What do I actually change now?

Supporting line: Recording is solved. The weekly call is the work.
Visual: a filled "recorded" box beside an empty "decided" box, the empty one outlined in amber.

**Slide 7 (close).**
Heading: Volyume is the connected system that makes that call.
Supporting line: It reads your training and your food, then decides change or hold with the reason shown.
Visual: the two streams from slide 5 resolving into a single change/hold decision card. Small wordmark.

**Caption:**
A workout logger is very good at remembering your sets, and a food app is very good at totalling your day, but neither one tells you what to change when the week is over. That decision is left to you. Volyume reads both your logged training and your nutrition, then makes the weekly call to change something or hold, and shows you the reason. Weekly Precision Coaching is part of Pro, and the app is on Google Play.

## Carousel 4 — "Why the app sometimes tells you to change nothing"

**Working title:** Holds are a feature.
**Format:** myth-calm-down / anti-hype. 6 slides.

**Slide 1 (cover).**
Heading: Why the app sometimes tells you to change nothing.
Supporting line: A hold is a decision, not a shrug.
Visual: the word "HOLD" set large and calm in amber on near-black. Type only.

**Slide 2.**
Heading: Not every week carries a real signal.
Supporting line: Some weeks are mostly noise, and noise is not a reason to act.
Visual: a jittery amber line with no clear direction, on black.

**Slide 3.**
Heading: Changing on noise makes progress harder to read.
Supporting line: Move too often and you can never tell what the last change did.
Visual: the same jittery line with several premature "change" markers cluttering it.

**Slide 4.**
Heading: When the data is thin, the app waits.
Supporting line: It needs enough observations before it will act, and it will not guess.
Visual: a half-filled data meter paused below an "act" threshold line, amber.

**Slide 5.**
Heading: Patience is the honest answer here.
Supporting line: Holding this week is often what keeps next week readable.
Visual: a steady, unhurried amber baseline holding flat, calm negative space.

**Slide 6 (close).**
Heading: Same inputs, same decision, every week explained.
Supporting line: Volyume tells you when to hold and why, not just when to push.
Visual: a change/hold card resting on "hold" with a short plain reason beneath. Small wordmark.

**Caption:**
A coaching tool that only ever tells you to change something is not reading your data, it is just keeping you busy. Plenty of weeks are mostly noise, and acting on noise makes your real progress harder to read later. When there is not enough to go on, Volyume holds and tells you why, because waiting a week is sometimes the honest call. It is on Google Play if that is the kind of steadiness you want.

## Self-check notes (writer)

- No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no emoji in body copy (one 🟡 in
  Carousel 1 caption only, within the §9.13 one-emoji social allowance).
- No negation-pivot sentences of the banned "it's not just X, it's Y" shape.
  Carousel 3 cover ("knows what you did / cannot tell you what to do next")
  and Carousel 4 slide 1 ("a decision, not a shrug") are contrastive on
  concrete content, not the empty "more than a tracker" pivot; flagged here
  for the reviewer to confirm.
- No decorative triads: the three-item lines (C1 S2, C2 S5, C3 S5) each carry
  distinct, load-bearing content rather than rhythm.
- Sentence rhythm varied; no repeated parallel cadence of three.
- No hype adjectives, no scene-setting openers, no rhetorical-question opener
  answered on the next line. Carousel titles are questions used as headings,
  not opener-and-answer copy.
- British English throughout.
- No trial, price, rating, or user-count claims. No before/after or outcome
  promise anywhere.
