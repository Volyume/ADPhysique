# CLAIMS STANDARDS — Volyume Marketing HQ

**Status:** Governing document. Supreme over every other marketing artefact.
**Applies to:** all outward-facing content, without exception.
**Owner of the gate:** the compliance-reviewer agent.
**Companion file:** `marketing/hq/PRODUCT-FACTS.md` (the single source of verified fact).

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document is the compliance constitution of the Volyume marketing
department. It defines what may and may not be said in public, and it is the
standard every artefact is gated against before it is allowed to reach a real
person.

It applies to **everything public**, including but not limited to:

- Google Play and Apple App Store listings (titles, subtitles, descriptions,
  screenshots, promotional text, release notes).
- The Volyume website (volyume.app) — every page and every article.
- Social posts on every platform, current and future.
- Community posts (Reddit and elsewhere), even when posted personally by the
  founder.
- Replies to store reviews and to comments.
- Emails, newsletters and waitlist messages.
- Any creator, affiliate or partner content produced on Volyume's behalf.

If a piece of content will be read by someone who is not the founder or an
internal agent, it is in scope.

Nothing in this document is a target or an aspiration. It is a floor. When a
rule here and a growth opportunity conflict, the rule wins, every time.

---

## 2. The Claim Rule

**Every factual claim in public content must be true of the shipped app and
traceable to `marketing/hq/PRODUCT-FACTS.md`.**

This has four parts, all binding:

1. **True of the shipped app.** Not the roadmap, not the beta, not the
   intention. If a feature is not live for the audience being addressed, it is
   not claimed as available. "Coming soon" is permitted only when
   PRODUCT-FACTS records it as genuinely imminent and it is clearly labelled
   as not yet available.
2. **Traceable.** Any number, mechanism, feature name or capability stated in
   public must correspond to an entry in PRODUCT-FACTS. If a reviewer cannot
   point to the line in PRODUCT-FACTS that backs a claim, the claim fails the
   gate.
3. **Unverified numbers are not used.** Prices, day counts, percentages,
   accuracy figures, user counts, ratings — if it is not verified in
   PRODUCT-FACTS, it does not appear in public copy. No estimates dressed as
   facts. No rounding that changes meaning.
4. **Unknowns are handled honestly.** Where a fact is not yet established, it
   is recorded internally as `UNKNOWN` (in briefs, drafts and review notes)
   and is simply **omitted** from public copy. An unknown is never filled with
   a guess, an industry average, or a hopeful figure.

When PRODUCT-FACTS and a draft disagree, PRODUCT-FACTS wins and the draft is
corrected. When PRODUCT-FACTS is silent, the claim is removed, not invented.

## 3. Approved trial wording

The trial is the single most distortion-prone thing we say, because it
combines two separate mechanisms. Get this wrong and it reads as a false
"free" promise. The exact structure is:

- **14 days of full Pro access with no card required.**
- To continue, the user opts into the Google Play subscription; **Google
  Play's introductory offer then provides 7 further free days before the first
  charge.**
- **Total access before the first charge is up to 21 days.**
- The first charge occurs after roughly 21 days of total access.

Two weekly check-ins can occur before the first payment **for a typical user**,
but this depends on the user's own timing and data and is never stated as a
guarantee (see the do-not-say list).

### 3.1 Approved concise line

> Start with 14 days of full Pro, no card needed. Continue through Google Play
> and its intro offer adds 7 more free days before your first payment.

### 3.2 Approved expanded paragraph

> Volyume Pro begins with 14 days of full access and no card required, so you
> can see the whole system before deciding. If you choose to continue, you opt
> into the subscription through Google Play, and Google Play's introductory
> offer adds a further 7 free days before your first payment is taken. That is
> up to 21 days of access before you are charged anything. You can cancel in
> Google Play at any time before the charge.

Both are written for public use: no em dashes, no exclamation marks. Reviewers
may trim these but must not merge the two mechanisms into one number.

### 3.3 DO NOT SAY (trial)

These phrasings are prohibited because they merge, round, or over-promise:

- "21 days free" — merges two distinct mechanisms into a single false headline.
- "No card needed for 21 days" — false; the card is added at the Google Play
  opt-in, before the 7 extra days.
- "Free for three weeks" — same merge, rounded, still false.
- Any phrasing that presents the 14 days and the 7 days as one continuous
  card-free free period.
- Any wording implying the subscription is not entered until day 21.
- Any guarantee of "two check-ins before you pay" or a specific number of
  check-ins, stated as certain regardless of the user's data or timing.
- Any claim that continuing is automatic or effortless in a way that hides the
  Google Play opt-in step.

When in doubt, describe the two steps plainly and separately. The truth is a
good offer; it does not need inflating.

## 4. Pricing

- Volyume Pro is **£2.99 per month** or **£19.99 per year**, UK.
- **The price shown by Google Play (or the App Store) at the point of purchase
  is always authoritative.** Public copy defers to it.
- Never advertise a price other than the current one. Old prices, launch
  prices, other regions' prices and hoped-for prices are never shown.
- If a price changes, all public copy must be updated before the next
  publish, and any pricing claim is re-gated. Pricing-claim changes are a
  founder-only decision (see OPERATING-CHARTER autonomy boundaries).
- Do not imply an annual saving with a specific figure unless that figure is
  verified in PRODUCT-FACTS and matches the live store prices.

## 5. Prohibited absolutely

Each of the following is banned outright. The rationale is one line; the ban is
not negotiable on the strength of a clever exception.

- **Guaranteed outcomes** (weight loss, muscle gain, transformation, or any
  timeline promise). — We cannot guarantee a person's result and it is unlawful
  and dishonest to imply we can.
- **Before/after transformation advertising.** — It sells an outcome we do not
  guarantee and it fuels body-image harm.
- **Body-shaming, guilt, or negative self-image messaging.** — Against the
  product's entire ethic and a regulatory red flag in this category.
- **Invented testimonials, reviews, ratings, user counts or results.** — They
  are fabrications; with zero users today any such claim is a lie.
- **Medical, diagnostic, therapeutic or eating-disorder-treatment claims.** —
  Volyume is not a medical device or a treatment and must never present as one.
- **Claiming Progress Scan measures body fat or is medical.** — It is a
  photo-based progress score with confidence, not a measurement and not
  clinical.
- **Claiming Volyume replaces a good human coach.** — It does not; the honest
  position is that it is a system for self-coached lifters.
- **Claiming barcode scanning is free.** — Barcode scanning is a Pro feature;
  saying otherwise misrepresents the free tier.
- **Superiority claims** ("best", "most accurate", "number one") without a
  verifiable, published basis. — Objective superiority claims require
  substantiation we do not have.
- **Mass unsolicited messaging or automated community posting.** — It is spam,
  it breaks platform rules, and it will destroy trust and reputation.

## 6. Qualified claims — say it this precise way

Some true claims are fine only when phrased correctly. These are the required
framings.

- **"Personalised."** Means built from the user's own inputs and applied
  through deterministic rules. Never "learns you", never "adapts to you like an
  AI", never any wording implying the software forms a model of the person.
- **"Coach" / "coaching."** Means the structured weekly Precision Coaching
  decision (change or hold, with reasons). Never implies a human coach, a chat
  assistant, or a conversational agent.
- **The engine.** Describe it as **"deterministic coaching, not generative
  coaching"** — explainable, rule-based, the same inputs give the same
  decision. Avoid an unqualified "no AI" slogan, which invites argument and
  mis-sets expectations; describe what it *is* (deterministic, explainable)
  rather than only what it is not.
- **Privacy.** Describe the actual posture, not a vague absolute. Approved
  substance: data is encrypted on the device, stored with EU data residency,
  there are no ads, no data is sold, and no personal identifiers are sent to
  analytics. Do not say "completely private" or "we never see your data" as a
  bare slogan; state the specific, true posture instead.

The test: a qualified claim passes only if a sceptical, informed reader would
still find it accurate after reading the app's own description of the feature.

## 7. UK advertising law basics (ASA / CAP)

We take a conservative line because advertising fitness and body image to
consumers is a sensitive category and the regulator treats it as such.

- **Ads must be obviously identifiable as ads.** No disguising marketing as
  neutral editorial or organic chatter.
- **`#ad`** (clear and prominent) on any future creator, influencer or paid
  partner content, and on any content where value has been exchanged.
- **No health claims implying the treatment, prevention or cure of any
  disease or condition.** This includes eating disorders and obesity as
  medical conditions.
- **Substantiation before publication.** Any objective, measurable claim must
  have documented evidence in PRODUCT-FACTS *before* it is published, not
  after a challenge.
- **Weight-loss-adjacent advertising must never target or feature under-18s.**
  No under-18 audiences, no under-18 talent, no creative that reads as aimed at
  minors.
- **Conservative by default.** Where a claim sits near a line, we do not make
  it. The cost of a withdrawn claim is far higher than the cost of a quieter
  one.

## 8. Tone

- Calm, plain, evidence-led. State what the app does and why.
- No hype, no fake urgency, no countdowns that are not real, no shame, no
  pressure.
- British English throughout (colour, behaviour, optimise, personalise).
- **In public copy: no em dashes and no exclamation marks.** (This internal
  governing document may use em dashes; public artefacts may not.)
- The voice matches the product: a system that respects the user's judgement,
  not a hype machine.

## 9. Human voice: banned patterns

Readers distrust copy that reads as machine-written, and rightly so. Every
outward artefact must read as though a knowledgeable person wrote it for
another adult. The patterns below are the recognisable tells of generated
text. Each is banned in all public copy. An artefact containing any of them
**fails the gate** and is returned for a rewrite.

1. **Em dashes anywhere in public copy.** Already banned under Tone; it is the
   first tell here because it is the most reliable one. Use a full stop, a
   comma, or a semicolon.
   - Banned: `Volyume is calm — and it stays out of your way.`
   - Human: `Volyume is calm. It stays out of your way.`

2. **The "X, forever" / "X. Every time." dramatic fragment.** A clipped
   fragment used for false weight.
   - Banned: `Your data, forever.` / `Honest coaching. Every time.`
   - Human: `Your data stays encrypted on your device.`

3. **"It's not just X, it's Y" and every negation-pivot variant.** Includes
   `This isn't about X. It's about Y.`, `More than a tracker.`, `Not another
   fitness app.`
   - Banned: `It's not just a food diary, it's a coaching system.`
   - Human: `The food diary feeds the weekly coaching decision.`

4. **Triadic flourishes used decoratively.** Three adjectives or phrases in a
   row for rhythm rather than information; a list of three where the items
   carry no distinct content.
   - Banned: `Calm, clear and confident.`
   - Human: `The weekly decision tells you what changed and why.`

5. **Audience-sweep openers.** `Whether you're a beginner or a seasoned
   lifter...` and any variant that opens by naming both ends of a spectrum.
   - Banned: `Whether you're new to the gym or a seasoned lifter, Volyume has
     you covered.`
   - Human: `Volyume works from your own logged sets, whatever your training
     history.`

6. **Empty intensifiers and hype adjectives.** Banned words include:
   game-changer, seamless, effortless, unlock, elevate, supercharge, dive in,
   delve, robust, comprehensive, cutting-edge, revolutionise (and revolutionary,
   revolution). Describe the concrete thing instead.
   - Banned: `Unlock effortless, seamless tracking.`
   - Human: `Log a set in two taps.`

7. **Scene-setting and false-confiding openers.** `In today's fast-paced
   world...`, `Let's face it`, `Here's the thing`.
   - Banned: `In today's fast-paced world, who has time to plan training?`
   - Human: `Planning a week of training by hand takes time most people don't
     have.`

8. **Rhetorical-question openers answered in the next line.**
   - Banned: `Tired of guessing your macros? Volyume works them out for you.`
   - Human: `Volyume sets your macro targets from your own inputs.`

9. **Perfectly parallel sentence structures repeated three or more times.**
   The mechanical `Track your X. Build your Y. Reach your Z.` cadence.
   - Banned: `Log your sets. Track your food. Reach your goals.`
   - Human: `You log your sets and your food, and the weekly coach reads both.`

10. **Summary sign-offs.** `In conclusion`, `At the end of the day`, `The
    bottom line`, `Ultimately`. End on a concrete point, not a wrap-up phrase.
    - Banned: `At the end of the day, Volyume just works.`
    - Human: `That is the whole system: log, review, adjust.`

11. **Exclamation marks.** Already banned under Tone; restated. Never in public
    copy.
    - Banned: `Start your first plan today!`
    - Human: `Start your first plan today.`

12. **American spellings and vocabulary.** British English is checked word by
    word. `color` → `colour`, `optimize` → `optimise`, `gotten` → `got`,
    `personalize` → `personalise`, `favorite` → `favourite`, `math` → `maths`.
    `workout program` is acceptable only as the established industry term;
    prefer `training plan`. Any American spelling or idiom fails the gate.

13. **Emoji in body copy.** Never in articles, website copy, store listings or
    email. Allowed sparingly in social captions only where the platform's
    convention plainly expects it, and then never more than one in a caption.

**Write like this instead.** Write like a knowledgeable person talking to
another adult. Vary sentence length naturally; do not fall into a single
cadence. Prefer the concrete over the abstract and a specific number over a
vague claim. It is better to be plain than clever. When a sentence could be
said more simply, say it more simply.

**The gate FAILS any artefact that contains a banned pattern above.** There is
no borderline allowance: a single em dash, one hype adjective, one American
spelling, or one rhetorical-question opener is a FAIL, returned with the exact
offending sentence quoted.

## 10. The gate

- **Nothing is marked publish-ready without a recorded PASS from the
  compliance-reviewer agent that cites this document.**
- A PASS record names the artefact, the version, the reviewer, the date, and
  the specific sections checked (at minimum: Claim Rule, trial wording if the
  trial is mentioned, pricing if price is mentioned, prohibited list, qualified
  claims, ASA/CAP, tone).
- A **FAIL** returns the artefact to the writer with cited reasons — the
  section and the exact wording at fault — and a corrected draft is re-gated.
- Borderline calls are failed, not waved through, and escalated per the
  OPERATING-CHARTER autonomy boundaries.
- The gate is blocking. No autonomous or founder-tap publish lane may bypass
  it. A PASS is logged in the growth ledger alongside the published artefact.

This document is supreme. If any brief, template, or other instruction
conflicts with it, this document governs and the conflict is escalated.
